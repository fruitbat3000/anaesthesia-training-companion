# Proposal: widen the site to all stages of anaesthetic training

_Drafted 2026-09-27 for Mark to decide on. Nothing here is built yet. Sources are S13–S20 in `SOURCES.md`, all checked live on 2026-09-27._

## 1. What changed while we weren't looking

Two changes shape this proposal more than anything else.

**The FRCA changes format from July 2027** (subject to GMC approval; S13–S15). The curriculum and syllabus stay the same, but every component is replaced:

| Now (legacy, last year Jul 2026 – Jun 2027) | From July 2027 | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Primary MCQ (90 SBA, 3 h) | **Primary AKT** | 2 papers of 80 SBA, 2 h 20 each, on separate days. Remote, twice a year (Sept, March). Paper A: pharmacology 40, physiology 40. Paper B: physics 30, data interpretation & clinical measurement 35, anatomy 10, statistics 5. |
| Primary OSCE + SOE | **Primary CASE** (Clinical Anaesthetic Sciences Exam) | 13 stations of 9 min, 2 h 23 in total. Three diets a year (Oct, Feb, June). Integrated clinical scenarios, role players, one SimMan station, charts and data. Minimum mix per circuit, e.g. 4 pre-op, 5 intra-op, 3 physics/equipment, 2 physiology, 2 pharmacology. |
| Final written (CRQ + MCQ) | **Final AKT** | 1 paper of 100 SBA, 3 h, no CRQ. GA 50, POM 10, RA 10, other 30. First sitting January 2028. |
| Final SOE | **Final FCPE** (Final Clinical Performance Exam) | 12 stations of 9 min. Two diets a year (Nov, April). Stage 2 content only: the Primary sciences are no longer retested. The matrix sets at least 2 neuro, 2 cardiac and 2 airway stations per exam week. |

- Marking of CASE and FCPE uses "key features" plus behavioural domains, with a global rating used to set the pass mark by borderline regression. There are no killer stations.
- Transition year Jul 2027 – Jun 2028: legacy OSCE/SOE only for people holding a partial pass. Attempts reset to 1 on the new format (maximum 6 still applies).
- **The Trainer Support Pack you remembered** (RCoA, June 2026, S14) is exactly this. Its core message is that the exams move from recall to reasoning. It tells trainers to use 9-minute structured discussions, "think aloud" practice, SLEs, simulation and integrated science teaching. A slide deck is available by emailing exams@rcoa.ac.uk.
- There are **no official CASE/FCPE sample stations yet**, only sample AKT SBAs (S15). Almost nothing exists anywhere for station practice in the new format. That gap is where this site could be most useful.

**Curriculum v1.5 went live on 17 August 2026** (S16). Everyone was moved onto the v1.5 HALOs, and the transition runs to 2028/29. ARCP targets by year: CT1 EPAs 1–2, CT2 EPAs 3–4, CT3 all Stage 1 HALOs, ST5 all Stage 2 HALOs, ST7 all Stage 3 HALOs plus SIA(s).

## 3. Revision notes: a Primary and a Final "in brief"

"FRCA in a Box" was a set of flashcard books by Armstrong, Clifton and Davis (Primary and Final editions; Routledge). We can't copy them, and we shouldn't borrow the name. We can write our own set that is built for the new exams.

**What makes ours different**
- **Mapped to the syllabus codes.** The Primary syllabus v2.2 (Jan 2026) has 324 coded items, of which 214 are basic science (anatomy 40, physiology 91, pharmacology 83). The Stage 2 exam syllabus has about 320. Grouping these gives roughly **120 Primary cards and 100 Final cards**. Every card shows which codes it covers, so coverage can be checked.
- **Built for application.** Each card has: key facts (one screen), "why it matters clinically", "how it could come up in a CASE/FCPE station" (a 9-minute prompt), linked e-LA sessions, and 2–3 practice SBAs.
- **Final cards follow the Final's new scope.** They cover Stage 2 clinical content only, weighted like the AKT (half GA), with priority on neuro, cardiac and airway because the FCPE samples those most.
- e-LfH already hosts free **e-LA Revision Guides** for Primary pharmacology, physiology and physics (S17). Our cards should link to those rather than repeat them, and act as the syllabus-mapped index to them.

**A sample card (draft, unreviewed; for style only)**
> **Context-sensitive half-time (CSHT)** · covers 1_GA_G3_36, 1_GA_G3_37
> - CSHT is the time for plasma concentration to fall by 50% after stopping an infusion that has kept it steady. It depends on how long the infusion has run.
> - It is not the same as the elimination half-life. It reflects redistribution from peripheral compartments, which fill during long infusions.
> - Remifentanil's CSHT is short and almost independent of duration, because non-specific esterases clear it. Fentanyl's rises steeply with duration.
> - CSHT does not predict waking directly. Recovery may need a fall bigger or smaller than 50%.
> - **Station prompt:** "Your supervisor asks why you chose remifentanil TIVA for a 6-hour neurosurgical case. Explain, and tell them what you will do about analgesia at the end."

## 4. Question practice

**a) A curated, checked list (quick, low risk).** Free and official first:
- RCoA sample SBAs with explanations for the Primary and Final AKT (S15).
- e-LA Exam Archive (free with an NHS e-LfH login; S17): e-LA Primary FRCA Exam, e-LA Final FRCA Exam (General MCQs, SBA MCQs), Module 06 Exam Preparation, and RCoA Primary Course webcasts.
- Then the commercial and community banks that were live on 2026-09-27 (S19): Pastest Primary FRCA (1,000+ SBAs; £99 for 6 months, £119 for 12 months as listed), TeachMeAnaesthetics (Primary and Final SBAs), FRCAWise (Final SBAs), FRCA-Revision (question bank), MedPrep (multi-exam, includes FRCA), FRCA.ai, and Anaestheasier (free Final SBAs and 2027 exam guides).
- List them neutrally, with no endorsements, and say that most were written for the legacy formats. The SBA style carries over to the AKT. Nothing yet targets CASE/FCPE.

**b) Our own SBA bank (the bigger job).**
- Blueprinted to the AKT splits in section 1, so a "mock Paper A" really is 40 pharmacology + 40 physiology.
- Every question gets a stem, 5 options, an explanation of why each distractor is wrong, a source, and its syllabus code(s).
- Modes: by topic, timed mock paper, review wrong answers. Progress stays in the browser, like the current ticks. Each question has a "report a problem" link, like the topic feedback links.
- A realistic first release is **about 200 Primary and 150 Final questions**, growing over time.

**Sample SBA (draft, unreviewed)**
> A patient is having total intravenous anaesthesia for a 7-hour operation. The team wants the opioid's offset at the end to be as rapid as after a 1-hour case. Which property of remifentanil best explains why it meets this aim?
> A. High lipid solubility · B. Low pKa · C. Metabolism by non-specific plasma and tissue esterases · D. Small volume of distribution · E. High potency
> **Answer C.** Rapid esterase clearance keeps the context-sensitive half-time at a few minutes whatever the infusion's duration. A small Vd (D) helps, but it does not make offset independent of duration. B affects onset. A and E do not explain offset.

**c) CASE/FCPE station practice packs (the gap nobody fills yet).** Each pack is a 9-minute scenario for a trainee and a trainer or peer. It includes a candidate brief, examiner prompts and "what if" extensions, the key features to look for, and the domains being assessed, using the RCoA descriptor language. Packs are tagged to the station matrices (pre-op, intra-op, critically ill and so on). This is exactly what the Trainer Support Pack asks departments to run, so it could double as a resource for College Tutors.

## 5. The honest constraints

1. **Clinical review is the bottleneck, not writing.** I can draft cards, SBAs and stations quickly and cite a source for each. But mistakes in exam material do real harm, and a wrong answer key is worse than none. As a rough guide, reviewing takes about 5–10 minutes per card or question. 220 cards plus 350 SBAs is about **50–70 hours of consultant or senior-trainee time**. I suggest recruiting reviewers, for example recent FRCA passers for a first check and a consultant for sign-off, perhaps through YAIRN Exam Resources. Everything shows a visible "draft / reviewed" status, and reviewers are credited on the About page, in line with the credits decision.
2. **Doses and numbers.** The current rule leaves doses out. Revision notes will need some numbers (MAC, maximum local anaesthetic doses and so on). I propose allowing a number only when it has a cited source and has been through review.
3. **Copyright.** All notes and questions must be original. Nothing is paraphrased from textbooks, commercial banks or the RCoA's sample questions. We link to RCoA documents but don't reproduce them.
4. **Keeping it current.** The exam details change through 2027–28, and the RCoA says it will keep adding to the resource hub. The weekly link check helps, but the Exams hub needs a named owner and a review date.
5. **Technology.** `data.js` won't scale to hundreds of notes and questions. Proposal: write notes and questions as Markdown files with a small header, and add a tiny build step to the existing Pages workflow that turns them into JSON the site loads on demand. The site still opens from a folder.
6. **Name and URL.** "novice-anaesthetist-syllabus" no longer fits. Renaming the GitHub repo changes the Pages address, so the link you sent Dr Brooks would stop working. The options are to keep the repo name and only change the site title, or to rename it and send the new link.
