## What Stage 3 is for

Stage 3 develops you into a consultant: managing complex cases independently, leading teams and services, supervising and teaching, and taking part in management, quality improvement and research. Alongside the general Stage 3 outcomes, you complete **12 months (whole-time equivalent) in one or more Special Interest Areas (SIAs)**.

## Year by year

What the ARCP panel expects at the end of each year.

| Year | What the ARCP panel expects |
|---|---|
| **ST6** | Progress with Stage 3 capabilities in generic professional and clinical domains; evidence for SIAs if started in ST6. A CV is strongly recommended at the end-of-ST6 ARCP |
| **ST7** | **All Stage 3 HALOs** and **HALOs for your SIA(s)**; the Stage 3 certificate, post-dated to your CCT date; a final ARCP (Outcome 6), usually up to 4 months before the CCT date |

### The Stage 3 certificate

The **Stage 3 certificate** is signed by the Regional Adviser (or deputy) and one other designated trainer once all 14 Stage 3 HALOs are approved, and records the time spent in each SIA (totalling 12 months WTE). It replaces the old notification of completion of training.

### From certificate to CCT

The College then reviews your training record and recommends you to the GMC for the Specialist Register, which you must be on before taking up a substantive consultant post.

### Choosing and evidencing an SIA

Each SIA has its own outcomes and capabilities: see [all 26 SIAs on this site](#stage/3/sias), as tickable checklists with study links, or the [RCoA SIA learning syllabus](https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-3-special-interest-areas/introduction). Discuss your choices with your TPD early: SIA posts are finite and planned across the school.

## Subspecialties and dual training

### Subspecialties

- **Pre-Hospital Emergency Medicine (PHEM)** is a subspecialty of anaesthetics.
- **Paediatric Intensive Care Medicine (PICM)** became a recognised anaesthetics subspecialty in **May 2026**.

### Dual training and acting up

- **Dual CCT in Anaesthetics and Intensive Care Medicine**: joint RCoA and FICM guidance describes the overlap and timelines.
- **Acting up**: you may act up as a locum consultant in the last 3 months of training, provided everything else is complete.

## Preparing for consultant practice

### Leadership, teaching and your CV

- Build leadership and management evidence: rota and service design, governance, clinical leads, QI leadership.
- Teach and supervise, and consider formal training (for example an educational supervisor course).
- Keep an up-to-date CV and portfolio; prepare for consultant interviews (service development, governance, leadership scenarios).

### Looking after yourself

- Look after yourself: see [wellbeing and flexibility](#resources/wellbeing).
