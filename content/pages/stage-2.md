## What Stage 2 is for

By the end of Stage 2 you should manage most elective and emergency work, including more complex patients and surgery, with distant supervision, and have completed defined periods in **cardiothoracic, neuro-, paediatric and obstetric anaesthesia**. You will increasingly lead: running the emergency list, supervising juniors and teaching.

## Year by year

What the ARCP panel expects at the end of each year (ARCP checklist, v1.5 curriculum).

| Year | What the ARCP panel expects |
|---|---|
| **ST4** | Progress with Stage 2 capabilities across generic professional and clinical domains; SLEs, personal activities, reflections, an MTR, a satisfactory MSF and an ESSR. The RCoA recommends having passed the Final written exam |
| **ST5** | **All Stage 2 HALOs** and the **Stage 2 certificate**. The Final FRCA should be passed by the end of ST5 |

### The Stage 2 certificate

It needs:

- approval of all Stage 2 HALOs, with MTRs that between them cover all 14 domains (consider MTRs specifically for cardiothoracic, neuro, paediatric and obstetric placements)
- evidence of completing the **discrete areas of practice** (cardiothoracic, neuro, paediatric, obstetric), which can be collated on a **Completion of Capability Cluster ("Triple C") form**
- a pass in the **Final FRCA**
- a satisfactory MSF, an ESSR, and an Outcome 1 at ARCP
- involvement in audit or QI, at a level that increases with each stage.

### What changed in curriculum v1.5

> [!key] Curriculum v1.5 changes for Stage 2
> New airway key capabilities to be managed independently (CICO in simulation, preventing unrecognised oesophageal intubation, supraglottic airways, direct and video laryngoscopy, low-risk extubation), plus high-flow nasal oxygen, flexible bronchoscopic intubation, at-risk extubation and intubation through a supraglottic airway, and a described understanding of awake intubation. **Low thoracic epidural is no longer a required skill.** Paediatric emergencies move to Resuscitation and Transfer.

## The units of Stage 2

### The discrete areas of practice

These four must be completed in Stage 2, and can be collated on a Triple C form.

| Area | Examples of what to aim for | e-LA (Specialty Training) |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiothoracic | Cardiopulmonary bypass principles; one-lung ventilation; post-cardiac surgery complications | [Module 12](https://portal.e-lfh.org.uk/Catalogue/Index?HierarchyId=0_14_37565_33997&programmeId=14) (cardiac), [Module 13](https://portal.e-lfh.org.uk/Catalogue/Index?HierarchyId=0_14_37565_34048&programmeId=14) (thoracic) |
| Neuro | Raised ICP, neurosurgical and neuroradiology cases, spinal surgery, head injury transfer | [Module 14](https://portal.e-lfh.org.uk/Catalogue/Index?HierarchyId=0_14_37565_34065&programmeId=14) |
| Obstetrics | Complex obstetric cases, obstetric haemorrhage, pre-eclampsia, general anaesthesia for caesarean section | [Module 08](https://portal.e-lfh.org.uk/Catalogue/Index?HierarchyId=0_14_37565_8747&programmeId=14) |
| Paediatrics | Children aged 1–5 under direct supervision and over 5 under distant supervision; neonatal principles | [Core Module 04b](https://portal.e-lfh.org.uk/Catalogue/Index?HierarchyId=0_14_37596_8723&programmeId=14) |

## The Final FRCA

From 2027–28 the Final is a 100-question **AKT** (first sitting January 2028) and the **FCPE**, a 12-station clinical exam.
