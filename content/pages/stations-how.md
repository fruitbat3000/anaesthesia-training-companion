---
title: How to run a practice station
lede: Each pack is designed for two people: a candidate and an "examiner", who can be a peer, a senior trainee or a consultant. A station takes about 15 minutes including feedback.
---
:::steps
1. **Choose a station** that fits the candidate's exam (CASE for the Primary, FCPE for the Final) and the time you have.
2. **The examiner opens the Examiner view** on their own device and reads the scenario, prompts and key features. The candidate opens the **Candidate view**.
3. **Start the timer.** The candidate has 2 minutes to read the brief. The timer then runs the 9-minute station, with a beep one minute before the end.
4. **Run the station.** Let the candidate lead. Use the examiner prompts to move the scenario on and to ask the "what if" extension questions. Do not teach during the station.
5. **Mark as you go.** Tick key features as the candidate identifies them. At the end, rate each clinical performance skill and choose a global rating.
6. **Give feedback** (5 minutes). Start with what went well, then the most important key feature missed, then one thing to practise. Use "What good looks like" and the learning points.
7. **Save and copy the summary.** It can support a reflection or an SLE discussion on the LLP. Never include patient-identifiable information.
:::

## Tips for examiners

- Keep to time. The real exam moves on at nine minutes whatever has happened.
- Be neutral: nod, "thank you", "what would you do next?". Real examiners follow a script and do not signal whether an answer is right.
- The candidate should show reasoning, not just facts. If they state a fact, ask "why does that matter for this patient?"
- For communication stations, the examiner can play the patient, relative or colleague using the scenario notes.

## Tips for candidates

- Use the reading time to plan: what is the task, who is the patient, what are the risks, what must I not miss?
- **Think aloud.** Examiners can only credit reasoning they hear.
- Structure: gather information → prioritise → plan → explain → anticipate complications → check understanding.
- Safety first: if something is life-threatening, say so and deal with it before anything else.
