## What Stage 1 is for

By the end of Stage 1 you should be able to provide anaesthesia for ASA 1–3 adults having non-complex elective and emergency surgery with distant supervision, contribute safely to obstetric, paediatric and intensive care work, and understand the science that underpins it all. 
### The 14 domains of learning

The curriculum describes this through **14 domains of learning**: seven generic professional domains and seven clinical ones.

:::cols
::: card Generic professional domains
Professional behaviours and communication · Management and professional and regulatory requirements · Team working · Safety and quality improvement · Safeguarding · Education and training · Research and managing data
:::
::: card Clinical domains
Perioperative medicine and health promotion · General anaesthesia · Regional anaesthesia · Resuscitation and transfer · Procedural sedation · Pain · Intensive care medicine
:::
:::

## Year by year

What the ARCP panel expects at the end of each year (ARCP checklist, v1.5 curriculum).

| Year | What the ARCP panel expects |
|---|---|
| **CT1** | The **IAC** (EPAs 1 and 2; see the [novice section](#start)) plus progress across several Stage 1 domains: SLEs, personal activities, reflections, an MTR (in addition to the IAC MTR), a satisfactory MSF and an ESSR |
| **CT2** | **EPAs 3 and 4**, and some evidence in all Stage 1 domains (ICM may still be outstanding). Some HALOs, such as procedural sedation or generic professional domains, may be completed |
| **CT3** | **All Stage 1 HALOs** and the **Stage 1 certificate** (signed by the Regional Adviser or deputy and one other designated trainer). The Primary FRCA should be passed by the end of CT3 |

### The Stage 1 certificate

It needs:

- approval of all Stage 1 HALOs (one per domain), with MTRs that between them give feedback against all 14 domains
- a pass in the **Primary FRCA**
- the **Initial Assessment of Competence (IAC)** and the **Initial Assessment of Competence in Obstetric Anaesthesia (IACOA)**
- a satisfactory MSF (at least 12 responses) and an ESSR submitted two weeks before the ARCP
- evidence of involvement in audit or quality improvement.

### What changed in curriculum v1.5

> [!key] Curriculum v1.5 (from 17 August 2026)
> All trainees moved automatically onto v1.5 HALOs. The main Stage 1 changes: new **airway key capabilities** (CICO and front-of-neck access in simulation, preventing unrecognised oesophageal intubation, supraglottic airways, direct and video laryngoscopy, low-risk extubation, and described skills such as high-flow nasal oxygen and flexible bronchoscopic intubation); **practical procedures** now sit inside the HALOs rather than a separate grid; and **paediatric emergencies** move from ICM to Resuscitation and Transfer. HALOs signed off under v1.4 remain valid. See the [RCoA v1.5 guidance](https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/training-careers/training-hub/2021-anaesthetics-curriculum/2021-curriculum-v15-guidance-resources).

## Clinical blocks in Stage 1

What to aim for in each area, and the matching e-Learning Anaesthesia (e-LA) module.

| Area | What to aim for | e-Learning Anaesthesia |
|---|---|---|
| Novice period | IAC within 3–6 months | [Module 01](#start) |
| Obstetrics | IACOA; labour analgesia, neuraxial anaesthesia for caesarean section, obstetric emergencies | Module 04a |
| Paediatrics | Anaesthesia for children aged 5 and over; paediatric resuscitation | Module 04b |
| Elderly and frail patients | Perioperative care of older people, hip fracture | Module 04c |
| Intensive care | Recognition and stabilisation of the critically ill; invasive procedures; transfer | Module 03 |
| Pain | Acute and acute-on-chronic pain, PCA and epidurals | Module 05a |
| Regional | Spinals, simple ultrasound-guided blocks (including femoral and fascia iliaca), epidurals | Module 05b |
| Basic sciences | Physiology, pharmacology, physics and clinical measurement for the Primary | Modules 07a–07e |
