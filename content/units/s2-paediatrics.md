> [!key] The Triple C form
> This is one of the four discrete areas of practice (cardiothoracic, neuro, obstetric and paediatric anaesthesia). A designated member of your Assessment Faculty with experience in the area can sign off its key capabilities on a **Completion of Capability Cluster ("Triple C") form**. You need the capabilities, suitable experience and logbook data, and a Multiple Trainer Report. If your ES can assess the evidence directly, a Triple C form is not strictly required.

## What the block is like

Usually a block in a specialist children's hospital, with exposure to younger children, complex medical problems and neonates, alongside DGH-style lists. Paediatric emergencies and the stabilisation of a sick child before transfer are a key part of Stage 2.

## Cases to seek out

- Lists for children aged 1–5: ENT, dental, urology and general surgery.
- Pyloromyotomy and other common neonatal or infant emergencies.
- Children with congenital heart disease, syndromes or complex airways (observe and plan).
- Paediatric resuscitation and stabilisation for transfer.

## Getting the evidence

- DOPS on airway management and vascular access in small children.
- CBDs on neonatal physiology and complex children.
- Neonatal and paediatric life support courses.
