## What the block is like

Labour ward is fast, busy and shared with midwives, obstetricians and neonatologists. You will site labour epidurals, give spinal anaesthesia for caesarean section, top up epidurals for theatre, and be part of the team for obstetric emergencies. Most of the work is urgent, much of it out of hours, and communication with the woman and her birth partner is central.

## Signing off the IACOA

- Record SLEs (DOPS for epidurals and spinals, CBDs and A-CEXs for cases) and link them to the EPA 3 and EPA 4 HALOs on the LLP.
- Link a completed **Multiple Trainer Report** to the IACOA certificate.
- Add obstetric simulation as a personal activity (type "Simulation training") and link it to the IACOA milestone.
- The RCoA's IACOA workbook (EPAs 3 and 4) sets out the details. Read it early and agree a plan with your obstetric lead.

In most departments you join the resident obstetric rota once the IACOA is signed off. Check your local arrangements.

## Cases to seek out

- Labour epidurals and combined spinal–epidurals, including the difficult back and the inadequate block.
- Category 1–4 caesarean sections, converting epidural analgesia to anaesthesia.
- General anaesthesia for caesarean section (in simulation if not in practice).
- Postpartum haemorrhage, pre-eclampsia and the post-dural puncture headache review.

> [!tip] Make it count
> Ask for a DOPS on your first few epidurals and again once you feel confident: the change in supervision level is the evidence.
