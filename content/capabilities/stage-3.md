## pbc | generic | Professional behaviours and communication
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-3/professional-behaviours-communication
outcome: Shows the professional values and behaviours needed to be a consultant.
- A: Leads the management of complications that arise during care.
- B: Plans care for patients with complex needs, including beyond guidelines, knowing their own limits and asking for help.
- C: Takes part in annual appraisal, and explains job planning, performance management and revalidation.
- D: Follows governance frameworks and helps the whole team do so.
- E: Looks after their own health and wellbeing and supports others', identifying colleagues in difficulty and supporting or escalating.
- F: Works within equality and diversity legislation.
evidence: Running lists as sole anaesthetist, including in your SIA (ALMAT) · leading MDT discussion of a complex patient · shared decision-making · leadership, management and equality training · a consultant-ready portfolio and CV · handling a difficult conversation · leading a QI project · time with governance leads, clinical and medical directors · experience of the complaints process · a satisfactory MSF
notes: professional-leadership

## sqi | generic | Safety and quality improvement
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-3/safety-quality-improvement
outcome: Supervises a local QI project and takes part in regional or national ones, and uses a systems approach to safety.
### Quality improvement
- A: Identifies and supervises a QI project in a special interest area, choosing measures that matter to patients.
- B: Explains how complexity theory applies to healthcare.
- C: Identifies the levers, drivers and psychology of change management that build a shared purpose.
- D: Identifies and engages the stakeholders affected by change.
- E: Understands how psychology, systems, processes and technical knowledge interact when implementing change.
- F: Works collaboratively on QI, using patient co-design where possible.
- G: Describes how to sustain improvement.
- H: Evaluates the impact of a QI intervention.
### Safety science
- I: Applies safety science at individual, team, organisational and system level.
- J: Uses measures of process reliability to monitor and improve safety.
- K: Predicts how system failures will put patients at risk.
- L: Uses a systems approach to assess risk proactively and to investigate incidents.
- M: Acts on national regulation and national patient safety findings.
- N: Explains how organisational culture drives failure or improvement.
- O: Analyses the strengths and weaknesses of safety interventions.
- P: Quantifies how context affects safety.
- Q: Addresses the limits of "human error" as an explanation in investigations.
- R: Reduces the effect of fixation error and unconscious and cognitive bias.
evidence: Leading departmental QI and taking part in regional or national projects (A-QIPAT) · presenting and implementing results · courses in QI, risk, change management, patient safety law, human factors, complexity and safety science · a root cause analysis or other investigation · mortality reviews · presenting at governance meetings
notes: qi-safety-stage2, sustainability-medication, safety-human-factors

## ga | clinical | General anaesthesia
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-3/general-anaesthesia
outcome: Gives safe, effective general anaesthesia independently for all non-specialist procedures and in defined special interest areas.
### Independent practice
- A [4]: Anaesthetises all patients for elective and emergency surgery in general settings, including maternity units, for common complex procedures.
- B [4]: Makes the decisions and organises lists so that care is safe, effective and efficient.
- C [4]: Applies understanding of comorbidity to give individualised care.
- E: Contributes to departmental expertise in one or more special interest areas.
- H [4]: Anaesthetises patients for procedures outside theatre, including remote sites.
- J [4]: Cares for high-risk surgical patients with significant comorbidity and the potential for massive haemorrhage.
### Trauma and the critically ill
- D [3]: Cares for multiply injured patients from arrival to definitive treatment, including complex cases such as severe burns or poisoning.
- I [3]: Anaesthetises critically ill patients returning to theatre from ICU.
### Airway (updated in v1.5)
- F [3]: Manages anticipated difficult airways safely and knows when more help is needed.
- G [3]: Manages the challenges of shared-airway surgery.
- P [3]: Manages awake tracheal intubation and knows when more help is needed.
- Q [4]: Manages "can't intubate, can't oxygenate" (including emergency front-of-neck access) in simulation, and describes current guidelines.
- R [4]: Uses high-flow nasal oxygen safely for pre-oxygenation in spontaneously breathing patients.
- S [4]: Intubates with a flexible bronchoscope.
- T [4]: Manages at-risk extubation safely.
- U [4]: Intubates through a supraglottic airway device, in a patient or in simulation.
### Coexisting neuro and cardiac disease
- K [3]: Manages patients with previous neurosurgery or intracranial pathology having unrelated surgery.
- L [3]: Manages patients with congenital or acquired heart disease having unrelated surgery, and refers to a specialist centre when appropriate.
### Obstetric and paediatric anaesthesia
- M [4]: Gives any elective or emergency obstetric anaesthetic in a general maternity unit.
- N [2b/3]: Anaesthetises children aged 1 and over for common non-complex elective and emergency surgery (ages 1–3 at 2b; 3 and over at a minimum of 3).
- O [3]: Provides emergency anaesthesia for children awaiting transfer to a tertiary unit.
evidence: CBDs and ALMATs across specialties, SIA and out of hours · major trauma centre and burns unit experience · ENT and maxillofacial work · ECT, radiology and interventional cardiology or neuroradiology · returns to theatre from ICU · SIA teaching, QI, guidelines and presentations · adult congenital heart disease learning · obstetric clinics · paediatric resuscitation courses
notes: complex-airway-ati, trauma-haemorrhage, burns-environmental, major-abdominal-surgery, valve-disease, neuroanaesthesia-principles, obstetric-ga-haemorrhage, paeds-common-surgery, paeds-complex, remote-ect-mri
stations: fcpe-airway-difficult-planning, fcpe-aaa-rupture, fcpe-obstetric-haemorrhage, fcpe-paeds-pyloric
