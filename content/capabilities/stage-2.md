## pbc | generic | Professional behaviours and communication
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-2/professional-behaviours-communication
outcome: Shows the professional values and behaviours expected of a senior anaesthetist in training.
- A: Guides and advises less experienced colleagues on professional matters.
- B: Makes management plans for patients with complex needs, recognises the limits of their own competence and asks for help.
- C: Recognises and reflects on how their own and others' behaviour affects care and patient safety.
- D: Is a good role model for junior colleagues and the wider team.
- E: Behaves professionally and keeps situation awareness in difficult situations such as emergencies.
- F: Breaks bad news to patients and relatives sensitively, with awareness of cultural and social differences.
- G: Describes how working patterns and lifestyle affect physical and mental health, and takes steps to limit the impact.
evidence: SLEs on a conversation with relatives on ICU, a complex-needs plan shared with the team, behaviour in an emergency, leading a list (ALMAT) and prescribing · resuscitation, communication and transfer courses · applying the Caldicott principles · junior doctors' forum · teaching with feedback · a satisfactory MSF
notes: professional-leadership, elderly-cognition
stations: fcpe-professional-error, fcpe-pom-frail-laparotomy

## mprr | generic | Management and professional and regulatory requirements
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-2/management-professional-regulatory-requirements
outcome: Understands and carries out the managerial, administrative and organisational roles expected of a senior anaesthetist in training.
- A: Takes part in organising anaesthetic services within the hospital, and understands links to regional tertiary services.
- B: Applies legal and ethical guidance, including the law on consent and shared decision-making.
- C: Engages with the department's management structures for delivering perioperative and anaesthetic services.
- D: Works effectively with digital systems for patient care.
evidence: SLEs using hospital IT, e-prescribing and electronic records · shared decision-making in pre-op assessment · consent for procedures · running a departmental project (teaching programme, QI project or trainee rota) · departmental business meetings · learning on NHS structure and NICE shared decision-making guidance
notes: professional-leadership, consent-capacity-safeguarding

## sqi | generic | Safety and quality improvement
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-2/safety-quality-improvement
outcome: Can lead a local quality improvement project, and applies patient safety principles across the hospital.
- A: Knows when and how to use improvement science to improve services safely.
- B: Recognises what makes care reliable.
- C: Understands variation in measurement, and tells expected from unwarranted variation.
- D: Uses the right measures to show whether a change has happened and what effect it had.
- E: Contrasts data for assurance with data for improvement, and uses both properly.
- F: Uses simple proactive safety tools, including assessing the likelihood and severity of risk.
- G: Matches expertise and resources to the level of clinical risk.
- H: Describes how anaesthetists' actions affect safety across the hospital and wider system.
- I: Describes the principles of medication safety.
- J: Explains how critical incidents are followed up.
evidence: Leading, presenting and implementing a local QI project (with an A-QIPAT) · completing incident forms · Stop Before You Block and the WHO checklist · QI, medicines management and human factors training · reflection on a critical incident · helping with an incident investigation · QI meetings
notes: qi-safety-stage2, sustainability-medication, research-appraisal
stations: fcpe-professional-error

## sg | generic | Safeguarding
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-2/safeguarding
outcome: Recognises safeguarding concerns in patients and healthcare professionals.
- A: Identifies, documents and acts on child protection and vulnerable patient concerns.
- B: Communicates with the right teams, with attention to confidentiality, consent, information sharing and data protection.
- C: Applies the six principles of adult safeguarding.
- D: Applies mental capacity law to protect individuals and society, and to obtain valid consent.
- E: Describes the needs of people with learning disabilities, autism, acute confusion, dementia and mental illness.
evidence: Consent with a child or adolescent and their parents · knowing the local route for child safeguarding referral · cases with safeguarding concerns · adjusting pre-op assessment and consent for vulnerable patients · mandatory safeguarding, information governance and MCA training · experience of an Independent Mental Capacity Advocate
notes: paeds-consent-safeguarding, elderly-cognition, consent-capacity-safeguarding

## rmd | generic | Research and managing data
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-2/research-managing-data
outcome: Is research ready: appraises evidence critically, understands data management and research methods, and explains evidence to patients and colleagues.
- A: Judges the quality of research and its place in the literature before changing practice.
- B: Explains the strengths and limits of the evidence behind practice to patients, the public and colleagues.
- C: Develops critical appraisal skills.
- D: Describes the main ways research improves outcomes: trials, stratified medicine, genomics, informatics, qualitative methods, systematic review and meta-analysis, and health services research.
- E: Explains data protection in research.
- F: Describes research governance, including ethics committees, translation into practice and the roles of Trust and university R&D.
- G: Uses a range of statistical techniques and understands their strengths and limits.
evidence: A CBD on interpreting evidence · data collection for a local, regional or national study · critical appraisal at journal club · a poster or presentation · writing a local guideline · trainee research network activity · a GCP certificate · research methods or information governance courses
notes: research-appraisal, stats-hypothesis-tests, stats-study-design

## pom | clinical | Perioperative medicine and health promotion
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-2/perioperative-medicine-health-promotion
outcome: Works with patients to reduce the risks of surgery.
### Risk and optimisation
- A [3]: Gives high-quality, individual perioperative care to ASA 1–4 elective and ASA 1–3 emergency patients, focused on experience and outcome.
- B [3]: Works with other professionals to optimise care.
- C [3]: Explains the principles of shared decision-making.
- D [3]: Plans how to manage less common cardiovascular, neurological, respiratory, endocrine, haematological and rheumatological disease around surgery.
- E [3]: Understands how integrated care pathways affect outcome.
- F [3]: Describes the uses and limits of common risk scores.
- G [3]: Recognises when advanced physiological testing (such as CPET) is needed and uses the results to stratify risk.
- H [3]: Applies basic science to perioperative care.
- O [2b]: Manages haematological problems around surgery, including anaemia and coagulopathy.
- P [2b]: Recognises poor nutritional status and reduces its risks.
- Q [2b]: Adjusts care for coexisting disease and complex surgery, including frailty, cognitive impairment, substance misuse and obesity.
### Health promotion and values
- I [2b]: Applies public health interventions such as smoking cessation, weight loss and reducing alcohol intake.
- J [2b]: Recognises that healthcare interventions can cause harm.
- K [2b]: Explains how religious, cultural and lifestyle factors affect choices about blood, implants and animal-derived products.
- L: Describes the needs and roles of carers around surgery and applies this in practice.
### Critical care and end of life
- M [3]: Describes when postoperative organ support is needed, and its limits.
- N [3]: Provides end-of-life care as part of the multidisciplinary team.
### Children and pregnancy
- R [2a/2b]: Adjusts perioperative care for children with coexisting disease (ages 1–5 at 2a; 5 and over at 2b).
- S [3]: Plans obstetric anaesthetic care for all women with the wider multidisciplinary team.
- T [3]: Recognises and manages critical illness in pregnancy, including immediate resuscitation, and leads the care of acute obstetric emergencies.
evidence: SLEs across emergency surgery, obstetrics, paediatrics, neuro and cardiac work, and in pre-op clinics · prehabilitation and smoking cessation work in clinic · blood product guidance for Jehovah's Witnesses · conversations with relatives on ICU · local and national anaemia guidance · obstetric anaesthesia clinics · learning on risk scores and CPET · the Final FRCA
notes: cardiac-risk-assessment, cpet-risk, anticoag-anaemia-pom, haematology-pom, endocrine-disease-pom, renal-liver-pom, respiratory-neuro-pom, elderly-cognition, emergency-laparotomy, pre-eclampsia-hellp
stations: fcpe-pom-frail-laparotomy, fcpe-paeds-pyloric

## ga | clinical | General anaesthesia
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-2/general-anaesthesia
outcome: Gives safe, effective general anaesthesia with distant supervision to ASA 1–3 patients for non-complex elective and emergency surgery in any setting.
### Core anaesthesia
- A: Explains, and puts into practice, safe anaesthesia at the extremes of age: neonates, children, and older people with frailty.
- B [3]: Provides day-case anaesthesia in any setting.
- C [2b]: Describes intraoperative haemostasis and manages major haemorrhage.
- G [3]: Recognises and manages the risks of positioning during surgery, including in obese patients.
- H: Applies anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pharmacology, physics and clinical measurement to practice.
- K [3]: Explains the problems of laparoscopic, endoscopic and open surgery (including major blood loss), and anaesthetises ASA 1–3 adults for them.
- L [3]: Anaesthetises ASA 1–3 adults for procedures outside theatre within the hospital, and recognises when this is inappropriate.
- X [2b]: Uses TIVA safely in all areas of practice.
### Trauma and head injury
- D [2b]: Cares for ASA 1–3 adults with multiple injuries from arrival to postoperative care, and gets help appropriately.
- E [2b]: Describes the anaesthetic problems of burns, poisoning, electrical injury and drowning.
- F [2b]: Applies physiology and pharmacology to reduce secondary brain injury after severe head injury.
### Airway (updated in v1.5)
- I [3]: Plans and delivers a safe airway strategy for known or possible difficult airways.
- J [3]: Manages non-complex shared-airway surgery.
- Y [4]: Manages "can't intubate, can't oxygenate" (including emergency front-of-neck access) in simulation, and describes current guidelines.
- Z [4]: Describes and uses measures to prevent unrecognised oesophageal intubation.
- AA [4]: Uses supraglottic airway devices safely.
- AB [4]: Intubates safely with direct laryngoscopy.
- AC [4]: Intubates safely with video laryngoscopy.
- AD [4]: Manages low-risk extubation safely.
- AE [2b]: Uses high-flow nasal oxygen for pre-oxygenation safely.
- AF [2b]: Intubates with a flexible bronchoscope.
- AG [2b]: Manages at-risk extubation safely.
- AH [2b]: Intubates through a supraglottic airway device, in a patient or in simulation.
- AI [2a]: Describes awake intubation.
### Neuroanaesthesia
- M [2a]: Applies anatomy, physiology and pharmacology to neurosurgical patients.
- N [2a]: Anaesthetises ASA 1–3 adults for simple elective and emergency intracranial, spinal and neuroradiology procedures.
### Cardiothoracic anaesthesia
- O [2a]: Applies basic science and clinical principles to cardiac and thoracic surgery.
- P [2a]: Describes anaesthesia for on- and off-bypass cardiac surgery and for thoracic surgery.
- Q [2a]: Anaesthetises ASA 1–3 adults for elective revascularisation, valve surgery and cardiology procedures under direct supervision.
- R [2a]: Anaesthetises adults for non-complex thoracic procedures, including one-lung ventilation, under direct supervision.
### Ophthalmic, obstetric and paediatric anaesthesia
- S: Explains the anaesthetic implications of eye surgery, especially penetrating eye injury and intraocular gas.
- T [3]: Anaesthetises elective and emergency obstetric patients, including those with comorbidity and obstetric complications.
- U [2a/2b]: Anaesthetises ASA 1–3 children for non-complex surgery: ages 1–5 with direct supervision (2a), 5 and over with distant supervision (2b).
- V: Explains anaesthesia for children of any age with complex medical problems or complex surgery.
- W: Explains the principles of general anaesthesia for neonates.
evidence: SLEs across specialties, out-of-hours work, a major trauma centre, neurosurgery, cardiac and thoracic surgery, ENT and maxillofacial surgery, obstetrics and paediatrics · trauma, airway, obstetric and paediatric simulation · blood transfusion training · learning on the obese patient and ophthalmic anaesthesia · obstetric anaesthesia clinics · the Final FRCA
notes: complex-airway-ati, tracheostomy-laryngectomy, ent-laser-jet, obstructed-airway-infections, trauma-haemorrhage, burns-environmental, tbi-neurocritical, neuroanaesthesia-principles, neurosurgical-procedures, cardiac-surgery-cpb, thoracic-olv, valve-disease, ophthalmic-anaesthesia, obstetric-ga-haemorrhage, obstetric-emergencies, paeds-common-surgery, paeds-complex, neonatal-anaesthesia, major-abdominal-surgery, aortic-surgery, tiva-positioning, remote-ect-mri
stations: fcpe-airway-difficult-planning, fcpe-aaa-rupture, fcpe-neuro-sah, fcpe-cardiac-post-cabg, fcpe-obstetric-haemorrhage, fcpe-paeds-pyloric
ela: 08, 09, 11, 12, 13, 14, 04b

## ra | clinical | Regional anaesthesia
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-2/regional-anaesthesia
outcome: Performs a wider range of regional anaesthetic techniques.
### Blocks
- A [3]: Performs ultrasound-guided brachial plexus blocks.
- B [3]: Performs ultrasound-guided fascial plane blocks of the chest or abdominal wall.
- C [3]: Gets an optimal ultrasound image and recognises common artefacts.
- J [3]: Performs ultrasound-guided lower limb blocks for lower limb surgery.
- D: Describes ophthalmic blocks for awake eye surgery.
### Judgement and complications
- E [3]: Involves patients in planning, and in understanding possible complications.
- F [3]: Recognises when a regional technique is not appropriate.
- G [3]: Manages an inadequate block, awake or in recovery when used with general anaesthesia.
- H: Describes the longer-term management of regional complications.
- I: Discusses regional anaesthesia when coagulation is abnormal.
evidence: DOPS for blocks · part-task simulators and ultrasound or regional courses · pre-op and obstetric clinics · pain rounds and postnatal follow-up · learning on regional complications · local and national guidance on regional anaesthesia with anticoagulants · the joint RA-UK/RCoA trainer guidance

## icm | clinical | Intensive care

outcome: Gives safe, effective care to critically ill patients with specialist help and guidance. Levels here are FICM capability levels (0–4).
- A [FICM 3]: Recognises the limits of intensive care and uses appropriate admission criteria.
- B [FICM 3]: Performs the invasive procedures needed for organ support: central, arterial and dialysis lines, ultrasound-guided cannulation, and chest drain and needle decompression in simulation.
- C [FICM 3]: Recognises, assesses and starts managing single or multiple organ failure.
- E [FICM 3]: Recognises and manages sepsis and follows local infection control policy.
- F [FICM 3]: Requests and interprets laboratory tests and imaging during a critical care stay.
- G [FICM 3]: Manages the medical and surgical needs and organ support of critically ill patients, with holistic care for patients and relatives.
- H [FICM 3]: Plans and communicates discharge from intensive care.
- I [FICM 2]: Manages end-of-life care in intensive care with patients, relatives and the team.
- J [FICM 1]: Works with transplant services, performs brainstem death testing and supports the organ donor.
- K [FICM 2]: Supports staff outside the ICU to detect deteriorating patients early.
evidence: SLEs on relevant ICU cases · procedures in theatre or ICU · adult resuscitation courses and simulation · reading or e-learning on organ donation
