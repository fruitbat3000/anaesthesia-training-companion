## pbc | generic | Professional behaviours and communication
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-1/professional-behaviours-communication
outcome: Shows the professional values and behaviours expected of a doctor in training.
- A: Shows the personal and professional values set out in the GMC's Good Medical Practice.
- B: Communicates well with patients, relatives and the multidisciplinary team, including being open and honest when things go wrong.
- C: Uses good interpersonal skills: empathy, compassion, courtesy and respect.
- D: Understands how their own behaviour affects patients and colleagues, and adjusts it.
- E: Reflects on their own practice to gain insight and learn from experience.
- F: Obtains valid consent in line with legal and professional principles.
- G: Takes part in the GMC National Training Survey and other quality processes required by the regulator.
- H: Keeps accurate, legible, contemporaneous clinical notes.
- I: Recognises the importance of physical and mental health, in themselves and others.
evidence: Logbook across theatre specialties, obstetrics, pre-op clinic and ICU · SLEs on pre-op communication, record keeping, handover, prescribing and the WHO checklist · a duty-of-candour discussion · an up-to-date LLP with regular ES meetings · the GMC survey · reflections on good and poor team behaviour · MSF · thank-you letters and emails
notes: consent-capacity-safeguarding, safety-human-factors, preop-assessment
stations: case-comms-cancelled-child, case-preop-asthma-consent

## sqi | generic | Safety and quality improvement
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-1-unapproved/safety-quality-improvement
outcome: Understands and applies quality improvement methods, and applies the principles of patient safety to their own practice.
- A: Describes the theories and methods of quality improvement.
- B: Contrasts quantitative and qualitative analysis, and the tools used to understand a system.
- C: Compares audit, research and quality improvement.
- D: Commits to continuous quality improvement.
- E: Describes common threats to patient safety in theatre and around surgery, and how routine practice reduces them.
- F: Describes how sharing problems through incident reporting and morbidity and mortality reviews improves care.
- G: Recognises and rewards excellence in quality and safety, not just errors.
- H: Shows the importance of non-technical skills: situation awareness, task management, decision-making and team working.
- I: Understands the role of interpersonal skills, structured communication and cognitive aids in emergencies, and of simulation to rehearse them.
- J: Describes what technology and equipment can and cannot do for patient safety.
- K: Describes how to raise concerns.
- L: Explains and practises the duty of candour.
- M: Prescribes and gives drugs safely.
evidence: Involvement in departmental QI (the minimum requirement) · an A-QIPAT for a project · learning from critical incidents, briefs and debriefs · ALMAT · safe prescribing · QI training · excellence and incident reports · crisis resource management simulation · governance meetings · reading on duty of candour · MSF
notes: safety-human-factors, stats-study-design, critical-incidents

## pom | clinical | Perioperative medicine and health promotion
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-1/perioperative-medicine-health-promotion
outcome: Identifies the clinical and social factors that increase risk for surgical patients, and understands sustainable clinical practice.
### Assessment and risk
- A [2b]: Explains the patient, anaesthetic and surgical factors that affect outcome.
- B [2b]: Assesses ASA 1–3 patients before surgery in a structured way, and recognises when more assessment or optimisation is needed.
- C [2b]: Explains how coexisting disease, its treatment and the surgery affect the anaesthetic, and plans accordingly.
- D [2b]: Explains individual anaesthetic and analgesic options and risks to patients.
- E [2b]: Describes why perioperative nutrition and fasting matter.
- F [2b]: Recognises and meets the needs of frail and older patients and those with cognitive impairment.
- G: Takes the patient's informed preferences into account when obtaining consent.
### Health, infection and sustainability
- H: Describes how socioeconomic, environmental and lifestyle factors affect health.
- I: Identifies opportunities to educate patients about their health.
- J [2b]: Uses antibiotic prophylaxis appropriately, and prevents and treats infection.
- K: Explains the environmental impact of healthcare and the principles of sustainable practice.
- L [2b]: Applies local VTE prevention policy and understands its implications for anaesthesia.
### Specific groups and pathways
- M [2a]: Explains the perioperative needs of children, including managing anxiety.
- N [2b]: Explains the perioperative implications of pregnancy, and starts management of common serious pregnancy-related disease.
- O [2b]: Liaises with critical care about postoperative care when appropriate.
- P: Describes the perioperative requirements of day-case surgery.
- Q [3]: Prescribes and gives blood products safely.
evidence: EPAs 1, 2 and 4 · SLEs across specialties and in pre-op assessment clinics · SLEs in paediatrics and obstetrics · blood transfusion training · reflection on reading, e-learning or courses about sustainability

## ga | clinical | General anaesthesia
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-1/general-anaesthesia
outcome: Gives safe, effective general anaesthesia with distant supervision for non-complex elective and emergency surgery in a general hospital.
### Core anaesthesia
- A [2b]: Carries out thorough pre-anaesthetic and pre-operative checks.
- B [2b]: Manages inhalational and intravenous induction and maintenance, extubation and emergence safely.
- C [2b]: Plans recovery care and uses safe discharge criteria.
- D [2b]: Diagnoses and manages common perioperative complications.
- E [2b]: Recognises anaesthetic critical incidents and explains their causes and management.
- F: Knows standard anaesthetic equipment and the physics and clinical measurement behind it.
- G: Knows the anatomy, physiology, biochemistry and pharmacology relevant to anaesthesia.
- H [2b]: Gives safe general anaesthesia to ASA 1–3 adults for non-complex elective and emergency surgery in general theatres.
- I [2b]: Describes the specific needs of obese, frail and older patients having general anaesthesia.
- J [2b]: Manages intraoperative fluid balance appropriately.
- S [2a]: Describes the principles of TIVA and uses it safely for non-complex cases.
### Airway (updated in v1.5)
- K [2a]: Identifies patients with known or difficult airways and gets help appropriately.
- L [2a]: Recognises the challenges of shared-airway surgery.
- T [2a]: Plans a safe airway strategy for patients with a known or possible difficult airway.
- U [3]: Manages "can't intubate, can't oxygenate" (including emergency front-of-neck access) in simulation, and describes current difficult airway guidelines.
- V [3]: Describes and uses measures to prevent unrecognised oesophageal intubation.
- W [3]: Uses supraglottic airway devices safely.
- X [3]: Intubates safely with direct laryngoscopy.
- Y [3]: Intubates safely with video laryngoscopy.
- Z [3]: Manages low-risk extubation safely.
- AA [1]: Uses high-flow nasal oxygen for pre-oxygenation safely.
- AB [1]: Describes intubation with a flexible bronchoscope.
- AC [1]: Describes intubation through a supraglottic device.
- AD [1]: Manages at-risk extubation safely.
### Outside theatre and trauma
- M [2a]: Gives safe anaesthesia to ASA 1–2 adults for procedures outside theatre (for example radiology, CT, MRI, ECT, the emergency department).
- N [1]: Explains the principles of anaesthesia for major trauma.
### Paediatric anaesthesia
- O: Explains how children differ from adults anatomically, physiologically, psychologically and pharmacologically, and what that means for safe anaesthesia.
- P [2a/2b]: Gives safe general anaesthesia to ASA 1–2 children for non-complex surgery: from age 5 with local supervision (2a), and over 10 with distant supervision (2b).
### Obstetric anaesthesia
- Q [3]: Explains the anaesthetic implications of pregnancy, and gives safe general anaesthesia to ASA 1–3 obstetric patients.
- R [3]: Resuscitates and cares for patients with acute obstetric emergencies, and recognises when more help is needed.
evidence: EPAs 1–4 · SLEs across surgical specialties, including ENT and head and neck for airway work · simulation of critical incidents and airway emergencies · experience in radiology, CT, MRI, ECT and the emergency department · trauma resuscitation in the ED or simulation · paediatric and obstetric SLEs · the Primary FRCA (for F and G)
notes: airway-assessment, difficult-airway-cico, rsi-aspiration, iv-induction-agents, extubation-recovery, critical-incidents, pk-csht-tci, obstetric-basics, paeds-basics, trauma-transfer, ent-shared-airway
stations: case-intraop-hypoxia, case-remi-tiva, case-equipment-machine-check, case-outside-mri-child
ela: 01, 04a, 04b

## ra | clinical | Regional anaesthesia
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-1/regional-anaesthesia
outcome: Performs simple peripheral nerve blocks, and spinal and lumbar epidural anaesthesia or analgesia, independently.
### Principles
- A [3]: Explains the risks and benefits of regional anaesthesia clearly to patients.
- B [3]: Describes the indications and contraindications for regional techniques.
- C [3]: Takes steps to prevent wrong-site blocks ("Stop Before You Block").
- J: Discusses the science of ultrasound and how images are generated.
- K: Discusses the drugs and equipment used in regional anaesthesia.
### Blocks
- D [3]: Performs spinal anaesthesia for ASA 1–3 surgical patients.
- E [2a/2b]: Performs simple peripheral nerve blocks with ultrasound (for example a wrist block at 2b; an ultrasound-guided upper limb plexus block at 2a).
- F [2b]: Performs ultrasound-guided femoral or fascia iliaca blocks.
### Obstetric regional anaesthesia
- G [3]: Recognises and starts managing complications of regional anaesthesia, including local anaesthetic toxicity, high spinal and post-dural puncture headache.
- H [3]: Provides epidural or combined spinal–epidural labour analgesia for ASA 1–3 women, and offers alternatives when neuraxial analgesia is contraindicated.
- I [3]: Provides neuraxial anaesthesia for operative delivery and other obstetric procedures in ASA 1–3 women, and manages an inadequate block.
evidence: EPAs 3 and 4 · SLEs across specialties including obstetrics, showing Stop Before You Block · the Primary FRCA (for ultrasound science) · the joint RA-UK/RCoA trainer guidance on achieving the regional outcomes
notes: neuraxial-basics, peripheral-blocks, local-anaesthetics, last-management, ultrasound-doppler, obstetric-basics
stations: case-obstetric-spinal, case-pharm-last, case-postop-recovery-hypotension
ela: 05b, 04a

## icm | clinical | Intensive care
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-1/intensive-care
outcome: Gives safe, effective care to critically ill patients under close supervision. Levels here are FICM capability levels (0–4), not anaesthetic supervision levels.
- A [FICM 2]: Recognises the limits of intensive care and uses appropriate admission criteria.
- B [FICM 2]: Performs the invasive procedures needed for respiratory, cardiovascular and renal support: central and arterial lines, dialysis lines, ultrasound-guided cannulation, and chest drain and needle decompression in simulation.
- C [FICM 2]: Recognises, assesses and starts managing acutely ill adults with single or multiple organ failure.
- E [FICM 2]: Recognises and manages sepsis and follows local infection control policy.
- F [FICM 2]: Requests and interprets laboratory tests and imaging during a critical care stay.
- G [FICM 2]: Manages the medical and surgical needs and organ support of critically ill patients, including holistic care of patients and relatives.
- H [FICM 2]: Plans and communicates discharge from intensive care to colleagues, patients and relatives.
- I [FICM 1]: Manages end-of-life care in intensive care with patients, relatives and the team.
- J [FICM 0]: Works with transplant services, can perform brainstem death testing, and supports the physiology of the organ donor.
- K [FICM 1]: Supports staff outside the ICU to detect deteriorating patients early.
evidence: SLEs on relevant ICU cases · procedures in theatre or ICU · adult resuscitation courses and simulation · reading or e-learning on organ donation. Capability D (paediatric emergencies) moved to Resuscitation and transfer in v1.5.
