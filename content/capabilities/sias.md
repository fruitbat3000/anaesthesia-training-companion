Special Interest Areas (SIAs) are paraphrased from the RCoA *2021 Curriculum learning syllabus: Stage 3 special interest areas*. Letters match the RCoA's. Lengths are the RCoA's indicative times. Unless stated, the suggested supervision level for SIA capabilities is 4 (independent, informing the consultant as local protocols require).

## pain-medicine | sia | Pain medicine
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-3-special-interest-areas/pain-medicine
group: Group 1
length: 1 year
outcome: Manages acute, acute-on-chronic, chronic and cancer pain comprehensively, using physical, drug, interventional and psychological treatments in a multidisciplinary setting.
- A: Manages referrals and triage in a pain service.
- B: Runs an outpatient pain clinic.
- C: Manages pain procedure lists.
- D: Works effectively in a pain MDT.
- E: Manages inpatient pain and acute pain rounds.
- F: Manages patients taking drugs of potential addiction.
- G: Understands the socioeconomic, occupational health and medicolegal aspects of pain medicine.
- H: Describes the healthcare infrastructure and the pain service.
- I: Manages chronic pain in children.
- J: Manages acute pain in children in hospital.
- K: Manages pain in patients with cancer.
- L: Performs interventions for cancer pain.
- M: Assesses patients for neuromodulation.
- N: Performs neuromodulation techniques.
notes: chronic-pain, neuropathic-cancer-pain, acute-pain-complex

## acute-inpatient-pain | sia | Acute inpatient pain
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-3-special-interest-areas/acute-inpatient-pain
group: Group 2
length: 6 months
outcome: Manages pain in hospital inpatients: acute, acute-on-chronic, chronic and cancer pain.
- A: Leads an acute inpatient pain team using a biopsychosocial model.
- B: Delivers complex, person-centred care built on shared decision-making.
- C: Practises evidence-based pain medicine.
- D: Manages acute and procedural pain with drugs safely at all ages.
- E: Performs the practical pain-relieving procedures needed for safe, evidence-based practice.

- H: Liaises with and refers to services such as liaison psychiatry and addiction medicine.
evidence: Leading the inpatient pain round with the MDT · safe short- and long-term opioid prescribing · managing complications of procedures and drugs · end-of-life care plans with anticipatory prescribing · pain meetings and guideline work · a satisfactory MSF

## additional-intensive-care | sia | Additional intensive care

outcome: Gives safe, effective care to critically ill patients with only occasional specialist help. Levels are FICM capability levels.

- C [FICM 4]: Recognises, assesses and starts managing single or multiple organ failure.
- D [FICM 3]: Recognises the acutely ill child and starts managing paediatric emergencies.

## paediatric | sia | Paediatric anaesthesia
rcoa: https://www.rcoa.ac.uk/documents/2021-curriculum-learning-syllabus-stage-3-special-interest-areas/paediatric-anaesthesia
group: Group 1
length: 6 months to 1 year
outcome: For a DGH consultant with regular paediatric lists: independently anaesthetises children for the full range of DGH procedures. For a tertiary paediatric specialist, additionally: complex paediatric and neonatal surgery, and leading the service. The RCoA advises the DGH pathway for anyone planning significant paediatric work in a non-tertiary centre.
### For district general hospital practice
- A: Anaesthetises children of all ages (including premature babies) electively and in emergencies, using techniques to reduce anxiety.
- B: Cares for all children needing surgery in a DGH, including those with complex coexisting disease.
- C: Gains arterial, intraosseous, peripheral and central access in children and babies.
- D: Uses a wide range of analgesia, including simple regional techniques, for routine DGH surgery.
- E: Manages massive transfusion in children.
- F: Explains NHS policy on paediatric service provision.
### Additionally, for tertiary practice
- G: Cares for all children needing surgery in a tertiary centre, including complex coexisting disease.
- H: Uses a wide range of analgesia for complex children having major surgery.
notes: paeds-common-surgery, paeds-complex, neonatal-anaesthesia, paeds-consent-safeguarding
