## Candidate instructions

You are the anaesthetist for a **45-year-old man (ASA 2, smoker)** having an elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy. He has been induced with propofol, fentanyl and rocuronium and intubated by your colleague, who has now left theatre. The ODP is with you.

**Task:** manage the situation that develops. Talk through what you are thinking and doing.

## Scenario

On the manikin (SimMan) or with a simulated monitor, SpO~2~ falls from 99% to 88% over 2 minutes after the surgeon starts insufflation and the table is tilted head-down. Airway pressures rise from 20 to 34 cmH~2~O; ETCO~2~ trace is present but the plateau slopes upwards. Breath sounds (if auscultated) are reduced on the left. If the candidate checks the tube length, it is at **26 cm at the lips** (was 22 cm). Pulling back to 22 cm restores bilateral air entry and SpO~2~ recovers to 97%.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) The ODP says: "The sats are dropping — what do you want me to do?"
- (3 min) If the candidate fixates on bronchospasm: "The wheeze is only on the right side…"
- (6 min) After correction: "What caused this, and how will you prevent it happening again?"
- (8 min) Extension: "If the tube position had been fine, what else would you consider?"

## Key features

- Calls for help and informs the surgeon early (pause insufflation/tilt)
- Increases FiO~2~ to 1.0 and hand-ventilates to assess compliance
- Uses a systematic check from machine to patient (oxygen supply, circuit, tube, breathing, circulation)
- Checks tube position/length and auscultates both sides
- Recognises endobronchial intubation caused by head-down tilt and pneumoperitoneum
- Withdraws the tube to the correct length and confirms bilateral ventilation and capnography
- Considers alternatives: bronchospasm, pneumothorax, aspiration, secretions, anaphylaxis, atelectasis
- Uses the QRH or a cognitive aid appropriately and communicates clearly with the team

## Domains assessed

Response to an evolving emergency, situational awareness, clinical reasoning (applying physiology of shunt and V/Q), team working and communication.

## What good looks like

Declares "we have a desaturation", asks the surgeon to release the pneumoperitoneum and level the table, switches to 100% O~2~ and hand-ventilates, then works methodically, finds the tube has migrated (cephalad movement of the carina with pneumoperitoneum and Trendelenburg), corrects it, and re-secures and documents. Explains the physiology: one-lung ventilation creates a large shunt; oxygen partially improves saturation.

## Learning points

- The carina moves up (and the tube relatively down) with pneumoperitoneum and head-down tilt: check tube length after positioning.
- Always "COVER ABCD"-type check: machine to patient.
- Shunt responds poorly to FiO~2~; correct the cause.
