## Candidate instructions

You are the obstetric anaesthetist. **Mrs Kaur, 32**, term, needs a **category 2 caesarean section** for failure to progress. She does not have an epidural. Your consultant (the examiner) is present and asks you to talk through your anaesthetic.

**Task:** discuss your plan, the anatomy and the management of problems.

## Scenario

She is healthy, platelets 210, BMI 29, no allergies. Fetal heart rate is reassuring. She is anxious but keen for a spinal.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) "Talk me through your plan."
- (3 min) "Which space will you choose and why? What structures will your needle pass through?"
- (5 min) "How will you test the block, and what level do you need?"
- (6 min) "Her blood pressure falls to 80/40 after the spinal. What do you do?"
- (8 min) "At skin incision she says she can feel sharp pain. What now?"

## Key features

- Plans WHO checklist, antacid prophylaxis, IV access, consent and team communication
- Chooses an interspace at or below L3–4 and explains the conus level and Tuffier's line inaccuracy
- Lists layers from skin to subarachnoid space
- Describes drugs: hyperbaric bupivacaine with intrathecal opioid(s)
- Tests the block bilaterally to touch at T4 (and cold higher) before surgery
- Manages hypotension: left uterine displacement, phenylephrine (infusion/boluses), fluids
- Manages inadequate block: stop surgery, assess, supplement (e.g. Entonox, IV opioid) only if appropriate, and convert to GA if needed with proper explanation
- Communicates with the patient throughout

## Domains assessed

Planning, explanation and articulation, technical skills (applied anatomy and block assessment).

## What good looks like

A safe, confident plan with correct anatomy, a T4 touch block target, prophylactic phenylephrine infusion, uterine displacement, and a clear, patient-centred approach to pain at incision (never ask the woman to "put up with it"; offer GA if the block is inadequate).

## Learning points

- Test the block to touch (T4) — a cold level alone may overestimate it.
- Failure to recognise an inadequate block is a common cause of complaints and litigation.
- Phenylephrine is the vasopressor of choice for spinal hypotension at caesarean section.
