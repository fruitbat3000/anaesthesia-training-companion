## Candidate instructions

Your consultant has asked you to plan the anaesthetic for **Leo, aged 6 (22 kg)**, who needs a 45-minute MRI of the brain for headaches. He has autism and is very anxious; a previous attempt with oral sedation failed.

**Task:** discuss your plan with your consultant (the examiner) and answer their questions.

## Scenario

Leo is otherwise well, with no airway concerns. His mother will come to the induction room. The MRI unit is on the ground floor, away from theatres, with an MR-conditional anaesthetic machine and monitoring. A 1.5 tesla scanner is used.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) "How would you approach this?"
- (3 min) "Why is the MRI environment hazardous? Explain the physics."
- (5 min) "What monitoring and equipment will you use, and what are the problems with them?"
- (7 min) "How would you manage a cardiac arrest in the scanner?"
- (8 min) "What reasonable adjustments will you make for his autism?"

## Key features

- Chooses GA (or deep sedation with appropriate airway plan) given failed sedation and scan length
- Plans induction outside zone 4 (induction room) with a parent present
- Explains the static magnetic field (projectile risk), gradient fields (noise, nerve stimulation) and radiofrequency heating (burns from looped cables)
- Screens patient and staff for ferromagnetic objects and implants
- Uses MR-conditional machine, monitors and pumps (or long lines through a waveguide) and hearing protection
- Recognises monitoring limitations (ECG artefact, capnography delay)
- States that resuscitation is done outside the scanner room after removing the patient
- Describes reasonable adjustments for autism (quiet room, familiar items, hospital passport, flexible induction, premedication)
- Plans recovery and discharge criteria

## Domains assessed

Planning, assessment of risk, explanation and articulation (explaining physics and safety in plain terms to a colleague).

## What good looks like

A clear, safety-focused plan with the physics explained accurately: "The main field is always on — a steel cylinder becomes a projectile; RF energy heats tissue and loops of cable, so I'll avoid loops and pad contact points." Recognises that in an emergency the child is taken out of the scanner room (magnet zone) for resuscitation and defibrillation.

## Learning points

- MR safe / MR conditional / MR unsafe labelling.
- Quench releases helium: asphyxia risk; the magnet is not simply "switched off".
- Remote-site anaesthesia requires the same standards as theatre.
