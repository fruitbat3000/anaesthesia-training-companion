## Candidate instructions

You are performing an **ultrasound-guided axillary brachial plexus block** on **Mr Chen, 55 (60 kg)**, for a wrist fracture fixation. You have injected **25 mL of 0.5% levobupivacaine** when he says his mouth feels numb and his ears are ringing. An ODP is with you.

**Task:** manage the situation and answer the examiner's questions.

## Scenario

Within a minute the patient becomes agitated, then has a generalised seizure. On the monitor: HR 45, broad QRS complexes, BP 70/40 mmHg, SpO~2~ 90%. If treated appropriately (airway, oxygen, seizure control, lipid), his rhythm and BP improve over several minutes. If the candidate gives large doses of adrenaline, the examiner notes arrhythmias worsen.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) ODP: "What do you need?"
- (3 min) "How much lipid emulsion will you give?"
- (5 min) "Why was this patient at risk? Was the dose too high?"
- (7 min) "How does lipid work?"
- (8 min) "What happens after he recovers?"

## Key features

- Stops injecting and calls for help, gets the lipid emulsion and QRH
- Manages the airway with 100% O~2~ and ventilation (avoids hypoxia and acidosis)
- Controls seizures with small doses of benzodiazepine, propofol or thiopental
- Gives 20% lipid emulsion: 1.5 mL/kg bolus (≈ 90 mL), then 15 mL/kg/h infusion, with repeat boluses if needed (maximum about 12 mL/kg)
- Supports the circulation (fluids; reduced-dose adrenaline if arrest) and prepares for prolonged CPR
- Calculates the dose given (125 mg = about 2.1 mg/kg — at the maximum) and discusses risk factors
- Explains mechanisms (sodium channel blockade; lipid sink and metabolic effects)
- Plans monitoring, reporting and follow-up (including checking for pancreatitis/lipid effects, and informing the patient)

## Domains assessed

Response to an emergency, prescribing (accurate lipid dosing), team working.

## What good looks like

Immediate recognition of LAST, clear instructions to the ODP, safe airway management, the correct lipid regimen without hesitation, and an explanation that bupivacaine's cardiotoxicity reflects slow dissociation from sodium channels; the S-enantiomer is less toxic but not safe in overdose or intravascular injection.

## Learning points

- The QRH LAST card and lipid should be immediately available wherever blocks are done.
- Maximum doses are guides: vascularity and site matter.
- Propofol is not a substitute for lipid.
