## Candidate instructions

You are the resident on the cardiothoracic ICU. **Mr Singh, 68**, had CABG × 3 six hours ago and was extubated 1 hour ago. The nurse calls you because his blood pressure is falling.

**Task:** assess the data you are given and manage the situation.

## Scenario

BP 78/50 mmHg (was 115/60), HR 118 sinus, CVP 18 mmHg (was 9), cardiac index 1.6 L/min/m^2^, ScvO~2~ 52%. Chest drains: 350 mL/h for 2 hours, now 20 mL in the last 30 minutes. Urine output falling. Pulsus paradoxus present. Hb 94 g/L. ACT normal, platelets 110, fibrinogen 1.6 g/L. Epicardial pacing wires in situ.

## Examiner prompts

- (2 min) "What is your differential diagnosis?"
- (4 min) "What is your immediate management?"
- (6 min) "The echo shows a posterior collection compressing the left atrium. What next?"
- (7 min) "He loses cardiac output." (examiner shows PEA on the monitor)
- (9 min) "Afterwards, how would you manage the coagulopathy?"

## Key features

- Interprets low output with rising CVP and falling drain output as likely tamponade; considers other causes (bleeding, vasoplegia, LV/RV failure, arrhythmia, ischaemia from graft failure)
- Calls the cardiac surgeon and senior anaesthetist early; prepares for re-sternotomy
- Supports the circulation cautiously: fluids, inotropes/vasopressors, maintain heart rate (pacing) and preload; avoids high PEEP and vasodilators
- Uses echocardiography (TTE/TOE) recognising localised collections may be missed on TTE
- Follows the post-cardiac surgery arrest protocol (CALS): external compressions, pacing, early emergency re-sternotomy within about 5 minutes
- Manages coagulopathy using viscoelastic tests/labs: fibrinogen, platelets, protamine if residual heparin, TXA
- Communicates clearly with the team

## Domains assessed

Data interpretation, clinical reasoning, response to an emergency, team working.

## What good looks like

A rapid, accurate diagnosis of tamponade, immediate escalation to the surgeon, avoidance of harmful interventions, and knowledge of the specific cardiac surgical arrest protocol including early re-sternotomy.

## Learning points

- Falling drain output can mean clotted drains, not stopped bleeding.
- Tamponade after cardiac surgery may be localised and atypical.
- CALS: emergency re-sternotomy is part of resuscitation.
