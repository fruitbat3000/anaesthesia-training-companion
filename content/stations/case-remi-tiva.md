## Candidate instructions

You are the anaesthetist for a 55-year-old man having a 6-hour elective free-flap reconstruction. You plan total intravenous anaesthesia with propofol and remifentanil by target-controlled infusion.

Your consultant supervisor has joined you in the anaesthetic room and wants to talk through your plan before induction.

**Tasks:** explain your choice of technique and drugs to your supervisor, and answer their questions.

## Scenario

The examiner plays the consultant supervisor. The patient is fit (ASA 2, treated hypertension), 84 kg, 178 cm. Surgery is expected to last 6 hours. The surgeon has asked for a smooth, rapid emergence and good flap perfusion.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) "Why TIVA for this case?"
- (3 min) "Why remifentanil rather than a fentanyl infusion for a 6-hour case?" Expect context-sensitive half-time. Offer paper: "Can you sketch how offset changes with infusion duration?"
- (5 min) "Which propofol model will you use, and what will you enter?"
- (6 min) "How will you make sure the patient is not aware?" Expect dedicated cannula, visibility, anti-reflux, alarms, processed EEG.
- (8 min) "What happens to analgesia when you stop the remifentanil?" Expect transition analgesia.

## Key features

- States a rationale for TIVA relevant to the case (smooth emergence, PONV reduction, haemodynamic control)
- Defines context-sensitive half-time correctly
- Explains remifentanil's duration-independent offset by esterase metabolism
- Contrasts with fentanyl accumulation after long infusions
- Names an appropriate propofol model and its inputs
- Describes measures to prevent awareness with TIVA
- Plans transition analgesia before stopping remifentanil
- Communicates clearly and checks the supervisor's agreement

## Domains assessed

Clinical reasoning (pharmacokinetic basis for the plan); explanation and articulation to a colleague; planning, including anticipating emergence and post-operative analgesia.

## What good looks like

A clear, structured plan that links the science to the patient: "Remifentanil's context-sensitive half-time stays around 3–4 minutes however long I run it, because esterases clear it; fentanyl's would be several hours after 6 hours of infusion." Awareness risks and their mitigation are volunteered, and transition analgesia (for example morphine timed before the end, plus regional or local anaesthetic infiltration if appropriate) is planned without prompting.

## Learning points

- CSHT is a property of the drug **and** the infusion duration.
- TIVA safety depends on the drug actually reaching the patient: see the TIVA guideline.
