## Candidate instructions

**Mr Price, 64**, has a supraglottic tumour and needs a **panendoscopy and biopsy**. He has intermittent stridor on exertion, no stridor at rest. You have his nasendoscopy report and CT.

**Tasks:** discuss your airway plan with the ENT surgeon (the examiner), then explain your recommended technique to the patient (role player).

## Scenario

Nasendoscopy: exophytic tumour on the epiglottis and aryepiglottic fold, obscuring the right half of the glottis; cords mobile; airway about 50% narrowed. CT: no subglottic extension; trachea normal. Mouth opening 4 cm, Mallampati 3, previous neck radiotherapy (no). BMI 31. Smoker with COPD.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) Surgeon: "Can't we just put him to sleep and use a small tube?"
- (4 min) "What's your backup if that fails?"
- (6 min) Patient: "Why do I need to be awake? That sounds horrible."
- (8 min) Patient: "What are the risks?"

## Key features

- Interprets nasendoscopy and CT: supraglottic obstruction likely to make mask ventilation, SGA and laryngoscopy difficult; normal subglottis and trachea
- Recommends awake tracheal intubation (flexible bronchoscope or video laryngoscope) with HFNO and topicalisation, or discusses alternatives (asleep with double set-up, awake tracheostomy) with justification
- Agrees plans A–D with the surgeon, including front-of-neck access (surgeon scrubbed, neck marked) and the tube size
- Discusses equipment (microlaryngeal tube, jet ventilation options) and the need for shared airway coordination
- Explains ATI to the patient in plain language: why, what it feels like, sedation, local anaesthetic, safety, and alternatives
- Obtains informed consent with material risks (discomfort, failure, bleeding, need for tracheostomy)
- Plans post-procedure care (airway oedema, steroids, monitoring)

## Domains assessed

Planning, information sharing (surgeon and patient), managing complexity, explanation.

## What good looks like

A collaborative, evidence-informed plan that respects the surgeon's needs, with ATI as plan A and front-of-neck access ready; a reassuring and honest explanation to the patient.

## Learning points

- Predicted difficulty with **both** intubation and rescue oxygenation is the main indication for ATI.
- Imaging and nasendoscopy help define the level and severity of obstruction.
- Shared decision-making with the surgeon and patient is part of the exam's assessment.
