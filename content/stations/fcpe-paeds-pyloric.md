## Candidate instructions

**Baby Oliver, 5 weeks (3.8 kg)**, has pyloric stenosis confirmed on ultrasound. The surgeon wants to operate this afternoon. You are shown his capillary blood gas and electrolytes. His mother (a role player, then the examiner) is anxious.

**Tasks:** interpret the results, explain the plan to his mother, then discuss your anaesthetic plan with your consultant.

## Scenario

Capillary gas: pH 7.53, PCO~2~ 6.4 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 36 mmol/L, BE +12, Na^+^ 132, K^+^ 2.8, Cl^−^ 86, glucose 3.9. Capillary refill 3 s, sunken fontanelle, wet nappies reduced.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) "What do the results show?"
- (3 min) Mother: "Why can't they operate now? He keeps being sick!"
- (6 min) Consultant: "When would you be happy to proceed, and how will you anaesthetise him?"
- (8 min) "What will you watch for after surgery?"

## Key features

- Identifies hypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis with compensatory hypoventilation, dehydration, borderline glucose
- Explains why resuscitation comes first (risk of postoperative apnoea, arrhythmia and instability) — surgery is not an emergency
- Prescribes fluid resuscitation and maintenance with isotonic saline plus glucose and potassium, with monitoring
- States targets before surgery (e.g. Cl^−^ > 100, HCO~3~^−^ < 28, normal K^+^, good urine output)
- Plans NG tube aspiration before induction (supine, left and right lateral), RSI or modified RSI, suitable airway and doses, warming
- Plans multimodal analgesia (local infiltration, paracetamol), minimising opioids
- Monitors for postoperative apnoea and hypoglycaemia
- Communicates with empathy and clarity to the mother

## Domains assessed

Data interpretation, information sharing (with a parent and colleague), planning, empathy.

## What good looks like

A clear, kind explanation ("Oliver's blood salts are out of balance because of the vomiting; fixing them first makes the anaesthetic much safer"), correct targets, and a safe, specific anaesthetic plan.

## Learning points

- Pyloric stenosis is a medical emergency, not a surgical one.
- The alkalosis can cause postoperative apnoea (reduced respiratory drive).
- Empty the stomach before induction.
