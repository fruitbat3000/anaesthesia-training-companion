## Candidate instructions

You are in the day surgery preassessment clinic. **Ms Jordan**, aged 28, is listed for a diagnostic laparoscopy under general anaesthesia next week. The nurse has flagged that she has asthma.

**Tasks:** take a focused anaesthetic history relevant to her asthma, explain what will happen on the day of her anaesthetic, and discuss the main risks. You do not need to examine her.

## Scenario

The examiner plays Ms Jordan (or an actor does). She has asthma since childhood, uses a salbutamol inhaler about three times a week and a beclometasone inhaler "when I remember". She was admitted to hospital with asthma two years ago (no ICU). She had a wheezy chest after an anaesthetic for a wisdom tooth extraction at 18. She takes ibuprofen for period pain without problems. She smokes 5 cigarettes a day and vapes. She is anxious about "being put to sleep" and asks whether she might wake up during surgery. She had severe sickness after the last anaesthetic.

## Examiner prompts

- (Start) Let the candidate lead the history.
- (4 min, if not covered) "Is there anything else about my asthma you need to know?"
- (6 min) "What's going to happen on the day?"
- (7 min) "Will I be awake during the operation?" and "Will I be sick again?"
- (8 min) "Is it safe for me to take ibuprofen afterwards?"

## Key features

- Establishes asthma control: symptom frequency, reliever use, preventer adherence, triggers
- Asks about severity markers: admissions, ICU or ventilation, steroid courses
- Explores the previous anaesthetic problem (wheeze) and PONV history
- Asks about NSAID tolerance before recommending ibuprofen
- Takes smoking history and advises stopping, at least before surgery
- Recommends optimising asthma (regular preventer, bring inhalers, see GP/asthma nurse if poorly controlled)
- Explains the day: fasting, taking inhalers on the morning, cannula, monitoring, induction, recovery, discharge with an adult
- Discusses risks proportionately (sore throat, PONV, bronchospasm, awareness is very rare) and PONV prophylaxis
- Checks understanding and invites questions

## Domains assessed

Information gathering (structured, focused history), explanation and articulation (jargon-free explanation, answering questions honestly), planning (optimisation and a safe plan for the day).

## What good looks like

A calm, structured consultation that establishes her asthma is **partially controlled** (reliever use three times a week, erratic preventer), identifies the previous perioperative bronchospasm and PONV, and agrees a plan: regular preventer until surgery, bring inhalers, salbutamol before induction, avoid triggers, consider an SGA rather than intubation if appropriate, PONV prophylaxis, and stop smoking. Explains awareness is rare (about 1 in 19,000 in NAP5) and how it is prevented, and that ibuprofen is reasonable **because she tolerates it** without wheeze.

## Learning points

- Assess asthma **control**, not just the diagnosis.
- NSAIDs are safe in many asthmatics who have taken them before without problems; avoid in NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease.
- Consent is a dialogue: material risks are those that matter to this patient (Montgomery).
