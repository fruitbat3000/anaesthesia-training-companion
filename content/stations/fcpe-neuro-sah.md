## Candidate instructions

**Ms Novak, 49**, has a WFNS grade 2 subarachnoid haemorrhage from a posterior communicating artery aneurysm. She is booked for **endovascular coiling** in the interventional radiology suite.

**Task:** discuss your plan with the consultant neuroradiologist (the examiner), then manage the complication that arises.

## Scenario

GCS 14, BP 160/95, HR 80. External ventricular drain not in situ. Nimodipine started. Sodium 134.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) "How will you anaesthetise her, and what are your targets?"
- (3 min) "Why is the blood pressure important before the aneurysm is secured?"
- (5 min) Neuroradiologist: "Contrast is extravasating — the aneurysm has ruptured!"
- (8 min) "What problems do you expect over the next 2 weeks?"

## Key features

- Plans GA with a smooth induction avoiding hypertensive surges (and hypotension), arterial line before or soon after induction
- Sets BP targets (avoid systolic surges before securing; maintain cerebral perfusion)
- Considers the remote environment: equipment, help, radiation protection, transfer
- Manages rupture: communicates with the radiologist, **heparin reversal with protamine** (if heparin given), controls BP, deepens anaesthesia/reduces ICP, prepares for EVD or craniotomy
- Anticipates vasospasm/delayed cerebral ischaemia, hydrocephalus, rebleeding, hyponatraemia, neurogenic pulmonary oedema and cardiac stunning
- Uses nimodipine and maintains euvolaemia
- Plans neurocritical care

## Domains assessed

Planning, clinical reasoning, response to an evolving emergency.

## What good looks like

Precise haemodynamic control (e.g. an arterial line, remifentanil or alfentanil at induction), a clear rupture drill (reverse heparin, lower BP transiently while maintaining CPP, communicate with the neurosurgeon), and a good understanding of the later complications.

## Learning points

- Transmural pressure across the aneurysm rises with hypertension — avoid surges before securing.
- Delayed cerebral ischaemia peaks from days 4–14.
- Remote-site anaesthesia demands careful preparation.
