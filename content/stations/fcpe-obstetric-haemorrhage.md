## Candidate instructions

**Mrs Adeyemi, 36**, had a category 2 caesarean section under spinal anaesthesia 20 minutes ago for a failed induction of labour. The uterus is atonic and she is bleeding heavily. Estimated blood loss is **2,200 mL**. She has had oxytocin 3 units IV and an oxytocin infusion.

**Task:** lead the resuscitation, interpreting the results you are given.

## Scenario

HR 132, BP 78/40, SpO~2~ 96% on oxygen, drowsy. One 16G cannula. Point-of-care Hb 71 g/L. ROTEM: EXTEM clotting time normal, EXTEM A5 low, **FIBTEM A5 6 mm** (low; a threshold of about 12 mm is commonly used). Platelets 110. She has asthma (salbutamol only).

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) Obstetrician: "The uterus isn't contracting — what can you give?"
- (3 min) "What do these ROTEM results tell you?"
- (5 min) "She's becoming more drowsy. Do we need to convert to GA?"
- (7 min) "Which products and in what order?"
- (9 min) "The lab calls: fibrinogen (Clauss) is 1.2 g/L."

## Key features

- Declares a major obstetric haemorrhage and activates the protocol; allocates roles; calls for senior help
- Secures large-bore access (second cannula or rapid infusion catheter), sends bloods, uses warmed fluids and a rapid infuser
- Gives blood (O-negative or group-specific) early; balanced resuscitation
- Chooses uterotonics appropriately: further oxytocin, ergometrine (no hypertension), avoids or cautions carboprost in asthma, considers misoprostol
- Gives tranexamic acid 1 g IV
- Interprets FIBTEM as low functional fibrinogen and gives fibrinogen concentrate or cryoprecipitate
- Monitors calcium, temperature and acid–base; uses cell salvage
- Decides on conversion to GA when consciousness or cardiovascular stability deteriorates, with RSI and head-up positioning
- Communicates with obstetrician, midwife, lab, haematologist and the partner; plans critical care

## Domains assessed

Response to an evolving emergency, team working, data interpretation, prescribing.

## What good looks like

Assertive leadership, early fibrinogen replacement guided by FIBTEM (obstetric coagulopathy is fibrinogen-led), sensible uterotonic choice mindful of asthma, and a timely decision to convert to GA before airway reflexes are lost.

## Learning points

- Fibrinogen falls early in PPH; below 2 g/L predicts severe bleeding.
- Carboprost can cause bronchospasm; avoid in asthma if possible.
- Communication loops with the lab are part of the treatment.
