## Candidate instructions

You are the ICU resident. **Mr Ahmed, 45 (actual weight 95 kg, height 175 cm; predicted body weight about 70 kg)**, is ventilated for pneumonia on day 2. The nurse calls you because his oxygenation is worsening.

**Task:** review the information, talk through your assessment and changes, and discuss next steps with the consultant (the examiner).

## Scenario

Ventilator: volume control, VT 650 mL, RR 18, PEEP 8 cmH~2~O, FiO~2~ 0.8. Peak 38, plateau 32 cmH~2~O. SpO~2~ 88%.
ABG: pH 7.24, PaCO~2~ 7.8 kPa, PaO~2~ 7.6 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 24.
CXR: bilateral diffuse infiltrates. Echo: normal LV function. MAP 70 on noradrenaline 0.1 µg/kg/min.

## Examiner prompts

- (2 min) "What is the P/F ratio, and what does it mean?"
- (4 min) "What would you change on the ventilator?"
- (6 min) "Despite your changes, P/F is now 12 kPa. What next?"
- (8 min) "When would you refer for ECMO?"

## Key features

- Calculates P/F (7.6/0.8 ≈ 9.5 kPa — severe) and applies the Berlin definition
- Recognises tidal volume is too high for predicted body weight (≈ 9 mL/kg) and plateau > 30
- Reduces VT to 6 mL/kg PBW (≈ 420 mL), increases rate within limits, accepts permissive hypercapnia (pH > 7.20)
- Optimises PEEP (higher PEEP table or recruitment assessment) and considers driving pressure
- Excludes other causes: tube position, secretions, pneumothorax, fluid overload
- Recommends prone positioning ≥ 16 h/day and considers neuromuscular blockade and conservative fluids
- Knows ECMO referral criteria (e.g. severe refractory hypoxaemia or hypercapnic acidosis despite optimal care; short ventilation duration; potentially reversible cause) and the UK national service model
- Communicates the plan and prognosis appropriately

## Domains assessed

Data interpretation, clinical decision-making, managing complexity.

## What good looks like

Immediate correction of an injurious tidal volume based on predicted body weight, a stepwise ARDS strategy (PEEP, proning, paralysis) and timely ECMO discussion.

## Learning points

- Use predicted, not actual, body weight for tidal volumes.
- Prone positioning is one of few ARDS interventions with a mortality benefit.
- Refer early for ECMO; late referral reduces options.
