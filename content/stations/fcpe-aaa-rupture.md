## Candidate instructions

**Mr Walsh, 78**, has a CT-confirmed ruptured infrarenal AAA and is on his way to theatre. He is conscious, BP 82/50, HR 110. The anatomy is **not suitable for EVAR**. You are the most senior anaesthetist immediately available; your consultant is 15 minutes away.

**Task:** lead the team brief (the examiner plays the surgeon and theatre team) and describe your anaesthetic management.

## Scenario

Two 16G cannulas in situ; 2 units of O-negative blood given in ED; group-specific blood available; major haemorrhage protocol activated. Hb 82 g/L, lactate 5. Background: IHD, COPD, CKD (creatinine 160). Weight 75 kg.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) Surgeon: "Let's get him asleep so I can start."
- (3 min) "What blood pressure are you aiming for before the clamp?"
- (5 min) "He arrests on induction. What happened and what do you do?" (if induction is done before prep)
- (6 min) "I'm about to take the clamp off."
- (8 min) "What are the main risks in the next 24 hours?"

## Key features

- Leads a focused team brief: roles, plan, critical steps, blood availability, rapid infuser and cell salvage
- Maintains permissive hypotension (conscious, systolic about 70–90) and minimal crystalloid before clamping
- Places arterial access (under local if possible) without delaying surgery
- Insists on prepping and draping before induction, with the surgeon scrubbed and ready to clamp
- Plans a cardiostable induction (reduced doses, ketamine or etomidate or titrated propofol/opioid, vasopressor) with RSI
- Manages massive transfusion with balanced products, TXA, calcium, warming and viscoelastic testing
- Anticipates unclamping hypotension: volume, gradual release, vasopressors, communication
- Plans critical care; anticipates AKI, abdominal compartment syndrome, bowel ischaemia, MI and coagulopathy
- Escalates to and updates the consultant

## Domains assessed

Planning, team working (leadership and communication), prioritisation, managing complexity and uncertainty.

## What good looks like

The candidate politely but firmly resists induction before the patient is prepped ("induction may remove his sympathetic drive — I need you ready to clamp"), leads a crisp brief, and gives an integrated plan of haemostatic resuscitation and haemodynamic management with clear communication at clamp on and off.

## Learning points

- Induction can precipitate cardiovascular collapse in ruptured AAA: prep and drape first.
- Permissive hypotension until proximal control.
- Unclamping hypotension is predictable: prepare for it together.
