## Candidate instructions

You are called to recovery to see **Mrs Patel, 84**, who has had a **cemented hemiarthroplasty** for a fractured neck of femur under spinal anaesthesia (hyperbaric bupivacaine 2.2 mL, no intrathecal opioid) with light propofol sedation. She arrived in recovery 20 minutes ago.

**Tasks:** assess her using the information provided by the recovery nurse (the examiner), explain your reasoning, and decide on management.

## Scenario

Observations: BP 78/40 mmHg (preop 150/85), HR 58 in sinus rhythm, SpO~2~ 94% on 4 L/min oxygen, RR 16, temperature 35.4°C, drowsy but rousable. Sensory block to cold at **T8**. Estimated blood loss 500 mL; she received 1 L of Hartmann's. Hb in theatre was 102 g/L (preop 118). Drug chart: amlodipine, ramipril (taken this morning), bisoprolol. Wound dressing dry; drain 150 mL. Urine output 20 mL in the last hour. The surgeon mentions "some wobbles" at cementation.

## Examiner prompts

- (2 min) "What do you think is going on?"
- (4 min) "What are you going to do right now?" (the nurse will do what is asked)
- (6 min) If fluids and vasopressor are given: BP rises to 105/55 mmHg. "What else would you check?"
- (8 min) "She's ready for the ward — are you happy with that?"

## Key features

- Assesses ABC promptly and confirms the reading (cuff size, manual check)
- Gives a fluid bolus and a vasopressor (metaraminol or phenylephrine; ephedrine given the bradycardia) with reassessment
- Identifies multiple contributors: sympathetic block, drugs (ACEi, β-blocker, calcium channel blocker), hypovolaemia/bleeding, hypothermia, possible BCIS
- Checks Hb (point-of-care), considers blood transfusion and looks for ongoing bleeding
- Considers cardiac causes: ECG, troponin if indicated; BCIS sequelae (oxygen requirement)
- Warms the patient
- Recognises the risk of delirium and AKI in a frail patient with hypotension and oliguria
- Decides on an appropriate level of care (does not discharge to the ward until stable; considers enhanced care)
- Hands over clearly (SBAR) to the surgical team/ward

## Domains assessed

Clinical reasoning (integrating physiology and pharmacology), prioritisation, assessment of risk, information sharing.

## What good looks like

Acts first (fluids, vasopressor, oxygen, warming), then thinks broadly: a residual sympathetic block (T8, likely higher for sympathetic fibres), antihypertensives taken this morning, blood loss (Hb falling, may be more to come), hypothermia, and possible BCIS. Orders an urgent Hb, gas and ECG, considers transfusion, continues monitoring in recovery or enhanced care until the block regresses and BP is stable, and plans to hold antihypertensives.

## Learning points

- Post-spinal hypotension in the elderly is usually multifactorial: treat and investigate.
- Frail hip fracture patients poorly tolerate hypotension (delirium, AKI, myocardial injury).
- Guideline: avoid hypotension and heavy sedation in hip fracture anaesthesia.
