## Candidate instructions

You are the anaesthetic resident on call. You are asked to see **Mr Evans, 67**, on the surgical ward, 3 days after an anterior resection. His NEWS2 score is 9.

**Tasks:** interpret the information provided, say what immediate treatment you would start, and then telephone the ICU consultant (the examiner) with a structured referral.

## Scenario

Observations: RR 28, SpO~2~ 91% on 4 L/min nasal cannulae, HR 124 (sinus), BP 84/46 mmHg, temperature 38.9°C, new confusion. Abdomen distended and tender. Urine output 60 mL in 6 hours.

Arterial blood gas on 4 L/min: pH 7.26, PaCO~2~ 3.9 kPa, PaO~2~ 8.1 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 13 mmol/L, BE −12, lactate 6.2 mmol/L, glucose 9.8.

Bloods: WCC 21, CRP 310, creatinine 168 (baseline 80), platelets 95.

Past history: COPD (mild), hypertension. He has said to his family he "would want everything done".

## Examiner prompts

- (2 min) "Talk me through the blood gas."
- (4 min) "What are you going to do in the next hour?"
- (5 min) Play the ICU consultant: "Hello, ICU consultant here — what have you got?"
- (8 min) "Does he need surgery? What happens next?"

## Key features

- Recognises septic shock (hypotension, lactate > 2) with likely anastomotic leak
- Interprets the gas: metabolic (lactic) acidosis with respiratory compensation and type 1 respiratory failure
- Starts oxygen titrated to target, IV access, cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour, fluid resuscitation with reassessment
- Plans vasopressor support and arterial monitoring (critical care)
- Identifies the need for source control (surgical review, CT) and informs the surgical team
- Gives a structured SBAR referral with the key numbers and the patient's wishes
- Considers ceiling of care and communicates with the family

## Domains assessed

Information gathering, clinical decision-making and prioritisation, information sharing (structured referral).

## What good looks like

A concise summary: "septic shock from a probable anastomotic leak with AKI and hypoxaemic respiratory failure; NEWS 9, lactate 6.2, MAP 59 despite 1 L". Actions already started (antibiotics, fluids, oxygen), a request for ICU admission for vasopressors and monitoring, and an urgent surgical decision about CT and laparotomy. States the patient's expressed wishes.

## Learning points

- Sepsis-3: septic shock = vasopressor need to keep MAP ≥ 65 with lactate > 2 despite fluids.
- Antibiotics and source control are time-critical.
- SBAR makes referrals clear and fast.
