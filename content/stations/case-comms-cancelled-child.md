## Candidate instructions

**Amira, aged 5**, is listed for tonsillectomy this morning for recurrent tonsillitis. On your preoperative visit she has a temperature of 38.2°C, a moist cough and coarse crackles on the right. You have decided with your consultant to **postpone** her surgery.

**Task:** explain this decision to her father, **Mr Hassan** (a role player), and answer his questions.

## Scenario

Mr Hassan has taken two days off work, the family has waited 9 months, and Amira has fasted since last night. He is frustrated and asks why she cannot "just have the operation since she's here". He worries she will keep getting tonsillitis. If the candidate is empathetic and clear he becomes calmer and asks when she can have the operation.

## Examiner prompts

(Role player cues)
- "We've waited nine months for this — can't you just do it?"
- "It's only a cough; she always has a cough."
- "What exactly is the risk?"
- "So when can she have it?"

## Key features

- Introduces themself and checks the parent's understanding and concerns
- Acknowledges his frustration and apologises for the disruption
- Explains the reason clearly without jargon (chest infection with fever and chest signs)
- Explains the specific risks (breathing problems during and after anaesthesia such as airway spasm, low oxygen levels, pneumonia)
- Balances the decision: why waiting is safer now
- Gives a plan: treatment, return timing (typically after a few weeks once well), rebooking priority, who will contact them
- Addresses practical issues (eating now, work letter if needed)
- Checks understanding and offers further questions; remains calm and respectful

## Domains assessed

Empathy, compassion and respect; explanation and articulation; honesty and integrity (not minimising the risk or the inconvenience).

## What good looks like

"I can see how frustrating this is after such a long wait, and I'm sorry." A simple explanation of airway irritability and infection, a clear, honest statement of risk, and a concrete plan (antibiotics if prescribed by the GP/team, reassessment, rebooking with priority), ending with a check that Mr Hassan feels his concerns have been heard.

## Learning points

- Postpone for systemic illness, fever, productive cough or chest signs.
- Airway hyper-reactivity can persist for several weeks after an URTI.
- Communication stations reward structure and empathy as much as facts.
