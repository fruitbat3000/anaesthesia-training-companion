## Candidate instructions

You are about to start a morning list. Your consultant (the examiner) asks you to talk through the **pre-use check of the anaesthetic machine** in front of you, and to explain any problems you find.

**Task:** perform and explain the check; identify and explain the significance of any faults.

## Scenario

The examiner uses a real or simulated machine with **three planted faults**:
1. The **oxygen pipeline hose is not fully engaged** (fails the tug test; pipeline gauge reads 0 while the cylinder is on).
2. The **circle system has a leak**: the reservoir bag does not stay inflated on the leak test with the APL closed (an absorber canister not seated).
3. The **soda lime has turned violet** (exhausted).

The oxygen analyser, suction and monitors are working.

## Examiner prompts

- (Start) "Take me through your check."
- (3 min) "Why do we do a tug test? What would happen if we didn't?"
- (5 min) "How does the oxygen failure alarm work?"
- (7 min) "Explain the chemistry of CO~2~ absorption. Why does the colour change?"
- (8 min) "What would you do if you had to start an emergency case now with a faulty machine?"

## Key features

- Uses a structured approach consistent with the Association of Anaesthetists' checking guidance
- Checks gas supplies: pipelines with tug test, cylinder contents, pressure gauges
- Identifies the disengaged oxygen hose and its risk (running on cylinder, silent depletion)
- Checks the oxygen analyser, flowmeters and hypoxic guard, and the vaporisers (seated, filled, locked)
- Performs a breathing system leak and function (two-bag) test and identifies the leak
- Identifies exhausted absorbent and explains rebreathing and rising inspired CO~2~
- Explains the oxygen failure alarm (powered by oxygen pressure, audible, cuts off other gases)
- Confirms alternative means of ventilation (self-inflating bag and oxygen cylinder), suction and monitoring
- Knows not to use a faulty machine and how to escalate (another machine, TIVA with a self-inflating bag and portable oxygen)

## Domains assessed

Technical skills (systematic check), clinical reasoning (linking faults to physics and patient risk), assessment and reduction of risk.

## What good looks like

A calm, logical check from supply to patient, verbalising why each step matters, finding all three faults, and refusing to proceed until they are fixed; explaining that CO~2~ reacts with hydroxides to form carbonates, water and heat, with a pH-sensitive indicator changing colour.

## Learning points

- Most machine-related incidents are detectable by a proper check.
- A two-bag test confirms the whole breathing system works with a "test lung".
- The ultimate backup is a self-inflating bag and an oxygen cylinder.
