## Candidate instructions

**Mr Hughes, 81**, fell at home and has **left-sided fractures of ribs 4–9** with a small haemothorax (drained). He takes **rivaroxaban** for AF (last dose 10 hours ago). His SpO~2~ is 92% on 4 L/min, RR 26, and he cannot cough effectively; pain score 9/10 despite paracetamol and oral morphine.

**Task:** discuss analgesia options with the trauma consultant (the examiner) and agree a plan.

## Scenario

eGFR 45; platelets 180; INR 1.3; weight 70 kg. He has COPD and mild cognitive impairment.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) "He's getting tired. What can you offer?"
- (3 min) "Why not a thoracic epidural?"
- (5 min) "Show me on this image/diagram where the erector spinae block goes."
- (7 min) "How will you dose a catheter, and what will you monitor?"
- (9 min) "He becomes confused overnight. What might be going on?"

## Key features

- Recognises high risk (age, number of fractures, COPD, poor cough) and the need for good analgesia to prevent pneumonia and respiratory failure
- Explains why thoracic epidural and paravertebral block are relatively contraindicated with recent rivaroxaban and reduced renal function
- Recommends a superficial fascial plane catheter (erector spinae or serratus anterior) with continuous infusion or programmed boluses
- Describes the sonoanatomy (transverse process, erector spinae muscle, spread) and safety (LAST, dose limits, monitoring)
- Combines with multimodal analgesia (regular paracetamol, careful opioids, avoiding NSAIDs in CKD), physiotherapy, incentive spirometry, HFNO or NIV if needed
- Considers a higher level of care and escalation criteria
- Considers delirium causes (hypoxia, hypercapnia, pain, opioids, infection, LAST) and management

## Domains assessed

Clinical reasoning, assessment of risk, planning, information sharing.

## What good looks like

A reasoned choice of an ESP or SAP catheter because of anticoagulation, with clear anatomy, dosing within safe limits, monitoring, and integration into a multidisciplinary rib fracture pathway.

## Learning points

- Rib fractures in older adults carry significant mortality; analgesia is a key intervention.
- Match the depth and compressibility of the block to the anticoagulation status.
- Fascial plane blocks are volume-dependent: watch the total local anaesthetic dose.
