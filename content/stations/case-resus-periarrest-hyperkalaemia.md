## Candidate instructions

**Mr O'Brien, 70**, with chronic kidney disease on haemodialysis, has missed two dialysis sessions and needs emergency surgery for an ischaemic foot. You are shown his **ECG** and blood results.

**Task:** interpret the results and describe your immediate management.

## Scenario

ECG: HR 48, broad QRS (0.16 s), tall peaked T waves, flattened P waves. Bloods: K^+^ 7.6 mmol/L, Na^+^ 136, urea 32, creatinine 890, HCO~3~^−^ 16, glucose 7.2. BP 110/60, alert.

## Examiner prompts

- (1 min) "What does the ECG show?"
- (3 min) "What will you do now?"
- (5 min) "Explain how calcium works."
- (6 min) "Can we give him suxamethonium for the anaesthetic?"
- (8 min) "His ECG becomes a sine wave. What now?"

## Key features

- Identifies hyperkalaemia on ECG (peaked T, wide QRS, loss of P waves, bradycardia)
- Recognises a life-threatening situation needing cardiac monitoring and senior help
- Gives IV calcium (chloride or gluconate) immediately to stabilise the myocardium
- Shifts potassium into cells: IV insulin with glucose (and glucose monitoring), nebulised salbutamol
- Plans removal: urgent dialysis (renal team), stop potassium-containing drugs and fluids
- Explains the membrane physiology (resting potential, threshold potential, sodium channel inactivation)
- Avoids suxamethonium and postpones non-life-saving surgery until potassium is controlled
- Manages peri-arrest/arrest per ALS (calcium, continuous CPR, treat reversible cause)

## Domains assessed

Data interpretation, response to an emergency, prescribing.

## What good looks like

A clear, rapid diagnosis and sequence (**protect, shift, remove**), accurate drug doses with glucose monitoring, and a correct explanation that calcium raises the threshold potential, restoring the gap to the partially depolarised resting potential.

## Learning points

- ECG changes do not correlate tightly with the potassium level; treat changes urgently.
- Insulin–glucose is the most reliable shifting treatment.
- Dialysis is the definitive treatment in end-stage renal disease.
