## Candidate instructions

**Mrs Green, 89**, has a perforated sigmoid diverticulum with generalised peritonitis. She is septic but conscious and has mild dementia; she is judged to **lack capacity** for this decision today. She lives in a care home (Clinical Frailty Scale 7). The NELA-predicted 30-day mortality is 38%. The surgeon has asked you to talk to her daughter, **Sarah** (a role player), who holds a lasting power of attorney for health and welfare.

**Task:** discuss the options with Sarah.

## Scenario

Sarah expects "everything to be done". As the conversation progresses she reveals that her mother often said she "never wanted to end up on machines", and she is upset about the thought of her mother dying in pain.

## Examiner prompts

(Role player cues)
- "She'll be fine — she's a fighter."
- "What are her chances?"
- "What happens if she doesn't have the operation?"
- "What would you do if she was your mother?"

## Key features

- Introduces self, establishes Sarah's understanding and role (LPA), and explores her mother's values and wishes
- Explains the situation honestly with understandable numbers (e.g. "about 4 in 10 people like your mother would not survive a month, and many survivors do not return to their previous level of function")
- Describes what surgery involves (ICU, possible stoma, prolonged recovery, delirium) and the alternative of comfort-focused care
- Makes clear that not operating is a valid option and would include good symptom control
- Responds to emotion with empathy and silence; checks understanding
- Avoids giving a personal choice while offering a clinical recommendation grounded in the patient's values
- Involves the surgeon and senior colleagues; documents the discussion and any treatment escalation plan
- Recognises the legal framework (LPA holder decides in best interests; MCA)

## Domains assessed

Empathy and compassion, explanation, honesty and integrity, clinical decision-making.

## What good looks like

A balanced, compassionate conversation in plain language that brings the patient's own wishes to the centre, presents surgery and comfort care as real options, and reaches a shared, documented decision.

## Learning points

- Frailty strongly predicts outcome; numbers should be personalised and explained.
- An LPA for health and welfare can make decisions in the patient's best interests when she lacks capacity.
- "What would you do?" — reflect the question back to the patient's values rather than your own.
