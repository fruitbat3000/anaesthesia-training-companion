## Indications

Upper airway obstruction (tumour, trauma, infection), prolonged ventilation or weaning, airway protection and secretion management (neurological disease), and as part of major head and neck surgery.

## Techniques

- **Surgical** (open) or **percutaneous dilatational** (in ICU, often with bronchoscopic guidance and ultrasound).
- Anaesthetic issues: shared airway, withdrawing the tracheal tube just above the stoma under direct vision, fire risk with diathermy into the trachea (reduce FiO~2~), confirming placement with capnography, securing the tube.
- **Early complications:** bleeding, misplacement (pre-tracheal), pneumothorax, surgical emphysema, tube displacement (the tract is immature for about 7 days). **Late:** tracheal stenosis, tracheomalacia, tracheo-innominate fistula (sentinel bleed), tracheo-oesophageal fistula.

## Emergencies (National Tracheostomy Safety Project principles)

**Know whether the patient has a patent upper airway** (tracheostomy) or **not** (**laryngectomy** — the trachea is sutured to the skin as an end stoma; the patient cannot be oxygenated or intubated from above). Bedhead signs show which applies.

For a patient with breathing difficulty and a tracheostomy:

1. **Call for help** (airway-trained staff); apply **high-flow oxygen to both the face and the tracheostomy** (only the stoma for a laryngectomy).
2. Remove any speaking valve or cap; remove the **inner tube** (a blocked inner cannula is the commonest problem).
3. **Pass a suction catheter:** if it passes easily, the tube is patent — consider partial obstruction and continue assessment.
4. If it will not pass: **deflate the cuff**; if still not improving, **remove the tracheostomy tube**.
5. Oxygenate via the **upper airway** (face mask/SGA, with the stoma covered) and/or via the **stoma** (paediatric face mask or LMA over the stoma).
6. Secondary emergency oxygenation: oral intubation (with a longer uncut tube advanced beyond the stoma) or intubation of the stoma with a small tube, guided by a bougie or fibrescope.

Children have separate algorithms (a small tube, stay sutures in new tracheostomies).
