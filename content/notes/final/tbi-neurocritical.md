## Traumatic brain injury

- **Primary injury** occurs at impact; management targets **secondary injury** from hypoxia, hypotension, hyper- and hypocapnia, hyperglycaemia, hyperthermia, seizures and raised ICP.
- **Targets** (typical UK practice; follow local neurocritical care protocols): SpO~2~ > 94% / PaO~2~ > 13 kPa; PaCO~2~ 4.5–5.0 kPa; MAP ≥ 80–90 mmHg to achieve **CPP 60–70 mmHg**; ICP < 20–22 mmHg; glucose 6–10 mmol/L; temperature < 37.5°C; sodium high-normal; head-up 30°; adequate sedation and analgesia; seizure prophylaxis in selected patients.
- **Tiered ICP management:** sedation, CSF drainage, osmotherapy, mild hyperventilation, neuromuscular blockade, barbiturates, decompressive craniectomy.
- **Surgery:** evacuation of extradural (lucid interval, arterial bleed) and subdural haematomas; urgent transfer to the neurosurgical centre ("time is brain").
- **Coagulopathy** and **hypotension** are common in multiple trauma; resuscitation with blood and permissive hypotension are **not** appropriate when there is a severe head injury.

## Aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage

- "Thunderclap" headache; **World Federation of Neurosurgical Societies (WFNS) grade** (based on GCS and motor deficit).
- **Complications:** rebleeding (early securing of the aneurysm by coiling or clipping), hydrocephalus (EVD), **delayed cerebral ischaemia / vasospasm** (days 4–14; **nimodipine** orally; maintain euvolaemia and adequate blood pressure; endovascular treatment), hyponatraemia (cerebral salt wasting or SIADH), neurogenic pulmonary oedema and stunned myocardium.
- **Anaesthesia for coiling/clipping:** avoid hypertension before securing (rupture risk), controlled emergence, heparin during coiling, readiness for intraoperative rupture.

## Status epilepticus

Continuous seizure > 5 minutes or recurrent seizures without recovery: ABC, glucose, **benzodiazepine** (IV lorazepam or buccal/IM midazolam), then **second-line** (levetiracetam, phenytoin or valproate), then **anaesthesia** (propofol, thiopental or midazolam infusion) with intubation and EEG monitoring; find the cause.

## Transfer of the brain-injured patient

Association of Anaesthetists 2019 guideline: stabilise, intubate if GCS ≤ 8 or deteriorating, secure lines, targets as above, avoid delay, experienced escort, full monitoring including capnography and invasive BP.
