## General anaesthesia for caesarean section

- Indications: contraindication to or failure of neuraxial block, or when the time to establish neuraxial anaesthesia would harm the woman or baby (category 1).
- **Preparation:** WHO checklist, antacid prophylaxis (sodium citrate, H~2~ antagonist), **head-up (ramped) position** with left uterine displacement, pre-oxygenation to ETO~2~ ≥ 0.9 (HFNO can help), a smaller tube (e.g. 6.0–7.0 mm), video laryngoscope, bougie and second-generation SGA ready.
- **RSI:** propofol or thiopental and rocuronium or suxamethonium; opioid at induction is now common (e.g. to reduce the pressor response, particularly in pre-eclampsia), with the neonatal team informed.
- **OAA/DAS failed intubation guideline:** after failed intubation, rescue oxygenation with an SGA and a **pre-planned decision to wake or proceed**, based on the urgency and maternal/fetal condition.
- **Maintenance:** volatile (about 1 MAC; N~2~O optional) — risk of **awareness** is higher than in other surgery; reduce volatile after delivery if the uterus is atonic; oxytocin; multimodal analgesia with TAP or QL blocks; extubate awake in the head-up or lateral position.

## Abnormal placentation

- **Placenta praevia** and **placenta accreta spectrum** (accreta, increta, percreta), especially with previous caesarean sections. Risk of massive haemorrhage.
- **Planning:** antenatal diagnosis (ultrasound, MRI), a multidisciplinary team at a specialist centre, elective delivery, cell salvage, blood availability, interventional radiology (balloons), large-bore access and arterial line, and discussion of anaesthetic technique (neuraxial with conversion to GA, or GA from the start).

## Major obstetric haemorrhage

- Defined variably (e.g. > 1,000 mL with signs of shock, or > 1,500 mL); **underestimation is common** — measure blood loss (weigh swabs).
- **Management:** call for help and activate the major haemorrhage protocol; ABC with large-bore access; uterotonics (oxytocin, ergometrine, carboprost, misoprostol); **tranexamic acid 1 g** early; blood and products; **fibrinogen replacement guided by Clauss fibrinogen or viscoelastic tests (FIBTEM)**, aiming for about ≥ 2 g/L; maintain platelets > 75 × 10^9^/L; correct calcium, temperature and acidosis; **cell salvage**; surgical measures (bimanual compression, balloon tamponade, B-Lynch suture, uterine artery ligation, hysterectomy) and interventional radiology.
- Pregnancy-related coagulopathy is often **fibrinogen depletion** (especially in placental abruption and amniotic fluid embolism).

## Refusal of blood

Jehovah's Witnesses may refuse whole blood and major components but individuals vary in what they accept (cell salvage, fractions such as fibrinogen concentrate or albumin). Discuss in detail antenatally, document an advance decision, optimise haemoglobin, and plan early escalation (cell salvage, tranexamic acid, early surgical control). A competent adult's refusal must be respected.
