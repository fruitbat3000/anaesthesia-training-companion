## Burns

- **Assessment:** total body surface area (TBSA) by the **Lund and Browder chart** (accounts for age) or rule of nines (adults); depth (superficial, partial-thickness, full-thickness). Referral criteria to burns services.
- **Airway burns and inhalation injury:** suspect with burns in an enclosed space, facial burns, singed nasal hair, soot in the mouth, hoarseness, stridor. **Early intubation** (uncut tube, large size) before swelling makes it impossible.
- **Carbon monoxide:** high-flow oxygen (COHb half-life about 4–5 h in air, about 1 h on 100% O~2~); SpO~2~ is falsely normal; co-oximetry. **Cyanide** (burning plastics): lactic acidosis — hydroxocobalamin.
- **Fluid resuscitation** (for burns > about 15% TBSA in adults, 10% in children): the **Parkland formula**: 4 mL/kg/%TBSA of crystalloid over 24 h, half in the first 8 h **from the time of the burn**. Titrate to urine output (0.5 mL/kg/h adults, 1 mL/kg/h children) to avoid over-resuscitation ("fluid creep": compartment syndromes, pulmonary oedema).
- **Other:** escharotomy for circumferential burns, temperature control, analgesia, early enteral feeding, infection.
- **Anaesthesia for burns surgery:** difficult airway and access, massive blood loss during excision (TXA, adrenaline infiltration), heat loss, **suxamethonium contraindicated from about 24–48 h** to over a year (hyperkalaemia), **resistance to non-depolarising NMBs**, increased opioid requirements, hypermetabolism, dressing changes (ketamine, sedation).

## Poisoning (principles)

ABC, supportive care, decontamination (activated charcoal within 1 h for suitable drugs), enhanced elimination (haemodialysis for some), antidotes; use **TOXBASE** and the National Poisons Information Service.

| Poison | Key features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Paracetamol | Delayed hepatotoxicity | **Acetylcysteine** per nomogram / timing |
| Aspirin (salicylate) | Respiratory alkalosis + metabolic acidosis, tinnitus, hyperthermia | Bicarbonate (urinary alkalinisation), haemodialysis |
| Opioids | Coma, miosis, respiratory depression | **Naloxone** (titrate; infusion for long-acting drugs) |
| Tricyclics | Anticholinergic, broad QRS, seizures, arrhythmias | **Sodium bicarbonate**, avoid Class Ia/Ic drugs, benzodiazepines |
| Digoxin | Arrhythmias, hyperkalaemia | Digoxin-specific antibody fragments |
| Theophylline (aminophylline) | Arrhythmias, seizures, hypokalaemia | Supportive, charcoal, haemodialysis |
| MDMA / stimulants | Hyperthermia, hyponatraemia, serotonin syndrome | Cooling, benzodiazepines |
| Alcohol, benzodiazepines | Coma, airway | Supportive (flumazenil rarely) |

## Environmental injuries

- **Drowning:** hypoxia is the key problem; ventilation with PEEP; hypothermia; cervical spine only if trauma is suspected.
- **Accidental hypothermia:** stages by core temperature; J (Osborn) waves on ECG; arrhythmias; "not dead until warm and dead"; rewarming (ECMO for arrest); drugs withheld below 30°C in arrest.
- **Electrical injury:** arrhythmias, deep tissue damage greater than the skin burn suggests (rhabdomyolysis, compartment syndrome), myoglobinuria.
- **Crush injury:** hyperkalaemia and arrhythmias on release, rhabdomyolysis and AKI.
