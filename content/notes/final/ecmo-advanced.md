## ECMO

| | **Veno-venous (VV)** | **Veno-arterial (VA)** |
|---|---|---|
| Supports | **Gas exchange** (lungs) | **Heart and lungs** |
| Cannulation | Femoral and jugular veins (or a dual-lumen jugular cannula) | Femoral vein → femoral artery (peripheral) or central |
| Indications | Severe ARDS with refractory hypoxaemia or hypercapnia despite optimal ventilation and prone positioning | Cardiogenic shock (MI, myocarditis, post-cardiotomy), refractory cardiac arrest (**ECPR**), PE |
| Issues | Recirculation; the native heart must work | Increased LV afterload (LV distension — venting); differential hypoxia ("Harlequin" syndrome) with peripheral cannulation and poor lung function |

- **Complications:** bleeding (anticoagulation), thrombosis, circuit failure, haemolysis, limb ischaemia (VA), infection, neurological events (stroke).
- **UK model:** severe respiratory failure ECMO is provided by a small number of commissioned **national centres** with retrieval teams; referral criteria include potentially reversible severe respiratory failure (e.g. Murray score) and a short duration of ventilation.

## Other mechanical support

Intra-aortic balloon pump, percutaneous ventricular assist devices (e.g. Impella), long-term LVADs (bridge to transplant or destination therapy) — patients with LVADs have continuous flow (often no palpable pulse; use Doppler or arterial line for BP), anticoagulation and driveline infection risks.

## Temperature control after cardiac arrest

Current guidance recommends **avoiding fever** (e.g. keeping temperature ≤ 37.5°C for at least 72 hours) rather than routine deep hypothermia, following trials showing no benefit of 33°C over normothermia with fever prevention. Methods: surface or intravascular devices, sedation, managing shivering.
