## Before you go

- **Is the transfer necessary and is the patient ready?** Stabilise (secure the airway, lines, drains, splints), but for **time-critical** transfers (e.g. extradural haematoma, aortic dissection) avoid delaying definitive treatment for non-essential interventions.
- **Pre-emptive interventions:** intubate if the airway may deteriorate; chest drains for pneumothorax before air transfer; arterial line; adequate sedation and analgesia; secure everything.
- **Packaging:** vacuum mattress, secured lines, pumps and ventilator mounted safely; warmed.
- **Equipment:** a transfer ventilator (know its oxygen consumption), full monitoring with **waveform capnography and invasive BP**, spare batteries, suction, drugs (sedation, vasopressors, resuscitation drugs), airway kit, and **calculated oxygen supply with a margin** (at least 50% extra, or double).
- **Personnel:** an experienced doctor and assistant, trained in transfer; regional critical care network protocols and retrieval services.

## During transfer

- **Physiological effects:** acceleration/deceleration (venous pooling or increased return, raised ICP), vibration, noise (alarms may not be heard), temperature changes, motion sickness; in aircraft, **altitude** (gas expansion in closed spaces, lower PaO~2~).
- **Problems in transit:** accidental extubation or tube displacement, ventilator or oxygen failure, loss of IV access, cardiovascular instability, agitation. Stop the vehicle if an intervention is needed. Have a plan and the kit at hand.
- **Deaths in transit:** follow the ambulance service and legal procedures (the coroner or procurator fiscal).

## Communication, law and ethics

- Clear handover (SBAR) at both ends; document the transfer (observations, drugs, events).
- Explain to the patient and relatives why transfer is needed; consent where possible; confidentiality.
- **Audit and incident reporting** for all transfers; regional standards and training.
