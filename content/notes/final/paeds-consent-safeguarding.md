## Consent

- **Parental responsibility:** mothers automatically; fathers if married to the mother at birth or named on the birth certificate (in England and Wales after 2003); others by court order or adoption. One person with PR can usually consent.
- **Gillick competence:** a child under 16 who has sufficient understanding and intelligence to understand fully what is proposed can consent. (The **Fraser guidelines** specifically concern contraceptive advice.)
- **16–17-year-olds** are presumed competent to consent (Family Law Reform Act 1969) and the Mental Capacity Act applies from 16. Refusal of life-saving treatment by a competent young person may be overridden by the court (or, in limited circumstances, those with PR) — seek senior and legal advice.
- **Emergencies:** treat in the child's best interests when consent cannot be obtained in time.

## Restraint and holding

- Clinical holding may be needed for essential procedures in young children. Use the least restrictive approach for the shortest time, with explanation, parental involvement where possible, and documentation. Consider alternatives (premedication, distraction, postponement). For older children and those with learning disabilities, restraint must be justified in their best interests and proportionate (MCA).

## Research

Children can participate in research with consent from those with PR and the child's assent where possible; ethics approval considers the balance of risk and benefit.

## Safeguarding

- **Recognise:** injuries inconsistent with the history or developmental stage, delayed presentation, bruising in non-mobile infants, patterned injuries, torn frenulum, unexplained fractures, repeated attendances, concerning interactions.
- **Act:** ensure the child's immediate safety, record findings objectively (body maps, photographs by trained staff), share concerns with the named safeguarding lead and follow local procedures; do not investigate yourself or confront parents. Information sharing to protect a child is justified even without consent.
- Anaesthetists may see concerns during examination under anaesthesia, dental GA, or trauma care.
