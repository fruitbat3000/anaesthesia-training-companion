## Neuropathic pain

- Burning, shooting, electric-shock pain, allodynia, numbness; e.g. diabetic neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia, trigeminal neuralgia, radiculopathy, post-surgical nerve injury, phantom limb pain.
- **First-line drugs** (NICE): **amitriptyline, duloxetine, gabapentin or pregabalin**, choosing by comorbidity and side effects, and switching if ineffective. **Carbamazepine** for trigeminal neuralgia. **Topical capsaicin** or lidocaine plasters for localised pain. Tramadol only for acute rescue.
- **Mechanisms of drug action:** tricyclics and SNRIs enhance descending inhibition (noradrenaline, serotonin) and block sodium channels; gabapentinoids bind the α~2~δ subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels (reducing transmitter release); antiarrhythmics (lidocaine, mexiletine) block sodium channels; opioids have limited efficacy and significant risks.
- Gabapentinoids are controlled drugs in the UK (misuse potential; respiratory depression with opioids).

## Complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS)

Pain disproportionate to the injury, with sensory, vasomotor (temperature and colour), sudomotor (sweating, oedema) and motor/trophic changes (**Budapest criteria**). Type I without and type II with a nerve injury. Early physiotherapy and desensitisation, analgesia, psychological support; specialist interventions (e.g. sympathetic blocks) in selected patients.

## Cancer pain

- Often mixed nociceptive and neuropathic; also bone pain, visceral pain and incident pain.
- **WHO approach:** regular analgesia by the clock and by mouth, escalating as needed with adjuvants — now applied flexibly (strong opioids earlier when needed).
- **Opioids:** morphine as first-line strong opioid; alternatives in renal impairment (e.g. oxycodone with caution, fentanyl, alfentanil); **breakthrough** doses; laxatives and antiemetics; opioid rotation for toxicity.
- **Adjuvants:** steroids (nerve compression, liver capsule pain, raised ICP), NSAIDs (bone pain), radiotherapy (bone metastases), bisphosphonates, antineuropathic agents.
- **Interventions:** coeliac plexus neurolysis (pancreatic cancer), intrathecal drug delivery (implanted pumps), cordotomy (in specialist centres), vertebroplasty.
- **Palliative care** integration; end-of-life symptom control with syringe drivers.

## Interventional pain techniques

Epidural steroid injections (radicular pain), facet joint and medial branch blocks and radiofrequency denervation, sympathetic blocks (stellate, lumbar sympathetic, coeliac), spinal cord stimulation (neuropathic and refractory pain), peripheral nerve stimulation, **TENS**, and acupuncture — all within a multidisciplinary, evidence-based framework.
