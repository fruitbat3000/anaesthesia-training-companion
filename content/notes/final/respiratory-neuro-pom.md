## COPD and asthma

- Assess severity (exercise tolerance, exacerbations, home oxygen, admissions, spirometry, CO~2~ retention on gases). Optimise: inhalers, treat infection, **smoking cessation**, pulmonary rehabilitation. Consider regional techniques. **Asthma:** well controlled before elective surgery; avoid triggers (NSAIDs in sensitive patients, histamine-releasing drugs, instrumentation in light planes); steroid cover if on long-term steroids.
- **Postoperative pulmonary complications** risk factors: age, COPD, smoking, upper abdominal and thoracic surgery, long duration, emergency surgery, obesity, low albumin. Reduce with protective ventilation, analgesia (regional), early mobilisation, physiotherapy, and avoiding residual neuromuscular block.

## Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) and obesity hypoventilation

- **Screen** with **STOP-BANG** (snoring, tiredness, observed apnoeas, blood pressure, BMI > 35, age > 50, neck > 40 cm, male); confirm with **sleep studies** (apnoea–hypopnoea index: mild 5–15, moderate 15–30, severe > 30).
- **Risks:** difficult mask ventilation and intubation, postoperative apnoea and hypoxaemia (sensitivity to opioids and sedatives), pulmonary hypertension, arrhythmias.
- **Management:** bring **CPAP** to hospital and use it after surgery; minimise opioids (regional, multimodal); avoid sedative premedication; extubate fully awake, sitting up; monitored bed for high-risk patients.
- **Obesity hypoventilation syndrome** (BMI > 30 with daytime hypercapnia): higher risk; may need NIV.

## Neuromuscular diseases

| Condition | Key issues |
|---|---|
| **Myasthenia gravis** | Continue pyridostigmine (adjust doses), **sensitive to non-depolarising NMBs** (reduced doses, monitoring; sugammadex useful), **resistant to suxamethonium**; risk of postoperative respiratory failure (predictors: disease duration, dose of pyridostigmine, vital capacity, respiratory disease); myasthenic versus cholinergic crisis |
| **Lambert–Eaton** | Sensitive to all NMBs |
| **Muscular dystrophies (Duchenne, Becker)** | **Avoid suxamethonium and volatile agents** (hyperkalaemic rhabdomyolysis); cardiomyopathy; restrictive lung disease |
| **Myotonic dystrophy** | Suxamethonium and anticholinesterases may trigger myotonia; sensitivity to sedatives and opioids; cardiac conduction defects; aspiration risk; avoid hypothermia and shivering |
| **Guillain–Barré** | Autonomic instability, respiratory failure (monitor vital capacity), avoid suxamethonium (hyperkalaemia) |
| **Motor neurone disease, multiple sclerosis** | Respiratory weakness, bulbar dysfunction; MS relapse with fever and stress; suxamethonium avoided in denervation |
| **Long-term spinal cord damage** | Autonomic dysreflexia, suxamethonium hyperkalaemia (see spinal injury) |

## Public health

Surgery is a "**teachable moment**": brief interventions for smoking, alcohol and obesity; referral to services; the effects of these on health and outcomes (wound healing, infection, pulmonary and cardiac complications).
