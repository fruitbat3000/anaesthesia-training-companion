## Levels and risks

Minimal, moderate ("conscious"), deep sedation and general anaesthesia form a continuum. **Deep sedation** carries airway and respiratory risks similar to GA and requires the same standards (an anaesthetist or appropriately trained practitioner, capnography, airway equipment).

## Drugs and techniques

| Drug / technique | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Propofol** (bolus or **TCI** at low effect-site targets) | Rapid titration; deepens quickly; no analgesia |
| **Remifentanil** (TCI) | Analgesia and sedation; apnoea and rigidity at higher doses; synergy with propofol |
| **Midazolam** | Anxiolysis and amnesia; slow offset in the elderly; flumazenil available |
| **Dexmedetomidine** | Rousable sedation with little respiratory depression; bradycardia, hypotension; useful in awake craniotomy and ATI |
| **Ketamine** | Dissociative sedation and analgesia; airway reflexes preserved; emergence phenomena |
| **Nitrous oxide / Entonox** | Short procedures |
| **Alternative routes** | Intranasal (dexmedetomidine, midazolam, fentanyl, ketamine) and rectal (children), oral premedication |

## Standards and scoring

- National guidance (e.g. the Academy of Medical Royal Colleges "Safe sedation practice" and RCoA/specialty guidance) sets standards for staff training, monitoring, equipment and recovery.
- Scoring: RASS, Ramsay, OAA/S (adults); University of Michigan Sedation Scale (children).
- **Discharge criteria:** return to baseline consciousness, stable observations, pain and nausea controlled, escort for 24 hours, written advice.

## Specific settings

Ophthalmic (brief, lying flat; avoid oversedation — patients may move unexpectedly), neurosurgical (awake craniotomy), dental (children and people with learning disabilities; conscious sedation standards), endoscopy (ERCP prone), cardiology (TAVI, ablations), radiology (MRI in children).

## Extremes of age and when not to sedate

Older patients: slower circulation, sensitivity, delirium. Children: unpredictable responses (paradoxical agitation, rapid airway obstruction). **Sedation is inappropriate** when the airway is at risk (e.g. severe OSA, full stomach, difficult airway), the patient cannot cooperate or lie still for the procedure, the procedure is too stimulating or long, or when staffing or location is unsuitable — choose GA or a regional technique instead.
