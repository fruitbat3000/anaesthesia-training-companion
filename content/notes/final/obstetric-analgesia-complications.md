## Epidural analgesia

- **Low-dose regimens** (e.g. 0.1% bupivacaine with fentanyl 2 µg/mL) preserve mobility and do not increase caesarean rates. **Programmed intermittent epidural boluses** with PCEA improve spread and reduce breakthrough pain compared with continuous infusions. **CSE** gives faster onset; **dural puncture epidural** (DPE) may improve quality.
- **The failing epidural:** assess the block (level, sidedness), the cause of pain (a full bladder, malposition, uterine rupture), pull back the catheter 1–2 cm, give a larger bolus of a stronger solution, and **resite early** if still inadequate — especially if the epidural may be needed for caesarean section.

## Accidental dural puncture and PDPH

- ADP with a 16–18G Tuohy needle causes PDPH in about half of women.
- Options at the time: resite the epidural at another space, or thread an **intrathecal catheter** (label clearly; dose as a spinal).
- **PDPH:** a positional fronto-occipital headache within days, with neck stiffness, tinnitus, photophobia, and sometimes cranial nerve palsies. Differential: pre-eclampsia, migraine, **cerebral venous sinus thrombosis**, **subdural haematoma** (a complication of untreated PDPH), meningitis.
- **Epidural blood patch** (about 20 mL of autologous blood) is the most effective treatment; follow-up is essential. **Sphenopalatine ganglion block** and occipital nerve blocks have been used, with limited evidence.

## Neurological complications

- Most postpartum nerve injuries are **obstetric** (e.g. lumbosacral trunk compression, lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh — meralgia paraesthetica, common peroneal compression), not anaesthetic.
- **Red flags:** a block that is denser or longer than expected, new motor weakness after the block should have worn off, back pain, fever → urgent assessment and **MRI** to exclude epidural haematoma or abscess. Follow the 2020 neurological monitoring guideline (regular checks of motor function; escalation criteria).

## Anticoagulation and thrombocytopenia

Plan neuraxial timing around LMWH doses; platelet thresholds as per local guidance; in HELLP or AFE check coagulation.

## Non-obstetric surgery in pregnancy

- Only urgent surgery; semi-elective in the **second trimester** if possible.
- Maintain uteroplacental perfusion: avoid hypotension, hypoxia, hyper- or hypocapnia; left uterine displacement after about 20 weeks; aspiration prophylaxis and RSI; fetal monitoring according to gestation and obstetric advice; tocolysis if needed.
- Most anaesthetic drugs in clinical doses are not known to be teratogenic; avoid unnecessary drugs in the first trimester. Radiation from necessary imaging is justified (shield where possible).
