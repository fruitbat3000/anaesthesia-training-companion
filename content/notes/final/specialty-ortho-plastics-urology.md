## Major orthopaedic surgery

- **Primary and revision arthroplasty** of hip and knee: spinal or GA with regional analgesia (periarticular infiltration, adductor canal and other blocks), **tranexamic acid**, **cell salvage** for revision surgery with major expected blood loss, VTE prophylaxis, early mobilisation; bone cement implantation syndrome risk with cemented components.
- **Pelvic and acetabular surgery:** major blood loss, prolonged prone or lateral positioning, nerve injury.

## Plastic and reconstructive surgery: free flaps

- Free tissue transfer depends on **flow through the microvascular anastomosis**. Blood flow ∝ perfusion pressure / resistance, and viscosity (Hagen–Poiseuille).
- **Goals:** a **high cardiac output**, normal or slightly high MAP, **vasodilated periphery** (warmth, analgesia, avoid vasoconstriction from hypothermia, hypovolaemia, pain and hypocapnia), **haematocrit about 0.30–0.35** (lower viscosity while preserving oxygen content), normothermia, adequate but not excessive fluids.
- **Vasopressors:** often feared but modest doses to maintain perfusion pressure are acceptable in many centres; avoid hypotension.
- Long procedures: pressure areas, heat loss, positioning, and postoperative flap monitoring.
- **Burns reconstruction** and dressing changes: see burns.

## Urology

- **TURP syndrome:** absorption of large volumes of hypotonic irrigation fluid (**1.5% glycine**) through opened prostatic venous sinuses → **dilutional hyponatraemia**, fluid overload (hypertension, bradycardia, pulmonary oedema), cerebral oedema (confusion, seizures, coma), and glycine toxicity (transient **visual disturbance**, ammonia). Risk increases with resection time (> 60 min), high irrigation pressure and large glands. **Spinal anaesthesia** allows early detection of confusion. Treatment: stop the procedure, fluid restriction, diuretics for overload, **hypertonic saline** for severe symptomatic hyponatraemia (correct cautiously). Bipolar resection with saline irrigation has largely replaced glycine and removes the hyponatraemia risk (fluid overload remains).
- Other issues: lithotomy position, bladder perforation (shoulder tip pain), obturator nerve stimulation (adductor jerk), bacteraemia, bleeding.

## Day surgery: organisation and advances

- Most elective surgery can be done as day cases, including many major procedures with enhanced recovery (e.g. laparoscopic cholecystectomy, hernia, some arthroplasty and spinal surgery). **BADS** and GIRFT set targets and directories of procedures.
- **Organisation:** dedicated units, nurse-led preassessment with protocols, efficient scheduling, audit of **unplanned admission**, cancellation and patient-reported outcomes, and follow-up telephone calls.
- **Advances:** short-acting spinal agents (chloroprocaine, prilocaine) for day-case lower limb and perineal surgery, opioid-sparing multimodal analgesia, and remote monitoring.
