## What is different

- **Airway:** large occiput and tongue, high anterior larynx, floppy epiglottis, short trachea (easy endobronchial intubation), obligate nasal breathing in early months.
- **Respiratory:** high oxygen consumption (6–8 mL/kg/min), low FRC, closing volume near FRC, immature ventilatory control (**apnoea**, especially in ex-prems), a fatigue-prone diaphragm.
- **Cardiovascular:** rate-dependent output, **transitional circulation** (reversion to fetal circulation with hypoxia, acidosis and hypothermia), limited contractile reserve.
- **Renal and hepatic:** immature concentrating ability and drug metabolism; reduced clearance of many drugs in the first weeks.
- **Pharmacology:** larger ECF (↑ Vd of water-soluble drugs), higher MAC (except in preterm neonates), sensitivity to non-depolarising NMBs varies; **reduced plasma protein binding**.
- **Temperature:** high surface area to volume ratio; **non-shivering thermogenesis** (brown fat) increases oxygen consumption.
- **Glucose:** limited glycogen stores → hypoglycaemia with prolonged fasting; give glucose-containing maintenance fluid.

## Conduct of anaesthesia

- **Preparation:** experienced team, warmed theatre (and warming mattress, overhead heater, warm fluids, humidification), correctly sized equipment, drugs by weight double-checked, and confirmation that vitamin K has been given.
- **Induction:** IV or inhalational; awake intubation is now rare; cuffed microcuff tubes are widely used. Avoid hyperoxia in preterm infants (retinopathy of prematurity) — titrate SpO~2~ to targets.
- **Monitoring:** pre- and post-ductal SpO~2~ where relevant, temperature, glucose, blood loss (small absolute volumes are significant).
- **Analgesia:** paracetamol (reduced dose), regional (caudal, spinal), careful opioids with monitoring.

## Common neonatal surgical problems

- **Pyloric stenosis** (2–8 weeks): **a medical, not surgical, emergency** — projectile vomiting causes **hypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis** (with paradoxical aciduria). **Resuscitate first** with fluids and correct electrolytes (e.g. chloride > 100 mmol/L, bicarbonate < 28 mmol/L), then surgery. Empty the stomach with a nasogastric tube before induction; RSI or modified RSI; laparoscopic or open pyloromyotomy; postoperative apnoea monitoring.
- **Inguinal hernia** in ex-premature infants, intestinal obstruction (malrotation with volvulus is an emergency), necrotising enterocolitis, gastroschisis and exomphalos.

## The ex-premature infant

**Postoperative apnoea** risk is highest in infants below about **60 weeks post-conceptual age**, especially with anaemia; they need overnight apnoea monitoring after GA. Spinal anaesthesia without sedation may reduce early apnoea. Also bronchopulmonary dysplasia, subglottic stenosis from prolonged intubation.
