## Communication and decisions

- **Discharge from ICU:** timely (avoid night-time discharges), clear written and verbal handover, rehabilitation plans, follow-up clinics (post-intensive care syndrome).
- **Treatment escalation plans and ceilings of care:** discussed with the patient where possible, or those important to them, based on likely benefit and the patient's values; decisions made by the senior clinician; documented clearly (e.g. ReSPECT).
- **Breaking bad news:** prepare (setting, people, information), check what they know, give a warning shot, deliver clearly and compassionately, allow silence, respond to emotion, summarise the plan (SPIKES framework). Be aware of cultural and religious differences.

## Withdrawing life-sustaining treatment

There is no ethical or legal difference between withholding and withdrawing treatment that is no longer in the patient's best interests. Focus on **comfort**: symptom control (opioids, anxiolytics, anti-secretory drugs) with doses titrated to effect (the principle of double effect), family presence, spiritual care, and stopping non-beneficial interventions (monitoring alarms, blood tests). Palliative care teams support end-of-life care in ICU and for patients with end-stage disease.

## Brainstem death and donation after brainstem death (DBD)

- **Preconditions and tests** as in the Primary note (Academy of Medical Royal Colleges code); two sets of tests; death certified after the first set.
- **Physiological support of the donor:** loss of brainstem function causes cardiovascular instability (initial catecholamine storm, then vasodilation), **diabetes insipidus**, hypothermia, coagulopathy, and endocrine failure. Targets and management (vasopressin, fluids, hormone therapy per protocols) optimise organ function for transplantation.

## Donation after circulatory death (DCD)

After a decision to withdraw treatment, with consent, death is confirmed after circulatory arrest and a 5-minute observation period; organs are retrieved promptly. Normothermic regional perfusion is used in some centres.

## Legal and ethical framework

- **Consent:** deemed consent ("opt-out") legislation in England, Wales and Scotland, with the family's views remaining central in practice; the Organ Donor Register; the **specialist nurse for organ donation** should be involved early in every potential donor.
- Coroner or procurator fiscal involvement when required; the ethics of living donation (donor safety, freedom from coercion); cultural and religious considerations.
