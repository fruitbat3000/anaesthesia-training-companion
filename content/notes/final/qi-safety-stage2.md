## Improvement science

- **Model for Improvement** and **PDSA cycles**, driver diagrams (aim → primary and secondary drivers → change ideas), process mapping, and stakeholder engagement.
- **Measures:** outcome, process and **balancing** measures (checking for unintended consequences).
- **Data for improvement** (frequent, small samples, plotted over time) versus **data for assurance** (performance against standards, e.g. national audits) versus **data for research**.

## Variation

- **Common-cause variation** is inherent in a stable process; **special-cause variation** signals a change (improvement or a problem).
- **Run charts** (rules: shifts, trends, runs, astronomical points) and **statistical process control (SPC) charts** with control limits distinguish them. Reacting to common-cause variation as if it were special ("tampering") makes things worse; unwarranted variation between units is a target for improvement.

## Reliable care

Human factors design (standardisation, checklists, forcing functions, reminders, simplification, reducing reliance on memory), high-reliability organisation principles, and matching the **expertise and resources to the risk** (senior presence for high-risk cases, the right place for postoperative care, out-of-hours staffing).

## Following up critical incidents

- Immediate care and support of the patient; debrief; **report**; investigation proportionate to harm (systems-focused learning reviews, e.g. under PSIRF).
- **Duty of candour:** tell the patient (and family), apologise, explain what is known, what will happen next, and provide written follow-up for notifiable incidents.
- **Unexpected difficult intubation:** explain to the patient, document fully (grade, what worked, what failed), give a **difficult airway letter** to the patient and GP, add a clinical alert, and encourage registration with a difficult airway database where available (DAS recommendations).
- Staff support (second victims) and learning shared through M&M meetings.
