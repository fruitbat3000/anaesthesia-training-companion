## Congenital anomalies

- **Tracheo-oesophageal fistula / oesophageal atresia:** frothy secretions, inability to pass a nasogastric tube; associated **VACTERL** anomalies (check the heart); positioning the tube below the fistula but above the carina (or blocking the fistula), avoiding gastric distension with IPPV.
- **Congenital diaphragmatic hernia:** pulmonary hypoplasia and pulmonary hypertension; gentle ventilation (permissive hypercapnia), avoid mask ventilation (gut distension); surgery once stabilised.
- **Congenital heart disease:** understand the lesion and the current physiology (shunt direction, pulmonary blood flow, duct dependency, Fontan circulation — passive pulmonary flow sensitive to PVR and intrathoracic pressure); avoid air bubbles (paradoxical embolism with right-to-left shunts); involve cardiac anaesthetists; endocarditis prophylaxis only in specific high-risk situations.
- **Trisomy 21:** airway (large tongue, subglottic stenosis — smaller tube), **atlantoaxial instability** (neutral neck), congenital heart disease (AVSD), OSA, hypothyroidism, bradycardia with sevoflurane induction, learning disability.

## Neurodiversity and learning disability

Autism and learning disability: reasonable adjustments (first on the list, quiet environment, familiar carers, hospital passport, flexible induction, premedication, possibly restraint only as a last resort under legal frameworks), capacity assessment (from 16) and best interests decisions, combining procedures under one anaesthetic.

## The sick and injured child

- **Recognition:** ABCDE with age-specific normal values; causes of deterioration differ from adults: arrest is usually **hypoxic**, preceded by respiratory failure or shock.
- **Specific conditions:** meningococcal sepsis (fluid, antibiotics, inotropes, early intubation), bronchiolitis, severe asthma, status epilepticus, DKA (cautious fluids — cerebral oedema risk), trauma (head injury is the main cause of death; non-accidental injury must be considered).
- **Airway:** cuffed tubes are now standard in critical illness (check cuff pressure); RSI with ketamine or reduced propofol doses; atropine for bradycardia.
- **Resuscitation (RCUK PLS):** 5 rescue breaths, 15:2 compressions, adrenaline 10 µg/kg, amiodarone 5 mg/kg for shockable rhythms, shocks 4 J/kg.
- **Transfer:** usually by regional paediatric retrieval teams; the local team stabilises and prepares.
