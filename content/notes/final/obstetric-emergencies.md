## Amniotic fluid embolism (AFE)

- Sudden **cardiorespiratory collapse**, hypoxia, and **coagulopathy (DIC)** during labour, caesarean section or shortly after delivery; may include seizures and fetal distress. An anaphylactoid-type reaction to fetal material entering the maternal circulation.
- A diagnosis of exclusion; management is supportive: **ABC, CPR with left uterine displacement, resuscitative hysterotomy** within about 5 minutes of arrest, massive transfusion with early **fibrinogen replacement**, and critical care (ECMO in some centres). Report to UKOSS.

## Maternal sepsis

A leading cause of maternal death. Recognise early (modified obstetric early warning scores; pregnancy masks signs), sepsis six, early antibiotics, source control (retained products, endometritis, chorioamnionitis, urinary, respiratory including influenza and COVID). Consider neuraxial block carefully (systemic infection, coagulopathy).

## Uterine rupture and inversion

- **Rupture:** usually through a previous caesarean scar; fetal distress, pain, bleeding, loss of station → immediate laparotomy (GA often needed).
- **Inversion:** shock out of proportion to blood loss (vagal); immediate manual replacement, uterine relaxation (GTN, volatile anaesthesia, terbutaline), then uterotonics.

## Other situations

- **Multiple pregnancy:** higher risk of pre-eclampsia, haemorrhage, and operative delivery; an epidural in labour is recommended to facilitate delivery of the second twin.
- **Intrauterine death:** compassionate care; coagulopathy risk if the fetus is retained for weeks; induction of labour with good analgesia.
- **Premature delivery:** steroids, magnesium for fetal neuroprotection (< 30–32 weeks), neonatal team.
- **VTE:** thromboprophylaxis by risk assessment; timing of neuraxial blocks around LMWH.

## MBRRACE-UK

The confidential enquiries into maternal deaths report leading causes (thromboembolism, cardiac disease, psychiatric causes, epilepsy and stroke, sepsis, haemorrhage), highlight **inequalities** (higher mortality in Black and Asian women and those living in deprived areas), and make recommendations for anaesthetic practice (early escalation, multidisciplinary care, recognising the sick woman).
