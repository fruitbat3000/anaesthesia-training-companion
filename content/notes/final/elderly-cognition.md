## Frailty

- A state of reduced physiological reserve and vulnerability to stressors. Assessed with the **Clinical Frailty Scale** (CFS 1–9; ≥ 5 is frail), gait speed, grip strength, and comprehensive geriatric assessment.
- Predicts complications, delirium, loss of independence, and mortality after surgery better than age alone. Proactive care (e.g. **POPS** — perioperative medicine for older people undergoing surgery — services) improves outcomes.

## Drugs in older people

Reduced doses and slower titration: reduced volume of the central compartment, reduced clearance (renal and hepatic), increased brain sensitivity (lower MAC, opioid sensitivity), longer circulation times (slow onset — don't chase with more drug). Polypharmacy and interactions.

## Delirium

- **Acute, fluctuating disturbance of attention and cognition**; hyperactive, hypoactive (commoner and often missed) or mixed. Common after emergency and major surgery, especially hip fracture (up to about half of patients).
- **Associated with** increased mortality, length of stay, institutionalisation and long-term cognitive decline.
- **Risk factors:** age, dementia, frailty, sensory impairment, depression, alcohol, polypharmacy, severity of illness, emergency surgery.
- **Prevention (multicomponent):** orientation, glasses and hearing aids, sleep, hydration and nutrition, early mobilisation, adequate analgesia, avoid **anticholinergics and benzodiazepines**, minimise catheters and restraints, treat infection and constipation; possibly processed EEG to avoid excessive anaesthetic depth.
- **Assessment:** 4AT (ward), CAM-ICU (ICU). **Management:** treat causes, non-drug measures; low-dose antipsychotics only for severe distress or danger (avoid in Parkinson's and Lewy body dementia).

## Postoperative cognitive dysfunction and neurocognitive disorders

The term "**perioperative neurocognitive disorders**" includes delirium, delayed neurocognitive recovery (to 30 days) and postoperative neurocognitive disorder (up to 12 months). Risk rises with age, pre-existing impairment, major surgery and complications. The anaesthetic technique (GA versus regional) has not been shown to make a consistent difference.

## Dementia and reasonable adjustments

Involve carers (hospital passport, "This is me"), familiar people present at induction and in recovery, continuity, a calm environment, capacity assessment and best interests decisions, and pain assessment tools for non-verbal patients (PAINAD, Abbey).
