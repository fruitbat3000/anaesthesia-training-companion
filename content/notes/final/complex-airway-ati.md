## Assessment

- **History:** voice change, stridor (at rest suggests severe narrowing), dyspnoea lying flat, dysphagia, previous radiotherapy or surgery, previous anaesthetic charts (grade, devices, difficulty).
- **Examination:** mouth opening, neck movement and tissue (radiotherapy fibrosis), trismus, tongue and floor of mouth, trachea position.
- **Investigations:** **flexible nasendoscopy** (often with the ENT surgeon, in clinic or the anaesthetic room) showing supraglottic and glottic pathology; **CT/MRI** (level, extent and length of narrowing, tracheal compression, distal airway); 3D reconstruction and virtual endoscopy; flow–volume loops for fixed or variable obstruction.
- **Shared decision-making** with the surgeon and patient: which route (awake or asleep; oral, nasal, front-of-neck), which device, and the backup plans (including awake tracheostomy under local anaesthesia).

## Awake tracheal intubation (ATI)

**Indications:** predicted difficult laryngoscopy **and** difficult rescue (mask, SGA or front-of-neck), significant risk of aspiration or rapid desaturation with a predicted difficult airway, limited mouth opening, unstable neck.

The DAS guideline for ATI (2019) uses the elements **sTOP**: **s**edation (optional, minimal), **T**opicalisation, **O**xygenation, **P**erformance:

- **Oxygenation:** high-flow nasal oxygen throughout.
- **Topicalisation:** lidocaine to the airway (e.g. atomised or "spray-as-you-go" through the scope), within a safe total dose (commonly quoted maximum about 9 mg/kg lean body weight for topical lidocaine); check adequacy before instrumentation. Nerve blocks are an alternative.
- **Sedation:** optional and minimal (e.g. remifentanil TCI or small doses of midazolam), with a sedationist separate from the operator where possible; the patient must remain cooperative and breathing.
- **Performance:** flexible bronchoscope (oral or nasal) or **video laryngoscope**; railroad a suitably small, soft-tipped tube; confirm with capnography and bronchoscopic view **before** inducing anaesthesia.
- **Limit attempts** (the DAS ATI guideline suggests a maximum of three attempts, plus one by a more experienced operator) and have a clear plan if ATI fails (e.g. awake front-of-neck access).

## Asleep techniques in complex airways

Inhalational induction maintaining spontaneous ventilation (less favoured now for many adult obstructions), IV induction with a double set-up for FONA, HFNO/apnoeic oxygenation, and jet ventilation (above or below the lesion) in selected laryngeal surgery.
