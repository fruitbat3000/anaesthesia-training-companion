## The vascular patient

High prevalence of **ischaemic heart disease**, hypertension, COPD (smoking), diabetes, CKD and frailty. Assessment includes functional capacity (CPET is common before open repair), echocardiography, renal function, and a multidisciplinary decision between open repair, EVAR or surveillance.

## Aortic cross-clamping

| | Clamp on (infrarenal) | Clamp off |
|---|---|---|
| Afterload | **↑↑** — hypertension, LV strain, ischaemia | **↓↓** — hypotension |
| Preload | ↑ (blood redistributed above the clamp) | ↓ |
| Distal tissues | Ischaemia, anaerobic metabolism | **Washout** of acid, potassium, lactate, and inflammatory mediators → myocardial depression, vasodilation |
| Management | Deepen anaesthesia, vasodilators (GTN), avoid hypertension and ischaemia | Volume loading before release, **gradual release** (communicate with the surgeon), vasopressors, correct acidosis and calcium |

Suprarenal and supracoeliac clamps cause more marked changes and greater renal and visceral ischaemia.

## Organ protection

- **Renal:** maintain perfusion and volume, minimise clamp time, avoid nephrotoxins; no drug has proven renal protective benefit (mannitol and dopamine are not recommended as protectors).
- **Spinal cord** (thoracoabdominal and complex endovascular repairs): the **artery of Adamkiewicz** may be compromised → paraplegia. Protection: **CSF drainage** (lumbar drain, keeping CSF pressure about ≤ 10–15 mmHg), maintaining MAP (spinal cord perfusion pressure = MAP − CSF pressure), distal perfusion, hypothermia, and neurological monitoring (MEPs).

## Open repair

Arterial and central lines, cardiac output monitoring, **thoracic epidural** (in selected patients) or other analgesia, cell salvage, warming, tranexamic acid, and critical care afterwards. Complications: bleeding, MI, AKI, bowel ischaemia, spinal cord ischaemia, respiratory failure.

## Endovascular repair (EVAR)

Less physiological stress and lower early mortality, but lifelong surveillance and endoleak risk. Anaesthesia: local anaesthesia with sedation, regional, or GA. Issues: contrast nephropathy, radiation, sudden conversion to open repair, and (complex/branched grafts) spinal cord ischaemia.

## Ruptured AAA

- A surgical emergency: **permissive hypotension** (e.g. systolic about 70–90 mmHg, conscious patient) until the aorta is controlled; minimal crystalloid; blood products via major haemorrhage protocol; tranexamic acid; large-bore access.
- **Prepare and drape before induction** (induction can cause cardiovascular collapse as sympathetic tone and tamponade are lost); the surgeon ready to clamp. **EVAR under local anaesthesia** where anatomy allows (lower mortality in some studies).
- Afterwards: abdominal compartment syndrome, AKI, coagulopathy, multi-organ failure.
