## Acute kidney injury

- **KDIGO staging** (creatinine rise and urine output). Causes: prerenal (hypoperfusion), intrinsic (ATN from sepsis and ischaemia, nephrotoxins, glomerulonephritis, rhabdomyolysis, contrast), post-renal (obstruction — exclude with ultrasound and catheter).
- **Management:** optimise haemodynamics and volume (avoid overload), stop nephrotoxins, adjust drug doses, treat hyperkalaemia and acidosis.
- **Indications for renal replacement therapy (RRT):** refractory hyperkalaemia, acidosis, fluid overload, uraemic complications (pericarditis, encephalopathy), and certain poisonings (lithium, methanol, salicylates).

## Renal replacement therapy

- **Modes:** continuous (CVVH — convection; CVVHD — diffusion; CVVHDF — both) in unstable ICU patients; **intermittent haemodialysis** (faster, more haemodynamic stress); peritoneal dialysis (less common in adult ICU).
- **Convection** (solvent drag) removes middle molecules better; **diffusion** removes small molecules (urea, potassium).
- **Anticoagulation of the circuit:** **regional citrate** (binds calcium in the circuit; monitor ionised calcium; citrate accumulation in liver failure) or heparin.
- **Drug dosing** depends on the mode and effluent rate: drugs with small Vd, low protein binding and renal elimination are removed (e.g. many antibiotics — dose to maintain levels; therapeutic drug monitoring).

## Liver failure

- **Acute liver failure** (e.g. paracetamol overdose, viral, drug-induced): encephalopathy, coagulopathy, hypoglycaemia, cerebral oedema (raised ICP), infection, renal failure, hyperdynamic circulation. Early referral to a liver unit (King's College criteria for transplant).
- **Acute-on-chronic liver failure:** decompensated cirrhosis with variceal bleeding (terlipressin, antibiotics, endoscopy, TIPSS), SBP, hepatorenal syndrome (albumin and terlipressin).
- **Porphyria:** acute attacks precipitated by drugs (barbiturates, some anticonvulsants), fasting and infection; use safe-drug lists; haem arginate.

## Gastrointestinal problems

Nausea and vomiting, ileus and gastric stasis (prokinetics), **pancreatitis** (severity scoring, fluids, analgesia, early enteral feeding, managing complications), bowel obstruction, swallowing disorders after prolonged intubation, stress ulcer prophylaxis, and the gut mucosal barrier (bacterial translocation).

## Nutrition in critical illness

- **Enteral feeding** preferred, started within 24–48 hours when possible; **post-pyloric** feeding for gastric intolerance.
- **Parenteral nutrition** when enteral feeding is not possible or inadequate (timing debated).
- **Energy** about 20–25 kcal/kg/day (lower early), **protein** 1.2–2 g/kg/day; avoid **overfeeding** (hyperglycaemia, raised CO~2~ production, hepatic steatosis, azotaemia, hypertriglyceridaemia).
- **Refeeding syndrome** (phosphate, potassium, magnesium, thiamine).
