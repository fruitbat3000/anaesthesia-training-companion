## Special groups

| Group | Considerations |
|---|---|
| Children and infants | Age-appropriate tools (FLACC, faces), weight-based dosing, regional techniques, nurse-controlled analgesia |
| Older people | Cognitive impairment (PAINAD, Abbey), reduced clearance, delirium risk, NSAID caution |
| Communication difficulties, learning disability | Carers' knowledge, behaviour-based tools |
| Unconscious / critically ill | Behavioural Pain Scale, CPOT; analgesia-first sedation |
| Renal or hepatic impairment | Drug choice and dose adjustment |
| **Long-term opioid users** | Continue baseline opioid; expect higher needs; ketamine; regional; avoid withdrawal; plan discharge tapering |
| **Buprenorphine** | Usually continue (especially low doses), add full agonists and regional; specialist advice for high doses |
| **Methadone** | Continue; consider QT; add multimodal analgesia |
| Substance misuse | Non-judgemental; treat pain adequately; manage withdrawal; addiction services |

## Complex analgesic needs

Opioid-induced hyperalgesia (increasing pain despite increasing opioids), neuropathic components after surgery (gabapentinoids short-term in selected patients), IV **ketamine** infusions (low dose), **IV lidocaine** infusions (abdominal surgery; with monitoring and dose limits), regional catheters, and acute pain team review.

## Persistent (chronic) post-surgical pain

- Pain persisting at least 3 months after surgery, not present before or different in character. Common after **amputation, thoracotomy, mastectomy, inguinal hernia repair, cardiac surgery and knee arthroplasty** (10–50% for some procedures).
- **Risk factors:** preoperative pain, psychological factors (anxiety, catastrophising), younger age, female sex, surgical nerve injury, **severe acute postoperative pain**, radiotherapy and chemotherapy.
- **Reducing risk:** nerve-sparing surgical techniques, good perioperative analgesia (regional where appropriate), identifying at-risk patients, early review of persistent pain, and transitional pain services.
