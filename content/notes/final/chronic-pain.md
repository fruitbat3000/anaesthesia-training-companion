## Definitions

- **Chronic pain:** pain lasting longer than 3 months. **Chronic primary pain** (e.g. fibromyalgia, chronic widespread pain, complex regional pain syndrome, chronic primary low back pain) is a condition in its own right; **chronic secondary pain** is due to another condition (e.g. osteoarthritis, cancer, post-surgical, neuropathic).
- **Mechanisms:** **nociceptive** (tissue damage; somatic or visceral), **neuropathic** (lesion or disease of the somatosensory nervous system), **nociplastic** (altered nociception without clear tissue or nerve damage — central sensitisation).

## Assessment

History (site, onset, character, radiation, severity, timing, aggravating and relieving factors), **impact on function, sleep, mood, work and relationships**, beliefs and expectations, previous treatments, and **red flags** (e.g. cauda equina, cancer, infection, fracture). Examination for sensory changes (allodynia, hyperalgesia). Validated questionnaires (e.g. Brief Pain Inventory; DN4 or LANSS for neuropathic features).

## The biopsychosocial model

Pain experience is shaped by biology, psychology (fear-avoidance, catastrophising, depression, anxiety, trauma including **adverse childhood experiences**) and social factors (work, family, culture, finances). Genetic and cultural differences influence perception and reporting.

## Management (NICE NG193 for chronic primary pain)

- **Supported self-management, exercise and physical activity**, psychological therapies (**CBT, ACT**), acupuncture (in some cases), and **pain management programmes** delivered by a multidisciplinary team (physicians, psychologists, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, nurses).
- **Medication:** for chronic primary pain NICE recommends **antidepressants** (e.g. amitriptyline, duloxetine, SSRIs) may be considered; it advises **against** starting opioids, gabapentinoids, NSAIDs, paracetamol, benzodiazepines or local anaesthetics for chronic primary pain. For neuropathic pain, see the separate note.
- **Barriers:** patient beliefs, societal and cultural factors, access to services, and health system organisation.
