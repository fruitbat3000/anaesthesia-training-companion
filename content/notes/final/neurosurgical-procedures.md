## Posterior fossa surgery and the sitting position

- Brainstem proximity: cardiovascular changes (bradycardia, hypertension) from surgical stimulation; cranial nerve damage (airway protection postoperatively).
- **Sitting or park bench** positions improve access but risk **venous air embolism (VAE)**, pneumocephalus, hypotension and quadriplegia (neck flexion).
- **VAE:** detected by precordial Doppler or TOE (most sensitive), a sudden fall in ETCO~2~, rise in end-tidal nitrogen, hypotension, arrhythmias. Management: tell the surgeon to flood the field, lower the head, jugular compression, 100% O~2~, aspirate via a multi-orifice CVC in the right atrium, support the circulation. A patent foramen ovale risks paradoxical embolism.

## Awake craniotomy

For tumours near eloquent areas (speech, motor) and epilepsy surgery: scalp blocks (supraorbital, supratrochlear, zygomaticotemporal, auriculotemporal, greater and lesser occipital nerves) and pin-site infiltration, then either **asleep–awake–asleep** (with an SGA) or **conscious sedation** (propofol and remifentanil or dexmedetomidine). Patient selection and preparation are key; manage seizures (cold saline to the cortex), airway obstruction and agitation.

## Pituitary surgery (trans-sphenoidal)

Endocrine issues (acromegaly — difficult airway; Cushing's; hypopituitarism — steroid cover), nasal packs and bleeding, a smooth emergence, and postoperative **diabetes insipidus** (or SIADH).

## Spinal surgery

- **Scoliosis surgery:** restrictive lung disease, associated neuromuscular disorders, major blood loss (tranexamic acid, cell salvage), prone positioning (eyes: **postoperative visual loss**; pressure areas; abdomen free), and **spinal cord monitoring** (SSEPs and MEPs with TIVA, no NMB; a wake-up test is a historical alternative).
- **Spinal trauma** (see spinal injury) and **postoperative analgesia** (intrathecal opioids, epidural catheters placed by the surgeon, multimodal).

## Neuroradiology

**Mechanical thrombectomy for stroke:** time-critical; general anaesthesia or conscious sedation (local practice varies); avoid hypotension (keep systolic ≥ 140 mmHg before recanalisation, typical); transfer and radiology suite safety. **Coiling** of aneurysms (see SAH).

## Other procedures

- **Shunt surgery** (hydrocephalus; raised ICP at induction).
- **Trigeminal neuralgia** procedures (balloon compression or thermocoagulation of the Gasserian ganglion): brief deep anaesthesia, trigeminocardiac reflex (bradycardia).
- **Stereotactic** surgery (frame on the head: airway access).
- **CSF drainage in aortic surgery** protects the spinal cord (see vascular).

## Prion diseases

Neurosurgical instruments used in patients with, or at risk of, CJD must be handled according to specific guidance (quarantine or disposal).
