## Lung isolation

- **Indications:** absolute — isolation to prevent contamination (infection, massive haemorrhage), control of ventilation (bronchopleural fistula, unilateral bullae), unilateral lung lavage; relative — surgical exposure (lung resection, oesophagectomy, thoracic spine, VATS).
- **Double-lumen tubes (DLTs):** left-sided DLTs are used for most procedures (the right upper lobe bronchus arises close to the carina, making right-sided DLTs harder to position). Size by height and imaging (women about 35–37 Fr, men 39–41 Fr). **Confirm position with a flexible bronchoscope** after insertion and after positioning.
- **Bronchial blockers:** useful with difficult airways, existing single-lumen tubes, nasal intubation or when postoperative ventilation is needed; slower lung deflation.

## Physiology of one-lung ventilation (OLV)

- In the **lateral position** with the non-dependent lung collapsed, blood flowing through it becomes **shunt**. **Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction** diverts flow to the ventilated lung, and gravity helps.
- HPV is inhibited by volatile agents (weakly, dose-dependently), vasodilators and alkalosis; TIVA may preserve it slightly better.

## Managing hypoxaemia during OLV

1. Increase FiO~2~ to 1.0; check the tube position with the bronchoscope; suction.
2. Optimise ventilation of the dependent lung: **recruitment**, appropriate **PEEP**, avoid excessive airway pressures (which divert blood to the non-ventilated lung).
3. **CPAP (or oxygen insufflation) to the non-ventilated lung** (warn the surgeon).
4. Intermittent two-lung ventilation; surgical clamping of the pulmonary artery in pneumonectomy.
- **Protective ventilation:** tidal volumes about 4–6 mL/kg predicted body weight to the single lung, PEEP, plateau pressure < 25 cmH~2~O, to reduce acute lung injury (especially after pneumonectomy).

## Procedures and postoperative care

- Lobectomy (often **VATS** or robotic), pneumonectomy (fluid restriction: post-pneumonectomy pulmonary oedema; avoid excessive fluid), rigid bronchoscopy (shared airway, jet ventilation), mediastinoscopy (compression of the innominate artery — right radial arterial line or pulse oximeter), oesophagectomy, pleurodesis and decortication.
- **Analgesia:** paravertebral catheter or erector spinae block (as effective as thoracic epidural for many procedures with fewer side effects), thoracic epidural in selected cases, multimodal systemic analgesia.
- **Complications:** air leak, atelectasis, pneumonia, arrhythmias (AF), bleeding, bronchopleural fistula, ARDS, and nerve injury (phrenic, recurrent laryngeal).
