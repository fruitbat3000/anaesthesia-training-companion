## Sepsis

- **Pathophysiology:** a dysregulated host response: cytokine release, endothelial activation and glycocalyx damage (capillary leak), vasodilation (NO), microcirculatory dysfunction, mitochondrial dysfunction, coagulation activation (DIC), immunosuppression later.
- **Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 (key points):** screen and treat urgently; **antibiotics within 1 hour** for probable septic shock; **30 mL/kg crystalloid** (balanced) in the first 3 hours for hypoperfusion (individualised with reassessment); **noradrenaline** first-line vasopressor to MAP ≥ 65 mmHg, adding **vasopressin**; hydrocortisone for ongoing vasopressor needs; lactate-guided resuscitation and capillary refill time; source control; restrictive transfusion (Hb 70 g/L); lung-protective ventilation; VTE and stress ulcer prophylaxis.
- **Antimicrobials:** principles of action and choice (see Primary), **stewardship** (cultures first, review at 48–72 hours, de-escalate, stop), antivirals and antifungals in selected patients (immunocompromised, specific infections).

## Shock

| Type | Examples | Profile |
|---|---|---|
| Hypovolaemic | Haemorrhage, GI losses | ↓ CO, ↑ SVR, ↓ filling |
| **Distributive** | Sepsis, anaphylaxis, neurogenic, adrenal crisis | ↑/normal CO (early sepsis), **↓ SVR** |
| Cardiogenic | MI, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, valve failure | ↓ CO, ↑ SVR, ↑ filling pressures |
| Obstructive | PE, tamponade, tension pneumothorax | ↓ CO, specific signs |

Assessment: clinical examination (perfusion, capillary refill, mentation, urine output), lactate, echocardiography, cardiac output monitoring, ScvO~2~.

## Cardiovascular drugs and devices

Vasopressors (noradrenaline, vasopressin, phenylephrine), inotropes (dobutamine, adrenaline, milrinone, levosimendan), and mechanical support (IABP, Impella, VA-ECMO) in cardiogenic shock; pulmonary hypertension and RV failure management; blood pressure targets individualised (e.g. higher in chronic hypertension).

## Investigations

Interpret blood tests (lactate, procalcitonin cautiously, troponin, coagulation), blood gases, imaging (CXR, CT, ultrasound), echocardiography, and microbiology.

## The deteriorating patient

**NEWS2**-triggered escalation, critical care outreach, rapid response teams, early senior review and treatment escalation plans. Scoring systems (SOFA, APACHE II) quantify severity in ICU (for audit and research more than individual prognosis).
