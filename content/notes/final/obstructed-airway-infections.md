## Principles

Keep the patient calm and sitting up; high-flow oxygen; **senior anaesthetist and ENT/maxillofacial surgeon early**; imaging only if the patient is stable; temporising measures (nebulised adrenaline, steroids, heliox); a **clear plan with a double set-up** for front-of-neck access (or awake tracheostomy under local anaesthesia). Avoid sedatives on the ward.

## Infections

- **Adult supraglottitis/epiglottitis:** sore throat, odynophagia, muffled voice, drooling; nasendoscopy (by experienced hands) shows swelling. Many settle with antibiotics, steroids and close observation in a monitored area; deteriorating patients need a secure airway (ATI or awake FONA).
- **Ludwig's angina** (bilateral submandibular cellulitis) and **dental abscesses:** trismus (may not improve with anaesthesia if caused by swelling rather than pain), floor-of-mouth elevation, tongue displacement. ATI (nasal) or awake tracheostomy are common strategies.
- **Retropharyngeal and peritonsillar (quinsy) abscess:** risk of rupture and aspiration with instrumentation.

## Maxillofacial trauma

Airway at risk from bleeding, swelling, loss of tongue support (bilateral mandibular fractures), and base-of-skull fractures (avoid nasal instrumentation). Cervical spine protection; may need submental intubation or tracheostomy.

## Anterior mediastinal mass

- Lymphoma, thymoma, germ cell tumours, retrosternal goitre. **Symptoms** (orthopnoea, stridor, SVC obstruction, syncope) and **CT** (tracheal compression > 50%, bronchial compression) predict risk.
- **General anaesthesia can cause catastrophic airway collapse or cardiovascular collapse** (loss of muscle tone, reduced FRC, compression of the airway or great vessels).
- Strategies: obtain tissue diagnosis under local anaesthesia if possible; pre-treatment (steroids, radiotherapy) where appropriate; if GA is needed, maintain **spontaneous ventilation**, avoid neuromuscular blockade until the airway is secured, have a rigid bronchoscope and surgeon ready, change position (lateral or prone) if collapse occurs, and consider **ECMO standby or pre-emptive femoral cannulation** in high-risk cases.
