## Professional behaviours

- **Guiding less experienced colleagues:** supervision (appropriate to their level), teaching, giving feedback, and knowing when to escalate. **Role-modelling** respectful, safe behaviour.
- **Complex patients:** formulate individualised plans with the patient (risks and benefits explained in understandable terms, shared decisions), and recognise your own limits.
- **Behaviour and safety:** incivility and unprofessional behaviour harm team performance and patient safety; speak up and support colleagues; reflect on your own behaviour.
- **Difficult situations:** leading under pressure, maintaining situation awareness, and professional judgement in emergencies.
- **Wellbeing:** the effects of shift work, fatigue, stress and lifestyle on health and performance; strategies (rest, rota design, peer support, professional help).

## Management and organisation

- How anaesthetic services are organised: theatres, preoperative assessment, critical care, pain, obstetrics, **day surgery** (facilities, staffing, protocols, audit), and links with regional tertiary services and networks.
- Departmental management structures (clinical directors, college tutors, rota coordinators), job planning, clinical governance, risk management, complaints and litigation.
- **Legal and ethical frameworks:** consent and shared decision-making, confidentiality, organ donation ethics, Mental Capacity Act, and Mental Health Act.
- **Digital working:** electronic records and prescribing, information governance, data protection, and safe use of digital tools and AI.

## Safeguarding adults and capacity

- Principles: **empowerment, prevention, proportionality, protection, partnership, accountability**.
- Communicate with safeguarding teams, sharing information appropriately (confidentiality versus protection).
- **Mental Capacity Act** in practice: capacity assessment, best interests decisions for procedures such as **ECT** and dental treatment under GA, deprivation of liberty safeguards, and independent advocates.
- **People with learning disabilities, autism, acute confusion, dementia and mental illness:** physical and psychological needs, reasonable adjustments, involvement of carers and those who support the patient (who have important roles in the perioperative period).
