## Why it matters

Emergency laparotomy carries a high mortality (around 10% at 30 days in national data, much higher in older and frail patients). The **National Emergency Laparotomy Audit (NELA)** has driven improvements through standards of care.

## Key standards (NELA-derived)

- **Documented risk assessment** before surgery (the **NELA risk calculator**; P-POSSUM) to guide the level of seniority and postoperative care.
- **Consultant surgeon and consultant anaesthetist present** in theatre for high-risk patients (predicted mortality ≥ 5%).
- **Timely surgery** matching the urgency (e.g. immediate for septic shock with peritonitis) and **antibiotics within 1 hour** of suspected sepsis.
- **Admission to critical care** for high-risk patients.
- **Elderly care (geriatrician) review** for older patients (e.g. over 65 or frail).
- CT reported by a consultant radiologist before surgery.

## Anaesthetic conduct

- **Resuscitation in parallel** with preparation (fluids, vasopressors, blood), arterial line before induction in unstable patients.
- **RSI** (aspiration risk, bowel obstruction; empty the stomach with a nasogastric tube) with **reduced-dose induction**, vasopressor running or drawn up.
- **Goal-directed haemodynamic management**, lung-protective ventilation, temperature control, glucose control, blood products as needed.
- **Analgesia:** opioids, rectus sheath or TAP catheters, wound infiltration, epidural in selected patients without sepsis or coagulopathy.
- **End of surgery:** decide on extubation versus ventilation, and the destination (ICU or enhanced care).

## Shared decision-making and ceilings of care

For frail or very high-risk patients, discuss with the patient (or those important to them) the likely outcomes, including **not operating** and palliative care. Record treatment escalation plans and ceilings of treatment; respect the patient's values.
