## The approach (RCUK peri-arrest algorithms)

Assess with ABCDE, give oxygen, obtain IV access and a 12-lead ECG, correct electrolytes, and look for **adverse features**: **shock, syncope, myocardial ischaemia, severe heart failure**. Their presence determines the urgency and route of treatment.

## Tachycardias

| Rhythm | Stable | With adverse features |
|---|---|---|
| **Broad regular** (VT unless proven otherwise) | Amiodarone (300 mg IV over 10–60 min) | **Synchronised DC cardioversion** (up to three attempts, with sedation or anaesthesia), then amiodarone |
| Broad irregular | AF with BBB (treat as AF), pre-excited AF (avoid AV nodal blockers; cardiovert), polymorphic VT/**torsades** (magnesium 2 g) | Cardioversion |
| **Narrow regular** (SVT, AVNRT) | **Vagal manoeuvres** (modified Valsalva), then **adenosine** (6, 12, 18 mg rapid boluses; flutter becomes visible); β-blocker or verapamil | Cardioversion |
| **Narrow irregular** (AF) | Rate control (β-blocker, diltiazem, digoxin in heart failure); anticoagulation assessment; rhythm control if onset < 48 h or anticoagulated | Cardioversion |

**Synchronised cardioversion:** the shock is timed to the R wave (to avoid inducing VF); higher energies for broad-complex rhythms and AF; conscious patients need sedation or anaesthesia (usually by an anaesthetist, considering fasting and airway).

## Bradycardias

- Treat if adverse features or **risk of asystole** (recent asystole, Mobitz II, complete heart block with broad QRS, ventricular pauses > 3 s).
- **Atropine 500 µg IV** (repeat to 3 mg); then **isoprenaline** or **adrenaline** infusion (or other drugs: aminophylline, glucagon for β-blocker overdose, dopamine); **transcutaneous pacing**, then **transvenous pacing** by an expert. **Percussion pacing** (fist pacing) is a temporising option in some circumstances.
- Heart transplant recipients do not respond to atropine.

## Thrombolysis

For **massive PE** with cardiac arrest or shock (alteplase), and in some STEMI and stroke settings where primary PCI or thrombectomy is not available. Continue CPR for 60–90 minutes after thrombolysis in arrest. Contraindications (recent surgery, bleeding) are weighed against the risk of death.

## Anaphylaxis (Stage 2)

Leadership of the crisis, refractory anaphylaxis (adrenaline infusion, vasopressin, glucagon, ECMO), investigation and allergy referral, and patient communication (see the Primary notes). Recognise allergy versus non-allergic reactions.

## Cardiac arrest in special positions and settings

- **Prone position** (e.g. spinal surgery): start CPR prone with compressions between the scapulae (a hand or sandbag under the sternum as counter-pressure) and defibrillation with pads placed posterolaterally, while preparing to turn the patient supine; turn when it can be done quickly and safely.
- **Open-chest cardiac compression** and **resuscitative thoracotomy** (penetrating trauma, post-cardiac surgery) — specialist indications.
- **Post-arrest care:** see temperature control in ECMO and advanced support.
