## Hip

- **Fascia iliaca** (infra- or supra-inguinal): hip fracture analgesia from the emergency department onwards (recommended in UK guidance), and hip surgery.
- **PENG (pericapsular nerve group) block:** targets the articular branches of the femoral and accessory obturator nerves between the iliopubic eminence and psoas tendon; aims to spare quadriceps strength.
- **Neuraxial** (spinal) for surgery; lumbar plexus block in specialist hands (deep; epidural spread risk).

## Knee

- **Adductor canal block** (saphenous nerve and nerve to vastus medialis): analgesia after knee arthroplasty with **less quadriceps weakness** than femoral block (better mobilisation, fewer falls).
- **iPACK** (infiltration between the popliteal artery and the capsule of the knee): posterior knee pain.
- **Femoral nerve block:** effective but weakens quadriceps.
- Periarticular local infiltration by the surgeon is also common.

## Lower leg, ankle and foot

- **Popliteal sciatic block** (before the bifurcation into tibial and common peroneal nerves) + **saphenous** block for the medial ankle: foot and ankle surgery.
- **Ankle block:** tibial, deep peroneal, superficial peroneal, sural and saphenous nerves: forefoot surgery.
- Beware **compartment syndrome** in trauma (e.g. tibial fractures): discuss with the surgeon; lower concentrations, monitoring, and clear documentation.

## Choosing neuraxial versus peripheral blocks

Consider the procedure, anticoagulation, the patient's comorbidities (e.g. severe AS), the need for early mobilisation, urinary retention, and patient preference. Spinal anaesthesia with short-acting agents supports day-case lower limb surgery.
