## Chronic kidney disease

- **Associated problems:** hypertension, cardiovascular disease, anaemia, platelet dysfunction (uraemia; desmopressin helps), hyperkalaemia, acidosis, fluid overload, delayed gastric emptying, renal osteodystrophy, and difficult vascular access (**protect arteriovenous fistulas**: no cannulas, cuffs or tight wraps on that arm).
- **Dialysis patients:** timing of dialysis (usually the day before surgery; check potassium and fluid status afterwards), avoid nephrotoxins, and consider residual function.
- **Drugs:** avoid or reduce renally excreted drugs and active metabolites (**morphine** — M6G; **pethidine**; high doses of gabapentinoids; enoxaparin adjustments). **Atracurium or cisatracurium**, fentanyl, alfentanil, remifentanil, propofol are suitable. Suxamethonium is acceptable if potassium is normal (rise of about 0.5 mmol/L). Sugammadex is not recommended in severe renal impairment.

## Liver disease

- **Severity:** **Child–Pugh** (bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites, encephalopathy: class A–C) and **MELD** (bilirubin, INR, creatinine ± sodium) predict perioperative mortality, which is high in decompensated cirrhosis.
- **Problems:** coagulopathy (rebalanced — use viscoelastic tests), thrombocytopenia, hypoalbuminaemia, ascites (reduced FRC; drain), encephalopathy (avoid sedatives), hyperdynamic circulation with low SVR, **hepatorenal syndrome**, **hepatopulmonary syndrome** (hypoxaemia from intrapulmonary shunts, worse upright), **portopulmonary hypertension**, varices (avoid oesophageal instrumentation), hypoglycaemia, alcohol withdrawal, malnutrition.
- **Drugs:** increased volume of distribution of water-soluble drugs, reduced clearance of hepatically metabolised drugs (benzodiazepines, opioids), reduced plasma cholinesterase; atracurium/cisatracurium and remifentanil are useful.

## Transplantation

- **Organ donation ethics:** deceased donation after brainstem death (DBD) or circulatory death (DCD); living donation (donor safety and consent); respecting the donor's and family's wishes (opt-out legislation in parts of the UK, with family involvement).
- **Anaesthesia for kidney transplant:** optimise volume to ensure graft perfusion (MAP targets), avoid nephrotoxins; immunosuppression.
- **Liver transplant:** a specialist field (massive transfusion, reperfusion syndrome, coagulopathy).
- **Patients with a transplanted organ having other surgery:** continue immunosuppression (drug interactions and levels), strict asepsis (infection risk), renal toxicity of calcineurin inhibitors, and the **denervated heart** (a heart transplant responds to circulating catecholamines but **not to atropine**; use direct-acting drugs such as isoprenaline or adrenaline; slower response to hypovolaemia).
