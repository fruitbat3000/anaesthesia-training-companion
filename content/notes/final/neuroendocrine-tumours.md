## Phaeochromocytoma

- Catecholamine-secreting tumour of the adrenal medulla (paragangliomas elsewhere); may be part of MEN 2, von Hippel–Lindau or neurofibromatosis.
- **Features:** paroxysmal or sustained hypertension, headache, sweating, palpitations, anxiety; cardiomyopathy; hyperglycaemia. Diagnosis by plasma or urinary **metanephrines**, then imaging.

**Preoperative preparation (usually 10–14 days)**

1. **α-blockade first:** **phenoxybenzamine** (non-selective, irreversible) or **doxazosin** (selective α~1~). Liberal salt and fluid intake to restore the contracted plasma volume.
2. **β-blockade only after adequate α-blockade** (for tachycardia): unopposed α stimulation after β-blockade can precipitate a hypertensive crisis and heart failure.
3. **Roizen's criteria** (targets such as BP < 160/90 for 24 h, orthostatic hypotension, no ST changes, few ventricular ectopics) are traditionally used to judge readiness.

**Intraoperative**

- Arterial line before induction, central access, deep anaesthesia and analgesia; avoid triggers (histamine-releasing drugs, desflurane surges, metoclopramide, ephedrine, hypoventilation, surgical handling).
- **Hypertension during tumour handling:** short-acting vasodilators (**phentolamine, nitroprusside, GTN, magnesium**), esmolol for tachycardia, clevidipine or nicardipine.
- **Hypotension after tumour vein ligation:** fall in catecholamines with downregulated receptors and vasodilation → fluids, vasopressors (noradrenaline, vasopressin).
- **Hypoglycaemia** after removal (rebound insulin); postoperative monitoring in a high-dependency area; steroids if bilateral adrenalectomy.

## Carcinoid tumours

- Neuroendocrine tumours (commonly small bowel) secreting **serotonin**, histamine, kinins and other mediators. **Carcinoid syndrome** (usually with liver metastases): flushing, diarrhoea, bronchospasm, and **carcinoid heart disease** (right-sided valve fibrosis: tricuspid regurgitation, pulmonary stenosis).
- **Carcinoid crisis** during anaesthesia: severe hypotension (or hypertension), bronchospasm, flushing — triggered by handling, catecholamines, histamine release.
- **Management:** **octreotide** (somatostatin analogue) before and during surgery, and IV boluses for crisis; avoid histamine-releasing drugs and catecholamines where possible (they may worsen mediator release); vasopressin or phenylephrine for hypotension; echocardiography before surgery.
