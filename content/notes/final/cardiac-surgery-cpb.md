## The bypass circuit

Venous blood drains (gravity or vacuum-assisted) from the right atrium or cavae to a **reservoir**, through a **membrane oxygenator** (gas exchange; the sweep gas flow controls CO~2~) and **heat exchanger**, and is pumped (roller or centrifugal) back through an **arterial filter** into the ascending aorta. Flows about **2.2–2.4 L/min/m^2^** at normothermia, with a MAP of about 50–80 mmHg.

## Anticoagulation

- **Unfractionated heparin** about **300–400 units/kg**, aiming for an **activated clotting time (ACT) > 400–480 s** before bypass. Heparin resistance (antithrombin deficiency) → more heparin, antithrombin concentrate or FFP.
- **Reversal:** **protamine** (about 1 mg per 100 units of heparin) after bypass; reactions include hypotension (rapid injection), anaphylaxis and catastrophic pulmonary hypertension.
- **HIT** patients need alternatives (e.g. bivalirudin).

## Myocardial protection and temperature

- **Cardioplegia** (high-potassium solutions, cold or warm blood, antegrade via the aortic root or retrograde via the coronary sinus) arrests the heart in diastole, reducing oxygen demand.
- **Hypothermia** reduces metabolic rate (about 7% per °C); deep hypothermic circulatory arrest for aortic arch surgery (with cerebral protection strategies). Rewarm slowly to avoid cerebral hyperthermia and bubble formation. **Alpha-stat** acid–base management in adults.

## Physiological effects of bypass

Haemodilution, systemic inflammatory response, platelet dysfunction and consumption of coagulation factors, altered drug pharmacokinetics (dilution, sequestration in the circuit, hypothermia), microemboli (neurocognitive dysfunction and stroke), renal injury, and vasoplegia.

## Weaning from bypass

Checklist: temperature, rhythm (pacing wires), potassium and calcium, acid–base, Hb, **ventilation restarted**, de-airing (TOE), adequate contractility (inotropes as needed), filling guided by TOE. Mechanical support if needed: **intra-aortic balloon pump** (inflates in diastole to augment coronary perfusion, deflates before systole to reduce afterload; timed to the ECG or arterial trace; contraindicated in significant aortic regurgitation and dissection), ventricular assist devices, ECMO.

## Off-pump coronary artery bypass

Avoids bypass but requires the heart to be displaced and stabilised while beating → haemodynamic instability (head-down tilt, fluids, vasopressors, pacing), ischaemia during grafting, and a need for rapid conversion to bypass.

## After cardiac surgery

- **Bleeding:** surgical versus coagulopathic (viscoelastic testing guides products; protamine, tranexamic acid, fibrinogen, platelets).
- **Cardiac tamponade:** falling cardiac output, rising and equalising filling pressures, hypotension, reduced drain output (clotted drains), pulsus paradoxus; echocardiography helps but can be misleading after surgery (localised clot). Treatment: **urgent re-sternotomy** (on the unit if arrest), fluids and inotropes while preparing; avoid excess PEEP and vasodilators.
- Arrhythmias (AF common), low cardiac output state, vasoplegia, AKI, stroke and delirium, respiratory failure.
- **Cardiac arrest after cardiac surgery:** modified ALS (up to three quick shocks for VF, early re-sternotomy within 5 minutes, pacing for asystole) — the CALS approach.
