## Chest wall

| Block | Target | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Thoracic paravertebral (PVB)** | Paravertebral space lateral to the vertebral body (spinal nerves as they exit) | Thoracotomy, VATS, breast surgery, rib fractures; unilateral somatic and sympathetic block; pneumothorax risk |
| **Erector spinae plane (ESP)** | Deep to erector spinae, on the transverse process | Rib fractures, thoracic and spinal surgery; simple, low-risk; variable spread |
| **Serratus anterior plane (SAP)** | Superficial or deep to serratus anterior (lateral cutaneous branches) | Rib fractures (lateral), VATS, breast surgery |
| **PECS I / II** | Between pectoralis major and minor (± serratus) | Breast surgery |
| **Thoracic epidural** | Epidural space | Bilateral analgesia; hypotension, urinary retention, motor block; contraindicated with coagulopathy |

**Rib fractures:** multimodal analgesia; regional techniques (epidural, PVB, ESP or SAP catheters) for multiple fractures or older patients, improving respiratory function and possibly outcomes; scoring tools to identify high risk.

## Abdominal wall

| Block | Target | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **TAP (lateral / subcostal)** | Between internal oblique and transversus abdominis | Somatic abdominal wall (T7–L1 depending on approach); **no visceral analgesia** |
| **Rectus sheath** | Posterior rectus sheath (terminal branches of T7–T11) | Midline incisions (bilateral, catheters common) |
| **Quadratus lumborum** | Around quadratus lumborum (several approaches) | Possibly some visceral spread (paravertebral) |
| **Ilioinguinal / iliohypogastric** | Between internal oblique and transversus, medial to the ASIS | Inguinal hernia, orchidopexy |
| **Penile block** | Dorsal nerves of the penis (subpubic space) | Circumcision |
| **Pudendal** | S2–4 | Perineal surgery |
| **Caudal** | Epidural via the sacral hiatus | Children (see paediatrics) |

Fascial plane blocks depend on **volume and spread**; watch total local anaesthetic dose (bilateral catheters), and use them as part of multimodal analgesia rather than stand-alone techniques.
