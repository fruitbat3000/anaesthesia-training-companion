## Pathophysiology

Abnormal placentation → placental ischaemia → release of anti-angiogenic factors (e.g. sFlt-1) → **widespread endothelial dysfunction**: vasoconstriction, capillary leak, activation of coagulation and platelets. Affects every organ: brain (eclampsia, stroke, PRES), kidneys (proteinuria, AKI), liver (HELLP, rupture), lungs (pulmonary oedema), blood (thrombocytopenia, DIC), placenta (fetal growth restriction, abruption).

## Diagnosis and severity (NICE NG133)

- New hypertension (≥ 140/90) after 20 weeks with proteinuria **or** other maternal organ dysfunction or uteroplacental dysfunction.
- **Severe features:** BP ≥ 160/110, headache, visual disturbance, epigastric or right upper quadrant pain, clonus, low platelets, rising creatinine or transaminases, pulmonary oedema, HELLP (haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets).

## Management

- **Antihypertensives:** oral labetalol (first-line), nifedipine, methyldopa; IV labetalol or hydralazine for severe hypertension. Target around 135/85.
- **Magnesium sulphate** (loading dose then infusion) for severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia: prevents and treats seizures (better than phenytoin or diazepam). Monitor reflexes, respiratory rate, urine output and SpO~2~; reduce dose in oliguria; **calcium gluconate** for toxicity; potentiates neuromuscular block.
- **Fluid restriction** (e.g. about 80 mL/h total unless ongoing losses) to avoid pulmonary oedema.
- **Timing of delivery** is the definitive treatment, balancing maternal risk and fetal maturity (steroids for fetal lung maturity).

## Anaesthesia

- **Neuraxial is preferred** for labour and caesarean section (reduces stress response, avoids airway risks). Check the **platelet count** (trend matters; many units accept > 75 × 10^9^/L without other coagulopathy) and coagulation if platelets are low or HELLP.
- Hypotension after spinal is usually less marked than in normal pregnancy; use vasopressors cautiously.
- **General anaesthesia:** airway oedema (smaller tubes), and **attenuate the hypertensive response to laryngoscopy** (risk of intracranial haemorrhage) with, for example, remifentanil, alfentanil, labetalol or magnesium; beware magnesium–NMB interactions.
- Postpartum: seizures and severe hypertension may occur in the first days; continue monitoring.
