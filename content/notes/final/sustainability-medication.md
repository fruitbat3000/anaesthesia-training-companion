## Sustainability

- The NHS aims for net-zero carbon emissions; anaesthesia contributes through **inhaled agents** (desflurane and nitrous oxide especially), energy use, single-use equipment and medicines waste.
- **Actions:** avoid desflurane (removed from routine use in many UK hospitals); decommission nitrous oxide manifolds and fix leaks; **low fresh gas flows**; TIVA and regional anaesthesia where appropriate; reusable equipment when safe; reduce drug waste (not drawing up unused emergency drugs routinely); switch off equipment; consider travel and procurement. Balance environmental benefit against patient safety.

## Medicines management

- **Existing medications:** which to continue (β-blockers, statins, antiepileptics, Parkinson's drugs, steroids, most inhalers), which to stop or adjust (anticoagulants, antiplatelets per risk, ACEi/ARB on the day, SGLT2 inhibitors, sulphonylureas, MAOIs by specialist decision, lithium), and when to restart.
- **Antibiotics and infection prevention:** surgical prophylaxis timing and choice, stewardship, and measures to prevent surgical site infection (hair removal by clipping, skin preparation, normothermia, glucose control, oxygenation).
- **Proactive safety:** risk assessment of likelihood and severity (e.g. FMEA), standardised drug concentrations, labelling and connectors (NRFit), and wrong-site block prevention.

## Drug toxicity, additives and MH

- **Toxicity:** causes (overdose, accumulation in organ failure, interactions, pharmacogenetics) and avoidance (dose checking, therapeutic monitoring).
- **Drug additives and excipients:** preservatives (e.g. metabisulfite, benzyl alcohol — avoid neuraxially), latex, allergens (egg and soya in propofol are not now considered contraindications for most patients), and lipid load from propofol.
- **Malignant hyperthermia:** see the Primary note; Stage 2 emphasis on leadership of the crisis and referral for testing.
