## Patients

Often **elderly with comorbidities** (diabetes, hypertension, cognitive impairment, anticoagulants); most surgery (e.g. cataract) is done under **local anaesthesia** as day cases. Anticoagulants are usually continued for sub-Tenon's and topical techniques.

## Local anaesthetic techniques

| Technique | Features | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Topical** (drops, gel) ± intracameral | Cataract surgery; no akinesia | Discomfort, patient movement |
| **Sub-Tenon's** | Blunt cannula into the sub-Tenon's space (inferonasal); akinesia with larger volumes | Chemosis, subconjunctival haemorrhage; serious complications rare |
| **Peribulbar (extraconal)** | Needle outside the muscle cone | Globe perforation (especially axial length > 26 mm), haemorrhage |
| **Retrobulbar (intraconal)** | Needle inside the cone; dense akinesia | Retrobulbar haemorrhage, optic nerve injury, **brainstem anaesthesia** (spread along the optic nerve sheath → apnoea, loss of consciousness), globe perforation |

## Intraocular pressure (IOP)

- Normal 10–20 mmHg; determined by aqueous humour production (ciliary body) and drainage (trabecular meshwork, canal of Schlemm), choroidal blood volume and extraocular muscle tone.
- **Increased by:** coughing, straining, vomiting, hypercapnia, hypoxia, venous congestion (head-down, tight ties), external pressure (facemask), **suxamethonium** (transient), ketamine (slight), laryngoscopy.
- **Decreased by:** most IV and volatile anaesthetics, hyperventilation, head-up tilt, **acetazolamide**, **mannitol**, timolol.
- **Oculocardiac reflex:** trigeminal afferents, vagal efferents (see anatomy).

## Specific situations

- **Penetrating eye injury with a full stomach:** balance aspiration risk against extrusion of ocular contents from raised IOP. RSI with **rocuronium** (avoid suxamethonium if possible, although its IOP rise is small and it has been used safely), avoid coughing and pressure on the eye; deep extubation is not appropriate in the full stomach — smooth awake extubation.
- **Intraocular gas** (SF~6~, C~3~F~8~ after vitreoretinal surgery): **avoid nitrous oxide** for weeks (expansion, raised IOP, blindness); avoid air travel.
- **Strabismus surgery** in children: oculocardiac reflex and high PONV risk.
- **Operating conditions:** a still patient, a soft eye, a clear field, and a lying-flat, comfortable patient (claustrophobia, orthopnoea, cough or tremor can make local anaesthesia unsuitable).
