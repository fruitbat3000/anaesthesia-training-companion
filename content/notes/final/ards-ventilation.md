## Respiratory failure

- **Type 1** (hypoxaemic; PaO~2~ < 8 kPa, normal or low PaCO~2~): V/Q mismatch, shunt (pneumonia, pulmonary oedema, ARDS, PE).
- **Type 2** (hypercapnic; PaCO~2~ > 6.5 kPa): pump failure (neuromuscular disease, drugs, chest wall disease, fatigue, COPD).
- **Support:** oxygen, **HFNO**, **CPAP** (hypoxaemia, pulmonary oedema), **NIV** (BiPAP: hypercapnic COPD exacerbations, obesity hypoventilation, neuromuscular disease), invasive ventilation.

## ARDS (Berlin definition)

Acute onset (within a week), bilateral opacities not fully explained by effusions or collapse, not fully explained by cardiac failure or fluid overload, and hypoxaemia on at least 5 cmH~2~O PEEP: **P/F ratio ≤ 40 kPa mild, ≤ 26.7 moderate, ≤ 13.3 severe** (300/200/100 mmHg). Causes: pneumonia, sepsis, aspiration, trauma, pancreatitis, transfusion.

## Lung-protective ventilation

- **Tidal volume 6 mL/kg predicted body weight** (4–8), **plateau pressure ≤ 30 cmH~2~O**, driving pressure (plateau − PEEP) ideally < 15 cmH~2~O, **PEEP** titrated (tables or individualised), permissive hypercapnia (pH > 7.20–7.25), oxygen targets (SpO~2~ 88–95%).
- **Prone positioning** for at least 16 hours a day in moderate–severe ARDS (P/F < 20 kPa) reduces mortality (PROSEVA).
- **Neuromuscular blockade** in early severe ARDS (selective use), conservative fluid strategy once shock resolves, avoid high-frequency oscillation.
- **Rescue:** **VV-ECMO** in specialist centres for refractory hypoxaemia or hypercapnia; inhaled nitric oxide or prostacyclin as temporary rescue.

## Obstructive airway disease

Severe **asthma** and **COPD** on the ventilator: low rates, long expiratory times, small tidal volumes, low PEEP (or matched to intrinsic PEEP in COPD), watch for **dynamic hyperinflation** (hypotension, rising plateau pressure — disconnect to decompress), bronchodilators, steroids; volatile anaesthetics and ketamine for refractory asthma; heliox; ECMO.

## Other gases and environments

Nitric oxide (pulmonary vasodilator; methaemoglobin and NO~2~ monitoring), heliox (upper airway obstruction), the effects of altitude and hyperbaric oxygen (see Primary).

## Postoperative ventilation

Indications: inadequate airway protection, inadequate gas exchange or respiratory effort, cardiovascular instability, hypothermia, major surgery with fluid shifts, planned (e.g. free flap airway oedema, major trauma), neurological reasons (raised ICP). Weigh the benefits of a controlled wake-up against ventilator-associated complications.
