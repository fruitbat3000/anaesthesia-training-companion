## Goals

Maintain **cerebral perfusion and oxygenation**, reduce **ICP** and brain bulk for surgical access, avoid secondary injury, allow **rapid, smooth emergence** for early neurological assessment, and facilitate neuromonitoring.

## Effects of anaesthetic drugs

| Drug | CBF | CMRO~2~ | ICP | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Propofol | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | Preserves autoregulation and coupling; TIVA popular |
| Thiopental | ↓ | ↓↓ | ↓ | Burst suppression for refractory ICP |
| Volatile (< 1 MAC) | ↑ (dose-dependent vasodilation) | ↓ | ↔/↑ | "Uncoupling"; sevoflurane least vasodilating; autoregulation impaired at higher doses |
| N~2~O | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ | Usually avoided |
| Ketamine | ↑ (if PaCO~2~ rises) | ↑/↔ | ↔ with controlled ventilation | Acceptable in TBI with ventilation |
| Opioids | ↔ | ↔ | ↔ (if normocapnic) | Remifentanil allows rapid emergence |
| Suxamethonium | Transient ↑ ICP | | | Not contraindicated when needed for RSI |

## Reducing ICP and brain bulk

Head-up **15–30°**, neutral neck, loose ties; adequate depth and analgesia (avoid coughing and straining); **normocapnia** (brief hyperventilation to PaCO~2~ ~4 kPa only as a rescue); normoxia; normothermia; normoglycaemia; **osmotherapy** (mannitol 0.25–1 g/kg or hypertonic saline); CSF drainage (external ventricular drain, lumbar drain); steroids (dexamethasone) for **tumour-related vasogenic oedema** only (not TBI); barbiturate coma or decompressive craniectomy for refractory ICP.

## Monitoring

Standard monitoring plus **arterial line** (transducer at the tragus for CPP when head-up), sometimes CVP, ICP, jugular bulb saturation, processed EEG. **Neurophysiological monitoring** (SSEPs, MEPs, EMG) requires TIVA and **avoidance of neuromuscular block** after intubation (for MEPs); volatile agents depress evoked potentials in a dose-dependent way.

## Other issues

- **Positioning:** prone (eyes, face, abdomen free), park bench, sitting (venous air embolism, pneumocephalus), pinned head (the pinning response needs analgesia or local anaesthetic).
- **VTE:** neurosurgical patients are at high risk, but anticoagulation risks intracranial bleeding: mechanical prophylaxis, then pharmacological at a time agreed with the neurosurgeon.
- **Emergence:** smooth, without hypertension or coughing (remifentanil, lidocaine, labetalol); early neurological examination.
