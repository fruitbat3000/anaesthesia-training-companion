## TIVA models

- **Propofol:** **Marsh** (weight-based; plasma targeting; overdoses the elderly and obese if actual weight is used), **Schnider** (age, height, weight, sex, lean body mass; effect-site targeting; problems with LBM formulae in morbid obesity), **Eleveld** (a newer general-purpose model from a large pooled dataset covering a wide range of ages and weights, including children and obese patients).
- **Remifentanil: Minto** (age, LBM); **Eleveld** remifentanil model.
- In **obesity**, follow model-specific guidance (e.g. adjusted weight for Marsh, or Eleveld) and use processed EEG.

## Safe TIVA (Association of Anaesthetists/SIVA 2018)

- Dedicated IV cannula visible throughout, **anti-reflux valves and non-return valves**, minimal dead space; syringes clearly labelled; programmed pumps with correct drug concentrations; alarms on.
- **Processed EEG** monitoring when neuromuscular blockers are used (monitoring standards 2021).
- Beware disconnection, occlusion, extravasation, empty syringes and drug errors — common causes of **awareness** in NAP5 (which found TIVA overrepresented in awareness reports, often from errors and transfer between areas).
- **Other NAP5 risk factors:** neuromuscular block, obstetric GA, cardiac surgery, RSI, obesity, difficult airway, junior anaesthetists, out-of-hours. Awareness can cause psychological harm (PTSD); follow up with an apology, explanation and support.

## Positioning

| Position | Physiological effects | Injuries |
|---|---|---|
| **Prone** | ↓ venous return if the abdomen is compressed (use supports), ↑ FRC and oxygenation, access to the airway limited | **Eye injury and postoperative visual loss** (ischaemic optic neuropathy, central retinal artery occlusion from pressure), facial pressure injury, brachial plexus, breasts and genitals, air embolism (spinal) |
| **Lateral** | V/Q mismatch (dependent lung better perfused, non-dependent better ventilated under IPPV) | Axillary neurovascular compression (axillary roll), ear, eye |
| **Sitting / beach-chair** | Hypotension and cerebral hypoperfusion (measure BP at head level), VAE | Cerebral ischaemia, pressure injuries |
| **Lithotomy** | ↑ venous return on raising legs, ↓ on lowering; ↓ FRC | **Common peroneal nerve**, lower limb compartment syndrome (long procedures) |
| **Trendelenburg** | ↑ ICP and IOP, ↓ FRC, airway oedema | Brachial plexus (shoulder braces), sliding |

Obese patients are at greater risk of pressure injury, rhabdomyolysis, and respiratory compromise in all positions.
