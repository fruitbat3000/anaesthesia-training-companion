## Ischaemic heart disease

- Assess symptoms and functional capacity; ECG; further testing (stress imaging, angiography) only when it would change management independent of surgery (ESC 2022).
- **Coronary stents:** elective surgery is usually delayed until **dual antiplatelet therapy** (DAPT) can safely be reduced: typically **6 months after an elective PCI** and **12 months after an acute coronary syndrome**, though shorter times may be acceptable with newer stents after cardiology discussion. Continue aspirin where possible. Premature DAPT cessation risks **stent thrombosis** (high mortality).
- Continue β-blockers and statins; do not start β-blockers just before surgery; aim to avoid tachycardia and hypotension.
- **Perioperative myocardial injury (MINS):** a troponin rise, often silent, associated with increased mortality; troponin monitoring in high-risk patients is recommended in some guidelines.

## Heart failure and cardiomyopathy

- Decompensated heart failure: defer elective surgery. Optimise medical therapy (see cardiac drugs); careful fluid management; invasive monitoring for major surgery; anticipate hypotension with ACEi/ARNI.
- **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy** with LVOT obstruction: avoid tachycardia, hypovolaemia, vasodilation and inotropes (which worsen obstruction); phenylephrine and fluids for hypotension; β-blockers.
- **Implanted devices:** see pacemakers and ICDs.

## Pulmonary hypertension

- High perioperative risk (RV failure). Avoid increases in **PVR** (hypoxia, hypercapnia, acidosis, pain, high airway pressures, hypothermia) and falls in **systemic pressure** (RV coronary perfusion depends on aortic pressure); maintain RV preload and contractility. Continue pulmonary vasodilators; have inhaled NO or prostacyclin available; specialist centre input.

## Adult congenital heart disease

Understand the anatomy and repair (e.g. **Fontan**: passive pulmonary blood flow — avoid high intrathoracic pressures, maintain preload; **Eisenmenger**: fixed pulmonary hypertension with right-to-left shunt — avoid falls in SVR and increases in PVR; air filters on lines). Arrhythmias, residual lesions, endocarditis risk. Involve an ACHD specialist.

## Common perioperative drugs to review

Anticoagulants and antiplatelets (bleeding versus thrombosis), antihypertensives (ACEi/ARB on the day), diuretics (volume and electrolytes), SGLT2 inhibitors (ketoacidosis), and hypoglycaemics.
