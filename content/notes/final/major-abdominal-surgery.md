## Enhanced recovery (ERAS)

A bundle of evidence-based elements: preoperative education, prehabilitation and optimisation (anaemia, nutrition, smoking, alcohol), minimal fasting with carbohydrate loading, **minimally invasive surgery**, goal-directed or balanced fluid therapy (avoiding overload), multimodal opioid-sparing analgesia, PONV prophylaxis, normothermia, avoiding drains and nasogastric tubes, **early feeding and mobilisation**. Reduces complications and length of stay.

## Liver resection

- **Low central venous pressure technique** during parenchymal transection (fluid restriction, head-up tilt, vasodilators) reduces hepatic venous bleeding, at the risk of air embolism and hypoperfusion; restore volume after resection.
- **Pringle manoeuvre** (clamping the portal triad) limits inflow; ischaemic tolerance limits.
- Postoperative liver dysfunction (small future remnant), coagulopathy (affects epidural catheter management), and analgesia (wound catheters, spinal opioids, epidural where coagulation allows).

## Pancreatic surgery (Whipple's)

Long procedure; jaundice (coagulopathy, renal risk), diabetes, malnutrition, fluid balance, and postoperative pancreatic fistula and delayed gastric emptying.

## Oesophagectomy

Two- or three-stage (abdominal and thoracic, ± neck); **one-lung ventilation** for the thoracic stage; aspiration risk (achalasia-like physiology, obstruction); malnutrition; thoracic epidural or paravertebral analgesia; avoid fluid overload and hypotension (anastomotic perfusion); complications include anastomotic leak, pneumonia, ARDS, AF, chylothorax and recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy.

## Robotic and laparoscopic surgery

Steep **Trendelenburg** (raised ICP and IOP, facial and airway oedema, reduced FRC, ventilation challenges) and pneumoperitoneum; limited access to the patient once docked (secure lines and tubes beforehand; plan for emergency undocking); positioning injuries; longer procedures.

## Bariatric surgery

- **Preoperative:** OSA (CPAP), obesity hypoventilation, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, a liver-reducing diet.
- **Intraoperative:** ramped positioning, pre-oxygenation with CPAP/HFNO, video laryngoscopy, lean-body-weight drug dosing, protective ventilation with PEEP and recruitment, TIVA or low-solubility agents, multimodal opioid-sparing analgesia.
- **Postoperative:** early mobilisation, CPAP for OSA, VTE prophylaxis (weight-adjusted), watch for anastomotic leak (tachycardia is an early sign).
- **After bariatric surgery:** dumping syndrome, micronutrient deficiencies (B~12~, iron, folate, thiamine), altered drug absorption, and aspiration risk after sleeve gastrectomy.

## Depth of anaesthesia monitoring

**Processed EEG** (e.g. BIS: 40–60 is the usual target for GA) reduces the risk of awareness with TIVA and neuromuscular block and may reduce anaesthetic exposure in older patients (possibly reducing delirium). Limitations: neuromuscular activity (EMG), ketamine and N~2~O (not reflected), hypothermia, age, and neurological disease.
