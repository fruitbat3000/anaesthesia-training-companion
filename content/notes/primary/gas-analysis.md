## Anaesthetic agents, CO~2~ and N~2~O: infrared absorption

Molecules with **two or more different atoms** absorb infrared at characteristic wavelengths (CO~2~ ~4.3 µm; N~2~O ~4.5 µm; volatile agents in the 8–12 µm range, allowing agent identification). O~2~, N~2~ and helium (single element) do not absorb infrared.

## Oxygen analysers

| Method | Principle | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Paramagnetic** | O~2~ is **paramagnetic** (attracted into a magnetic field, because of unpaired electrons). Classic: nitrogen-filled glass dumbbells in a non-uniform field rotate as O~2~ concentration changes; modern: differential pressure in a switched magnetic field | Fast (breath-by-breath), accurate, used in anaesthetic monitors |
| **Fuel cell (galvanic cell)** | O~2~ diffuses through a membrane and is reduced at a gold cathode with a lead anode, generating a **current proportional to PO~2~**; no external power | Slower response (about 20 s); the cell is consumed over months; used in anaesthetic machine O~2~ analysers |
| **Polarographic (Clark) electrode** | Platinum cathode and silver/silver chloride anode with an applied voltage (~0.6 V); current ∝ PO~2~ | Blood gas analysers; needs temperature control at 37°C |

## Blood gas electrodes

- **pH:** a **glass electrode** with an H^+^-sensitive glass membrane; potential difference against a reference electrode (calomel or Ag/AgCl) relates to [H^+^] (Nernst equation). Maintained at 37°C.
- **PCO~2~: Severinghaus electrode** — a pH electrode behind a CO~2~-permeable membrane with a bicarbonate solution; CO~2~ diffuses in and changes pH.
- **PO~2~: Clark electrode** (above).
- **Derived values:** bicarbonate (Henderson–Hasselbalch), base excess, saturation (unless co-oximetry measures it), anion gap.
- **Co-oximetry:** multi-wavelength spectrophotometry that measures oxy-, deoxy-, carboxy- and methaemoglobin separately.
- **Temperature:** analysers measure at 37°C. **Alpha-stat** management (reporting values at 37°C) is standard in adults during hypothermia; pH-stat corrects to the patient's temperature (adds CO~2~; used in some paediatric bypass).

## Other methods

**Mass spectrometry** (ionised gas separated by mass-to-charge ratio; very fast, multiple gases, historically shared between theatres), **Raman scattering** (shift in wavelength of scattered laser light), **piezoelectric** quartz crystals coated with oil (volatile absorption changes resonant frequency), **refractometry** (calibration of vaporisers).
