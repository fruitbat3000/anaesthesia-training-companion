## Phases (left ventricle)

1. **Isovolumetric contraction:** mitral valve closes (S1); pressure rises with no change in volume until it exceeds aortic pressure.
2. **Ejection:** aortic valve opens; rapid then reduced ejection. Stroke volume ~70 mL from an end-diastolic volume of ~120 mL (**ejection fraction ≈ 60%**).
3. **Isovolumetric relaxation:** aortic valve closes (S2, with the dicrotic notch on the arterial trace); pressure falls with no change in volume.
4. **Filling:** mitral valve opens; rapid passive filling, diastasis, then **atrial systole** (about 20% of filling at rest, rising towards 40% in tachycardia when passive filling time is short; more important in the stiff or hypertrophied ventricle and lost in atrial fibrillation).

**Heart rate:** as rate rises, **diastole shortens much more than systole**. Filling time falls, and so does coronary perfusion of the left ventricle, which happens mainly in diastole.

**Heart sounds:** S1 (AV valves close), S2 (semilunar valves close; physiological splitting on inspiration). **S3** in early rapid filling: normal in the young and in pregnancy, otherwise a sign of a dilated, volume-loaded ventricle. **S4** with atrial systole into a **stiff ventricle** (hypertrophy, ischaemia); absent in AF.

**The CVP waveform:** a wave (atrial contraction), c wave (tricuspid bulging during isovolumetric contraction), x descent (atrial relaxation and descent of the valve ring), v wave (atrial filling against a closed tricuspid valve), y descent (tricuspid opening). Cannon a waves occur in complete heart block and junctional rhythms; large v waves in tricuspid regurgitation; absent a waves in AF.

## The pressure–volume loop

- Plotted with **LV volume on the x-axis and pressure on the y-axis**; the loop runs anticlockwise.
- Bottom-left corner = mitral valve opens; bottom-right = mitral closes (end-diastolic point); top-right = aortic opens; top-left = aortic closes (end-systolic point).
- **Width = stroke volume.** **Area = stroke work.**
- The **end-systolic pressure–volume relationship (ESPVR)** is the line joining end-systolic points under different loading: its slope (**end-systolic elastance**) is a relatively load-independent index of **contractility**.
- The **end-diastolic pressure–volume relationship (EDPVR)** describes passive filling: a steeper curve means a stiffer ventricle (diastolic dysfunction).
- **Arterial elastance** (Ea ≈ end-systolic pressure / stroke volume) represents afterload.

| Change | Effect on the loop |
|---|---|
| ↑ Preload | Wider loop; end-diastolic point moves right along the EDPVR |
| ↑ Afterload | Taller, narrower loop; smaller stroke volume |
| ↑ Contractility | Steeper ESPVR; wider loop with smaller end-systolic volume |
| Aortic stenosis | Very high systolic pressures; steep EDPVR (hypertrophy) |
| Mitral regurgitation | No true isovolumetric phases; large end-diastolic volume |

> - **AKT:** identify points on a loop; what the ESPVR slope means; ejection fraction and load-dependence; the effect of heart rate on diastolic time; S3 and S4.
> - **CASE:** draw a PV loop and show the effect of a vasopressor, a fluid bolus and an inotrope; explain why ejection fraction is load-dependent.
