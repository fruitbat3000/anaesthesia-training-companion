| Drug | Mechanism | Onset and use |
|---|---|---|
| **Sodium citrate 0.3 M** (30 mL) | **Non-particulate antacid**: neutralises acid already in the stomach | Immediate; lasts ~20–30 min; before GA for caesarean section. Particulate antacids (magnesium trisilicate) cause lung injury if aspirated |
| **H~2~ receptor antagonists** (**ranitidine** withdrawn in the UK; **famotidine**, cimetidine) | Block histamine-stimulated acid secretion | Reduce volume and acidity of subsequent secretions; oral ~1–2 h before; cimetidine inhibits CYP450 |
| **Proton pump inhibitors** (omeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole) | Irreversibly inhibit H^+^/K^+^-ATPase (pro-drugs activated in acid) | Most effective suppression, but needs time (best given the evening before and morning of surgery); IV forms available |
| **Metoclopramide** | D~2~ antagonist (central and peripheral), 5-HT~4~ agonist | ↑ gastric emptying and LOS tone; antiemetic; extrapyramidal effects |
| **Domperidone** | Peripheral D~2~ antagonist (does not cross the BBB well) | Prokinetic; QT prolongation |
| **Erythromycin** | Motilin agonist | Prokinetic in critical care (feed intolerance) |
| **Antimuscarinics** (glycopyrronium, atropine) | Reduce secretions | Reduce LOS tone |

## Aspiration prophylaxis in obstetrics (typical practice)

For women at risk of needing general anaesthesia (e.g. high-risk labour), an **H~2~ antagonist or PPI** is given regularly; before GA for caesarean section add **sodium citrate** and consider metoclopramide. Follow local policy.

## Aspiration of gastric contents

- **Mendelson's syndrome:** chemical pneumonitis from acid aspiration (classically volume > 25 mL and pH < 2.5 were quoted as risk thresholds, from animal data).
- Management: suction, head-down and lateral position if not intubated, secure the airway, bronchoscopy for particulate matter, supportive ventilation; antibiotics only if infection develops (not routinely); steroids are not indicated.
