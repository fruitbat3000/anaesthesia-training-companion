## A systematic approach to the chest X-ray

1. **Details:** patient, date, projection (PA or AP: AP magnifies the heart), position (erect, supine: supine films hide pneumothorax and effusions).
2. **Quality:** rotation (spinous processes midway between clavicle heads), inspiration (about 6 anterior ribs or 10 posterior ribs above the diaphragm), penetration.
3. **Lines and tubes:** tracheal tube tip about **5 cm above the carina** (mid-trachea, between the clavicles and the carina in a neutral neck); **central venous catheter tip at the cavo-atrial junction / lower SVC** (around the level of the carina on the right); nasogastric tube below the diaphragm in the stomach, crossing the carina in the midline and deviating left; chest drains; pacing wires.
4. **Airway:** trachea central? Carina, main bronchi.
5. **Breathing:** lung fields (compare zones), pleura and costophrenic angles, apices, behind the heart.
6. **Circulation:** heart size (**cardiothoracic ratio < 0.5 on a PA film**), borders, mediastinal width, hila.
7. **Diaphragm:** position (right usually higher), free gas under the diaphragm.
8. **Everything else:** bones, soft tissues, **review areas** (apices, behind the heart, below the diaphragm).

## Common abnormalities

| Finding | Features |
|---|---|
| **Pneumothorax** | Visible lung edge with no markings beyond; deep sulcus sign on supine films; **tension**: mediastinal shift away (a clinical diagnosis — do not wait for a film) |
| **Pleural effusion** | Blunted costophrenic angle, meniscus |
| **Consolidation** | Air bronchograms; loss of borders (silhouette sign locates it: right heart border = middle lobe; left heart border = lingula; hemidiaphragm = lower lobe) |
| **Collapse** | Volume loss, shifted fissures and mediastinum; left lower lobe collapse gives a "sail sign" behind the heart |
| **Pulmonary oedema** | Upper lobe diversion, Kerley B lines, bat's wing shadowing, effusions, cardiomegaly |
| **Endobronchial intubation** | Tube tip in the right main bronchus; collapse of the left lung or right upper lobe |

## Airway and cervical spine

- Lateral neck and **CT** imaging can show tracheal deviation or compression (goitre, mediastinal mass), and **flow–volume loops** show the functional effect.
- **Cervical spine trauma:** CT is the first-line imaging in adults with significant head or neck injury (NICE NG232 and NG41 criteria); manual in-line stabilisation during intubation.

## Radiation safety

- **IR(ME)R** regulations define the roles of **referrer, practitioner and operator** and require justification of each exposure. Staff protection: **time, distance (inverse square law), shielding** (lead aprons, thyroid shields, screens), dosimeters.
- Pregnancy: ask about the possibility of pregnancy before exposing people of childbearing potential; the fetus is most sensitive in the first trimester. Iodinated contrast risks: allergy-like reactions and contrast-associated nephropathy.
