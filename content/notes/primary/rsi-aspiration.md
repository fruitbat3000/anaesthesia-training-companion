## Indications

Patients at increased risk of regurgitation and aspiration: **inadequate fasting**, emergency surgery, bowel obstruction or ileus, **pregnancy** (from about the second trimester), symptomatic reflux or hiatus hernia, trauma and opioid administration (delayed gastric emptying), diabetic gastroparesis, reduced consciousness, GLP-1 agonist therapy with residual contents, obesity (in some circumstances).

## Preparation

- **Team brief** with the airway plan (A–D) and roles; a skilled assistant; working suction under the pillow; drugs drawn up and labelled; a range of tubes, a bougie and a video laryngoscope; second-generation SGA; front-of-neck access kit.
- **Position:** head-up or ramped (improves FRC and view; reduces passive regurgitation).
- **Nasogastric tube:** aspirate before induction if one is present (can leave in place).
- **Pre-oxygenation** to an end-tidal O~2~ ≥ 0.9 (typically 3 minutes of tidal breathing or 8 vital capacity breaths); consider HFNO or nasal cannulae for apnoeic oxygenation.

## Technique

- Rapid injection of a **predetermined dose** of induction agent followed immediately by a fast-onset neuromuscular blocker: **suxamethonium 1–1.5 mg/kg** or **rocuronium 1–1.2 mg/kg** (with sugammadex available).
- **Cricoid pressure** (10 N awake, 30 N after loss of consciousness) is traditional; it may worsen the laryngoscopic view or mask ventilation, so reduce or release it if it does; do not release if active vomiting occurs without first turning the patient/tilting head-down and suctioning.
- **Gentle facemask ventilation** (e.g. inflation pressures < 20 cmH~2~O) before intubation is increasingly accepted when hypoxaemia is a risk.
- Intubate, inflate the cuff, confirm with **waveform capnography**, then release cricoid pressure.
- **Titrate drugs in the shocked patient** (reduced doses of induction agent; vasopressor ready): "modified RSI" is common in practice.

## Aspiration

- **Risk:** about 1 in 3,000–4,000 anaesthetics overall (higher in emergencies), commonest at induction and emergence.
- **Recognition:** gastric contents in the pharynx, laryngospasm, bronchospasm, hypoxaemia, rising airway pressures, later infiltrates on the chest X-ray.
- **Management:** head-down and lateral tilt if not intubated, **suction**, 100% O~2~, **intubate and suction the trachea before ventilating** if appropriate, bronchoscopy for particulate matter, PEEP, supportive care. Antibiotics only if pneumonia develops; steroids are not indicated. Admit to an appropriate care area if significant.
