## Structure

Arterioles (the main site of **resistance**, under neural, humoral and local control) → capillaries (exchange) → venules. Precapillary sphincters and arteriolar tone control how many capillaries are perfused; in exercising muscle, recruitment can increase flow many times over.

## Starling's equation

**Jv = Kf [(Pc − Pi) − σ(πc − πi)]**

- Jv: net fluid filtration; Kf: filtration coefficient (surface area × permeability).
- Pc and Pi: capillary and interstitial hydrostatic pressures.
- πc and πi: plasma and interstitial oncotic pressures (plasma about 25 mmHg, mainly **albumin**).
- σ: the reflection coefficient for protein (1 = impermeable, 0 = freely permeable).

**The revised Starling principle:** the endothelial **glycocalyx** (a gel layer of glycoproteins and proteoglycans on the luminal surface) means the relevant oncotic gradient is between plasma and the small space just beneath the glycocalyx, not the whole interstitium. In most capillaries there is **net filtration along the whole length, with little or no reabsorption**; the filtrate returns via **lymph** (about 8 L/day enters the lymphatics; roughly half is reabsorbed in lymph nodes, so about 4 L/day returns to the circulation via the thoracic and right lymphatic ducts). The glycocalyx is damaged by sepsis, surgery, hyperglycaemia and rapid volume loading.

## Causes of oedema

- ↑ capillary hydrostatic pressure: heart failure, venous obstruction, fluid overload.
- ↓ plasma oncotic pressure: hypoalbuminaemia (liver disease, nephrotic syndrome, malnutrition).
- ↑ permeability / glycocalyx damage: sepsis, burns, inflammation, ARDS.
- Lymphatic obstruction.

## Functions of the endothelium

- **Vascular tone:** releases **nitric oxide** (from L-arginine by eNOS, raising cGMP in smooth muscle) and **prostacyclin** (vasodilators), and **endothelin-1** (vasoconstrictor).
- **Haemostasis:** normally anticoagulant (thrombomodulin, heparan sulphate, tissue factor pathway inhibitor, tPA); becomes procoagulant when damaged (von Willebrand factor, tissue factor).
- Barrier function, inflammation (adhesion molecules), angiogenesis, and metabolism (ACE in the lung).
