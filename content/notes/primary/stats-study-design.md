## Study designs

| Design | Description | Strengths / weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| Case report / series | Descriptions of patients | Hypothesis-generating; no comparison |
| **Cross-sectional** | Snapshot at one time | Prevalence; cannot show causation |
| **Case–control** | Start with outcome (cases vs controls), look back at exposures | Good for rare outcomes; **recall bias**; gives **odds ratios** |
| **Cohort** (prospective or retrospective) | Follow exposed and unexposed groups over time | Incidence and **relative risk**; confounding; long and costly for rare outcomes |
| **Randomised controlled trial** | Random allocation to intervention or control | Controls known **and unknown** confounders; the best design to test interventions |
| **Systematic review and meta-analysis** | Systematic search and pooling of studies | Highest level of evidence if studies are good; **heterogeneity**, **publication bias** (funnel plot) |

## Key concepts in trials

- **Randomisation** (with **allocation concealment**) prevents selection bias. **Blinding** (single, double) prevents performance and detection bias.
- **Intention-to-treat analysis** keeps patients in their allocated groups (preserves randomisation); per-protocol analysis may exaggerate effects.
- **Bias** is systematic error (selection, performance, detection, attrition, reporting). **Confounding** is a third factor associated with both exposure and outcome. **Chance** is addressed by statistics.
- **Outcome measures** should be clinically meaningful and precisely defined; composite outcomes, surrogate outcomes (e.g. blood pressure instead of stroke) and subjective measures introduce uncertainty. Core outcome sets (e.g. for perioperative medicine) improve comparability.
- **Equivalence and non-inferiority trials** test whether a new treatment is not worse by more than a set margin.

## Evidence-based medicine and meta-analysis

- Combining the best research evidence with clinical expertise and patient values.
- **Hierarchy (roughly):** systematic reviews of RCTs → RCTs → cohort → case–control → case series → expert opinion. GRADE rates the certainty of evidence.
- **Forest plot:** each study's effect estimate and CI (square size reflects weight), the pooled estimate (diamond) and the line of no effect. I^2^ describes heterogeneity.

## Audit, research and quality improvement

| | Research | Audit | Quality improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question | What is best practice? (generates new knowledge) | Are we meeting a standard? | How can we improve this system? |
| Method | Protocol, often randomisation; ethics approval | Measure against a standard; re-audit (the audit cycle) | Iterative small tests of change (**PDSA cycles**), measured over time (**run charts**, statistical process control) |
| Data | Data for research | **Data for assurance** (judgement against a standard) | **Data for improvement** (frequent, "good enough", to learn) |

- The **Model for Improvement** asks: what are we trying to accomplish; how will we know a change is an improvement; what changes can we make? Then PDSA (plan–do–study–act).
- Deming's **System of Profound Knowledge**: appreciation of a system, knowledge about variation (common-cause versus special-cause), theory of knowledge, and psychology.
