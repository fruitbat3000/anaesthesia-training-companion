## Structure

**Lipophilic aromatic ring – intermediate chain – hydrophilic tertiary amine.** The intermediate link defines the class:

| **Esters** (–COO–) | **Amides** (–NHCO–) |
|---|---|
| Procaine, chloroprocaine, tetracaine, cocaine, benzocaine | Lidocaine, prilocaine, bupivacaine, levobupivacaine, ropivacaine, articaine |
| Hydrolysed by **plasma cholinesterase** (short half-life) | Metabolised in the **liver** |
| **PABA** metabolite → more allergic reactions | Allergy very rare |

(Tip: amides have an "i" before the "-caine".)

## Mechanism

Weak bases (pKa 7.6–8.1). The **un-ionised** form crosses the axon membrane; inside the cell the **ionised** form binds a site in the inner vestibule of the **voltage-gated Na^+^ channel**, blocking it from the inside. Binding is greater when channels are open or inactivated (**use-dependent** or frequency-dependent block).

## Determinants of the block

| Property | Determines | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **pKa** | **Speed of onset** (closer to pH 7.4 = more un-ionised = faster) | Lidocaine (7.9) faster than bupivacaine (8.1) |
| **Lipid solubility** | **Potency** | Bupivacaine > lidocaine > procaine |
| **Protein binding** | **Duration** | Bupivacaine ~95%, lidocaine ~65% |
| Vasodilator activity | Shorter duration and more systemic absorption | Lidocaine is a vasodilator; cocaine a vasoconstrictor |

- Site of injection affects **systemic absorption** (highest to lowest): intercostal / intravenous regional > caudal > epidural > brachial plexus > subcutaneous.

## Commonly quoted maximum doses (adult guide)

| Drug | Plain | With adrenaline |
|---|---|---|
| Lidocaine | 3 mg/kg | 7 mg/kg |
| Prilocaine | 6 mg/kg | 8 mg/kg |
| Bupivacaine / levobupivacaine | 2 mg/kg | 2 mg/kg (UK guidance) |
| Ropivacaine | 3 mg/kg | 3 mg/kg |

**These are guides only; safe doses depend on site, patient (age, frailty, cardiac, hepatic and renal disease, pregnancy), and total dose from all sources. Always check the product information, BNF and local guidance.**

## Individual agents

- **Bupivacaine:** long-acting; high cardiotoxicity (binds Na^+^ channels "fast in, slow out"); racemic. **Levobupivacaine** (S-enantiomer) and **ropivacaine** (S, propyl) are less cardiotoxic; ropivacaine has more sensory–motor separation.
- **Prilocaine:** least toxic amide; **methaemoglobinaemia** at high doses (o-toluidine); used for IV regional anaesthesia (Bier's block) and hyperbaric spinal.
- **Chloroprocaine:** short-acting ester for spinal day-case surgery.
- **Cocaine:** ester; blocks noradrenaline reuptake → vasoconstriction (nasal surgery), hypertension, arrhythmias.
- **EMLA** (eutectic mixture of lidocaine and prilocaine) and **tetracaine gel** for venepuncture in children.

## Additives

- **Adrenaline** (e.g. 1:200,000 = 5 µg/mL): vasoconstriction → prolongs block, reduces peak plasma level, marks intravascular injection (tachycardia). Avoid in end-artery areas.
- **Bicarbonate:** speeds onset (more un-ionised base). **Opioids, clonidine, dexamethasone** (perineural or IV) prolong or intensify blocks. **Glucose** makes spinal solutions hyperbaric.
