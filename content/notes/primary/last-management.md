## Presentation

- Usually within minutes of injection (intravascular), but can be delayed (up to an hour or more) after absorption from tissues.
- **CNS:** perioral tingling, tinnitus, metallic taste, light-headedness, agitation, confusion, muscle twitching, **seizures**, then coma.
- **Cardiovascular:** initially hypertension and tachycardia (or bradycardia), then **arrhythmias** (broad QRS, ventricular arrhythmias), hypotension, conduction block, **cardiovascular collapse** and asystole. Bupivacaine is particularly cardiotoxic; cardiac arrest may occur without CNS warning, especially under general anaesthesia or sedation.
- Acidosis, hypoxia and hypercapnia worsen toxicity.

## Prevention

- Use the lowest effective dose; know the maximum doses; **ultrasound guidance** (reduces vascular puncture and allows lower volumes); aspirate before and during injection; inject incrementally (for example 3–5 mL aliquots) with pauses; consider adrenaline-containing test doses where appropriate; monitor the patient during and after blocks.
- Reduce doses in the frail, elderly, and those with cardiac, hepatic or renal disease, and in pregnancy.
- **Stop Before You Block** checks help prevent wrong-sided blocks (a different but related safety issue).

## Management (follow the QRH LAST card)

1. **Stop injecting**; call for help.
2. Airway, 100% O~2~, ventilate (avoid hypercapnia and acidosis).
3. **Control seizures** with a benzodiazepine, propofol or thiopental in small doses.
4. **Circulatory arrest:** start CPR (ALS); **lipid emulsion**; **reduce adrenaline doses** (smaller boluses) as recommended in LAST guidance; prolonged CPR may be needed (over an hour); consider ECMO.
5. **Lipid emulsion (20%)** — as per the QRH card: an initial IV bolus of **1.5 mL/kg** over about a minute, then an infusion of **15 mL/kg/h**, with repeat boluses and a doubled infusion rate if instability persists (maximum cumulative dose about **12 mL/kg**). Mechanisms: a "lipid sink" for the lipophilic drug, and direct metabolic and inotropic effects.
6. Report and follow up; propofol is **not** a substitute for lipid emulsion (its lipid content is too low and it is cardiodepressant).
