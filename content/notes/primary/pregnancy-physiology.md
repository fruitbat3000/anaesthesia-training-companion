## Cardiovascular

| Change | Size (by term) | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiac output | ↑ **~40–50%** (rate and stroke volume); further ↑ in labour and immediately after delivery (autotransfusion) | Risk for women with cardiac disease, especially at delivery |
| SVR | ↓ ~20–30% (progesterone, prostacyclin, low-resistance placenta) | BP falls in the second trimester |
| Blood volume | ↑ **~40–50%** (plasma more than red cells) | Physiological (dilutional) anaemia |
| **Aortocaval compression** | Clinically important from about 20 weeks when supine (can begin earlier) | Hypotension, reduced uteroplacental flow: use **left lateral tilt** or uterine displacement |

## Respiratory

- **Minute ventilation ↑ ~45–50%** (mainly tidal volume; progesterone increases CO~2~ sensitivity) → **PaCO~2~ about 4.0–4.2 kPa**, with renal compensation (bicarbonate ~18–21 mmol/L).
- **FRC ↓ ~20%** (more when supine) and **O~2~ consumption ↑ ~20–30%** → rapid desaturation during apnoea.
- **Airway:** mucosal oedema and capillary engorgement (bleeding with nasal instrumentation; use smaller tracheal tubes), breast enlargement, weight gain. Failed intubation is more common than in the general population: follow the **OAA/DAS obstetric guidelines**.

## Haematological

- **Hypercoagulable:** ↑ fibrinogen (often 4–6 g/L at term) and factors VII, VIII, X and vWF; ↓ protein S; reduced fibrinolysis → **VTE risk** (a leading direct cause of maternal death).
- Fibrinogen below about 2 g/L in obstetric haemorrhage predicts severe bleeding.
- Mild gestational thrombocytopenia is common.

**Oxygen carriage:** the respiratory alkalosis would shift the maternal curve left, but a rise in 2,3-DPG (about 30%) produces a net **right shift**, helping oxygen unloading to the placenta. Uterine blood flow rises to about 10–12% of cardiac output at term.

## Gastrointestinal

- Progesterone lowers **LOS tone**; the gravid uterus raises intragastric pressure and displaces the stomach. Gastric emptying is normal in pregnancy but **delayed in labour, especially with opioids**. Hence antacid prophylaxis and RSI for general anaesthesia from about the second trimester onwards (practice varies).

## Other systems

- **Renal:** renal plasma flow and **GFR ↑ ~50%** → normal pregnant creatinine is lower (a "normal" non-pregnant creatinine may indicate renal impairment).
- **CNS:** **MAC falls by up to ~30–40%** (progesterone, endorphins); local anaesthetic requirement for neuraxial block is reduced (engorged epidural veins reduce the CSF volume, hormonal effects).
- **Plasma cholinesterase** falls by about 25% (little clinical effect on suxamethonium).
- **Endocrine:** insulin resistance (gestational diabetes), raised thyroid-binding globulin.
