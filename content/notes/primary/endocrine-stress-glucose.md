## Insulin and glucagon

- **Insulin** (pancreatic β cells; released by glucose via GLUT2 uptake → ATP → closure of K~ATP~ channels → depolarisation → Ca^2+^ entry): the only hormone that lowers glucose. Promotes glucose uptake (GLUT4 in muscle and fat), glycogen, fat and protein synthesis; shifts K^+^ into cells; inhibits lipolysis and ketogenesis.
- **Counter-regulatory hormones** raise glucose: **glucagon** (α cells: glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis, ketogenesis), **adrenaline**, **cortisol**, **growth hormone**.
- Normal fasting plasma glucose about 4–6 mmol/L. The brain needs about **120 g of glucose a day** (it can adapt to ketones in prolonged starvation). Red cells depend entirely on glucose.

## The stress response to surgery

| System | Change | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| Hypothalamic–pituitary | ↑ ACTH, GH, ADH, prolactin | ↑ cortisol, water retention |
| Sympathetic / adrenal medulla | ↑ catecholamines | Tachycardia, hypertension, hyperglycaemia |
| Pancreas | ↑ glucagon, relative ↓ insulin, insulin resistance | **Hyperglycaemia** |
| Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone | ↑ | Na^+^ and water retention, K^+^ loss |
| Metabolic | Catabolism: protein breakdown, lipolysis, negative nitrogen balance | Muscle loss, delayed recovery |
| Inflammatory | IL-6 (peaks at about 24 h), acute phase proteins (CRP), fever | |

- The response is proportional to the size of surgery. It is reduced by **neuraxial blockade** (especially for lower body surgery, blocking afferent input), high-dose opioids, minimally invasive surgery and avoiding hypothermia; **etomidate** blocks cortisol synthesis. Enhanced recovery aims to limit it.

## Starvation

- **Early (first 24 h):** hepatic glycogen (about 100 g) is exhausted within about a day.
- **Days:** gluconeogenesis from amino acids (muscle protein), lactate and glycerol; lipolysis releases fatty acids; ketogenesis increases.
- **Prolonged:** the brain adapts to use **ketones**, sparing protein; metabolic rate falls.
- **Refeeding syndrome:** in malnourished patients, feeding drives insulin release → intracellular shift of **phosphate**, potassium and magnesium, and thiamine depletion → arrhythmias, respiratory failure, Wernicke's encephalopathy. Start feeding slowly with electrolyte monitoring and thiamine.

## Preoperative fasting and carbohydrate loading

- Standard adult elective fasting: **6 hours for food**, **2 hours for clear fluids** (many units now allow clear fluids until called to theatre, following newer guidance); breast milk 4 h in infants; for children, clear fluids up to 1 hour before is endorsed by consensus.
- Carbohydrate drinks before surgery reduce insulin resistance and thirst (with caution in diabetes).
