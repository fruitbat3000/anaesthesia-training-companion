## Classes

| Class | Examples | Mechanism | Perioperative points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACE inhibitors / ARBs** | Ramipril, lisinopril; losartan, candesartan | Block angiotensin II production or its AT~1~ receptor | **Hypotension after induction** that responds poorly to phenylephrine; many units omit on the morning of surgery (especially if used for hypertension alone); cough and angioedema with ACEi; hyperkalaemia; AKI risk with hypovolaemia |
| **Calcium channel blockers** | Amlodipine, nifedipine (dihydropyridines: vascular); verapamil, diltiazem (cardiac: rate) | L-type channel block | Continue; verapamil + β-blocker → heart block |
| **β-blockers** | Bisoprolol, atenolol, metoprolol, propranolol | β~1~ (± β~2~) antagonism | **Continue** (withdrawal → rebound tachycardia and ischaemia); do not start high doses just before surgery (POISE); mask signs of hypovolaemia and hypoglycaemia; bronchospasm with non-selective agents |
| **Thiazide / loop diuretics** | Indapamide, bendroflumethiazide; furosemide | See diuretics | Hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypovolaemia |
| **α-blockers** | Doxazosin; **phenoxybenzamine** (irreversible, non-selective) | α~1~ antagonism | Postural hypotension; phenoxybenzamine for phaeochromocytoma |
| **Centrally acting** | Clonidine, methyldopa (pregnancy) | α~2~ agonism | Rebound hypertension if clonidine stopped |
| **Direct vasodilators** | Hydralazine, minoxidil | Arteriolar dilatation | Reflex tachycardia |

## Acute control in theatre

| Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Labetalol** | α~1~ + β blockade (β:α about 3:1 IV) | Bolus titration; pre-eclampsia; avoid in asthma |
| **Esmolol** | Short-acting β~1~ (red cell esterases; t~½~ ~9 min) | Tachycardia and pressor response to intubation |
| **Hydralazine** | Arteriolar dilator | Pre-eclampsia; slow onset, reflex tachycardia |
| **GTN** | NO donor; venodilator > arteriolar | Also coronary vasodilator; tachyphylaxis; methaemoglobinaemia |
| **Sodium nitroprusside** | NO donor; arterial and venous | Very potent; **cyanide toxicity** (treat with hydroxocobalamin, sodium thiosulfate); protect from light |
| **Magnesium** | Ca^2+^ antagonism, catecholamine release inhibition | Pre-eclampsia, phaeochromocytoma |
| Deepening anaesthesia / analgesia, remifentanil | | Often the right first step |

## Hypertension and surgery

- Elective surgery can usually proceed if blood pressure in primary care is below **160/100 mmHg** (joint AAGBI/BHS guidance, 2016); very high readings in clinic should be confirmed and assessed for end-organ damage.
- Intraoperative targets: avoid falls below about 20% of baseline (or MAP < 65 mmHg) in at-risk patients.
