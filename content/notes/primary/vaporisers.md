## Variable-bypass (plenum) vaporiser (e.g. Tec 5 and 7)

- Fresh gas is split at the **splitting valve**: a small fraction passes through the **vaporising chamber**, where it becomes **saturated** with vapour (large surface area from wicks and baffles); the rest bypasses. They recombine at the outlet.
- The dial controls the **splitting ratio**. Output ≈ (chamber flow / total flow) × SVP / (atmospheric − SVP) as a fraction, calibrated for a specific agent.
- **Temperature compensation:** as liquid cools with vaporisation, SVP falls. A **bimetallic strip** (or expanding bellows) at the bypass narrows the bypass as temperature falls, directing more gas through the chamber. A heat sink (copper, large metal mass) limits temperature change.
- **Flow compensation:** accurate over about **0.5–15 L/min**. Below that, gas may not pick up enough vapour; at very high flows, it may not be fully saturated.
- **Pumping effect:** back-pressure from IPPV or oxygen flush can compress gas in the chamber and push extra vapour into the bypass, raising output. Reduced by a long inlet tube, check valves and baffles.
- **Altitude:** the dial gives about the same partial pressure (and so the same effect), although the percentage delivered is higher.
- Filling systems are **agent-specific** (keyed fillers); a **interlock** prevents two vaporisers being on at once; vaporisers are sited outside the breathing system (VOC) on the back bar.
- Filling with the wrong agent: a higher-SVP agent in a lower-SVP vaporiser (e.g. sevoflurane in an isoflurane vaporiser → higher output than dialled; in practice keyed fillers prevent this).

## Desflurane (Tec 6-type)

Desflurane's SVP is near atmospheric at room temperature, so it is **heated to 39°C** in a sump, raising its pressure to about **2 atmospheres (~200 kPa)**. Vapour is injected into the fresh gas flow through a **pressure-regulating valve** that matches a differential pressure transducer, controlled by the dial. It needs electrical power and warm-up, and alarms if it fails. At altitude, the percentage output stays the same, so the **partial pressure falls** — the dial must be turned up.

## Other types

- **Draw-over vaporisers** (e.g. OMV, EMO) have **low internal resistance** so the patient's breathing (or a self-inflating bag) draws air through them; used in field and resource-limited settings. They sit within the breathing system (VIC).
- **Direct injection / electronic vaporisers** (e.g. Aladin cassette, and some modern workstations) inject liquid agent under computer control.
