## Compartments (70 kg man)

- **Total body water ≈ 60% of body weight (~42 L)**: less in women and older people (more fat), about 75–80% in term neonates.
- **Intracellular ≈ 2/3 (~28 L)**; **extracellular ≈ 1/3 (~14 L)**: interstitial ~10.5 L, plasma ~3 L, plus transcellular fluid.
- **Blood volume ≈ 70 mL/kg** in adults (about 80–90 mL/kg in infants).
- Measured by dilution: total body water (deuterium oxide), ECF (inulin, bromide), plasma (Evans blue, labelled albumin).

## Osmolality and tonicity

- **Osmolarity:** osmoles per litre of solution. **Osmolality:** osmoles per kg of solvent (measured by freezing point depression). Normal plasma osmolality **275–295 mOsm/kg**.
- **Calculated osmolality ≈ 2Na^+^ + glucose + urea.** An **osmolar gap** above about 10 suggests an unmeasured osmole (ethanol, methanol, ethylene glycol, mannitol).
- **Tonicity** is the effective osmolality: only solutes that cannot cross the membrane move water. Urea is an ineffective osmole; glucose is effective when insulin is lacking. **5% dextrose is iso-osmolar in the bag but hypotonic in the body** once the glucose is metabolised.
- The **colligative properties** (osmotic pressure, freezing point depression, boiling point elevation, vapour pressure depression) depend on the number of particles, not their nature.

## Electrolytes

| Ion | Normal plasma | Key roles and points |
|---|---|---|
| Na^+^ | 135–145 mmol/L | Main ECF cation; determines ECF volume. Correct chronic hyponatraemia slowly (limit about 8–10 mmol/L per 24 h) to avoid osmotic demyelination |
| K^+^ | 3.5–5.0 mmol/L | Main ICF cation (~150 mmol/L inside). Shifted into cells by insulin, β~2~ agonists and alkalosis. Both high and low levels cause arrhythmias |
| Ca^2+^ | Total 2.2–2.6 mmol/L; ionised ~1.1–1.3 | About 45% bound to albumin; alkalosis increases binding and lowers ionised Ca^2+^. Citrate in transfused blood binds Ca^2+^ |
| Mg^2+^ | 0.7–1.0 mmol/L | Cofactor for ATP; NMDA and calcium channel antagonism; used in pre-eclampsia, asthma, torsades and arrhythmias. Potentiates neuromuscular block |
| Cl^−^ | 95–105 mmol/L | Affects acid–base (Stewart) |
| PO~4~^3−^ | 0.8–1.5 mmol/L | ATP, 2,3-DPG; falls in refeeding syndrome |

## Unit conversions

- **1% = 10 mg/mL** (1 g per 100 mL). So 2% lidocaine = 20 mg/mL; 1:1000 adrenaline = 1 mg/mL; 1:200,000 = 5 µg/mL.
- mmol = mass (mg) ÷ molecular weight. 1 g of NaCl ≈ 17 mmol Na^+^; 0.9% saline contains 154 mmol/L each of Na^+^ and Cl^−^.
