## Determinants

**CO = HR × SV.** Resting adult CO ≈ 5 L/min; cardiac index ≈ 2.5–4 L/min/m^2^.

- **Preload:** ventricular wall stretch at end-diastole (approximated by end-diastolic volume; pressures such as CVP are poor surrogates).
- **Afterload:** the wall stress the ventricle must generate during ejection. **Laplace:** wall stress ∝ **P × r / (2 × wall thickness)**, so a dilated ventricle faces higher afterload at the same pressure, and hypertrophy reduces wall stress.
- **Contractility:** the intrinsic strength of contraction at a given preload and afterload; increased by sympathetic stimulation and inotropes, reduced by ischaemia, acidosis, hypoxia and many anaesthetic drugs.
- **Heart rate:** increasing rate raises output until diastolic filling time (and coronary perfusion time) becomes limiting; the **Bowditch (staircase) effect** gives a small inotropic effect with higher rates.

## Frank–Starling mechanism

- Within physiological limits, **greater end-diastolic fibre length → greater force of contraction** (optimal actin–myosin overlap and increased calcium sensitivity of troponin).
- Starling curves: stroke volume against preload. Sympathetic stimulation and inotropes shift the curve up and left; heart failure shifts it down and right.
- On the steep part, a fluid bolus raises stroke volume (**fluid responsive**); on the flat part it only raises filling pressures and causes oedema.

## Venous return (Guyton)

- **Venous return = (mean systemic filling pressure − right atrial pressure) / resistance to venous return.** Mean systemic filling pressure is about 7 mmHg; about 70% of blood volume is in the veins.
- At steady state, cardiac output equals venous return. Venoconstriction (sympathetic tone, vasopressors) raises mean systemic filling pressure and venous return; anaesthetic vasodilation lowers it.

## The fluid challenge

- Give a defined bolus (for example 250 mL crystalloid over 5–10 minutes) and measure a **stroke volume** response (a rise of 10% or more is commonly used) rather than relying on blood pressure or CVP.
- Dynamic indices (pulse pressure or stroke volume variation during controlled ventilation, passive leg raising) predict response better than static pressures, within their limitations.
