## Vertebral column

- 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 fused sacral, 4 coccygeal vertebrae. Curvatures: cervical and lumbar lordosis, thoracic and sacral kyphosis (the highest point of the lumbar lordosis at about **L3** and the lowest of the thoracic kyphosis at about **T5–6** influence hyperbaric spinal spread in the supine patient).
- Thoracic spinous processes are steeply angled (paramedian approach helps); lumbar spinous processes are horizontal.

## Ligaments (from skin to dura in the midline)

**Skin → subcutaneous tissue → supraspinous ligament → interspinous ligament → ligamentum flavum** (the "give" / loss of resistance) **→ epidural space → dura (and arachnoid) → subarachnoid space (CSF).** Posterior and anterior longitudinal ligaments lie within the canal on the vertebral bodies.

## Spinal cord and meninges

- The cord ends at about **L1–L2** in adults (the conus medullaris; as low as L3 in some), and at about **L3 at birth**. The **dural sac ends at S2** in adults (S3–4 in neonates). Below the conus, the **cauda equina** floats in CSF — why spinals are performed below L2–3 in adults.
- **Tuffier's line** (the intercristal line joining the iliac crests) usually crosses **L4 or the L4–5 interspace**, but clinical estimation of level is often inaccurate (frequently a level higher than thought). Ultrasound improves accuracy.
- **Meninges:** dura mater, arachnoid mater (the subdural space between them is a potential space: accidental subdural injection causes patchy, high, slow-onset block), pia mater (with denticulate ligaments).
- **Epidural space contents:** fat, **epidural veins** (valveless, **Batson's plexus**; engorged in pregnancy and with raised intra-abdominal pressure), lymphatics, spinal nerve roots, arteries. It is widest in the lumbar region (about 5–6 mm) and narrow in the cervical region.

## Blood supply of the cord

- **One anterior spinal artery** (supplying the anterior two-thirds) and **two posterior spinal arteries**, reinforced by segmental (radicular) arteries. The largest, the **artery of Adamkiewicz**, usually arises on the left between **T9 and T12** and supplies the lower anterior cord: at risk in aortic surgery.
- **Anterior spinal artery syndrome:** paralysis and loss of pain and temperature below the level with preserved proprioception and vibration (dorsal columns). Causes: hypotension, aortic cross-clamping, compression.

## Dermatomes and key sensory levels

C4 clavicles · T4 nipples · T6 xiphisternum · **T10 umbilicus** · L1 inguinal ligament · L4 medial calf/knee · L5 dorsum of foot and big toe · S1 lateral foot and little toe · S2–4 perineum.

Block heights for surgery (approximate): caesarean section **T4** (bilaterally to touch); inguinal hernia T10; TURP T10; hip and lower limb L1–T12; perineal S1–S5.

## Sacrum and caudal space

The **sacral hiatus** (failure of fusion of the 5th sacral laminae) is covered by the **sacrococcygeal ligament**, between the two sacral cornua at the apex of an equilateral triangle with the posterior superior iliac spines. It is easily palpable in children (caudal block) but variable in adults.
