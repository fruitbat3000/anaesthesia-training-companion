## How CO~2~ is carried in arterial blood

| Form | Share of total (approx.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bicarbonate** | ~90% | Formed in red cells by **carbonic anhydrase**: CO~2~ + H~2~O ⇌ H~2~CO~3~ ⇌ H^+^ + HCO~3~^−^ |
| **Carbamino compounds** | ~5% | CO~2~ bound to terminal amine groups, mostly on haemoglobin |
| **Dissolved** | ~5% | CO~2~ is about 20 times more soluble than O~2~ |

The *arteriovenous difference* in CO~2~ content is carried in different proportions: carbamino compounds account for a much larger share (around a third) of CO~2~ unloaded in the lungs than of the total.

## Chloride (Hamburger) shift

HCO~3~^−^ formed in red cells diffuses out into plasma in exchange for Cl^−^ (via band 3 protein). Venous red cells therefore contain more chloride, and they swell slightly because of the extra osmotically active particles.

## The Haldane effect

- **Deoxygenated haemoglobin carries more CO~2~** than oxyhaemoglobin at the same PCO~2~. It is a better buffer for H^+^ (a weaker acid) and forms carbamino compounds more readily.
- In the tissues, oxygen unloading helps load CO~2~; in the lungs, oxygenation helps release CO~2~.
- This is why venous pH is only slightly lower than arterial pH despite the added CO~2~ ("isohydric" transport).
- Clinical relevance: giving high-concentration oxygen to some patients with chronic CO~2~ retention can raise PaCO~2~, partly through the Haldane effect and partly through worsening V/Q mismatch (release of hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction), rather than solely through loss of "hypoxic drive".

## The Bohr effect (for contrast)

The **Bohr effect** is the effect of CO~2~ and H^+^ on **oxygen** binding (a right shift of the O~2~ curve). The **Haldane effect** is the effect of **oxygen** on CO~2~ carriage.

## CO~2~ dissociation curve

Nearly **linear** over the physiological range and much steeper than the O~2~ curve. So hyperventilating healthy lung units can offload extra CO~2~ to compensate for poorly ventilated units, whereas they cannot add extra oxygen. This is why V/Q mismatch causes hypoxaemia well before hypercapnia.
