## Thinking about error

- **Reason's Swiss cheese model:** harm occurs when holes in multiple layers of defence align; most incidents have **latent conditions** (system problems) as well as active failures.
- **Error types:** slips and lapses (skill-based), mistakes (rule- or knowledge-based), violations. Fixation error, confirmation bias and task saturation are common in crises.
- **Safety-I** (learning from what goes wrong) and **Safety-II** (understanding how things usually go right, with adaptation).

## Non-technical skills (ANTS framework)

| Category | Elements |
|---|---|
| **Task management** | Planning and preparing, prioritising, maintaining standards, using resources |
| **Team working** | Coordinating, exchanging information, using authority and assertiveness, assessing capabilities, supporting others |
| **Situation awareness** | Gathering information, recognising and understanding, anticipating |
| **Decision making** | Identifying options, balancing risks and selecting, re-evaluating |

Useful tools: **closed-loop communication**, **SBAR** handovers, "10 seconds for 10 minutes", speaking up (graded assertiveness, e.g. "PACE" or "CUS"), allocating a leader and roles, **cognitive aids** (the QRH) with a reader.

## Safety systems in theatre

- The **WHO Surgical Safety Checklist** (sign in, time out, sign out) with **team briefings and debriefings**; **Stop Before You Block**; the **NatSSIPs/LocSSIPs** standards for invasive procedures.
- **Drug safety:** see drug errors (labelling, NRFit connectors, checking, standardisation). Prescribe clearly and safely, with allergies checked.
- **Fatigue**, hunger, interruptions and production pressure reduce performance.

## Reporting and learning

Report incidents (local systems; national learning systems) without blame; investigate systemically (e.g. under the Patient Safety Incident Response Framework in England); share learning through morbidity and mortality meetings; the **National Audit Projects (NAPs)** have shaped UK practice (NAP4 airway, NAP5 awareness, NAP6 anaphylaxis, NAP7 cardiac arrest).

## Sustainability

Healthcare is a major source of carbon emissions, and anaesthetic gases contribute significantly. Choose lower-impact techniques (**avoid desflurane and nitrous oxide**, use low-flow anaesthesia, TIVA and regional anaesthesia where appropriate), reduce waste (reusables where safe, avoid opening unnecessary equipment), and consider medicines waste.
