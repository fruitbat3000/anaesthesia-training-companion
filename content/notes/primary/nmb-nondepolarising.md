## Mechanism

**Competitive antagonists** at the postjunctional nicotinic receptor (binding one α subunit is enough to prevent opening). Block at the prejunctional receptor reduces ACh mobilisation, causing **fade** on train-of-four and tetanic stimulation. They are **quaternary ammonium** compounds: they do not cross the BBB or placenta and have small volumes of distribution.

## Classes

| | **Aminosteroids** | **Benzylisoquinoliniums** |
|---|---|---|
| Drugs | Rocuronium, vecuronium, pancuronium | Atracurium, cisatracurium, mivacurium |
| Histamine release | Minimal | Atracurium and mivacurium (flushing, hypotension) |
| Vagolytic | Pancuronium (tachycardia) | No |
| Reversal by sugammadex | **Yes** (rocuronium > vecuronium) | No |
| Elimination | Hepatic/biliary and renal | **Hofmann degradation and esterases** (atracurium, cisatracurium); plasma cholinesterase (mivacurium) |

## Individual drugs (typical adult intubating doses)

- **Rocuronium** 0.6 mg/kg (1–1.2 mg/kg for RSI, giving intubating conditions in about 60 s); duration ~30–40 min at 0.6 mg/kg; largely unchanged in bile; prolonged in liver failure.
- **Vecuronium** 0.1 mg/kg; powder; active 3-desacetyl metabolite accumulates in renal failure.
- **Atracurium** 0.5 mg/kg; Hofmann degradation (slowed by hypothermia and acidosis) and ester hydrolysis; **laudanosine** metabolite; histamine release.
- **Cisatracurium** 0.15 mg/kg; a single stereoisomer, ~4× as potent; mainly Hofmann; minimal histamine release; ideal in hepatic and renal failure; slower onset.
- **Mivacurium:** short-acting; hydrolysed by plasma cholinesterase (prolonged in cholinesterase deficiency).

**Check the BNF and local guidance for doses.**

## Onset: the Bowman principle

**Less potent drugs have a faster onset**, because a larger dose (more molecules) is given, so more molecules reach the junction quickly (law of mass action). Rocuronium is about one sixth as potent as vecuronium and has a faster onset. Onset is also faster with higher dose and cardiac output, and in muscles with high blood flow (the diaphragm and larynx are affected before the adductor pollicis but are more resistant).

## Factors potentiating block

Volatile agents; **aminoglycosides**, magnesium, lithium, calcium channel blockers, local anaesthetics; hypothermia; acidosis; hypokalaemia, hypocalcaemia, hypermagnesaemia; myasthenia gravis and Lambert–Eaton; neonates (for some drugs); hepatic or renal failure (drug-dependent).

**Resistance:** burns, denervation, prolonged immobility (upregulated receptors); anticonvulsants (chronic phenytoin, carbamazepine); hypercalcaemia.
