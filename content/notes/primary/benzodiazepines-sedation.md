## Benzodiazepines

- **Mechanism:** bind the **benzodiazepine site** (between α and γ subunits) of the GABA~A~ receptor → increase the **frequency** of Cl^−^ channel opening (barbiturates increase the duration).
- **Effects:** anxiolysis, sedation, **anterograde amnesia**, anticonvulsant, muscle relaxation (spinal). Respiratory depression is **synergistic with opioids**.
- **Midazolam:** water-soluble in the ampoule (ring open at pH ~3.5), lipid-soluble at body pH; hydroxylated by **CYP3A4** (inhibited by erythromycin, azoles) to active 1-hydroxymidazolam (accumulates in renal failure); elimination half-life about 2 h, longer in the elderly and obese.
- **Diazepam:** long half-life with active metabolites (desmethyldiazepam).
- **Lorazepam:** glucuronidated (no active metabolites); used in status epilepticus.
- **Remimazolam:** ultra-short-acting ester benzodiazepine (tissue esterases).
- **Reversal:** **flumazenil** (competitive antagonist) has a shorter duration than most benzodiazepines → re-sedation; may precipitate seizures in dependent patients or tricyclic overdose.

## α~2~-agonists

- **Clonidine** and **dexmedetomidine** (much more α~2~-selective): sedation resembling natural sleep (locus coeruleus), analgesia, sympatholysis, reduced anaesthetic requirement, reduced shivering and emergence agitation, with **little respiratory depression**.
- Side effects: bradycardia and hypotension (initial hypertension with rapid loading), rebound hypertension on stopping clonidine.

## Procedural sedation

**Levels (continuum):**

| Level | Response | Airway and breathing |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal (anxiolysis) | Normal response to voice | Unaffected |
| **Moderate ("conscious") sedation** | Purposeful response to voice or light touch | No intervention needed; adequate spontaneous ventilation |
| Deep sedation | Purposeful response only to repeated or painful stimulation | May need airway support |
| General anaesthesia | Unrousable | Often needs airway support |

- Patients can slip unintentionally into a deeper level: those giving sedation must be able to rescue from the next level.
- **Assessment:** ASA status, airway, OSA, fasting, comorbidities, frailty, drug history; consent and explanation (reassurance reduces drug requirement).
- **Monitoring:** as for anaesthesia when deep sedation is used; **capnography** is recommended for moderate and deep sedation (Association of Anaesthetists 2021 monitoring standards); pulse oximetry, BP, ECG as indicated. Scoring tools (e.g. RASS) quantify depth.
- **Complications:** airway obstruction, hypoventilation, hypoxaemia, hypotension, aspiration, paradoxical agitation (children, elderly). Keep reversal agents, oxygen, suction and airway equipment ready.
- **Discharge:** recovery criteria; a responsible adult for day cases; no driving or legal decisions for 24 hours.
- Particular risks: the **frail and elderly** (smaller doses, slower titration), the critically ill, and patients needing transfer.
