## Vapours

- A **vapour** is the gaseous phase of a substance below its critical temperature.
- **Saturated vapour pressure (SVP):** the partial pressure of vapour in equilibrium with its liquid in a closed container. It depends **only on temperature** (and the substance), not on ambient pressure.
- **Boiling point:** the temperature at which SVP equals ambient pressure. So at altitude liquids boil at lower temperatures, and desflurane (boiling point 22.8°C) boils in a warm room.
- **Maximum concentration** of a vapour at 1 atmosphere = SVP / atmospheric pressure (e.g. isoflurane 32/101 ≈ 32%): hence the need for vaporisers that dilute to a safe output.

## Latent heat

- **Specific latent heat of vaporisation:** the heat needed to convert 1 kg of liquid to vapour **without a change in temperature**. It falls as temperature rises (zero at the critical temperature).
- Vaporisation takes heat from the remaining liquid, cooling it and lowering its SVP — why vaporisers need a heat sink (copper, a large mass) and **temperature compensation**, and why an N~2~O cylinder in heavy use gets cold (and ice forms on the outside).
- **Specific heat capacity:** heat to raise 1 kg by 1 K (water ~4.2 kJ/kg/K; blood similar). Warming 1 L of fluid from room temperature to body temperature takes about 70 kJ — why cold fluids cool patients.

## Humidity

- **Absolute humidity:** mass of water vapour per unit volume of gas (g/m^3^). Air saturated at 37°C contains about **44 g/m^3^** (SVP 6.3 kPa).
- **Relative humidity:** actual mass / mass needed to saturate at that temperature (%). Warming gas lowers its relative humidity without changing absolute humidity; cooling it produces condensation (dew point).
- **Measurement:** hair hygrometer (hair lengthens with humidity), wet and dry bulb hygrometer (evaporative cooling), Regnault's hygrometer (dew point), electrical humidity sensors.
- The **isothermic saturation boundary** (where gas reaches 37°C, 100% RH) normally lies just below the carina. Tracheal intubation bypasses the nose and upper airway; dry gas causes mucosal damage, thick secretions, tube blockage and heat loss.

## Humidifiers

| Device | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Heat and moisture exchanger (HME)** | Passive; hygroscopic material retains exhaled heat and water; about 60–70% efficiency; adds dead space and resistance; can be combined with a filter (HMEF); can block with secretions |
| **Hot water bath humidifier** | Active; can achieve full saturation at 37°C; risk of scalding, infection, water in the tubing (heated wires reduce condensation) |
| **Nebulisers** (gas-driven, ultrasonic) | Produce droplets that can deliver water or drugs; risk of over-humidification and infection |
