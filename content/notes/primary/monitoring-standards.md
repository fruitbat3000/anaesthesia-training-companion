## Recommendations for standards of monitoring (2021) — the essentials

- The **anaesthetist must be present** throughout general anaesthesia (and sedation they provide), with a clear handover if they leave.
- **Before induction:** pulse oximetry, NIBP, ECG and capnography should be in use (induction may start before all are attached in uncooperative patients or children, but monitoring should follow promptly).
- **During general anaesthesia:** continuous **pulse oximetry**, **NIBP** (at least every 5 minutes), **ECG**, **airway gases** (oxygen, CO~2~ with waveform capnography, and vapour concentration), **airway pressure** (for ventilated patients), **peripheral nerve stimulator (quantitative) whenever neuromuscular blockers are used**, **temperature** (for procedures over 30 minutes), and **processed EEG (depth of anaesthesia) monitoring with TIVA when neuromuscular blockers are used**.
- **Sedation:** capnography for moderate and deep sedation.
- **Recovery:** pulse oximetry, NIBP, capnography if the airway is still in place, and other monitoring as indicated; a trained member of staff for each patient until they can maintain their airway.
- **Transfer:** the same standard as in theatre, including waveform capnography for intubated patients.
- Alarm limits should be set appropriately and alarms left on.

## Point-of-care (near-patient) testing

| Test | Principle | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| **Haemoglobin** (e.g. HemoCue) | Spectrophotometry of converted haemoglobin (azide-methaemoglobin) | Capillary samples less accurate; technique |
| **Glucose** | Glucose oxidase or dehydrogenase electrochemical strips | Inaccurate with poor perfusion, extremes of haematocrit, some interfering substances |
| **Blood gases and electrolytes** | Electrodes (see gas analysis), ion-selective electrodes | Calibration and QC essential |
| **Coagulation**: ACT, INR meters, **viscoelastic (TEG, ROTEM)** | Clot detection in whole blood | Operator training; quality control |
| **Ketones** | β-hydroxybutyrate strips | |
| Lactate | Enzymatic electrode | |

Point-of-care devices need training, quality control, and connection to the patient record; results should be interpreted in clinical context and confirmed by the lab when needed.
