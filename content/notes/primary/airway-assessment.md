## Four difficulties to predict

1. **Difficult facemask ventilation:** beard, obesity, age > 55, edentulous, OSA/snoring, neck radiotherapy, Mallampati IV, limited jaw protrusion.
2. **Difficult supraglottic airway:** reduced mouth opening, upper airway pathology, obesity, fixed cervical spine flexion.
3. **Difficult laryngoscopy and intubation:** previous difficulty (the best predictor), **Mallampati III–IV**, **reduced mouth opening** (< 3 cm), **short thyromental distance** (< 6 cm), reduced **jaw protrusion** (upper lip bite test), **limited neck movement**, prominent incisors, large tongue, obesity, pregnancy, airway pathology (tumour, abscess, trauma, burns).
4. **Difficult front-of-neck access:** obesity, short or fixed neck, previous radiotherapy or surgery, goitre, tumour — **identify the cricothyroid membrane** (palpation or ultrasound) before induction when difficulty is expected.

No single test is reliable; combinations are better, and even then many difficult airways are unanticipated. **Always have a plan and backups.**

## Grading laryngoscopy

**Cormack–Lehane:** grade 1 (most of the glottis visible), 2 (posterior glottis / arytenoids only), 3 (epiglottis only), 4 (no laryngeal structures). Record the grade, the device used, and any adjuncts (bougie, video laryngoscope) in the anaesthetic chart.

## Airway devices

- **Oropharyngeal airways** (size: incisors to the angle of the mandible) and **nasopharyngeal airways** (avoid with base-of-skull fracture and coagulopathy).
- **Supraglottic airways:** first-generation (classic LMA) and **second-generation** devices with a drain tube and higher seal pressure (i-gel, ProSeal, LMA Supreme), preferred for rescue and for many routine cases. Contraindications: high aspiration risk, need for high ventilation pressures, some surgical positions and procedures.
- **Tracheal tubes:** cuffed tubes, sizes (typical adult 7.0–8.0 mm ID), reinforced, RAE preformed tubes, microlaryngeal tubes, double-lumen tubes.
- **Laryngoscopes:** Macintosh (curved), McCoy (levering tip), Miller (straight: infants), and **video laryngoscopes** (standard-geometry or hyperangulated blades; hyperangulated blades need a stylet). DAS 2025 promotes video laryngoscopy as a first-line tool for competent users.
- **Bougie and stylet**; **flexible bronchoscope** for awake or asleep fibreoptic intubation.

## Making a plan

- Plan A (the primary technique), B (rescue oxygenation with a second-generation SGA), C (facemask ventilation, then wake up or proceed), D (front-of-neck access) — and state them in the team brief.
- Consider **awake tracheal intubation** when difficulty is predicted and awake techniques are feasible.
- Optimise **pre-oxygenation** (head-up, tight seal, end-tidal O~2~ ≥ 0.9; HFNO for apnoeic oxygenation), positioning ("sniffing" or ramped in the obese), and the right team and equipment.
- **Confirm tracheal placement with sustained waveform capnography** every time ("no trace = wrong place").
