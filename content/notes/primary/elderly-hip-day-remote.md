## Hip fracture

- Surgery **on the day of or the day after admission** (NICE CG124); correct reversible problems quickly, but do not delay for tests that will not change management.
- **Analgesia:** paracetamol, cautious opioids, and a **fascia iliaca** (or femoral) block from the emergency department.
- **Anaesthesia:** spinal or general anaesthesia — neither is clearly superior for mortality; the Association of Anaesthetists 2020 guideline emphasises avoiding hypotension, avoiding deep sedation with spinals, cautious drug dosing, and an experienced anaesthetist.
- **Bone cement implantation syndrome:** hypoxia, hypotension, arrhythmias, raised pulmonary vascular resistance or cardiac arrest around cementation. Reduce risk by identifying high-risk patients, communicating with the surgeon before cementing, vigilant monitoring, and adequate fluid loading; treat with oxygen, fluids, vasopressors and CPR. Other orthopaedic complications: fat embolism, compartment syndrome.
- **Frail and older patients:** delirium prevention (avoid anticholinergics and benzodiazepines, maintain hearing aids and glasses, adequate analgesia), pressure care, thromboprophylaxis, early mobilisation, orthogeriatric input.

## Day surgery

- **Patient selection** is based on physiological status rather than arbitrary age or BMI limits: stable comorbidity, understanding and a responsible adult escort and carer for 24 hours (for GA), suitable home circumstances.
- **Anaesthetic technique:** short-acting drugs (propofol, remifentanil, sevoflurane or desflurane where appropriate), multimodal analgesia with regional or local techniques, PONV prophylaxis, and spinal anaesthesia with short-acting agents (e.g. chloroprocaine, prilocaine).
- **Preassessment** is often nurse-led with protocols and anaesthetic review of complex patients; consider medical (stable comorbidity), surgical (short, low blood loss, manageable pain) and social factors (escort, carer, telephone, distance from hospital).
- **Discharge criteria:** stable observations, controlled pain and nausea, mobile, having passed urine where relevant, written instructions and a contact number. Patients should not drive, operate machinery or make important decisions for 24 hours after GA or sedation (and should check with their insurer and the DVLA guidance for driving after surgery).
- **Unplanned admission** is most often due to pain, PONV, surgical complications (bleeding, extended surgery), drowsiness, urinary retention or social reasons: track it as a quality measure.

## Positioning and procedures

- **Positioning hazards:** nerve injuries (ulnar at the elbow, common peroneal in lithotomy, brachial plexus with arm abduction > 90°), pressure injuries, eye injury (prone), compartment syndrome, cardiovascular changes (sitting, prone, lateral), and airway access.
- **Tourniquets:** pressure about 50–75 mmHg above systolic for the arm and 100–150 mmHg for the leg (or by limb occlusion pressure); limit time (about 1.5–2 hours); complications include nerve injury, ischaemia, pain, and metabolic acidosis, hyperkalaemia and a fall in blood pressure on release.
- **Laparoscopy:** CO~2~ pneumoperitoneum (raised intra-abdominal pressure → reduced FRC and compliance, raised PaCO~2~, reduced venous return, vagal reflexes), and Trendelenburg or reverse Trendelenburg effects; risks of gas embolism, subcutaneous emphysema and pneumothorax.

## Remote sites (MRI, CT, radiology, endoscopy, ED)

- The same standards of monitoring, equipment, assistance and recovery as in theatre; unfamiliar environments and limited access increase risk.
- **MRI:** the powerful static magnetic field makes **ferromagnetic objects dangerous projectiles**; implants and devices must be checked (MR safe, MR conditional or MR unsafe); use MR-conditional equipment and monitoring; burns from cables; noise (ear protection); limited access to the patient; quench risk. Contrast agents (gadolinium) and iodinated contrast in CT (allergy-like reactions, nephropathy).
- **Radiation safety:** IR(ME)R roles (referrer, practitioner, operator), lead protection, distance.
- Anaesthesia in the emergency department carries the risks of an unprepared patient (full stomach, head injury, sepsis, airway obstruction).
