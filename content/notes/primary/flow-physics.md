## Laminar flow

- Smooth, parallel layers; fastest in the centre (parabolic profile).
- **Hagen–Poiseuille:** **Q = (π P r^4^) / (8 η l)**. Flow is proportional to the **fourth power of radius**, and inversely to **viscosity (η)** and length.
- Doubling cannula radius increases flow **16-fold**; a short, wide cannula (e.g. 14G, 45 mm) is best for rapid infusion. Warming blood reduces viscosity and increases flow.

## Turbulent flow

- Chaotic eddies; flow ∝ **√pressure** and inversely to **density (ρ)**; resistance is much higher.
- Occurs above a critical velocity, at bends, branches, constrictions and irregularities (the larynx, kinked tubes, orifices).
- **Reynolds number: Re = ρ v d / η**. Below about **2000** flow is usually laminar; above about 2000 it becomes turbulent (the precise value depends on the geometry).
- **Helium–oxygen (heliox):** helium's **low density** reduces Re and resistance where flow is turbulent (upper airway obstruction), buying time. Its viscosity is similar to oxygen's, so it helps less where flow is laminar (small airways).

## Bernoulli and Venturi

- **Bernoulli:** along a streamline, total energy is constant, so where fluid **speeds up** through a constriction, its **pressure falls**.
- **Venturi effect:** the low pressure at a constriction **entrains** a second fluid through a side port.
- **Uses:** **Venturi (fixed-performance) oxygen masks**, where a jet of oxygen entrains a fixed proportion of air to give a known FiO~2~ (e.g. 24%, 28%, 35%, 40%, 60%) provided total flow exceeds the patient's peak inspiratory flow; nebulisers; suction devices; jet ventilation injectors; the **Coanda effect** (a jet tends to follow a curved surface: relevant to flow at vessel and airway bifurcations and some fluidic logic devices).

## Measuring flow

Variable-orifice flowmeters (rotameters): at low flows the bobbin's annular gap acts like a tube (laminar flow: depends on viscosity); at high flows it acts like an orifice (turbulent: depends on density). They are calibrated for a specific gas. See clinical measurement of flow.
