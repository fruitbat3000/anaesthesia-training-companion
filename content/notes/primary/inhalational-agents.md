## Common effects of volatile agents

- **CNS:** dose-dependent unconsciousness; cerebral vasodilation (↑ CBF, may ↑ ICP) with reduced CMRO~2~; EEG suppression. Sevoflurane can cause epileptiform EEG at high concentrations and emergence agitation in children.
- **CVS:** ↓ SVR and BP (vasodilation; isoflurane and desflurane most), ↓ contractility, blunted baroreflex. **Desflurane** can cause sympathetic stimulation and tachycardia with rapid increases in concentration. Halothane sensitises the myocardium to catecholamines (arrhythmias).
- **Respiratory:** ↓ tidal volume, ↑ rate, ↑ PaCO~2~; depress hypoxic and hypercapnic drives; bronchodilation; depress HPV slightly.
- Uterine relaxation (dose-dependent: relevant at caesarean section), potentiation of neuromuscular block, triggers of **malignant hyperthermia**.
- **Sevoflurane with CO~2~ absorbent:** compound A (nephrotoxic in rats) with low flows and strong bases; modern absorbents lack strong bases.

## Nitrous oxide

- Analgesic (NMDA antagonism, and endogenous opioid and noradrenergic mechanisms); weak anaesthetic (MAC > 100%).
- **34 times more soluble in blood than nitrogen**, so it enters air-filled spaces faster than N~2~ leaves: **expands** pneumothorax, bowel, middle ear, air emboli, intraocular gas bubbles (SF~6~, C~3~F~8~: contraindicated for weeks), and **increases pressure** in tracheal tube cuffs and LMA cuffs.
- **Oxidises vitamin B~12~**, inactivating methionine synthase → megaloblastic changes and subacute combined degeneration with prolonged or repeated exposure (and recreational use); possible teratogenicity.
- PONV, diffusion hypoxia. Large environmental impact, much from **pipeline leakage**: many UK hospitals have decommissioned central N~2~O manifolds.
- **Entonox** (50:50 O~2~/N~2~O): pseudo-critical temperature about −6°C (separates in the cold: the lower layer becomes rich in N~2~O).

## Xenon

Noble gas; NMDA antagonist; MAC about 63–71%; blood:gas ~0.115 (very fast onset and offset); cardiostable, no MH trigger, environmentally neutral, but very expensive and needs special equipment.

## Environmental impact

Anaesthetic gases are a major contributor to NHS carbon emissions. Strategies: avoid desflurane and N~2~O, use **low fresh gas flows**, consider **TIVA** and regional techniques.
