## Ventricular myocyte action potential

| Phase | Event | Main current |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Resting potential **about −90 mV** | K^+^ efflux through inward rectifier (I~K1~) channels |
| 0 | Rapid depolarisation | Fast **Na^+^** influx (voltage-gated Na^+^ channels) |
| 1 | Early partial repolarisation | Transient K^+^ outflow (I~to~) |
| 2 | **Plateau** (~200 ms) | **Ca^2+^ influx through L-type channels** balanced by K^+^ efflux |
| 3 | Repolarisation | Delayed rectifier K^+^ currents (I~Kr~, I~Ks~) |

- The long plateau gives a long **absolute refractory period**, so cardiac muscle cannot be tetanised and has time to relax and refill.
- **Hyperkalaemia** makes the resting potential less negative, inactivating some Na^+^ channels, slowing conduction (wide QRS) and eventually causing ventricular fibrillation or asystole.

## Pacemaker (SA node) action potential

- **No stable resting potential.** Phase 4 is a slow diastolic depolarisation from about −60 mV, due mainly to the **"funny" current (I~f~, HCN channels; Na^+^ and K^+^)** plus T-type Ca^2+^ current.
- **Phase 0** in nodal tissue is caused by **L-type Ca^2+^** influx (slower upstroke), not Na^+^.
- Sympathetic stimulation (β~1~, cAMP) steepens phase 4 → faster rate. Vagal stimulation (M~2~, ACh → more K^+^ conductance) hyperpolarises and flattens phase 4 → slower rate.
- **Hierarchy of pacemakers:** SA node 60–100/min, AV node 40–60/min, ventricular (Purkinje) 20–40/min.

## Conduction

SA node → atria → **AV node** (slow conduction, about 0.1 s delay, allowing ventricular filling; the delay comes from small cells with few gap junctions and a Ca^2+^-dependent upstroke. Elsewhere the atria and ventricles are electrically insulated by the fibrous skeleton, so the AV node is the only normal route) → bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje fibres (fastest conduction, about 4 m/s).

## Excitation–contraction coupling

- The action potential spreads down **T-tubules** (in cardiac muscle short, wide and at the Z-lines, each meeting one SR cisterna as a *diad*; skeletal muscle has *triads*); L-type channels (dihydropyridine receptors) let in a small amount of Ca^2+^, which triggers a much larger release from the **sarcoplasmic reticulum via ryanodine receptors (RyR2)**: **calcium-induced calcium release**.
- Ca^2+^ binds **troponin C**, moving tropomyosin to expose myosin-binding sites on actin; cross-bridges cycle using ATP.
- Relaxation: Ca^2+^ is pumped back into the SR by **SERCA** (regulated by phospholamban) and expelled by the **Na^+^/Ca^2+^ exchanger**.
- **Digoxin** inhibits Na^+^/K^+^-ATPase, raising intracellular Na^+^ and hence Ca^2+^ (reduced exchange) → positive inotropy.
