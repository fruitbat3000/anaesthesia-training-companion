## Brain and blood supply

- **Subdivisions:** cerebral hemispheres (frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital), diencephalon (thalamus, hypothalamus), brainstem (midbrain, pons, medulla), cerebellum.
- **Circle of Willis:** internal carotid arteries and the basilar artery (from the two vertebral arteries) joined by the anterior and posterior communicating arteries; gives off the anterior, middle and posterior cerebral arteries. An anastomosis that may compensate for occlusion, though often incomplete. Berry aneurysms occur at its junctions (subarachnoid haemorrhage).
- **Venous drainage:** dural venous sinuses to the internal jugular veins; head-down position and jugular compression raise ICP.

## Cranial nerves (anaesthetic relevance)

| Nerve | Function | Anaesthetic relevance |
|---|---|---|
| I Olfactory | Smell | |
| II Optic | Vision | Postoperative visual loss (prone spine surgery, ischaemic optic neuropathy) |
| III Oculomotor | Most eye muscles, levator palpebrae, pupil constriction (parasympathetic) | **Fixed dilated pupil** with uncal herniation |
| IV Trochlear | Superior oblique | Eye blocks |
| V Trigeminal | Facial sensation (V1, V2, V3); muscles of mastication | Oculocardiac and nasal reflexes (afferent); trigeminal neuralgia procedures |
| VI Abducens | Lateral rectus | Long intracranial course: palsy with raised or low ICP (e.g. post-dural puncture headache) |
| VII Facial | Facial expression; taste (anterior two-thirds) | Facial nerve monitoring in parotid surgery (avoid NMB); nerve stimulator site |
| VIII Vestibulocochlear | Hearing, balance | PONV; N~2~O and middle ear |
| IX Glossopharyngeal | Posterior tongue and pharynx sensation; carotid sinus | Gag reflex (afferent); airway block |
| X Vagus | Larynx (SLN, RLN), parasympathetic to heart and gut | Laryngospasm; bradycardia; aortic arch baroreceptors |
| XI Accessory | Sternocleidomastoid, trapezius | |
| XII Hypoglossal | Tongue muscles | Tongue deviation; injury with LMA or laryngoscopy (rare) |

## The orbit and eye

- **Extraocular muscles** arise from the annulus of Zinn (the common tendinous ring) and form a **cone**: intraconal (retrobulbar) versus extraconal (peribulbar) blocks. **Sub-Tenon's block** injects under Tenon's capsule (fascial sheath of the eyeball) with a blunt cannula; the safest common technique.
- Globe axial length > 26 mm (high myopia) increases the risk of globe perforation with needle blocks.
- **Oculocardiac reflex:** traction on extraocular muscles (especially medial rectus in strabismus surgery) or pressure on the globe → **bradycardia**, arrhythmias, even asystole. Afferent: **trigeminal (V1, ciliary ganglion)**; efferent: **vagus**. Stop the stimulus; atropine or glycopyrronium.
- **Intraocular pressure** 10–20 mmHg; see drugs and the eye.

## Autonomic ganglia of the head and neck

- **Sympathetic:** superior, middle and inferior cervical ganglia (the inferior fuses with T1 as the **stellate ganglion**, at C7 anterior to the neck of the first rib and transverse process).
- **Parasympathetic:** ciliary (III), pterygopalatine (VII), submandibular (VII), otic (IX) ganglia.

## Endocrine anatomy (brief)

The **pituitary** sits in the sella turcica below the optic chiasm; the **thyroid** lies at C5–T1 anterior to the trachea with the recurrent laryngeal nerves in the tracheo-oesophageal grooves and the parathyroids posteriorly; the **adrenals** sit on the upper poles of the kidneys (medulla surrounded by cortex).
