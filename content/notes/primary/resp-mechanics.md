## Compliance

- **Compliance = ΔV / ΔP.** Normal total respiratory system compliance is about **100 mL/cmH~2~O** (lung ~200 and chest wall ~200 mL/cmH~2~O, which add as reciprocals: 1/C~total~ = 1/C~lung~ + 1/C~chest wall~).
- **Static compliance** is measured with no gas flow (plateau pressure), so it reflects elastic properties only. **Dynamic compliance** is measured during flow (peak pressure) and is lower because it includes airway resistance.
- The **pressure–volume curve** of the lung is sigmoid: compliance is lowest at very low volumes (alveolar collapse) and near TLC (overdistension). Normal breathing at FRC sits on the steep part.
- Compliance is reduced by pulmonary fibrosis, oedema, ARDS, atelectasis, obesity (chest wall), and increased in emphysema and old age (loss of elastic tissue).
- **Hysteresis:** the inflation and deflation limbs differ, because of surfactant and alveolar recruitment.

## Surface tension and surfactant

- **Laplace's law** for a sphere: **P = 2T / r**. Without surfactant, small alveoli would empty into large ones.
- **Surfactant** (dipalmitoyl phosphatidylcholine, from type II pneumocytes) lowers surface tension, more so as alveoli shrink. This stabilises alveoli, increases compliance, reduces the work of breathing and helps keep alveoli dry (less fluid is drawn in from capillaries).
- Deficiency: neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (surfactant production matures from about 34 weeks' gestation).

## Airway resistance

- Laminar flow: resistance ∝ **η·l / r^4^** (Hagen–Poiseuille). Halving the radius increases resistance 16-fold.
- Most resistance in normal lungs is in the **medium-sized bronchi**, not the small airways, because small airways are so numerous that their total cross-sectional area is huge.
- Resistance falls as lung volume increases (radial traction), so it rises at low lung volumes, with bronchospasm, secretions, and narrow or kinked tracheal tubes.

## Work of breathing

- Work = pressure × volume. It has an **elastic** component (overcome compliance; greater with large tidal volumes) and a **resistive** component (overcome airway and tissue resistance; greater with high flow rates and fast breathing).
- At rest breathing uses only about 1–2% of total oxygen consumption; this can rise dramatically in respiratory disease.
- Patients naturally choose a respiratory rate that minimises work: stiff lungs → rapid shallow breathing; obstructed airways → slow deep breathing.
