## Definitions (Sepsis-3)

- **Sepsis:** life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection (an acute rise in **SOFA score ≥ 2**).
- **Septic shock:** sepsis needing **vasopressors to keep MAP ≥ 65 mmHg** with **lactate > 2 mmol/L** despite adequate fluid resuscitation (mortality over 40%).
- Screening tools: NEWS2 and the NICE NG51 risk stratification; qSOFA (RR ≥ 22, altered mentation, systolic BP ≤ 100) is less sensitive.

## Early management

- **Recognise, escalate and treat within an hour** for high-risk patients: cultures (without delaying antibiotics), **broad-spectrum antibiotics** per local policy (then de-escalate), **fluid resuscitation** with balanced crystalloids guided by reassessment (the Surviving Sepsis Campaign suggests up to 30 mL/kg in the first hours for hypoperfusion, individualised), **lactate**, **source control** (surgery, drainage), oxygen, urine output monitoring.
- **Vasopressors:** **noradrenaline** first-line (peripheral administration is possible initially), adding vasopressin; corticosteroids (hydrocortisone) for ongoing vasopressor requirement.
- **Anaesthesia for the septic patient** (e.g. for source control): expect profound hypotension at induction (reduced doses, vasopressor ready, arterial line), aspiration risk, coagulopathy.

## Critical care

- **Admission** decisions balance potential benefit, the patient's wishes and prognosis; involve senior clinicians. Levels of care: 1 (ward with outreach), 2 (single organ support, e.g. vasopressors or NIV), 3 (advanced respiratory support or two or more organ supports).
- **Invasive procedures:** arterial and central lines, chest drains (see anatomy), renal replacement access — performed with full asepsis and ultrasound guidance.
- **Communication:** clear, compassionate, regular updates to patients and families; honest discussion of uncertainty and ceilings of care; handover and discharge summaries to ward teams.

## End of life and organ donation

- Withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment focuses on comfort, dignity and family support, with a clear plan documented.
- **Brainstem death testing** (UK Academy of Medical Royal Colleges code): preconditions (a known cause of irreversible brain damage; exclusion of reversible causes such as drugs, hypothermia, and metabolic and endocrine disturbances), then two sets of tests by two doctors (one a consultant; both registered for more than 5 years): absent pupillary, corneal, vestibulo-ocular, gag and cough reflexes, no motor response in the cranial nerve distribution, and **apnoea test** (no respiratory effort with PaCO~2~ ≥ 6.5 kPa and pH < 7.40). Death is confirmed at the completion of the first set.
- Early involvement of the **specialist nurse for organ donation** in every potential donor.
