## Corticosteroids

| Drug | Glucocorticoid potency | Mineralocorticoid potency | Equivalent dose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrocortisone | 1 | 1 | 20 mg |
| Prednisolone | 4 | 0.8 | 5 mg |
| Methylprednisolone | 5 | ~0.5 | 4 mg |
| Dexamethasone | 25–30 | ~0 | 0.75 mg |
| Fludrocortisone | ~10 | 125–250 | (mineralocorticoid replacement) |

- **Uses in anaesthesia:** PONV prophylaxis and analgesic adjunct (dexamethasone), airway oedema, cerebral oedema around tumours (dexamethasone), anaphylaxis (adjunct), asthma and COPD exacerbations, adrenal insufficiency and septic shock (hydrocortisone), prolonging nerve blocks.
- **Long-term side effects:** HPA axis suppression, hyperglycaemia, hypertension, fluid retention, hypokalaemia, osteoporosis, myopathy, thin skin and poor wound healing, infection, peptic ulcer, cataracts, psychiatric effects, Cushingoid features.
- **Perioperative steroid cover:** patients taking **≥ 5 mg prednisolone (or equivalent) daily for more than 4 weeks** are at risk of adrenal insufficiency. Continue their usual dose and give additional **hydrocortisone** according to the size of surgery (per the 2020 joint guideline from the Association of Anaesthetists and endocrine societies); give IV equivalents if nil by mouth.

## Thyroid drugs

- **Hypothyroidism:** **levothyroxine (T~4~)**; long half-life (~7 days), so a missed dose or two is unimportant. **Liothyronine (T~3~)** acts faster (myxoedema coma, with caution).
- **Hyperthyroidism:**
  - **Carbimazole** and **propylthiouracil** inhibit thyroid peroxidase (PTU also blocks peripheral T~4~ → T~3~ conversion); agranulocytosis (sore throat → check FBC).
  - **β-blockers** (propranolol) for symptoms; **iodide** (Lugol's) briefly before surgery reduces gland vascularity and hormone release.
  - **Thyroid storm:** β-blockade, PTU or carbimazole, then iodide, hydrocortisone, cooling and supportive care.
- **Amiodarone** can cause either hypo- or hyperthyroidism.
