## The ALS algorithm (follow the current RCUK 2025 guideline)

- Recognise arrest; call the team; start **high-quality chest compressions** (5–6 cm deep, 100–120/min, full recoil, **minimal interruptions**), ventilate with oxygen (30:2 until the airway is secured, then continuous compressions with ventilation at about 10/min — **avoid hyperventilation**, which reduces venous return and coronary perfusion).
- **Shockable (VF/pulseless VT):** shock, resume CPR immediately for 2 minutes, reassess. **Adrenaline 1 mg IV/IO** after the third shock, then every 3–5 minutes; **amiodarone 300 mg** after the third shock (150 mg after the fifth). Consider lidocaine as an alternative.
- **Non-shockable (PEA/asystole):** adrenaline 1 mg as soon as access is available, then every 3–5 minutes; look for reversible causes.
- **IV or intraosseous** access (IO when IV is not quickly available).
- **Waveform capnography:** confirms tube placement, shows CPR quality, and a sudden rise suggests ROSC.

## Reversible causes: 4 Hs and 4 Ts

**Hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypo-/hyperkalaemia and metabolic, hypothermia**; **thrombosis (coronary or pulmonary), tension pneumothorax, tamponade, toxins**. Point-of-care ultrasound may help identify some causes (without interrupting compressions).

## Special circumstances

Anaphylaxis, asthma, pregnancy, trauma (treat reversible causes: haemorrhage, hypoxia, tension, tamponade), drowning, hypothermia (no drugs below 30°C; prolonged resuscitation), local anaesthetic toxicity (lipid), hyperkalaemia (calcium, insulin–glucose), poisoning, and **perioperative cardiac arrest** (NAP7: incidence about 3 per 10,000 anaesthetics, commonly from major haemorrhage, bradyarrhythmia, cardiac ischaemia and anaphylaxis; an anaesthetist is usually present, so causes can often be identified and treated early).

## Post-resuscitation care

ABCDE; **targeted oxygenation** (SpO~2~ 94–98%) and **normocapnia**; blood pressure (MAP > 65 mmHg as a minimum); 12-lead ECG and coronary angiography when a cardiac cause is likely; **temperature control** (avoid fever); glucose control; seizure management; prognostication after at least 72 hours with a multimodal approach; investigations for the cause.

## Ethical and legal aspects

- **DNACPR** decisions and **treatment escalation plans** (e.g. ReSPECT) should be discussed with the patient (or those close to them) and documented; they do not mean "do not treat".
- Allow relatives to be present during resuscitation if they wish, with a staff member supporting them.
- Stopping resuscitation is a senior team decision.
- **Debrief** the team afterwards (hot and cold debriefs) and support relatives compassionately.

## Recognising deterioration

**NEWS2** (respiratory rate, SpO~2~ and oxygen, systolic BP, heart rate, consciousness including new confusion, temperature) triggers escalation. Critical care scores (APACHE, SOFA) quantify severity of illness.
