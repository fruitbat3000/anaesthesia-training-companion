## Ageing

| System | Change | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | Stiffer arteries (↑ systolic BP, wide pulse pressure), diastolic dysfunction, reduced β-receptor responsiveness, conduction disease | Exaggerated hypotension at induction; poor tolerance of tachycardia and fluid overload |
| Respiratory | ↑ closing capacity, ↓ compliance of chest wall, ↓ PaO~2~ (a rule of thumb: about 100 − age/3 mmHg breathing air), blunted responses to hypoxia and hypercapnia | Hypoxaemia; sensitivity to respiratory depressants |
| Renal | ↓ GFR (about 1 mL/min per year after 40), ↓ concentrating ability | Reduced drug clearance; creatinine underestimates impairment (low muscle mass) |
| CNS | ↓ neurones and neurotransmitters, **↓ MAC (about 6% per decade after 40)** | Postoperative delirium and cognitive dysfunction |
| Body composition | ↑ fat, ↓ muscle and total body water | Larger Vd for lipid-soluble drugs, smaller for water-soluble ones |
| Other | Thinner skin, osteoporosis, impaired thermoregulation, polypharmacy | Positioning injury, hypothermia, drug interactions |

**Frailty** (reduced physiological reserve, often measured with the Clinical Frailty Scale) predicts complications and mortality better than age alone.

## Obesity

- **BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)^2^.** Obese ≥ 30; class III ≥ 40. **Central (android, visceral) fat** carries more cardiovascular and metabolic risk than peripheral (gynaecoid) fat, so waist circumference predicts morbidity better than BMI.
- **Energy balance:** **leptin** from adipose tissue acts on the hypothalamus to reduce appetite and raise energy expenditure; obesity is usually a leptin-resistant state.
- **Respiratory:** ↓ FRC and ERV (especially supine), atelectasis, ↑ oxygen consumption and CO~2~ production, ↑ work of breathing, **obstructive sleep apnoea** (screen with STOP-BANG) and obesity hypoventilation syndrome. Rapid desaturation; potential difficult mask ventilation.
- **Cardiovascular:** ↑ blood volume and cardiac output, hypertension, LV hypertrophy, pulmonary hypertension (OSA), arrhythmias.
- **Other:** diabetes, reflux, fatty liver, VTE risk, difficult venous access, pressure injuries.

## Size scalars for drug dosing

- **Ideal body weight (IBW)** (Devine): men 50 kg + 0.9 kg/cm above 152 cm; women 45.5 kg + 0.9 kg/cm above 152 cm. A simple alternative: height (cm) − 100 (men) or − 105 (women).
- **Lean body weight (LBW)**: fat-free mass; formulae (e.g. Janmahasatian) plateau at high BMI.
- **Adjusted body weight** = IBW + 0.4 × (actual − IBW).
- General principles: **induction agents and opioids** dosed on **LBW** (then titrated); **suxamethonium** on **total body weight** (increased plasma cholinesterase and ECF); **non-depolarising NMBs** on **IBW** (or LBW); **sugammadex** on actual or adjusted weight per product guidance; LMWH per local obesity protocols. TCI models must be used within their validated ranges.
