## Muscarinic receptors

M~1~ (CNS, gastric parietal cells), **M~2~ (heart: bradycardia)**, **M~3~ (smooth muscle, glands, endothelium)**, M~4~ and M~5~ (CNS).

## Antimuscarinics compared

| | **Atropine** | **Glycopyrronium** | **Hyoscine (scopolamine)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | Tertiary amine (crosses BBB and placenta) | **Quaternary** (does not cross) | Tertiary |
| Heart rate | ↑↑ (initial paradoxical slowing at low doses) | ↑ (less, smoother) | ↑ (less) |
| Antisialagogue | ++ | +++ | +++ |
| Central effects | Excitation, confusion in the elderly | **None** | **Sedation, amnesia**, antiemetic (motion sickness), confusion |
| Onset / duration | Rapid, shorter | Slower, longer (matches neostigmine) | |
| Uses | Bradycardia, organophosphate poisoning | Reversal with neostigmine; drying secretions | Motion sickness, secretions, bowel spasm (hyoscine butylbromide is quaternary: no central effects) |

Other effects of antimuscarinics: bronchodilation, reduced gut motility, urinary retention, mydriasis and cycloplegia (glaucoma risk with atropine), reduced sweating (hyperthermia in children).

## Central anticholinergic syndrome

Agitation, confusion, hallucinations, hyperthermia, dry flushed skin, mydriasis, tachycardia — after atropine, hyoscine, tricyclics, antihistamines. Treatment: supportive; **physostigmine** (a tertiary anticholinesterase) in severe cases.

## Cholinergic agonists

- **Direct:** pilocarpine (glaucoma), bethanechol, carbachol.
- **Indirect** (anticholinesterases): see reversal of neuromuscular block. **Cholinergic crisis** (excess ACh): muscarinic signs (**DUMBELS**: diarrhoea, urination, miosis, bradycardia/bronchorrhoea/bronchospasm, emesis, lacrimation, salivation) plus nicotinic weakness and paralysis.
