## Respiratory effects

- Normal breathing is by **negative** intrapleural pressure; IPPV reverses this, pushing gas in with positive airway pressure.
- **V/Q:** ventilation goes preferentially to non-dependent, more compliant lung, while perfusion stays dependent → increased V/Q mismatch and **dead space**. Zone 1 may appear.
- **Atelectasis** in dependent regions (reduced FRC, absorption with high FiO~2~); PEEP and recruitment help.
- **Ventilator-induced lung injury:** volutrauma (overdistension), barotrauma (pneumothorax), atelectrauma (cyclical opening and closing), biotrauma (inflammatory mediators). Protective ventilation: tidal volume **~6 mL/kg predicted body weight** in ARDS (6–8 mL/kg in theatre), plateau pressure under 30 cmH~2~O, and appropriate PEEP.
- Loss of mucociliary clearance, and risk of ventilator-associated pneumonia when prolonged.

## Cardiovascular effects

- Raised intrathoracic pressure **reduces venous return** and right ventricular preload → lower cardiac output and blood pressure, especially if hypovolaemic (the basis of pulse pressure variation as a marker of fluid responsiveness).
- **Increases RV afterload** at high lung volumes (compression of alveolar vessels).
- **Reduces LV afterload** (lower transmural pressure) — helpful in LV failure, which is one reason CPAP helps acute cardiogenic pulmonary oedema.

## Other organs

- **Renal:** reduced renal blood flow and GFR, raised ADH and renin–angiotensin–aldosterone activity → fluid retention.
- **Hepatic and splanchnic:** reduced blood flow.
- **Cerebral:** raised intrathoracic pressure impedes cerebral venous drainage and can raise ICP; hyperventilation lowers PaCO~2~ and cerebral blood flow.
