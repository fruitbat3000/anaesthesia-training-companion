## Receptors and mechanism

- **μ (MOP)**, **κ (KOP)**, **δ (DOP)** and the nociceptin receptor (NOP), all **G~i~-coupled**: ↓ cAMP, **open K^+^ channels** (hyperpolarisation) and **close voltage-gated Ca^2+^ channels** (less transmitter release).
- **Sites:** dorsal horn (pre- and postsynaptic), periaqueductal grey and rostroventral medulla (activating descending inhibition), and peripheral nerve endings in inflamed tissue.
- μ: analgesia, respiratory depression, euphoria, miosis, constipation, dependence. κ: analgesia, sedation, dysphoria, diuresis. δ: analgesia.

## Comparison

| Drug | Relative potency (morphine = 1) | pKa / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Morphine** | 1 | Glucuronidated to **M6G (active, accumulates in renal failure)** and M3G (neuroexcitatory); histamine release |
| **Diamorphine** | ~2–3 | Pro-drug; lipid-soluble → rapid onset; deacetylated to 6-monoacetylmorphine and morphine |
| **Oxycodone** | ~1.5–2 (oral) | High oral bioavailability; CYP3A4/2D6 |
| **Fentanyl** | ~100 | Very lipid-soluble; pKa 8.4; accumulates with infusions; CYP3A4 |
| **Alfentanil** | ~10–20 | pKa 6.5 (fast onset); small Vd; CYP3A4 |
| **Remifentanil** | ~100–200 | Esterase metabolism; CSHT 3–4 min regardless of duration; hyperalgesia on withdrawal |
| **Tramadol** | ~0.1 | Weak μ agonist plus **serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake inhibition**; CYP2D6 → active metabolite; seizures, serotonin syndrome risk |
| **Codeine** | ~0.1 | Pro-drug via **CYP2D6** → morphine; contraindicated for analgesia after tonsillectomy in under-18s |
| **Buprenorphine** | ~30 | **Partial μ agonist**, high affinity and slow dissociation (hard to reverse with naloxone) |
| **Methadone** | ~variable | NMDA antagonist too; long, unpredictable half-life; QT prolongation |
| **Pethidine** | ~0.1 | Norpethidine (seizures); serotonin syndrome with MAOIs; anticholinergic |

**Doses and conversions vary with patient and context: check the BNF and local guidance.**

## Side effects

Respiratory depression (↓ rate > ↓ tidal volume; synergy with sedatives), sedation, nausea and vomiting (CTZ), pruritus (especially neuraxial), constipation and ileus, urinary retention, miosis, muscle rigidity (chest wall rigidity with rapid high-dose fentanyl/remifentanil), bradycardia, **hyperalgesia**, tolerance and dependence, immunosuppression.

## Naloxone

Competitive antagonist at all opioid receptors; titrate IV in small increments (for example 40–100 µg in adults) to avoid abrupt return of severe pain, hypertension and pulmonary oedema. Its duration (~30–60 min) is shorter than many opioids: **re-narcotisation** is common, so observe and consider an infusion.

## Patient-controlled analgesia

Settings: bolus dose, **lockout interval** (long enough for each dose to reach peak effect), optional background infusion (increases respiratory depression; usually avoided in opioid-naïve adults), and a maximum dose limit. Safety: dedicated line or anti-reflux valve, monitoring of sedation and respiratory rate, standardised concentrations. Only the patient presses the button.
