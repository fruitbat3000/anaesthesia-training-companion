## The ideal induction agent

Rapid, smooth onset in one arm–brain time; rapid recovery without accumulation; water-soluble and stable; painless on injection; minimal cardiovascular and respiratory depression; no excitatory effects, histamine release or allergy; analgesic; antiemetic; non-toxic; cheap; safe if injected intra-arterially.

## Comparison

| | **Propofol** | **Thiopental** | **Ketamine** | **Etomidate** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class | Phenol (2,6-diisopropylphenol) | Barbiturate (thiobarbiturate) | Phencyclidine derivative | Imidazole ester |
| Mechanism | GABA~A~ potentiation | GABA~A~ potentiation | **NMDA antagonist** | GABA~A~ potentiation |
| Formulation | 1% or 2% lipid emulsion (soya oil, egg lecithin) | Sodium salt powder, pH ~10.5 | Racemic or S(+), aqueous | In lipid or propylene glycol |
| Adult induction dose | 1.5–2.5 mg/kg | 3–5 mg/kg | 1–2 mg/kg IV (5–10 mg/kg IM) | 0.3 mg/kg |
| Blood pressure | **↓↓** (vasodilation) | ↓ | **↑** (sympathomimetic) | **Stable** |
| Respiration | Apnoea common; ↓ airway reflexes (good for LMA) | Apnoea; laryngospasm | Airway reflexes largely preserved; bronchodilator | Apnoea |
| Other | Antiemetic; pain on injection; propofol infusion syndrome | Anticonvulsant; ↓ ICP; porphyria trigger; intra-arterial injection causes gangrene | **Analgesic**; emergence phenomena; ↑ secretions | **Adrenal suppression** (11β-hydroxylase inhibition); myoclonus; PONV |
| Elimination | High clearance (exceeds hepatic flow: extrahepatic metabolism) | Slow; zero-order at high doses; accumulates | Hepatic (norketamine is active) | Ester hydrolysis |

**Doses are adult starting points and must be reduced in the elderly, frail, hypovolaemic and those with cardiac disease; check the BNF and local guidance.**

## Clinical points

- **Offset after a single bolus** of propofol or thiopental is by **redistribution**, not metabolism.
- **Propofol** is the usual agent for day surgery and TIVA; **propofol infusion syndrome** (metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, hyperkalaemia, cardiac failure) is associated with high doses (> 4 mg/kg/h) for long periods, particularly in children and critical illness.
- **Ketamine** is useful in shocked patients (though a direct myocardial depressant in catecholamine-depleted patients), asthma, and analgesia at sub-anaesthetic doses; it causes hallucinations and dysphoria (reduced by benzodiazepines).
- **Thiopental** is still used in some units for RSI and status epilepticus; accidental intra-arterial injection → crystal formation and ischaemia (leave the cannula, give saline, heparin, vasodilators, consider sympathetic block).
- **Etomidate:** a single dose suppresses cortisol for up to about 24 hours; concern in sepsis.
