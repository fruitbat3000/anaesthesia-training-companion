## The fetal circulation

- Oxygenated blood leaves the **placenta** in the **umbilical vein** (PO~2~ only about 4 kPa, but saturation about 80% thanks to **fetal haemoglobin**, which has a **P50 of about 2.5 kPa** and does not bind 2,3-DPG well).
- **Ductus venosus:** about half of umbilical venous blood bypasses the liver to the IVC.
- **Foramen ovale:** the better-oxygenated IVC stream is directed across the atrial septum to the left atrium → left ventricle → **brain and coronary arteries**.
- **Ductus arteriosus:** most right ventricular output bypasses the high-resistance fluid-filled lungs from the pulmonary artery to the descending aorta.
- Deoxygenated blood returns to the placenta in the **two umbilical arteries**.
- Pulmonary vascular resistance is **high** and systemic (placental) resistance **low** before birth.

## The placenta

- Gas exchange by **diffusion**, aided by the double Bohr effect (fetal CO~2~ loss increases fetal affinity; maternal CO~2~ gain decreases maternal affinity) and the double Haldane effect.
- Transfer of drugs depends on lipid solubility, ionisation, protein binding and molecular size. Most anaesthetic drugs cross; **neuromuscular blockers (quaternary, ionised)** and heparin do not cross to any significant degree. **Ion trapping:** weak bases such as local anaesthetics become more ionised in the more acidic fetus, especially when it is acidotic.

## Changes at birth

1. **First breaths** expand the lungs; alveolar PO~2~ rises and PVR falls dramatically.
2. **Clamping the cord** removes the low-resistance placenta, so SVR rises.
3. Left atrial pressure now exceeds right, closing the **flap of the foramen ovale** (functional closure at once; anatomical closure over months; probe-patent in about a quarter of adults).
4. The **ductus arteriosus** constricts within hours to days in response to higher PO~2~ and falling prostaglandins (functional closure in 1–3 days). NSAIDs such as indometacin or ibuprofen promote closure; prostaglandin E infusions keep it open in duct-dependent congenital heart disease.
5. The **ductus venosus** closes over days.

**Transitional circulation:** hypoxia, acidosis, hypothermia or sepsis in the neonate can raise PVR again and reopen right-to-left shunting (persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn).
