## Measurement

- CO~2~ absorbs **infrared light** strongly at about **4.3 µm**. The sample chamber is compared with a reference; absorption is proportional to CO~2~ partial pressure.
- **Sidestream:** gas is drawn (about 50–200 mL/min) to the monitor. Slight delay; can also measure other gases; water traps needed; the sampled gas should be returned to the circuit or scavenged.
- **Mainstream:** the sensor sits in the airway; instant response but adds weight and dead space.
- Collision broadening by N~2~O causes small errors (corrected by modern monitors).

## The normal capnogram

- **Phase I:** inspiratory baseline (dead space gas, ~0).
- **Phase II:** rapid expiratory upstroke (mixing of dead space and alveolar gas).
- **Phase III:** alveolar plateau, slightly upsloping; **end-tidal** value at its end.
- **Phase 0 (or IV):** inspiratory downstroke.
- **α angle** (between II and III) increases with airway obstruction; **β angle** (between III and inspiration) increases with rebreathing.
- Normal P~a~CO~2~ − P~ET~CO~2~ gap: about **0.5–1 kPa**, widened by increased **alveolar dead space** (low cardiac output, PE, hypovolaemia, high PEEP, COPD).

## Abnormal traces

| Trace | Causes |
|---|---|
| **No trace / flat line** | Oesophageal intubation, disconnection, apnoea, total airway obstruction, cardiac arrest (very low), sampling line failure — **"no trace = wrong place"** until proven otherwise; a trace is also seen during effective CPR |
| **Sudden fall in ETCO~2~** | Massive PE (thrombus, air, fat), cardiac arrest, sudden severe hypotension, disconnection or leak |
| **Gradual fall** | Hyperventilation, hypothermia, falling cardiac output |
| **Gradual rise** | Hypoventilation, rising temperature, **malignant hyperthermia**, CO~2~ insufflation (laparoscopy), sepsis, tourniquet release |
| **Raised baseline (inspired CO~2~)** | **Rebreathing**: exhausted soda lime, faulty expiratory valve, inadequate FGF in a Mapleson system |
| **Sloping "shark fin" upstroke** | **Bronchospasm**, COPD, kinked or obstructed tube |
| **Cleft ("curare cleft") in plateau** | Spontaneous breathing efforts against the ventilator (returning neuromuscular function) |
| **Oscillations on plateau** | Cardiogenic oscillations |

## Capnography in practice

Mandatory for all general anaesthetics and for moderate and deep sedation, in recovery until the airway is removed, and for **all intubated patients** (in ICU, ED and transfer). In cardiac arrest it confirms tube placement, shows CPR quality and can signal return of spontaneous circulation (a sudden rise).
