## Receptor types

| Type | Response time | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Ligand-gated ion channels** | Milliseconds | Nicotinic ACh, GABA~A~, NMDA, 5-HT~3~, glycine |
| **G-protein coupled receptors** | Seconds | Adrenoceptors, muscarinic, opioid, dopamine; G~s~ (↑ cAMP), G~i~ (↓ cAMP), G~q~ (IP~3~/DAG, ↑ Ca^2+^) |
| **Enzyme-linked (tyrosine kinase)** | Minutes | Insulin, growth factors |
| **Intracellular / nuclear** | Hours | Steroids, thyroid hormones |

Other targets: **voltage-gated ion channels** (Na^+^: local anaesthetics; Ca^2+^: dihydropyridines; K^+^), **transporters/pumps** (Na^+^/K^+^-ATPase: digoxin; H^+^/K^+^-ATPase: PPIs; reuptake transporters: SSRIs), and **enzymes** (acetylcholinesterase, COX, phosphodiesterase, ACE).

## Dose–response relationships

- Plotting response against **log(dose)** gives a sigmoid curve with a linear central portion.
- **Affinity:** how strongly a drug binds (K~D~, the concentration occupying 50% of receptors; the affinity constant K~A~ = 1/K~D~).
- **Occupancy theory:** response ∝ fractional occupancy × intrinsic activity. Signal amplification through second messengers means a maximal response can occur with only a fraction of receptors occupied (**spare receptors**, as at the neuromuscular junction).
- **Efficacy (intrinsic activity):** the ability to produce a response once bound: the **maximum** of the curve.
- **Potency:** the dose needed for a given effect, reflected by **EC~50~ / ED~50~** (position along the x-axis). Potency is not the same as efficacy.
- **Full agonist:** maximal efficacy. **Partial agonist:** submaximal efficacy even at full occupancy, and can antagonise a full agonist (buprenorphine at the μ receptor). **Inverse agonist:** reduces constitutive receptor activity (e.g. some benzodiazepine-site ligands).
- **Therapeutic index:** LD~50~/ED~50~ (or TD~50~/ED~50~).

## Antagonists

- **Competitive reversible:** binds the same site; can be overcome by more agonist → **parallel rightward shift** of the log dose–response curve, same maximum (e.g. non-depolarising NMBs, naloxone, atropine).
- **Irreversible (or non-competitive):** reduces the **maximum** response; the curve flattens (e.g. phenoxybenzamine at α receptors).
- **Allosteric modulators** bind elsewhere and change the receptor's response (benzodiazepines, propofol and barbiturates enhance GABA~A~ receptor function).
- **Physiological antagonism:** opposing effects via different receptors (adrenaline versus histamine in anaphylaxis). **Chemical antagonism:** direct binding (protamine–heparin, sugammadex–rocuronium).

## Receptor regulation and tachyphylaxis

- **Down-regulation / desensitisation** with prolonged agonist exposure (β receptors in heart failure and with β-agonist therapy). **Up-regulation** with antagonists or denervation (β-blocker withdrawal → rebound tachycardia; extrajunctional ACh receptors).
- **Tachyphylaxis:** a rapidly diminishing response to repeated doses, e.g. **ephedrine** (depletion of noradrenaline stores), GTN (nitrate tolerance), desmopressin. **Tolerance** develops more slowly (opioids).

## Enzymes as targets

Michaelis–Menten kinetics describe the rate of enzyme action. Drugs may inhibit **competitively** (edrophonium, neostigmine: reversible carbamylation), **irreversibly** (organophosphates, aspirin acetylating COX in platelets for their lifespan), or allosterically.
