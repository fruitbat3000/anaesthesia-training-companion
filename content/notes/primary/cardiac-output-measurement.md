## Fick principle

- The amount of a substance taken up by an organ per unit time equals the arterial–venous difference × blood flow.
- **CO = VO~2~ / (CaO~2~ − CvO~2~).** Example: 250 mL/min / (200 − 150 mL/L) = 5 L/min.
- Requires mixed venous blood (pulmonary artery) and measurement of oxygen consumption; the reference method in a steady state. Indirect Fick using CO~2~ (partial rebreathing) is also used.

## Indicator dilution (Stewart–Hamilton)

- A known amount of indicator is injected and its concentration measured downstream over time: **CO = amount of indicator / area under the concentration–time curve** (∫C dt). Recirculation must be excluded (extrapolate the curve).
- **Thermodilution (pulmonary artery catheter):** cold saline injected into the right atrium; a **thermistor** at the catheter tip measures the temperature change in the pulmonary artery. **A smaller area under the curve = a higher cardiac output.** Errors: tricuspid regurgitation, intracardiac shunts, injection technique. Continuous versions use a heating filament.
- **Transpulmonary thermodilution** (e.g. PiCCO): central venous injection, detection in a femoral arterial line; calibrates pulse contour analysis and gives extravascular lung water and global end-diastolic volume.
- **Lithium dilution** (LiDCO): lithium injected, detected by an ion-selective arterial electrode; calibrates pulse power analysis. Not with lithium therapy or some neuromuscular blockers (interference).

## Arterial waveform analysis

Stroke volume is estimated from the shape and area of the arterial pressure waveform (pulse contour or pulse power), either calibrated (PiCCO, LiDCO) or uncalibrated (population-based algorithms, e.g. FloTrac). Less accurate with arrhythmias, aortic regurgitation, damping problems, and rapid changes in vascular tone. Also give pulse pressure and stroke volume variation.

## Doppler and echocardiography

- **Oesophageal Doppler:** a probe in the distal oesophagus measures descending aortic blood velocity; **stroke distance** (velocity–time integral) × aortic cross-sectional area (from a nomogram) = stroke volume (only about 70% of cardiac output goes to the descending aorta; the algorithm corrects). **Corrected flow time** relates to preload; peak velocity relates to contractility. Widely used for goal-directed fluid therapy.
- **Echocardiography (TTE, TOE):** stroke volume from the LVOT velocity–time integral × LVOT area; also shows function, filling and valves.

## Other methods

Thoracic electrical bioimpedance and bioreactance (changes in thoracic impedance with blood flow), and CO~2~ rebreathing (indirect Fick).
