## Safety first

- **Consent** (benefits, risks: failure, nerve injury, LAST, block-specific complications), **"Stop Before You Block"** immediately before needle insertion (re-confirm the site and side), aseptic technique, monitoring, IV access, resuscitation drugs and lipid emulsion available.
- **Ultrasound guidance** (with or without nerve stimulation): see the needle tip, avoid intraneural injection (stop if high injection pressure, pain or paraesthesia), hydrodissect, inject incrementally with aspiration.
- Document the block, the drug, dose and volume, and give the patient instructions on protecting the numb limb.

## Stage 1 blocks

| Block | Target and view | Indications | Key complications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interscalene** | C5–6 roots between scalenus anterior and medius ("traffic lights") | Shoulder and proximal humerus surgery | **Ipsilateral phrenic nerve palsy** (avoid in poor respiratory reserve), Horner's, hoarseness, vertebral artery/epidural/intrathecal injection, pneumothorax (rare) |
| **Axillary** | Median, ulnar and radial nerves around the axillary artery; musculocutaneous in coracobrachialis | Elbow, forearm and hand surgery | Vascular puncture, LAST; tourniquet pain |
| **Distal (forearm/elbow/wrist)** | Individual median, ulnar and radial nerves | Hand surgery, rescue of incomplete proximal blocks | |
| **Femoral** | Lateral to the femoral artery, deep to fascia iliaca | Femoral shaft and knee surgery, hip fracture analgesia | Quadriceps weakness (falls risk) |
| **Fascia iliaca** | Deep to fascia iliaca (infra- or supra-inguinal) | **Hip fracture** analgesia (recommended in UK guidance) | Volume-dependent spread; LAST with large volumes |

## Complications of peripheral nerve blocks

- **Nerve injury:** transient symptoms are common (a few per cent); persistent injury is rare. Risk factors: intraneural injection, pre-existing neuropathy, surgical and positioning factors, tourniquets.
- **LAST**, vascular puncture and haematoma, infection, pneumothorax (supraclavicular), wrong-site block, falls (lower limb blocks).
- **Rebound pain** when a single-shot block wears off: plan multimodal analgesia to start before this.
