## Oxygen delivery devices

| Device | Performance | Approximate FiO~2~ |
|---|---|---|
| Nasal cannulae (1–4 L/min) | Variable | ~0.24–0.4 (roughly +0.03–0.04 per L/min) |
| Simple face mask (Hudson) (5–10 L/min) | Variable | ~0.3–0.6 |
| Non-rebreathe mask with reservoir (15 L/min) | Variable | ~0.6–0.85 |
| **Venturi masks** | **Fixed**: entrain a fixed ratio of air | 0.24, 0.28, 0.35, 0.40, 0.60 (colour-coded) |
| **High-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO)** | Fixed (flow exceeds inspiratory demand) | Up to ~1.0 at 30–70 L/min |

- **Variable performance** devices deliver an FiO~2~ that depends on the patient's inspiratory flow and pattern (more air is entrained when breathing fast and deep).
- **Fixed performance** requires the total gas flow to exceed the patient's peak inspiratory flow (about 30 L/min at rest, more when distressed).
- **HFNO** delivers warmed, humidified gas: washes out anatomical dead space, provides a little PEEP (a few cmH~2~O with the mouth closed), improves comfort, and is used for pre-oxygenation and apnoeic oxygenation (THRIVE) and in respiratory failure.

## Hazards of oxygen

- **Absorption atelectasis:** high FiO~2~ washes out nitrogen, and alveoli behind closed or narrow airways collapse as their oxygen is absorbed. This is why FiO~2~ is often reduced below 1.0 after pre-oxygenation and for emergence where safe.
- **Oxygen-induced hypercapnia** in some patients with chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure: aim for SpO~2~ 88–92% (see [control of breathing](#notes/resp-control)).
- **Toxicity:** pulmonary (prolonged high FiO~2~), CNS (hyperbaric), retinopathy of prematurity; fire risk.

## Suction

- A vacuum source (pipeline at about −53 kPa or more negative, or a portable pump) with a **reservoir jar**, **bacterial filter** and **overflow protection** (a float valve), and a regulator to set negative pressure (lower settings for airway suction in children and for chest drains).
- Flow depends on the tubing and catheter (Hagen–Poiseuille): **wide-bore Yankauer** suckers for vomit; fine catheters for tracheal suction (catheter diameter ideally no more than half the tube's internal diameter to avoid atelectasis and hypoxia).

## Scavenging

- Reduces staff exposure to waste anaesthetic gases (UK workplace exposure limits (8-hour time-weighted averages): N~2~O **100 ppm**, isoflurane 50 ppm, halothane 10 ppm, enflurane 50 ppm).
- **Active (typical UK AGSS):** the APL valve and ventilator exhaust connect via 30 mm connectors (distinct from 22/15 mm breathing system connectors) to a receiving system with a reservoir and **air breaks** (open to atmosphere) so that neither positive nor negative pressure is transmitted to the patient. A fan draws gas out of the building at high flow (e.g. 75–130 L/min).
- **Passive:** gas vented outside by the patient's own expiratory effort (limited by tube length and wind).
- **Other measures:** theatre air changes (about 15–20 per hour), low-flow anaesthesia, avoiding N~2~O, careful filling of vaporisers, and cuffed tubes/LMAs. Scavenging does not remove the environmental impact: waste gases still reach the atmosphere.
