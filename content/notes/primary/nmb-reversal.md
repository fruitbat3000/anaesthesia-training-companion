## Anticholinesterases

- **Neostigmine** (a quaternary carbamate) inhibits acetylcholinesterase, raising ACh at the junction to outcompete the blocker. Typical adult dose about 50 µg/kg up to 2.5–5 mg (check local guidance), with an **antimuscarinic** (glycopyrronium, or atropine) to block the muscarinic effects.
- **Ceiling effect:** once acetylcholinesterase is fully inhibited, more neostigmine adds nothing. It **cannot reverse deep block** and should be given only when there is evidence of recovery (e.g. **at least 2 and ideally 4 twitches** on TOF). Peak effect takes about **10 minutes**.
- **Muscarinic effects:** bradycardia, bronchospasm, secretions, gut and bladder stimulation, PONV. Excessive doses may cause a paradoxical depolarising-type weakness.
- **Prolongs suxamethonium** (inhibits plasma cholinesterase too).
- Other anticholinesterases: **edrophonium** (short-acting, reversible; formerly used to diagnose myasthenia), **pyridostigmine** (oral, myasthenia gravis), **physostigmine** (tertiary; crosses BBB; central anticholinergic syndrome), and irreversible **organophosphates** (nerve agents, insecticides → cholinergic crisis; treat with atropine, oximes, benzodiazepines).

## Sugammadex

- A modified **γ-cyclodextrin** that **encapsulates** aminosteroid molecules (rocuronium > vecuronium; not benzylisoquinoliniums) in plasma in a 1:1 complex, creating a concentration gradient that draws drug away from the junction. Excreted renally as the complex.
- Dose by depth of block (per product information): lower dose for moderate block, higher for deep block, and a high dose for **immediate reversal** shortly after rocuronium in a "can't intubate" scenario (but reversal of paralysis does not solve an obstructed airway).
- No muscarinic effects, so no anticholinergic needed. Issues: hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, bradycardia (rare), reduced efficacy of **hormonal contraceptives** (advise extra contraception for 7 days), interference with some coagulation assays, not recommended in severe renal impairment. Re-blocking with rocuronium soon afterwards requires higher doses or a benzylisoquinolinium.

## Adequate recovery

- **Quantitative monitoring** is recommended whenever neuromuscular blockers are used (Association of Anaesthetists 2021): extubate only with a **TOF ratio ≥ 0.9** at the adductor pollicis.
- Clinical tests (head lift, grip) and tactile or visual assessment of fade are unreliable: fade is rarely detectable once the TOF ratio exceeds about 0.4.
- **Residual block** (TOF < 0.9) impairs pharyngeal function and the hypoxic ventilatory response, increases aspiration risk and postoperative pulmonary complications, and is distressing for the patient.
