## Blood groups

| Group | Antigens on red cells | Antibodies in plasma | UK frequency (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| O | None | Anti-A and anti-B | ~45% |
| A | A | Anti-B | ~40% |
| B | B | Anti-A | ~10% |
| AB | A and B | None | ~5% |

- ABO antibodies are **naturally occurring IgM**, which fix complement: ABO-incompatible transfusion causes **intravascular haemolysis** and can be fatal.
- **Group O** red cells are the universal donor for red cells (O RhD-negative in emergencies for women of childbearing potential and unknown group); **AB plasma** is the universal plasma donor.
- **Rhesus D:** about 85% RhD-positive. Anti-D is IgG and develops only after exposure (transfusion or pregnancy); it can cross the placenta → **haemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn**. Anti-D immunoglobulin prevents sensitisation.

## Compatibility testing

- **Group and screen:** determines ABO and RhD group and screens the plasma for irregular antibodies.
- **Crossmatch:** serological (patient plasma against donor cells) or **electronic issue** when the screen is negative and valid samples exist. Emergency O-negative is available immediately; group-specific blood within about 10–15 minutes.
- **Two independent samples** (or a second check) are usually needed before group-specific blood is issued, to prevent wrong-blood-in-tube errors, which are a major cause of ABO incompatibility.

## Components

| Component | Storage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Red cells | 2–6°C, up to 35 days | Hct ~0.5–0.7 in SAG-M; one unit raises Hb by about 10 g/L in an adult |
| Platelets | 20–24°C with agitation, up to 7 days | Highest risk of bacterial contamination |
| Fresh frozen plasma | −25°C or below | Contains all coagulation factors; 12–15 mL/kg |
| Cryoprecipitate | Frozen | Rich in fibrinogen, VIII, vWF, XIII |

Changes in stored red cells: rising K^+^, falling pH and 2,3-DPG, microaggregates, cold. Citrate binds calcium.

## Transfusion reactions and complications

- **Acute haemolytic (ABO incompatible):** fever, hypotension, loin pain, haemoglobinuria, DIC; under anaesthesia may present only as hypotension, tachycardia and bleeding. **Stop the transfusion**, keep the line, resuscitate, check the patient ID against the unit, inform the lab, send samples.
- **Febrile non-haemolytic reactions** (cytokines), **allergic reactions** (urticaria) up to **anaphylaxis** (IgA-deficient patients with anti-IgA).
- **TACO** (transfusion-associated circulatory overload): the commonest serious cause of transfusion-related morbidity; elderly and cardiac patients; hypertension, pulmonary oedema.
- **TRALI:** acute lung injury within 6 hours, from donor antibodies against recipient leucocytes.
- **Bacterial contamination** (platelets), **delayed haemolytic reaction** (days to weeks), **transfusion-associated graft-versus-host disease** (prevented by irradiation in at-risk patients).
- **Massive transfusion:** hypothermia, hypocalcaemia, hyperkalaemia, dilutional coagulopathy, acidosis.
- Serious hazards are reported to the UK haemovigilance scheme (SHOT) and MHRA (SABRE).
