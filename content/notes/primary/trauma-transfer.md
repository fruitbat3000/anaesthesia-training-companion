## Primary survey (<C>ABCDE)

**Catastrophic haemorrhage** control (tourniquets, pressure, haemostatic dressings) → **Airway with cervical spine protection** → **Breathing** (tension pneumothorax, open chest wound, flail, haemothorax) → **Circulation** (haemorrhage control, access including **intraosseous**, blood-based resuscitation, **tranexamic acid within 3 hours**, permissive hypotension in penetrating trauma without head injury) → **Disability** (GCS, pupils, glucose) → **Exposure** (avoid **hypothermia**, part of the lethal triad with acidosis and coagulopathy). Then the secondary survey (head-to-toe, history "AMPLE").

**Imaging:** whole-body CT for major trauma in adults; FAST ultrasound as an adjunct; plain films in the resuscitation room.

## Airway in trauma

- RSI with **manual in-line stabilisation** (remove the front of the collar); video laryngoscopy; expect blood and vomit; plan for failed intubation.
- **Indications for intubation** after head injury include GCS ≤ 8, loss of protective reflexes, inadequate ventilation or oxygenation, seizures, significant facial injury, agitation needing sedation for imaging, and before transfer if deterioration is likely.

## Head injury: preventing secondary brain injury

**Glasgow Coma Scale** (eyes 4, verbal 5, motor 6). Targets: **SpO~2~ ≥ 94% / PaO~2~ > 13 kPa**, **PaCO~2~ 4.5–5.0 kPa**, **MAP ≥ 80 mmHg** (or as locally advised to keep CPP ≥ 60), normoglycaemia, normothermia, head-up 30° with the neck neutral and ties loose, adequate sedation, treat seizures; osmotherapy (mannitol or hypertonic saline) for signs of raised ICP while arranging neurosurgery.

## Transfer

- **Indications and planning:** the benefits must outweigh the risks. Stabilise before transfer ("secure the airway before you move").
- **Personnel:** a doctor with airway and critical care skills (at least a trained anaesthetist or intensivist for ventilated patients) plus an assistant.
- **Equipment and monitoring:** as in theatre, including **waveform capnography**, invasive BP for unstable patients, a transport ventilator, enough oxygen (calculate with a 50% reserve), infusion pumps with batteries, a drugs bag including controlled drugs (documented), and communication equipment.
- **Documentation and communication:** a transfer record, handover to the receiving team (SBAR), consent where possible, and informing relatives.
- **Hazards:** equipment failure, limited space and light, acceleration/deceleration effects on haemodynamics, vibration, noise, cold, and the agitated or aggressive patient (sedation, restraint within the law). Intra-hospital transfers (to CT, theatres, ICU) carry the same risks.
