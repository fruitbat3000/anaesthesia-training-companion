## MAC

- **Minimum alveolar concentration:** the alveolar concentration at **steady state** (1 atmosphere) that prevents movement in response to a standard surgical incision in **50%** of subjects. MAC-awake (about 0.3–0.5 MAC) and MAC-BAR (block of adrenergic response, about 1.5 MAC) are related concepts. MACs of different agents are roughly **additive**.
- **Increased by:** infants (peak at about 1–6 months), hyperthermia, hypernatraemia, chronic alcohol use, acute amphetamine or cocaine use, red hair (MC1R variants; modest).
- **Decreased by:** age (about 6% per decade after 40), hypothermia, pregnancy, opioids, benzodiazepines, α~2~-agonists, ketamine, N~2~O, acute alcohol intoxication, hypotension, hypoxia, anaemia, hyponatraemia, lithium.
- **Not changed by:** sex, duration of anaesthesia, hyper- or hypothyroidism (in practice), hyperkalaemia, weight.

## Partition coefficients

- **Blood:gas** (solubility in blood) determines the **speed of onset and offset**: the lower it is, the faster the alveolar partial pressure (and so the brain) rises towards the inspired.
- **Oil:gas** correlates with **potency** (Meyer–Overton: MAC × oil:gas ≈ constant).

## Uptake: what makes F~A~/F~I~ rise fast

- **Low blood solubility** (desflurane, N~2~O, sevoflurane rise fast; halothane slow).
- **High alveolar ventilation** (more delivery; especially important for soluble agents).
- **Low cardiac output** (less removal from the alveoli → faster rise in F~A~; so shocked patients may be overdosed quickly). High cardiac output slows induction.
- **High inspired concentration** (overpressure) and **high fresh gas flow** (reduces breathing system time constant).
- **Concentration effect:** at high inspired concentrations (only possible with N~2~O), uptake itself concentrates the remaining gas and draws in more gas.
- **Second gas effect:** rapid uptake of a large volume of N~2~O concentrates a second gas (volatile or O~2~) in the alveoli.
- Right-to-left shunt slows inhalational induction (more so with insoluble agents); left-to-right shunt has little effect.

## Elimination and emergence

- Largely by exhalation, the reverse of uptake; recovery is faster with insoluble agents and after short anaesthetics (tissues less saturated).
- Metabolism: halothane ~20%, sevoflurane ~3–5% (fluoride; compound A with CO~2~ absorbents), isoflurane ~0.2%, desflurane ~0.02%.
- **Diffusion hypoxia:** at the end of N~2~O anaesthesia, the rapid outflow of N~2~O into the alveoli dilutes oxygen: give 100% O~2~ for several minutes.
