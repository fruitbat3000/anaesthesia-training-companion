## The basics

**MAP ≈ CO × SVR** (strictly, MAP − CVP = CO × SVR). MAP ≈ diastolic + ⅓ pulse pressure at normal heart rates.

## Short-term: the baroreceptor reflex (seconds)

- **High-pressure baroreceptors** in the **carotid sinus** (glossopharyngeal nerve, IX) and **aortic arch** (vagus, X) fire in proportion to stretch.
- Signals go to the **nucleus tractus solitarius**; a fall in pressure reduces firing → less vagal (parasympathetic) output and more sympathetic output → tachycardia, increased contractility, vasoconstriction and venoconstriction.
- The reflex **resets** within days in chronic hypertension. It is blunted by volatile agents, propofol, age, diabetic autonomic neuropathy and β-blockers.
- **Low-pressure receptors** in the atria and great veins respond to volume: stretch releases **ANP** and reduces ADH and sympathetic output. The **Bainbridge reflex** is a rise in heart rate with atrial stretch.

## Medium and long term (minutes to days)

- **Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system:** renal hypoperfusion, sympathetic β~1~ stimulation and low distal tubular sodium release renin → angiotensin II (vasoconstriction, thirst, ADH and aldosterone release) → sodium and water retention.
- **ADH (vasopressin):** released for raised osmolality and (at larger volume losses) hypovolaemia; V~1~ receptors cause vasoconstriction, V~2~ receptors retain water.
- **Pressure natriuresis:** the kidney's long-term set point for pressure.

## The Valsalva manoeuvre (forced expiration against a closed glottis, ~40 mmHg)

| Phase | Blood pressure | Heart rate |
|---|---|---|
| I (onset) | Brief **rise** (intrathoracic pressure squeezes blood out of the aorta) | Reflex slight fall |
| II (strain) | **Falls** as venous return drops, then partly recovers with sympathetic vasoconstriction | **Rises** |
| III (release) | Brief further fall (sudden drop in intrathoracic pressure) | — |
| IV (recovery) | **Overshoot** as restored cardiac output meets constricted vessels | Reflex **bradycardia** |

A **square-wave response** (BP rises and stays up with no overshoot or heart rate change) occurs in heart failure; loss of the phase IV overshoot and bradycardia indicates **autonomic neuropathy**.

## Response to haemorrhage

- Baroreflex tachycardia and vasoconstriction (skin, gut, kidney) preserve flow to the brain and heart; venoconstriction mobilises blood from venous reservoirs.
- **Transcapillary refill:** lower capillary hydrostatic pressure draws interstitial fluid into the vessels (can restore about 0.5–1 L over hours).
- Hormonal: catecholamines, ADH, RAAS, cortisol. Erythropoiesis restores red cells over weeks.
- Blood pressure is often maintained until about 30% of blood volume is lost in healthy young patients, so a normal BP does not exclude major haemorrhage. Children and fit young adults may decompensate abruptly; β-blockers mask tachycardia.
