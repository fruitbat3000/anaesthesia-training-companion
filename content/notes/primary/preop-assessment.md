## Structure

1. **Presenting condition and planned surgery** (urgency, extent, position, expected blood loss).
2. **Anaesthetic history:** previous anaesthetics and problems (difficult airway, PONV, awareness, suxamethonium apnoea), **family history** (MH, cholinesterase deficiency).
3. **Medical history by system**, focusing on **functional capacity** (can the patient climb two flights of stairs? — roughly ≥ 4 METs), cardiorespiratory disease, diabetes, renal, liver, neurological, rheumatoid disease, OSA (STOP-BANG), reflux.
4. **Drugs and allergies** (what reaction?); anticoagulants and antiplatelets; alcohol, smoking, recreational drugs.
5. **Examination:** cardiovascular, respiratory, **airway**, and relevant neurology; sites for cannulation and regional blocks.
6. **Investigations** as indicated, not routine (NICE NG45: by ASA grade and surgical severity).
7. **Plan and discussion:** technique, risks and alternatives, fasting, medication instructions, postoperative care level (ward, enhanced care, critical care). Document it and escalate concerns.

## Classifications

- **ASA physical status:** I healthy; II mild systemic disease; III severe systemic disease; IV severe systemic disease that is a constant threat to life; V moribund, not expected to survive without surgery; VI brain-dead organ donor; "E" for emergency. Simple and widely used, but subjective and not a full risk score (updated statement 2025).
- **NCEPOD classification of intervention:** **immediate** (life- or limb-saving, within minutes), **urgent** (acute onset or deterioration, within hours), **expedited** (stable but needs early intervention, within days), **elective** (planned).

## Risk stratification

- **Cardiac:** the **Revised Cardiac Risk Index** (high-risk surgery, ischaemic heart disease, heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, insulin-treated diabetes, creatinine > 177 µmol/L); functional capacity; BNP/NT-proBNP in some pathways (ESC 2022).
- **General mortality:** P-POSSUM, **SORT**, NELA risk score (emergency laparotomy), and frailty scores.
- **Pulmonary:** ARISCAT and similar; OSA screening.
- Higher-risk patients may need CPET, echocardiography or specialist review; in Stage 1 the key skill is **recognising risk and telling a senior** (EPA 1): severe comorbidity (ASA III+), previous anaesthetic problems, and a known or predicted difficult airway.

## Emergency versus elective

Emergency patients may be hypovolaemic, septic, in pain, not fasted and with less time to optimise. Resuscitate and optimise in parallel with preparation; do not delay life-saving surgery for tests that will not change management.

## Fasting and aspiration risk

Standard adult fasting: 6 h for food, 2 h for clear fluids (see local policy, which may allow clear fluids until called). Assess factors that increase aspiration risk (see RSI).
