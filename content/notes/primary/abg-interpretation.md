## A systematic approach

1. **Patient and context:** FiO~2~, ventilation, clinical picture. Is the sample arterial?
2. **Oxygenation:** PaO~2~ relative to FiO~2~ (expected PaO~2~ in kPa ≈ FiO~2~ (%) − 10 in healthy lungs; **P/F ratio** in kPa: < 40 mild, < 26.7 moderate, < 13.3 severe ARDS-range impairment); calculate the **A–a gradient** if needed.
3. **pH:** acidaemia (< 7.35) or alkalaemia (> 7.45)?
4. **Primary process:** PaCO~2~ (respiratory) versus HCO~3~^−^ / base excess (metabolic), in the direction that explains the pH.
5. **Compensation:** appropriate? (see acid–base note; compensation never overcorrects).
6. **Anion gap** (corrected for albumin) in metabolic acidosis; **delta ratio** (Δ anion gap / Δ HCO~3~^−^) to look for a mixed picture.
7. **Other values:** lactate, glucose, Na^+^, K^+^, Cl^−^, ionised Ca^2+^, Hb, COHb, MetHb.
8. **Synthesis:** a diagnosis, with causes and actions.

## Worked patterns

| Gas | Interpretation | Typical causes |
|---|---|---|
| pH 7.21, PaCO~2~ 8.9 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 26, BE +1 | **Acute respiratory acidosis** (no metabolic compensation yet) | Opioid overdose, residual neuromuscular block, exhaustion |
| pH 7.36, PaCO~2~ 8.0 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 33, BE +8 | **Chronic (compensated) respiratory acidosis** | COPD with CO~2~ retention |
| pH 7.12, PaCO~2~ 3.2 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 8, BE −19, lactate 9 | **Raised anion gap metabolic acidosis** with respiratory compensation | Septic or haemorrhagic shock, ischaemic bowel |
| pH 7.30, PaCO~2~ 4.3 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 16, Cl^−^ 116, lactate 1.1 | **Normal anion gap (hyperchloraemic) acidosis** | Large volumes of 0.9% saline; diarrhoea |
| pH 7.52, PaCO~2~ 6.2 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 37, K^+^ 2.9 | **Metabolic alkalosis** with respiratory compensation | Vomiting / nasogastric losses, diuretics |
| pH 7.51, PaCO~2~ 3.4 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 20 | **Acute respiratory alkalosis** | Pain, anxiety, over-ventilation, early PE, pregnancy (chronic) |
| pH 7.25, PaCO~2~ 6.8 kPa, HCO~3~^−^ 22 | **Mixed respiratory and metabolic acidosis** (HCO~3~^−^ lower than expected for acute respiratory acidosis) | Cardiac arrest, severe pneumonia with shock |

## Pitfalls

- **Venous samples:** pH about 0.03–0.04 lower and PCO~2~ about 0.8 kPa higher than arterial; PO~2~ is not interpretable.
- **Air bubbles** raise PO~2~ towards ~20 kPa and lower PCO~2~; **delay** (cells keep metabolising) lowers PO~2~ and raises PCO~2~; **excess heparin** dilutes and lowers PCO~2~.
- **Temperature:** analysers measure at 37°C (alpha-stat).

> [!exam] In the exam
> - **AKT:** interpret gases quickly and name a likely cause; calculate the anion gap and P/F ratio.
> - **CASE:** you are handed a gas from a deteriorating patient in recovery: talk through it aloud, then say what you will do.
