## Mechanism

- Two acetylcholine molecules joined together; a **depolarising agonist** at nicotinic receptors. It opens the channel, causing **fasciculations**, then the end-plate stays depolarised and perijunctional Na^+^ channels stay **inactivated**, so the muscle cannot respond: **phase I block**.
- Onset about **30–60 s** at 1–1.5 mg/kg; offset in about **5–10 minutes** by diffusion away from the junction and hydrolysis by **plasma cholinesterase** (butyrylcholinesterase) in plasma (there is little at the junction).

## Phase I versus phase II block

| | Phase I (depolarising) | Phase II |
|---|---|---|
| When | Normal dose | Large or repeated doses, infusions, abnormal cholinesterase |
| Train-of-four | Reduced equally, **no fade** | **Fade** |
| Post-tetanic facilitation | Absent | Present |
| Anticholinesterase | **Worsens** block | May partly reverse (unpredictable) |

## Side effects

- **Hyperkalaemia:** normally a rise of about **0.5 mmol/L**; life-threatening rises with **upregulated extrajunctional receptors**: burns, denervation (spinal cord injury, stroke), prolonged immobility, muscular dystrophies, and severe crush or abdominal sepsis. Risk begins about 24–48 h after injury.
- **Bradycardia** (muscarinic stimulation of the SA node), especially after a second dose and in children; tachycardia can also occur.
- **Myalgia** (young adults, early mobilisation), **raised intraocular, intragastric and intracranial pressure** (small, transient), **masseter spasm**.
- **Malignant hyperthermia trigger.**
- **Anaphylaxis** (one of the commoner NMB triggers in NAP6).

## Suxamethonium apnoea

- Caused by reduced plasma cholinesterase **activity**:
  - **Inherited:** variant genes (e.g. atypical dibucaine-resistant, fluoride-resistant, silent). Homozygotes for the atypical gene may be paralysed for hours; silent homozygotes longer.
  - **Acquired:** pregnancy, liver disease, renal failure, malnutrition, cardiopulmonary bypass, plasmapheresis, drugs (**neostigmine**, echothiophate, cyclophosphamide, oral contraceptives, ester local anaesthetics competing, esmolol).
- **Dibucaine number:** the % inhibition of the enzyme by dibucaine; normal about **80**, atypical homozygote about **20**, heterozygote about **40–60**.
- **Management:** keep the patient **anaesthetised and ventilated** (awareness is a real risk) until neuromuscular function has recovered on a nerve stimulator; FFP would provide enzyme but is rarely needed. Test the patient and family, warn them, and document ("alert card").

## Contraindications

Known MH susceptibility; history of suxamethonium apnoea; hyperkalaemia or risk of hyperkalaemic response (see above); some myopathies; allergy.
