## The high-, intermediate- and low-pressure systems

1. **High pressure:** cylinders → yoke → Bourdon gauge → pressure regulator (down to ~4 bar).
2. **Intermediate pressure:** pipelines (4 bar) and regulated cylinder gas → non-return valves → **oxygen failure warning device** → flow control valves. The **oxygen flush** (35–75 L/min) draws from here, bypassing flowmeters and vaporisers.
3. **Low pressure:** flowmeters → back bar (vaporisers) → **pressure relief valve** (about 30–40 kPa, protecting the machine) → **common gas outlet**.

## Flowmeters

- **Variable-orifice (Thorpe tube) rotameters:** a bobbin rises in a tapered glass tube; read at the **top of a bobbin** or the **middle of a ball**. Low flows are laminar (viscosity-dependent), high flows turbulent (density-dependent), so each tube is calibrated for one gas. Errors: static electricity, dirt, tubes not vertical, bobbin sticking.
- **Oxygen is added last** (downstream) on the back bar, so a crack in an upstream tube leaks other gases rather than oxygen.
- Many modern machines have **electronic flow control** and virtual flowmeters, with a backup flowmeter.

## Safety features

- **Oxygen failure alarm:** audible (at least 7 s), powered by the oxygen supply itself, triggered when oxygen pressure falls below about 2 bar, and shuts off other gases (so air or N~2~O cannot flow without oxygen).
- **Hypoxic guard / anti-hypoxic device:** mechanically or electronically links N~2~O and O~2~ flows so the mixture cannot fall below **25% oxygen**.
- **Minimum oxygen flow** (basal flow) when the machine is on.
- Colour-coded, touch-coded oxygen control knob (larger, fluted, on the left in the UK).
- Vaporiser interlock; keyed fillers; pin index and NIST; non-return valves.
- **Monitoring:** inspired oxygen with alarm, airway pressure (disconnection and high pressure), capnography, vapour analysis.
- **Scavenging**, **APL valve**, and a **self-inflating bag and oxygen cylinder** as backup.

## The machine check

Follow the Association of Anaesthetists' current guidance for checking anaesthetic equipment: a full check at the start of each session (power, gas supplies and pipelines with **tug test**, oxygen analyser, flowmeters, vaporisers, breathing system leak and function, ventilator, scavenging, suction, monitors, airway equipment and alternative means of ventilation), and a shorter check between cases. Record it in a logbook. Take particular care with the **two-bag test** and after changing breathing systems or vaporisers.
