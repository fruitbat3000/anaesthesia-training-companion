## The neuromuscular junction

1. The motor nerve action potential opens **voltage-gated Ca^2+^ channels** in the terminal.
2. Ca^2+^ entry triggers **exocytosis of acetylcholine** (quanta of about 5,000–10,000 molecules per vesicle) through **SNARE proteins** that fuse the vesicle with the membrane; **botulinum toxin** cleaves SNARE proteins and prevents release.
3. ACh crosses the cleft and binds the **nicotinic receptor** (a ligand-gated ion channel of five subunits: **2α, β, δ, ε** in adults; γ replaces ε in fetal and extrajunctional receptors). **Both α subunits** must be occupied to open the channel.
4. Na^+^ in and K^+^ out produce the **end-plate potential**; if it reaches threshold, a muscle action potential spreads.
5. **Acetylcholinesterase** in the cleft hydrolyses ACh within about 1 ms.

- **Large safety margin:** about 70–80% of receptors must be blocked before twitch height falls, and over 90% for complete block.
- **Prejunctional nicotinic receptors** (α3β2) mobilise ACh during high-frequency stimulation; blocking them by non-depolarising drugs is thought to cause **fade**.
- **Upregulation** of extrajunctional (immature, fetal-type and α7) receptors after denervation, burns, prolonged immobility or upper motor neurone injury → exaggerated K^+^ release with suxamethonium and resistance to non-depolarising drugs.

## Skeletal muscle excitation–contraction coupling

- The action potential travels down the **T-tubules**; the **dihydropyridine receptor** (voltage sensor) is **mechanically coupled** to the **ryanodine receptor (RyR1)** on the sarcoplasmic reticulum, releasing Ca^2+^ (no calcium-induced release needed, unlike the heart).
- Ca^2+^ binds **troponin C** → tropomyosin moves → cross-bridge cycling. ATP is needed both to detach myosin and to pump Ca^2+^ back (SERCA).
- **Malignant hyperthermia:** abnormal RyR1 (or the dihydropyridine receptor, CACNA1S) → uncontrolled Ca^2+^ release with volatile agents or suxamethonium.
- **Motor unit:** one motor neurone and all the fibres it supplies (few fibres for fine control, e.g. extraocular muscles; thousands in large limb muscles). Force is graded by recruitment and firing rate (summation, tetanus).

## Smooth muscle

- No troponin; Ca^2+^ binds **calmodulin**, which activates **myosin light-chain kinase**. Relaxation by myosin light-chain phosphatase.
- Ca^2+^ comes from the extracellular space and the SR (IP~3~ receptors). Slow, sustained contraction ("latch" state) with low energy cost.

## Disorders relevant to anaesthesia

| Condition | Problem | Anaesthetic implications |
|---|---|---|
| Myasthenia gravis | Antibodies to postjunctional ACh receptors | Sensitive to non-depolarising NMBs; resistant to suxamethonium; postoperative respiratory weakness |
| Lambert–Eaton syndrome | Antibodies to presynaptic Ca^2+^ channels (often small-cell lung cancer) | Sensitive to both depolarising and non-depolarising NMBs |
| Muscular dystrophies | Muscle membrane defects | Suxamethonium can cause hyperkalaemia and rhabdomyolysis; cardiomyopathy |
| Myotonic dystrophy | Abnormal chloride channels (myotonia) | Suxamethonium may cause sustained contraction; sensitive to respiratory depressants |
