## Anatomy

| | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
|---|---|---|
| Outflow | **Thoracolumbar (T1–L2)** | **Craniosacral**: cranial nerves III, VII, IX, X and S2–4 |
| Ganglia | Paravertebral chain (and prevertebral: coeliac, mesenteric) — close to the cord | In or near the target organ |
| Preganglionic fibre | Short, myelinated (B fibres), via **white rami** | Long |
| Postganglionic fibre | Long, unmyelinated (C fibres), via **grey rami** to spinal nerves | Short |
| Ganglionic transmitter | ACh (nicotinic) | ACh (nicotinic) |
| Postganglionic transmitter | **Noradrenaline** (except sweat glands: ACh, muscarinic) | **ACh** (muscarinic) |

- The **adrenal medulla** is a modified sympathetic ganglion: preganglionic fibres release ACh onto chromaffin cells, which secrete mainly **adrenaline** (about 80%) into the blood.
- The **stellate ganglion** (fused inferior cervical and first thoracic ganglia) lies anterior to the neck of the first rib at C7; blocking it causes **Horner's syndrome** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis) and can treat upper limb sympathetic pain.
- Cardiac sympathetic fibres come from **T1–T4**: a high neuraxial block above T4 removes cardiac accelerator fibres, allowing bradycardia.

## Receptors

| Receptor | Second messenger | Main effects |
|---|---|---|
| α~1~ | G~q~ → IP~3~/DAG → ↑ Ca^2+^ | Vasoconstriction, mydriasis, bladder sphincter contraction |
| α~2~ | G~i~ → ↓ cAMP | Presynaptic inhibition of NA release; central sedation and analgesia (clonidine, dexmedetomidine) |
| β~1~ | G~s~ → ↑ cAMP | ↑ Heart rate, contractility, conduction; renin release |
| β~2~ | G~s~ → ↑ cAMP | Bronchodilation, vasodilation (muscle), uterine relaxation, glycogenolysis, K^+^ shift into cells |
| M~2~ | G~i~ | ↓ Heart rate and AV conduction |
| M~3~ | G~q~ | Bronchoconstriction, secretions, gut motility, bladder contraction, endothelial NO (vasodilation) |

## Transmitter handling

- **Noradrenaline:** tyrosine → DOPA → dopamine → noradrenaline (→ adrenaline by PNMT in the adrenal medulla). Action ended mainly by **reuptake (uptake 1)** into the nerve terminal, then metabolism by **MAO** and **COMT** (urinary VMA).
- **ACh:** from choline and acetyl-CoA (choline acetyltransferase); hydrolysed by acetylcholinesterase.

## Physiological balance

Most organs have dual innervation; at rest the heart is under dominant **vagal tone** (intrinsic SA rate about 100/min falls to about 70/min), while vessels are under **sympathetic tone** (few blood vessels have parasympathetic innervation). Loss of sympathetic tone with neuraxial block or anaesthesia → vasodilation and hypotension.
