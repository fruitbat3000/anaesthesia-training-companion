## Cyclo-oxygenase

- **COX** converts arachidonic acid (released from membranes by phospholipase A~2~) to prostaglandin H~2~, the precursor of prostaglandins, **thromboxane A~2~** (platelets: aggregation, vasoconstriction) and **prostacyclin** (endothelium: anti-aggregation, vasodilation).
- **COX-1** is constitutive (gastric protection, platelets, renal blood flow). **COX-2** is induced by inflammation (also constitutive in the kidney and brain).
- Blocking COX diverts arachidonic acid to the **lipoxygenase pathway** → **leukotrienes** → bronchospasm in **aspirin-/NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease** (asthma with nasal polyps).

## Paracetamol

- Analgesic and antipyretic with little anti-inflammatory action. Mechanism not fully understood: central COX inhibition (in the low-peroxide environment of the CNS), serotonergic descending pathways, and the active metabolite AM404 acting via TRPV1 and cannabinoid receptors.
- Well absorbed orally (bioavailability ~80–90%); IV and rectal forms.
- Metabolism: mainly glucuronidation and sulphation; a small fraction by CYP2E1 to **NAPQI**, detoxified by **glutathione**. In overdose (or glutathione depletion: malnutrition, chronic alcohol, low weight), NAPQI causes **hepatic necrosis**. Treatment: **acetylcysteine** (replenishes glutathione).
- **Dose** by weight in low-weight adults (under 50 kg) and children; check the BNF and local guidance.

## NSAIDs

- **Non-selective** (ibuprofen, diclofenac, naproxen, ketorolac) inhibit COX-1 and COX-2; **COX-2-selective** (celecoxib, etoricoxib, parecoxib) spare platelets and cause less GI ulceration.
- **Side effects:** GI ulceration and bleeding; **renal impairment** (loss of prostaglandin-mediated afferent dilatation, especially with hypovolaemia, ACE inhibitors or diuretics); platelet dysfunction (non-selective); bronchospasm in susceptible asthmatics; fluid retention and hypertension; increased cardiovascular thrombotic risk (especially COX-2 inhibitors and high-dose diclofenac); possible impairment of bone healing (debated).
- **Cautions / contraindications:** active peptic ulcer or bleeding, significant renal impairment, hypovolaemia, severe heart failure, NSAID-sensitive asthma, third trimester of pregnancy (premature closure of the ductus arteriosus).

## Aspirin

- **Irreversibly acetylates COX**; in platelets (no nucleus, cannot make new enzyme) this lasts the **7–10-day life of the platelet**. Low dose (75 mg) is antiplatelet; high doses are analgesic and anti-inflammatory.
- Avoid in children under 16 (**Reye's syndrome**). Overdose: respiratory alkalosis then metabolic acidosis, tinnitus, hyperthermia.
- Usually continued perioperatively for secondary prevention (except some neurosurgery, spinal and ophthalmic procedures).
