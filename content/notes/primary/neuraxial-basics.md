## Indications and contraindications

- **Uses:** lower limb, pelvic, perineal and lower abdominal surgery; caesarean section; labour analgesia; postoperative analgesia.
- **Absolute contraindications:** patient refusal, infection at the site, raised ICP with mass effect, allergy to local anaesthetic, uncorrected hypovolaemia (relative in some texts).
- **Relative:** coagulopathy and anticoagulants (see timing guidance), systemic sepsis, fixed cardiac output states (severe aortic stenosis), spinal deformity or surgery, pre-existing neurological disease, inability to cooperate.

## Consent

Discuss: common (hypotension, itching with opioids, urinary retention, failed or incomplete block needing supplementation or GA), less common (**post-dural puncture headache** ~1 in 100–200 for spinals with fine pencil-point needles, higher after accidental dural puncture with an epidural needle), rare but serious (nerve damage: temporary about 1 in 1,000–10,000; permanent about 1 in 25,000–50,000 in the NAP3 range; epidural haematoma and abscess; meningitis; high or total spinal; LAST). Use local and national patient information.

## Technique

Full asepsis (chlorhexidine 0.5% in alcohol allowed to dry, away from needles and drugs), sitting or lateral position, identify the space (below L2–3 for spinal; ultrasound if difficult), pencil-point 25–27G spinal needle, free flow of CSF before injection. Epidural: Tuohy needle, loss of resistance to saline or air, catheter 4–5 cm into the space, test dose, then incremental dosing.

## Spread of spinal anaesthesia

Main factors: **baricity** of the solution relative to CSF (hyperbaric "heavy" solutions contain glucose and flow downhill), **patient position** during and after injection, and **dose (mass)** of drug. Others: CSF volume (reduced in pregnancy, obesity), height (minor), curvature of the spine, site of injection, speed of injection.

## Physiological effects

**Sympathetic block** (vasodilation → hypotension; bradycardia if above T4, removing cardiac accelerator fibres; Bezold–Jarisch reflex with severe hypovolaemia), reduced respiratory reserve with high blocks (intercostal paralysis), gut contraction (unopposed vagal tone), urinary retention.

## Complications

- **Hypotension:** fluids, vasopressors (phenylephrine or metaraminol; ephedrine if bradycardic), uterine displacement in pregnancy.
- **High or total spinal:** rising block with arm weakness, dyspnoea, difficulty speaking, bradycardia, hypotension, loss of consciousness: support airway and breathing (intubate), vasopressors, atropine, reassure; the block will recede.
- **Accidental dural puncture and PDPH:** postural (worse sitting or standing), fronto-occipital headache, neck stiffness, possible cranial nerve palsies (abducens), within days. Conservative management (analgesia, hydration, caffeine has limited evidence), then **epidural blood patch** for significant symptoms; exclude other causes (e.g. cerebral venous thrombosis, subdural haematoma, pre-eclampsia).
- **Failed or inadequate block:** test the block before surgery; options include repeat block, supplementation or GA.
- **Serious:** epidural haematoma and abscess — new or worsening leg weakness or numbness after block should have worn off, back pain, sphincter disturbance → **urgent MRI and neurosurgical referral** (decompression within hours). Monitor motor function after neuraxial blocks (neurological monitoring guidance).
