## Infusion devices

- **Syringe drivers:** a lead screw pushes the plunger; accurate at low rates; used for TCI, drugs, PCA. **Volumetric pumps:** peristaltic mechanism; for larger volumes.
- **Safety features:** occlusion (pressure) alarms, air-in-line detection (volumetric), free-flow prevention, anti-siphon mechanisms, syringe size and brand recognition, drug libraries with dose limits ("smart pumps"), battery backup, locked settings (PCA, epidural).
- **Siphonage:** an unclamped syringe above the patient can empty under gravity if the plunger is not held (use anti-siphon valves). **Bolus on release of an occlusion:** clamp and relieve pressure before clearing. Place syringe drivers at the level of the patient.
- **Epidural and neuraxial devices** use dedicated yellow lines and non-Luer (NRFit) connectors to prevent wrong-route errors.
- **Calculations:** mg/kg/h → mL/h: rate (mL/h) = dose (mg/kg/h) × weight / concentration (mg/mL). Example: noradrenaline 4 mg in 50 mL = 80 µg/mL; 0.1 µg/kg/min for 70 kg = 7 µg/min = 420 µg/h ÷ 80 = **5.25 mL/h**.

## Warming devices

- **Forced-air warming** (convection) is the most effective common method for surface warming. Also conductive (resistive) mattresses and blankets.
- **Fluid warmers:** counter-current or dry-heat plate warmers; high-capacity warmers for rapid infusion. They reduce heat loss from cold fluids (warming blood from 4°C to 37°C takes a lot of energy).
- **Theatre ventilation (plenum systems):** positive-pressure filtered air, about 15–25 air changes per hour; laminar flow ventilation (ultraclean) for arthroplasty.

## Decontamination

| Level | Definition | Methods | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cleaning** | Physical removal of organic material | Detergent, washer-disinfectors | Always the first step |
| **Disinfection** | Removes most micro-organisms but not all spores | Heat (pasteurisation, washer-disinfectors at 70–90°C), chemicals (alcohol, chlorine, peracetic acid, glutaraldehyde) | Semi-critical items (e.g. flexible endoscopes: high-level disinfection) |
| **Sterilisation** | Kills all micro-organisms including spores | **Autoclave** (steam at 134°C for 3 min), ethylene oxide, gas plasma, irradiation | Critical items entering sterile tissue |

- **Spaulding classification:** critical (sterile tissue: sterilise), semi-critical (mucous membranes: high-level disinfection or sterilise), non-critical (intact skin: clean or low-level disinfection).
- **Prions** resist conventional sterilisation: single-use equipment for high-risk procedures and quarantine of instruments when prion disease is suspected.
- Single-use items must not be reused; follow manufacturer and local infection control guidance.
