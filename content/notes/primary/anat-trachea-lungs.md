## Trachea and bronchi

- **Trachea:** from **C6** to the **carina at about T4–T5** (the sternal angle); about **10–12 cm** long and 1.5–2 cm wide in adults; 16–20 C-shaped cartilage rings, open posteriorly (trachealis muscle next to the oesophagus).
- **Right main bronchus:** **shorter, wider and more vertical** (about 25° from the vertical) — hence endobronchial intubation and aspiration favour the right. The **right upper lobe bronchus arises only about 2–2.5 cm from the carina** (sometimes from the trachea), relevant to right-sided double-lumen tubes.
- **Left main bronchus:** about 4.5–5 cm long, angled about 45°; passes under the aortic arch. Left-sided double-lumen tubes are preferred for most thoracic surgery.
- Bronchial tree: about 23 generations to the alveoli; conducting zone (to terminal bronchioles, generation ~16) and respiratory zone.

## Lungs

- **Right lung:** 3 lobes (upper, middle, lower), **10 bronchopulmonary segments**; oblique and horizontal fissures.
- **Left lung:** 2 lobes (upper with lingula, lower), **8–10 segments**; oblique fissure.
- **Blood supply:** pulmonary arteries (deoxygenated) and **bronchial arteries** (from the aorta; nutritive; some drain into pulmonary veins — part of the normal shunt).

## Pleura and surface markings

- Visceral and parietal pleura; the **parietal pleura** is sensitive (intercostal and phrenic nerves).
- Surface markings (pleural reflections): mid-clavicular line at rib 8, mid-axillary line at rib 10, posteriorly at rib 12. The lungs extend about two ribs higher. The **pleura rises above the clavicle** in the root of the neck (pneumothorax risk with supraclavicular and interscalene blocks and subclavian lines).

## Diaphragm

- Main muscle of inspiration; **phrenic nerve C3–5** ("keeps the diaphragm alive"). Openings: IVC at T8, oesophagus at T10, aorta at T12.
- Phrenic nerve block is almost inevitable with **interscalene** brachial plexus block (hemidiaphragmatic paresis).

## Emergency landmarks

- **Chest drain:** the **"triangle of safety"**: anterior border of latissimus dorsi, lateral border of pectoralis major, a line at the level of the nipple (5th intercostal space), with the apex below the axilla. Insert **just above a rib** to avoid the neurovascular bundle, which runs under each rib (vein, artery, nerve from above down).
- **Needle decompression / thoracostomy:** 2nd intercostal space mid-clavicular line, or (increasingly preferred in adults, because of chest wall thickness) the **4th–5th intercostal space anterior to the mid-axillary line**. Finger thoracostomy in ventilated trauma patients.
