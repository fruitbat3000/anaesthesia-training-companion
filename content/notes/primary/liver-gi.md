## The liver

- **Carbohydrate:** glycogen storage, gluconeogenesis. **Fat:** β-oxidation, ketogenesis, lipoprotein and cholesterol synthesis. **Protein:** albumin, clotting factors (except VIII, made mainly in endothelium), deamination and the **urea cycle** (ammonia → urea).
- **Drug metabolism** (phase I and II), bilirubin conjugation and excretion, bile production (fat absorption), hormone inactivation, storage of vitamins A, D, B~12~ and iron, and Kupffer cell immune function.
- **Liver function tests:** albumin and INR reflect **synthetic function**; transaminases (ALT, AST) hepatocellular injury; ALP and GGT cholestasis; bilirubin excretion.
- Anaesthetic relevance of liver disease: coagulopathy, hypoalbuminaemia (more free drug), encephalopathy (sensitivity to sedatives), ascites, portal hypertension and varices, hepatorenal and hepatopulmonary syndromes, altered drug handling.

## Gastric physiology

- Parietal cells secrete **HCl** (proton pump; stimulated by **gastrin**, histamine via H~2~ receptors and ACh via M~3~) and intrinsic factor; chief cells secrete pepsinogen. About 2 L of gastric juice a day.
- **Gastric emptying:** clear fluids leave with a half-time of about 10–20 minutes; solids take hours, fatty meals longest. Emptying is delayed by opioids, pain, anxiety, labour, diabetes (autonomic neuropathy), trauma, bowel obstruction and pregnancy near term.
- **Lower oesophageal sphincter** tone protects against reflux (barrier pressure = LOS pressure − gastric pressure). Reduced by pregnancy, opioids, anticholinergics and volatile agents (propofol has little effect); increased by metoclopramide and suxamethonium (which also raises gastric pressure, so barrier pressure is maintained).
- **Aspiration risk** is increased by a full stomach, delayed emptying, reflux, obesity, pregnancy, obstruction and reduced consciousness. Strategies include fasting, antacids and H~2~ blockers or PPIs, nasogastric drainage, RSI, and extubation awake.

## Nausea and vomiting

- Coordinated by the **vomiting centre** (the "central pattern generator" in the medulla, around the nucleus tractus solitarius), with inputs from:
  - the **chemoreceptor trigger zone** in the **area postrema** (outside the blood–brain barrier; D~2~, 5-HT~3~, NK~1~ and opioid receptors) → opioids, toxins;
  - **vagal afferents** from the gut (5-HT~3~; serotonin release by enterochromaffin cells);
  - the **vestibular system** (H~1~, muscarinic) → motion;
  - the cortex (anticipation, smells, pain).
- Each antiemetic targets one or more of these receptors, which is why combinations work better.
- **PONV risk factors (Apfel, adults):** female sex, non-smoker, history of PONV or motion sickness, postoperative opioids. Also: volatile agents and nitrous oxide, longer surgery, certain operations (e.g. laparoscopic gynaecological, strabismus), and age (children over 3 and young adults).
