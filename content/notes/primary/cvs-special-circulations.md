## Share of cardiac output at rest (approximate)

| Organ | Flow | % of CO | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liver and gut | ~1.3–1.5 L/min | ~25–30% | Dual supply |
| Kidneys | ~1.1–1.2 L/min | ~20–25% | Highest flow per gram |
| Brain | ~750 mL/min | ~15% | ~50 mL/100 g/min |
| Heart | ~250 mL/min | ~5% | ~70–80 mL/100 g/min |
| Muscle | ~1 L/min | ~20% | Up to 20-fold increase in exercise |

## Coronary

- **LV perfusion occurs mainly in diastole** because systolic intramyocardial pressure compresses subendocardial vessels. **Coronary perfusion pressure ≈ aortic diastolic pressure − LVEDP.** The RV is perfused through systole and diastole.
- Myocardial **oxygen extraction is already ~70% at rest**, so extra demand must be met by **increased flow** (metabolic autoregulation: adenosine, NO, low PO~2~, K^+^, CO~2~; opening of **ATP-sensitive K^+^ channels** as ATP falls is a key vasodilator mechanism). Sympathetic α-constriction is normally overridden by this metabolic dilation.
- Tachycardia shortens diastole (reduces supply) while increasing demand; the subendocardium is most vulnerable.
- Anatomy: left main → LAD and circumflex; RCA supplies the SA node in about 60% and the AV node in about 80–90% (right dominance).

## Cerebral

- Autoregulation keeps flow roughly constant between a **MAP of about 50–150 mmHg** (shifted right in chronic hypertension; impaired after brain injury and by volatile agents at high concentration).
- **CO~2~ is the most powerful regulator:** flow changes by about **15 mL/100 g/min per kPa** of PaCO~2~ (roughly 2–4% per mmHg) between about 3 and 10 kPa. Hypoxia (PaO~2~ below ~6.7 kPa) greatly increases flow.
- **CPP = MAP − ICP** (or − CVP if higher).
- Flow–metabolism coupling: flow follows metabolic rate (reduced by propofol, barbiturates and hypothermia).

## Renal

- High flow serves filtration, not oxygen need: extraction is low overall, but the **medulla** is relatively hypoxic and vulnerable.
- Autoregulation (MAP about 80–180 mmHg) by the **myogenic response** and **tubuloglomerular feedback**.

## Hepatic

- **Portal vein ~75% of flow** (but ~50% of oxygen) and **hepatic artery ~25% of flow** (~50% of oxygen).
- The **hepatic arterial buffer response**: when portal flow falls, hepatic arterial flow rises (adenosine washout).
- No true autoregulation of portal flow. Flow is reduced by IPPV, PEEP, hypocapnia, sympathetic stimulation and neuraxial blockade with hypotension.
