## Resting membrane potential

- Set mainly by **K^+^ permeability**: the resting membrane is far more permeable to K^+^ than to Na^+^, so the potential (about **−70 mV in neurones, −90 mV in muscle**) lies close to the K^+^ equilibrium potential.
- **Nernst equation** (one ion): **E = (RT / zF) ln([ion]~out~ / [ion]~in~)** ≈ 61.5 log~10~([out]/[in]) mV at 37°C for a monovalent cation. E~K~ ≈ −90 mV; E~Na~ ≈ +60 mV.
- **Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz equation** combines all permeant ions weighted by their permeability.
- The **Na^+^/K^+^-ATPase** (3 Na^+^ out, 2 K^+^ in) maintains the gradients and is slightly electrogenic; it is not what generates most of the resting potential.
- **Raising extracellular K^+^** makes the resting potential less negative (depolarises), because E~K~ changes. Changing extracellular Na^+^ has little effect at rest because the membrane is barely permeable to Na^+^.

## The action potential

1. A stimulus depolarises the membrane to **threshold (~−55 mV)**.
2. **Voltage-gated Na^+^ channels** open (positive feedback): rapid depolarisation towards E~Na~ (overshoot to about +30 mV).
3. Na^+^ channels **inactivate** (the inactivation gate closes) within about 1 ms; **voltage-gated K^+^ channels** open more slowly → repolarisation and a brief **after-hyperpolarisation**.
- **Absolute refractory period:** Na^+^ channels inactivated; no stimulus can trigger another action potential. **Relative refractory period:** a larger stimulus is needed.
- **All-or-none:** intensity is coded by frequency, not amplitude.

## Conduction

| Fibre | Myelin | Diameter (µm) | Velocity (m/s) | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aα | Yes | 12–20 | 70–120 | Motor, proprioception |
| Aβ | Yes | 5–12 | 30–70 | Touch, pressure |
| Aγ | Yes | 3–6 | 15–30 | Muscle spindle motor |
| **Aδ** | Yes | 2–5 | 12–30 | **Fast, sharp pain**, cold |
| **B** | Yes (thin) | < 3 | 3–15 | **Preganglionic autonomic** |
| **C** | **No** | 0.4–1.2 | 0.5–2 | **Slow, dull pain**, warmth, postganglionic autonomic |

- Velocity rises with **diameter** (lower internal resistance) and **myelination** (saltatory conduction between **nodes of Ranvier**, where Na^+^ channels cluster).
- **Local anaesthetic sensitivity:** thin fibres are generally blocked before thick ones, and myelinated fibres before unmyelinated fibres of similar diameter (about three consecutive nodes must be blocked to stop conduction). Clinically the order is sympathetic (B) → temperature and pinprick (Aδ, C) → touch (Aβ) → motor (Aα), which is why sympathetic and sensory blocks extend higher than motor block after a spinal.
