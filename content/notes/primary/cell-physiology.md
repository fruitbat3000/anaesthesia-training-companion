## Cells and homeostasis

- **Homeostasis** (Claude Bernard's *milieu intérieur*; Cannon): the maintenance of a stable internal environment by negative feedback (sensor → integrator → effector).
- **Organelles:** nucleus (DNA; transcription to mRNA), ribosomes and rough ER (protein synthesis; translation), smooth ER (lipid synthesis, drug metabolism: the "microsomal" enzymes), Golgi (packaging), mitochondria (oxidative phosphorylation; own DNA, inherited maternally), lysosomes, peroxisomes, cytoskeleton.
- **Gene expression:** DNA → (transcription) → mRNA → (translation) → protein, regulated by transcription factors; steroid and thyroid hormones act here.

## The cell membrane

- A **phospholipid bilayer** with cholesterol and proteins (the fluid mosaic model): lipid-soluble substances (O~2~, CO~2~, anaesthetic agents) cross easily; ions and polar molecules need channels or carriers.
- Cell junctions: tight junctions (barriers such as the blood–brain barrier), gap junctions (electrical coupling in cardiac and smooth muscle), desmosomes (mechanical strength).

## Transport across membranes

| Mechanism | Energy | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Simple diffusion | None; down the gradient (Fick's law) | O~2~, CO~2~, lipid-soluble drugs |
| Facilitated diffusion | None; carrier or channel; saturable | Glucose via GLUT transporters |
| Primary active transport | ATP directly | **Na^+^/K^+^-ATPase**, H^+^/K^+^-ATPase, Ca^2+^-ATPase, P-glycoprotein |
| Secondary active transport | Uses a gradient set up by primary transport | SGLT (Na^+^–glucose), Na^+^/H^+^ exchange, Na^+^/Ca^2+^ exchange |
| Endocytosis / exocytosis | ATP | Neurotransmitter release |

- **Fick's law:** rate of diffusion ∝ (area × concentration gradient × solubility) / (thickness × √molecular weight) — the square-root term is Graham's law.

## Receptors

Drugs and hormones act through four main receptor families (detail in [Pharmacodynamics](#notes/pd-receptors)):

| Family | Speed | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Ligand-gated ion channels** (ionotropic) | Milliseconds | Nicotinic ACh, GABA~A~, NMDA, 5-HT~3~ |
| **G-protein coupled receptors** (seven transmembrane domains) | Seconds | Adrenoceptors, muscarinic, opioid. G~s~ ↑ cAMP; G~i~ ↓ cAMP; G~q~ → phospholipase C → IP~3~ and DAG |
| **Enzyme-linked** (tyrosine kinase) | Minutes | Insulin, growth factors |
| **Intracellular (nuclear)** | Hours | Steroid and thyroid hormones: alter gene transcription |

- Second-messenger cascades **amplify** the signal: one occupied receptor can activate many G-proteins and generate many cAMP molecules.

## Enzymes

- Biological catalysts that lower activation energy; activity depends on temperature, pH and cofactors.
- **Michaelis–Menten:** rate = V~max~[S] / (K~m~ + [S]). K~m~ is the substrate concentration at half V~max~. At low [S] the rate is proportional to [S] (**first-order**); when saturated it is constant (**zero-order**), e.g. ethanol, phenytoin and high-dose aspirin.
- Inhibition: competitive (raises apparent K~m~), non-competitive (lowers V~max~), irreversible (organophosphates, aspirin on COX).

## Protective mechanisms

Skin and mucosal barriers, mucociliary clearance, gastric acid, inflammation, the immune system, coagulation, and pain as a warning signal.
