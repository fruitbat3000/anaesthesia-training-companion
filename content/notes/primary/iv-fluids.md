## Crystalloids

- Distribute through the **ECF**: roughly a quarter or less stays intravascular after equilibration (less when the glycocalyx is damaged).
- **Balanced solutions** (Hartmann's, Plasma-Lyte) cause less hyperchloraemic acidosis than 0.9% saline and are generally preferred for resuscitation and replacement; saline is preferred in hypochloraemic alkalosis and is traditionally used with blood transfusion (calcium in Hartmann's can clot citrated blood in the same line) and in brain injury (Hartmann's is slightly hypotonic).

## Colloids

- **Albumin:** used in specific settings (paracentesis, some critical care uses); expensive.
- **Gelatins:** short-acting; highest rate of anaphylaxis among colloids.
- **Hydroxyethyl starches:** associated with **renal injury and increased mortality in sepsis**; their marketing authorisations have been suspended in the UK and EU.
- Colloids expand plasma volume more per mL initially, but trials have not shown outcome benefits over crystalloids in most settings.

## Principles (NICE CG174 "5 Rs": resuscitation, routine maintenance, replacement, redistribution, reassessment)

- **Routine maintenance (adults):** about **25–30 mL/kg/day of water**, **1 mmol/kg/day each of Na^+^, K^+^ and Cl^−^**, and **50–100 g/day glucose**. Reduce in the elderly, frail, renal and cardiac failure.
- **Resuscitation:** a bolus of balanced crystalloid (e.g. 500 mL in under 15 minutes in adults), then reassess.
- **Replacement:** match ongoing losses (GI losses are rich in K^+^; gastric losses in H^+^ and Cl^−^).
- **Children** (NICE NG29): isotonic fluids with glucose as needed for maintenance; boluses of 10 mL/kg (5 mL/kg in some groups) with reassessment.
- **Avoid fluid overload** (weight gain, oedema, anastomotic and pulmonary complications) as much as hypovolaemia.
