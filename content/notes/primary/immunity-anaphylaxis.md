## Immunity

- **Innate:** barriers, neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells, **complement** (classical, alternative and lectin pathways), mast cells and acute phase proteins. Rapid, non-specific, no memory.
- **Adaptive:** **B cells** (antibodies: humoral) and **T cells** (CD4 helper, CD8 cytotoxic: cellular). Specific, with memory.
- **Antibodies:** IgG (most abundant; crosses the placenta), IgM (first response, pentamer, fixes complement), IgA (mucosal), **IgE** (mast cells and basophils; allergy and parasites), IgD.

## Hypersensitivity (Gell and Coombs)

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| I (immediate) | **IgE** on mast cells → degranulation | Anaphylaxis, asthma, hay fever |
| II (cytotoxic) | IgG/IgM against cell surface antigens | ABO transfusion reaction, haemolytic disease of the newborn, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia |
| III (immune complex) | Antigen–antibody complexes deposit | Serum sickness, lupus |
| IV (delayed) | T cell-mediated | Contact dermatitis, chlorhexidine skin reactions, tuberculin test |

## Anaphylaxis

- **Allergic (IgE-mediated) anaphylaxis** requires prior sensitisation. **Non-allergic anaphylaxis** (previously "anaphylactoid") results from direct mast cell activation (e.g. via the MRGPRX2 receptor) or complement, and can occur on first exposure. Clinically they are managed identically.
- Mediators: **histamine**, tryptase, leukotrienes, prostaglandins, PAF → vasodilation, capillary leak (up to half the plasma volume within minutes), bronchospasm, angioedema.
- Perioperative presentation: **hypotension** is the commonest first feature; bronchospasm, rising airway pressure, falling ETCO~2~, rash (may be absent), and cardiac arrest. Most reactions occur within minutes of induction.
- **NAP6** (UK, 2018): incidence about **1 in 10,000 anaesthetics**; commonest triggers **antibiotics** (especially teicoplanin and co-amoxiclav), **neuromuscular blockers** (notably suxamethonium and rocuronium; atracurium less often) and **chlorhexidine**, then patent blue dye. Most deaths were in older patients with cardiovascular disease, often on β-blockers or ACE inhibitors.
- **Treatment:** adrenaline is first-line (IV titrated boluses under anaesthesia, e.g. 50 µg in adults, or IM 0.5 mg if no IV access), fluids, and remove triggers — follow the QRH and RCUK guidance. See the clinical note on managing anaphylaxis.
- **Investigation:** mast cell **tryptase** samples at about **1 hour** (up to 2 h) and **at least 24 hours** after the reaction (baseline), with an initial sample as soon as resuscitation allows; refer to an allergy clinic; tell the patient and GP and report to MHRA.
