## Red cells

- Produced in bone marrow under the control of **erythropoietin** (from peritubular cells of the kidney, released in response to hypoxia); lifespan about **120 days**; removed by the spleen.
- No nucleus or mitochondria: energy from **anaerobic glycolysis** (Embden–Meyerhof). The **Rapoport–Luebering shunt** produces **2,3-DPG**; the **pentose phosphate pathway** produces NADPH to protect against oxidative damage (**G6PD deficiency** → haemolysis with oxidant drugs and infection). **Methaemoglobin reductase** keeps iron in the ferrous (Fe^2+^) state.
- Haematinics: **iron, vitamin B~12~ and folate** (B~12~ and folate deficiency → macrocytic anaemia; nitrous oxide inactivates B~12~-dependent methionine synthase).

## Haemoglobin

- Tetramer of four globin chains, each with a haem group (protoporphyrin + **Fe^2+^**) binding one O~2~. Adult **HbA = α~2~β~2~** (~97%), HbA~2~ = α~2~δ~2~, **HbF = α~2~γ~2~** (higher affinity; P50 ~2.5 kPa).
- **Sickle haemoglobin (HbS):** a β-chain glutamate → valine substitution; polymerises when deoxygenated → sickling, vaso-occlusion and haemolysis. Avoid hypoxia, dehydration, hypothermia, acidosis and stasis; sickle cell disease patients need specialist perioperative planning, often including transfusion.
- **Thalassaemias:** reduced synthesis of α or β chains.
- **Methaemoglobin (Fe^3+^)** cannot carry oxygen; causes include nitrates, prilocaine (o-toluidine), dapsone. Pulse oximetry reads around 85% whatever the true saturation. Treat with **methylthioninium chloride (methylene blue)** unless G6PD deficient.
- **Carboxyhaemoglobin:** see oxygen transport.

## Iron

- Absorbed in the **duodenum** as Fe^2+^ (enhanced by acid and vitamin C); regulated by **hepcidin**, which rises in inflammation and blocks absorption and release (the anaemia of chronic disease). Transported by **transferrin**, stored as **ferritin** (an acute phase protein; low ferritin is diagnostic of iron deficiency, but normal or high ferritin does not exclude it when inflammation is present: check transferrin saturation).
- **Preoperative iron deficiency anaemia** should be treated (often with IV iron when time is short), as part of patient blood management.

## Anaemia

- WHO definition: Hb < 130 g/L in men and < 120 g/L in non-pregnant women.
- **Acute adaptations:** increased cardiac output (lower viscosity, sympathetic activity), increased oxygen extraction. **Chronic:** raised 2,3-DPG (right shift), increased plasma volume.
- Perioperative anaemia is associated with worse outcomes and more transfusion; restrictive transfusion thresholds (e.g. 70 g/L, or 80 g/L with cardiovascular disease) are generally recommended.

## Polycythaemia

Primary (polycythaemia vera) or secondary (hypoxia, altitude, smoking, EPO-secreting tumours). Raised viscosity increases thrombotic risk; in polycythaemia vera, venesection before elective surgery reduces complications.
