## Vaughan Williams classification

| Class | Action | Examples | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Na^+^ channel block** | Ia: quinidine, procainamide, disopyramide; **Ib: lidocaine**; **Ic: flecainide** | Ia: ↑ AP duration; Ib: ↓ AP duration (ischaemic tissue, ventricular); Ic: marked slowing of conduction (avoid in structural heart disease) |
| **II** | **β-blockade** | Bisoprolol, esmolol, metoprolol, propranolol | Slow SA and AV nodes |
| **III** | **K^+^ channel block** (↑ AP duration and refractory period) | **Amiodarone**, sotalol | QT prolongation |
| **IV** | **Ca^2+^ channel block** | Verapamil, diltiazem | Slow AV node |
| Unclassified | | **Adenosine, digoxin, magnesium, atropine** | |

Many drugs have multiple actions (amiodarone has class I–IV effects; sotalol II and III).

## Key drugs

- **Amiodarone:** broad spectrum (AF, VT, **VF/pulseless VT after the third shock in cardiac arrest**, per RCUK). IV via a central line if possible (phlebitis), in glucose. Very long half-life (weeks), large Vd, iodine-containing. Long-term toxicity: **thyroid** (hypo- or hyper-), pulmonary fibrosis, hepatitis, corneal deposits, photosensitivity, grey skin, neuropathy. Inhibits CYP (↑ warfarin, digoxin). Hypotension and bradycardia when given IV.
- **Adenosine:** A~1~ agonist → transient **AV node block** (half-life ~10 s). Terminates **AV nodal re-entrant tachycardia** and reveals atrial activity in flutter. Rapid bolus via a large vein with a flush. Chest tightness, flushing, dyspnoea, brief asystole. Caution in asthma; effect **enhanced by dipyridamole**, reduced by theophylline; lower doses via central lines and in heart transplant recipients.
- **Digoxin:** inhibits Na^+^/K^+^-ATPase → ↑ intracellular Ca^2+^ (inotropy) and ↑ vagal tone (AV slowing). Rate control in AF with heart failure. Narrow therapeutic index; toxicity worsened by **hypokalaemia**, hypomagnesaemia, hypercalcaemia, renal failure, amiodarone; causes any arrhythmia, nausea, visual disturbance. Treatment: digoxin-specific antibody fragments.
- **Magnesium:** torsades de pointes; adjunct in AF and digoxin toxicity.
- **Lidocaine:** ventricular arrhythmias (alternative to amiodarone in cardiac arrest).
- **β-blockers and verapamil/diltiazem:** rate control; never give IV verapamil for a broad-complex tachycardia of uncertain origin or with a β-blocker.

## Perioperative arrhythmias

Look for and correct causes first: hypoxia, hypercapnia, electrolytes (K^+^, Mg^2+^, Ca^2+^), light anaesthesia or pain, hypovolaemia, drugs, myocardial ischaemia, surgical stimulation (vagal reflexes). Unstable tachyarrhythmias need **synchronised DC cardioversion** (RCUK algorithm).
