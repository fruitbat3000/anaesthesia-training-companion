## Classification

- **Power source:** pneumatic, electric or both.
- **Mechanism:** **flow generators** deliver a set flow regardless of the patient's compliance (so tidal volume is preserved when compliance falls, but pressure rises); **pressure generators** deliver a set pressure (so tidal volume falls when compliance falls or resistance rises).
- **Cycling** (inspiration to expiration): **time-cycled** (most), volume-, pressure- or flow-cycled.
- **Bag-in-bottle ventilators** (e.g. on many anaesthetic machines): driving gas compresses a bellows containing breathing-system gas; ascending bellows (rise on expiration) are safer because a disconnection is obvious.
- **Minute volume dividers** (e.g. the Manley) use the fresh gas flow itself to power ventilation.

## Modes

| Mode | Set | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Volume control** | Tidal volume, rate, I:E | Guaranteed VT; pressure varies with compliance — watch peak and plateau pressures |
| **Pressure control** | Inspiratory pressure, rate, I:E | Decelerating flow; VT varies with compliance (watch minute ventilation) |
| **Pressure-regulated volume control** | Target VT | Adjusts pressure breath by breath |
| **SIMV** | Mandatory breaths synchronised with spontaneous effort | Weaning |
| **Pressure support (PSV)** | Pressure assist triggered by the patient, flow-cycled | Spontaneous mode for weaning or under LMA |
| **CPAP / PEEP** | End-expiratory pressure | Increases FRC and oxygenation |

## Other ventilators

- **Transport ventilators:** compact, gas-driven, with limited modes; know their oxygen consumption (driving gas) when calculating cylinder needs.
- **High-frequency jet ventilation:** a jet via a narrow catheter at high rates entrains gas (Venturi); used in laryngeal and tracheal surgery. Risk of **barotrauma** if exhalation is obstructed.
- **High-frequency oscillatory ventilation:** very small tidal volumes at 3–15 Hz with active expiration.

## Disconnection and alarms

**Low pressure / low volume alarms** (the pressure fails to reach a threshold within a set time), **capnography** (the most reliable disconnection monitor), high pressure alarms, apnoea alarms and, on ICU ventilators, minute volume alarms.
