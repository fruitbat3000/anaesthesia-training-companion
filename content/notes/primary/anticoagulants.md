## Parenteral anticoagulants

| | **Unfractionated heparin (UFH)** | **Low molecular weight heparin (LMWH)** |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Binds **antithrombin**; inactivates thrombin (IIa) and Xa equally | Antithrombin-mediated; mainly **anti-Xa** |
| Kinetics | Short half-life (~1–2 h); IV infusion or SC | Longer (~4–6 h); predictable; SC once or twice daily; renally excreted |
| Monitoring | APTT (or ACT during bypass) | Usually none; **anti-Xa** in renal impairment, obesity, pregnancy |
| Reversal | **Protamine** (1 mg per 100 units, fully) | Protamine only partially |
| HIT risk | Higher | Lower |

**Fondaparinux** (synthetic pentasaccharide, indirect anti-Xa) is not reversed by protamine. **Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia** (type II, immune): platelet fall of > 50% at 5–10 days with **thrombosis**; stop all heparin and use an alternative (e.g. argatroban).

## Warfarin

- Inhibits **vitamin K epoxide reductase** → reduced II, VII, IX, X (and proteins C and S: transient hypercoagulability when started). Monitored by **INR**.
- Highly protein-bound, CYP2C9-metabolised: many interactions (enzyme inhibitors ↑ INR; inducers ↓).
- **Reversal:** for urgent surgery or major bleeding, **prothrombin complex concentrate** plus **IV vitamin K**; for elective surgery, stop about 5 days before and check INR; bridge with LMWH only in high thrombotic risk (e.g. mechanical mitral valve, recent VTE).

## Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs)

| Drug | Target | Renal elimination | Reversal agent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dabigatran** | Thrombin (IIa) | ~80% | **Idarucizumab** |
| **Rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban** | Xa | ~25–50% | **Andexanet alfa** (licensed for apixaban/rivaroxaban major bleeding); PCC often used |

Stopping time before surgery depends on **renal function** and the bleeding risk of the procedure (typically 1–2 days for low risk, longer for high risk and neuraxial block, and longer for dabigatran with renal impairment). Bridging is not needed.

## Antiplatelet drugs

| Drug | Mechanism | Typical stop before neuraxial / high-risk surgery (guide) |
|---|---|---|
| Aspirin | Irreversible COX-1 | Not usually needed for neuraxial block |
| **Clopidogrel** | Irreversible P2Y~12~ (pro-drug, CYP2C19) | 7 days |
| **Prasugrel** | Irreversible P2Y~12~ | 7 days |
| **Ticagrelor** | Reversible P2Y~12~ | 5 days |
| GPIIb/IIIa inhibitors (tirofiban, eptifibatide, abciximab) | Block aggregation | Hours to days |
| Dipyridamole | PDE inhibitor, adenosine reuptake | |

## Neuraxial block and anticoagulation

Timing of needle insertion and catheter removal relative to anticoagulant doses is set out in the Association of Anaesthetists/OAA/RA-UK guidance (e.g. about **12 h after prophylactic LMWH**, 24 h after treatment doses). **Always check the current guideline and local policy**, and assess renal function and the patient's overall risk.

## VTE prophylaxis

NICE NG89: assess every surgical patient for VTE and bleeding risk; use mechanical (graduated compression stockings, intermittent pneumatic compression) and pharmacological prophylaxis as indicated; early mobilisation.
