## Absorption

Passage across membranes depends on **lipid solubility**, **ionisation** (only the un-ionised form crosses readily), molecular size, the concentration gradient, blood flow and surface area at the site, and transporters (e.g. P-glycoprotein efflux in the gut).

## Bioavailability

- **F = AUC~oral~ / AUC~IV~** (dose-corrected): the fraction of an administered dose reaching the systemic circulation unchanged.
- Reduced by incomplete absorption, destruction in the gut, and **first-pass metabolism** in the gut wall and liver (e.g. oral morphine ~20–40%; oral GTN almost nil; lidocaine too low to be useful orally).

## Routes

| Route | Advantages | Drawbacks / examples |
|---|---|---|
| Oral | Simple, cheap | First pass, variable absorption, delayed gastric emptying, needs a working gut |
| Sublingual / buccal | Avoids first pass; rapid | GTN, buprenorphine |
| Rectal | Partly avoids first pass (lower rectal veins drain systemically) | Paracetamol, diclofenac; erratic |
| Intranasal | Rapid, avoids first pass, needle-free | Fentanyl, diamorphine, midazolam, dexmedetomidine in children |
| Transdermal | Steady levels, avoids first pass | Fentanyl, buprenorphine patches: slow onset and offset, depot in skin; heat increases absorption |
| Inhalational | Rapid onset and control | Volatiles, bronchodilators |
| Intramuscular / subcutaneous | No IV access needed | Absorption depends on perfusion (poor in shock) |
| Intravenous | 100% bioavailable, immediate | Most risk of error and toxicity |
| Neuraxial / perineural | Direct to site of action, low doses | Opioids and LAs; specific toxicities |
| Intraosseous | Rapid access in emergencies | Equivalent to IV for drugs and fluids |

## Drug delivery systems

- **Modified release** (sustained release, enteric coating) spreads absorption over time; do not crush.
- **Iontophoresis** uses a small electric current to drive ionised drug across skin (e.g. fentanyl systems).
- **Oral absorption of weak acids and bases** depends on pH and pKa, but in practice most absorption occurs in the small intestine because of its enormous surface area, even for weak acids.
