## Brachial plexus (C5–T1)

**Roots** (between scalenus anterior and medius) → **trunks** (upper C5–6, middle C7, lower C8–T1; in the posterior triangle above the clavicle) → **divisions** (behind the clavicle) → **cords** (lateral, posterior, medial — named by their relation to the **axillary artery**) → **branches** (in the axilla). Mnemonic: "Real Teenagers Drink Cold Beer".

| Terminal branch | From | Supplies (key) | Injury sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Musculocutaneous** (C5–7) | Lateral cord | Biceps, brachialis; lateral forearm skin | Weak elbow flexion |
| **Axillary** (C5–6) | Posterior cord | Deltoid; "regimental badge" skin | Weak abduction |
| **Radial** (C5–T1) | Posterior cord | Extensors; dorsum of hand (first web space) | **Wrist drop** (e.g. humeral fracture, compression) |
| **Median** (C5–T1) | Lateral + medial cords | Forearm flexors, thenar muscles; lateral palm and 3½ fingers | Loss of thumb opposition; carpal tunnel |
| **Ulnar** (C8–T1) | Medial cord | Intrinsic hand muscles; medial 1½ fingers | Claw hand; commonest perioperative nerve injury (elbow) |

- Other branches: **suprascapular nerve** (upper trunk), long thoracic (C5–7, serratus anterior), phrenic (C3–5, close to the plexus in the neck).

**Blocks:**

| Approach | Level | Covers | Main issues |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interscalene** | Roots/trunks (C5–7) | **Shoulder** and upper arm | **Phrenic nerve block (almost 100%)**, Horner's, hoarseness; spares C8–T1 (ulnar) |
| Supraclavicular | Trunks/divisions | Whole arm below the shoulder | Pneumothorax risk; phrenic block less common |
| Infraclavicular | Cords | Elbow, forearm, hand | Deep; vascular |
| **Axillary** | Terminal branches | Elbow, forearm and hand | Musculocutaneous must be blocked separately (it leaves the sheath high) |

## Lumbar plexus (L1–L4) and sacral plexus (L4–S4)

- **Lumbar plexus** (in psoas): iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal (L1), genitofemoral (L1–2), **lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh** (L2–3), **femoral** (L2–4: quadriceps, anterior thigh, and the **saphenous nerve** to the medial leg), **obturator** (L2–4: adductors, a small patch of medial thigh).
- **Femoral nerve** lies **lateral** to the femoral artery, deep to the fascia iliaca (not inside the femoral sheath).
- **Fascia iliaca block** (below or above the inguinal ligament) spreads under fascia iliaca to reach the femoral and lateral femoral cutaneous nerves (± obturator): used for **hip fractures**. PENG block targets articular branches to the hip.
- **Sacral plexus:** **sciatic nerve** (L4–S3): leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen below piriformis, divides (usually in the popliteal fossa) into **tibial** and **common peroneal (fibular)** nerves. Common peroneal injury (at the fibular head, e.g. from lithotomy stirrups) → **foot drop**. Also: pudendal (S2–4), posterior cutaneous nerve of the thigh.
- **Ankle block:** five nerves — tibial, deep peroneal, superficial peroneal, sural and saphenous.

## Trunk

- **Intercostal nerves** (T1–T11) run in the **subcostal groove below each rib**, between internal and innermost intercostal muscles, with the vein and artery above.
- The **anterior abdominal wall** is supplied by T7–T12 (thoracoabdominal nerves) and L1 (iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal), which run in the plane **between internal oblique and transversus abdominis** — the target of the **TAP block**. The **rectus sheath block** targets the terminal branches behind the rectus muscles.
- **Inguinal region:** ilioinguinal, iliohypogastric and genital branch of genitofemoral nerves (inguinal hernia field block).
