## Hormone signalling

- **Peptide and catecholamine hormones** act on cell surface receptors (G-protein coupled or tyrosine kinase). **Steroid and thyroid hormones** cross the membrane and act on **intracellular (nuclear) receptors**, changing gene transcription (slower onset, longer effect).
- Most axes use **negative feedback**; oxytocin in labour is an example of positive feedback.

## Pituitary

- **Anterior** (adenohypophysis; hypothalamic releasing hormones via the portal circulation): ACTH, TSH, GH, prolactin, FSH, LH.
- **Posterior** (neurohypophysis; axons from the hypothalamus): **ADH** and **oxytocin**.
- Anatomy: in the sella turcica, below the optic chiasm (bitemporal hemianopia), with the cavernous sinuses laterally. Acromegaly (excess GH) causes airway difficulty (macroglossia, prognathism, laryngeal changes) and OSA.

## Adrenal gland

| Zone | Hormone | Controlled by |
|---|---|---|
| Zona glomerulosa | **Aldosterone** | Angiotensin II, K^+^ |
| Zona fasciculata | **Cortisol** | ACTH |
| Zona reticularis | Androgens | ACTH |
| Medulla | **Adrenaline**, noradrenaline | Preganglionic sympathetic fibres (ACh) |

- **Cortisol** (about 20 mg/day, far more under stress): gluconeogenesis, anti-inflammatory, permissive for catecholamine vasoconstriction. **Adrenal insufficiency** (Addison's, or suppression by exogenous steroids) → hypotension resistant to vasopressors, hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia, hypoglycaemia; give **hydrocortisone**. Patients on long-term steroids need perioperative steroid cover.
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** catecholamine-secreting tumour; requires preoperative **α-blockade first**, then β-blockade.

## Thyroid

- Iodide trapping → thyroglobulin iodination (thyroid peroxidase) → **T~4~** (mostly) and **T~3~** (more active; formed peripherally by deiodination). Highly protein-bound.
- Increase metabolic rate, oxygen consumption, heat production, heart rate (upregulated β receptors).
- **Hyperthyroidism:** risk of **thyroid storm** perioperatively (fever, tachycardia, AF, agitation) — make euthyroid before elective surgery. **Hypothyroidism:** sensitivity to anaesthetic drugs, hypothermia, hypoventilation.
- Anatomy: the **recurrent laryngeal nerves** run close to the inferior thyroid arteries (at risk in thyroidectomy); the **parathyroids** lie behind.

## Calcium homeostasis

- **PTH** (low Ca^2+^ stimulates): increases bone resorption, renal Ca^2+^ reabsorption and phosphate excretion, and renal **1α-hydroxylation** of vitamin D.
- **Vitamin D (calcitriol):** increases gut absorption of Ca^2+^ and phosphate.
- **Calcitonin** (thyroid C cells): lowers Ca^2+^ (minor role in humans).
- Post-thyroidectomy **hypocalcaemia** (parathyroid damage): perioral tingling, Chvostek's and Trousseau's signs, laryngospasm, prolonged QT.
