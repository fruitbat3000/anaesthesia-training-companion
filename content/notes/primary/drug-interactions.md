## Adverse drug reactions

- **Type A (augmented):** predictable from pharmacology and dose-related (e.g. hypotension with propofol, respiratory depression with opioids). The commonest type.
- **Type B (bizarre):** unpredictable, idiosyncratic (anaphylaxis, MH, blood dyscrasias, pulmonary fibrosis with amiodarone or bleomycin).
- Others: chronic (C), delayed (D, e.g. teratogenicity), end-of-use (E, withdrawal), failure of therapy (F).
- Report suspected reactions to the MHRA via the **Yellow Card** scheme.

## Interactions

- **Pharmaceutical:** in the syringe or line (thiopental precipitates with acidic drugs such as suxamethonium or rocuronium; blood and calcium-containing fluids).
- **Pharmacokinetic:** absorption, distribution (protein binding displacement), metabolism (induction or inhibition), elimination (NSAIDs reduce lithium clearance).
- **Pharmacodynamic:** at the receptor or physiological level — **additive** (effects sum), **synergistic** (more than additive: midazolam with propofol or opioids), **antagonistic** (naloxone with opioids), or potentiation (one drug with no effect of its own increases another's).
- **Isobologram:** plots combinations giving an equal effect. A straight line = additive; bowed towards the origin = synergy; bowed away = antagonism.

## Drug errors

Common causes: similar-looking ampoules and names, unlabelled syringes, calculation errors, wrong route (e.g. IV–intrathecal errors), fatigue and interruptions. Prevention: **standardised labelling** (colour-coded ISO labels), reading the ampoule, prefilled syringes, **non-Luer (NRFit) connectors for neuraxial devices**, double-checking high-risk drugs, drug-trolley organisation, electronic prescribing, reporting and learning from incidents.

## Drugs and the eye

- **Intraocular pressure (IOP)** is raised by suxamethonium (transient), ketamine (slight), coughing, straining, hypercapnia, hypoxia, venous congestion; lowered by most IV and volatile agents, hyperventilation.
- **Glaucoma drugs:** timolol (β-blocker: bradycardia, bronchospasm), acetazolamide (acidosis, hypokalaemia), pilocarpine, prostaglandin analogues. **Ecothiopate** (organophosphate eye drops, now rare) prolongs suxamethonium.
- Atropine can precipitate acute angle-closure glaucoma in susceptible eyes (rare with standard doses).

## Recreational drugs, alcohol and tobacco

| Substance | Anaesthetic relevance |
|---|---|
| **Tobacco** | ↑ COHb (↓ O~2~ content; stopping for 12–24 h helps), ↑ airway reactivity and secretions, impaired wound healing; CYP1A2 induction. Stopping at any time before surgery helps; longer abstinence reduces pulmonary complications |
| **Alcohol** | Acute: ↓ MAC, aspiration risk. Chronic: ↑ MAC and enzyme induction, liver disease, cardiomyopathy, **withdrawal** (seizures, delirium tremens) — prescribe benzodiazepine regimens and thiamine |
| **Cocaine** | Sympathomimetic (↓ reuptake): hypertension, myocardial ischaemia and arrhythmias, vasospasm; avoid β-blockers alone in acute toxicity; benzodiazepines |
| **Amphetamines, MDMA** | Sympathetic stimulation, hyperthermia, hyponatraemia (MDMA), serotonin syndrome |
| **Opioids** (heroin, methadone, buprenorphine) | Tolerance (higher analgesic needs), withdrawal, venous access difficulty, blood-borne viruses; continue substitution therapy |
| **Cannabis** | Airway reactivity (smoked), tachycardia, possible ↑ anaesthetic requirement; hyperemesis syndrome |
| **Ketamine (recreational)** | Tolerance; urological damage |
| **Nitrous oxide (recreational)** | B~12~ deficiency: subacute combined degeneration |
