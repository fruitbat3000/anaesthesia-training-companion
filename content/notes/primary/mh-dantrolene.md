## Pathophysiology

- An inherited (autosomal dominant, variable penetrance) disorder, usually of the skeletal muscle **ryanodine receptor (RYR1)**, less often **CACNA1S** (dihydropyridine receptor) or STAC3.
- **Triggers:** all **volatile anaesthetics** and **suxamethonium**. (N~2~O, IV agents, opioids, local anaesthetics, non-depolarising NMBs are safe.)
- Uncontrolled Ca^2+^ release from the SR → sustained contraction and hypermetabolism → ↑ O~2~ consumption and **CO~2~ production**, heat, lactic acidosis, rigidity, rhabdomyolysis (**hyperkalaemia**, myoglobinuria, raised CK), arrhythmias, DIC.
- Estimated incidence of reactions of about 1 in 10,000–250,000 anaesthetics; genetic prevalence much higher.

## Recognising a crisis

- **Early signs:** unexplained **rising ETCO~2~** despite increased ventilation, tachycardia, masseter spasm after suxamethonium, generalised rigidity, rising O~2~ requirement, soda lime heating up quickly.
- **Later:** hyperthermia (can be late), metabolic and respiratory acidosis, hyperkalaemia, arrhythmias, myoglobinuria.
- **Differential diagnosis:** light anaesthesia, sepsis, thyroid storm, phaeochromocytoma, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, serotonin syndrome, overheating, hypercapnia from laparoscopy or rebreathing.

## Management (see the QRH MH card and Association of Anaesthetists guideline)

1. **Call for help**; declare the emergency; get dantrolene and the MH kit.
2. **Stop triggers:** turn off the vaporiser; high-flow 100% O~2~; insert **activated charcoal filters** in the breathing system (or change the machine); maintain anaesthesia with **TIVA**. Hyperventilate.
3. **Dantrolene 2.5 mg/kg IV**, then 1 mg/kg boluses as needed (up to about 10 mg/kg). Each vial is 20 mg (with 3 g of mannitol), reconstituted with **60 mL sterile water**: needs several people. A newer formulation (e.g. Ryanodex) is more concentrated.
4. **Treat complications:** active cooling (stop when < 38.5°C), hyperkalaemia (insulin–glucose, calcium), acidosis, arrhythmias (avoid calcium channel blockers with dantrolene); aim for urine output > 2 mL/kg/h (myoglobinuria); check CK, coagulation.
5. **Transfer to ICU**; watch for recrudescence over 24 h.

## Dantrolene

Binds RyR1 and reduces Ca^2+^ release from the SR (skeletal muscle only; no effect on the NMJ). Side effects: muscle weakness (respiratory in patients with poor reserve), phlebitis (high pH), hepatotoxicity with chronic oral use.

## Afterwards

Refer the patient (and family) to the **UK MH investigation unit (Leeds)** for **in vitro contracture testing** of a muscle biopsy and/or genetic testing; counsel, document, and give an alert card. Known or suspected MH patients have trigger-free anaesthesia (vapour-free machine prepared per guidance or charcoal filters) and are first on the list where possible.
