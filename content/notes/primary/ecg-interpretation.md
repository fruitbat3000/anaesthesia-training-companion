## Recording the ECG

- The ECG records the **potential differences on the body surface** generated by cardiac depolarisation and repolarisation (about 1 mV).
- **Silver/silver chloride electrodes** with conductive gel reduce skin impedance and polarisation.
- **Leads:** bipolar limb leads (I, II, III), augmented unipolar limb leads (aVR, aVL, aVF) and six chest leads (V1–V6) give 12 views. In theatre, **lead II** (rhythm, P waves, inferior ischaemia) and **V5** or **CM5** (lateral ischaemia) are commonly monitored.
- **Filters:** **monitoring mode** uses a narrow bandwidth (about 0.5–40 Hz) to reduce baseline wander and interference, which **distorts ST segments**; **diagnostic mode** (0.05–150 Hz) is needed to assess ST changes accurately.
- Differential amplifiers with high common-mode rejection reduce mains interference; diathermy causes gross artefact.
- Paper speed 25 mm/s: 1 small square = 0.04 s; 1 large square = 0.2 s. Rate = 300 / number of large squares between R waves.

## Normal values

PR 0.12–0.20 s; QRS < 0.12 s; **QTc** < about 0.44 s (men) / 0.46 s (women) (Bazett: QTc = QT/√RR); axis −30° to +90°.

## Changes to recognise

| Finding | Causes and relevance |
|---|---|
| **ST elevation** | Acute MI (territorial, reciprocal changes), pericarditis (widespread, saddle-shaped, PR depression), LV aneurysm, early repolarisation |
| **ST depression / T inversion** | Ischaemia (subendocardial), digoxin (reverse tick), LVH strain |
| **Hyperkalaemia** | Peaked T waves → flattened P waves, prolonged PR → broad QRS → sine wave → VF/asystole |
| **Hypokalaemia** | Flattened T waves, U waves, ST depression, prolonged QU; arrhythmias |
| **Hypocalcaemia / hypercalcaemia** | Long QT / short QT |
| **Long QT** | Torsades de pointes; drugs (ondansetron, droperidol, macrolides, antipsychotics, methadone), electrolytes, congenital |
| **Heart block** | 1st degree (PR > 0.2 s); 2nd degree Mobitz I (Wenckebach: progressive PR lengthening) and **Mobitz II** (dropped beats without PR change: risk of complete block); **3rd degree** (AV dissociation) |
| **Bundle branch block** | RBBB (RSR' in V1); LBBB (broad, notched R in V5–6; new LBBB may indicate MI) |
| **Tachyarrhythmias** | Narrow regular (sinus tachycardia, SVT, flutter with 2:1 block at ~150/min), narrow irregular (**AF**), broad regular (**VT** until proven otherwise), broad irregular (AF with BBB, polymorphic VT) |
| **Pre-excitation (WPW)** | Short PR, delta wave; risk of fast AF down the accessory pathway (avoid AV nodal blockers in pre-excited AF) |

**Territories:** inferior (II, III, aVF) — usually the **right coronary artery**; anterior and septal (V1–V4) — the **left anterior descending**; lateral (I, aVL, V5–V6) — the **circumflex** (or diagonal branches). The left ventricle, having the greatest muscle mass, dominates the QRS. Posterior and far-lateral territory is poorly seen on the standard 12 leads: posterior infarction shows as **ST depression with tall R waves in V1–V3** (confirm with posterior leads V7–V9).

Management of peri-arrest arrhythmias follows the **RCUK** bradycardia and tachycardia algorithms, based on **adverse features** (shock, syncope, myocardial ischaemia, heart failure).
