## Drugs

| Drug | Mechanism | Uses and notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tranexamic acid** | Lysine analogue: blocks plasminogen binding to fibrin (antifibrinolytic) | Trauma (**within 3 hours** of injury: CRASH-2), obstetric haemorrhage (WOMAN), major orthopaedic and cardiac surgery; seizures at high doses; reduce in renal impairment |
| **Vitamin K (phytomenadione)** | Cofactor for γ-carboxylation of II, VII, IX, X | Warfarin reversal (IV acts within 6–12 h) |
| **Protamine** | Basic protein binding acidic heparin | Hypotension, pulmonary hypertension, anaphylaxis (fish allergy, previous exposure, protamine insulin); excess is itself anticoagulant |
| **Desmopressin (DDAVP)** | V~2~ agonist: releases vWF and factor VIII | Type 1 von Willebrand disease, mild haemophilia A, uraemic platelet dysfunction; hyponatraemia; tachyphylaxis |
| **Prothrombin complex concentrate** | Factors II, VII, IX, X (± proteins C and S) | Rapid warfarin reversal; small volume; thrombotic risk |
| **Fibrinogen concentrate** | Fibrinogen | Hypofibrinogenaemia (e.g. obstetric haemorrhage guided by FIBTEM or Clauss) |
| **Recombinant factor VIIa** | Activates X on platelets at the site of injury | Haemophilia with inhibitors; off-label rescue |

## Blood components in haemorrhage

- **FFP:** all factors; 12–15 mL/kg; needs thawing (about 20–30 min).
- **Cryoprecipitate:** fibrinogen, VIII, vWF, XIII; about 2 pools raise fibrinogen by about 1 g/L in an adult.
- **Platelets:** aim > 75 × 10^9^/L in active major bleeding (> 100 × 10^9^/L in multiple trauma or CNS injury).
- **Major haemorrhage protocols:** early high ratios of FFP (and platelets) to red cells in trauma; goal-directed replacement with laboratory or viscoelastic results; correct **hypothermia, acidosis and hypocalcaemia** (the lethal triad plus calcium).

## Blood conservation

Treat preoperative anaemia; stop antithrombotic drugs appropriately; surgical technique and haemostasis; positioning; tranexamic acid; **cell salvage** (intraoperative collection, washing and reinfusion of red cells; acceptable to many Jehovah's Witnesses in a continuous circuit; caution in malignancy and contamination — leucocyte filters help); acute normovolaemic haemodilution; restrictive transfusion thresholds; controlled hypotension in selected cases.
