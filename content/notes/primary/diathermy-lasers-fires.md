## Surgical diathermy

- Uses **high-frequency alternating current (~0.4–1 MHz; radio frequency)**. At these frequencies current does not stimulate nerves or muscle (no time for depolarisation) or cause VF, but it heats tissue where **current density** is high.
- **Cutting:** continuous sine wave; **coagulation:** pulsed (damped) waveform.
- **Monopolar:** current passes from the fine active electrode (high current density → heating) through the patient to a large **patient return electrode** ("plate"), where current density is low. **Burns** occur if the plate is poorly applied (small contact area), or if current finds an alternative path to earth (e.g. ECG electrodes, contact with metal). Modern isolated (floating) generators and plate-contact monitoring reduce this.
- **Bipolar:** current passes only between the tips of the forceps: no plate needed; low power; preferred for extremities, near pacemakers, and in circumcision and digital surgery.
- **Implanted devices:** monopolar diathermy may inhibit or reprogramme pacemakers and trigger ICD shocks. Use bipolar where possible, keep the current path away from the device, and follow device-management guidance (e.g. reprogramming or a magnet as advised).

## Lasers

- **L**ight **A**mplification by **S**timulated **E**mission of **R**adiation: **monochromatic** (single wavelength), **coherent** (in phase) and **collimated** (parallel) light.
- A lasing medium is "pumped" to create a population inversion; photons stimulate emission of identical photons, amplified between mirrors.
- Examples: **CO~2~** (10,600 nm, infrared; absorbed by water; superficial precise cutting, e.g. airway surgery); **Nd:YAG** (1064 nm; deeper penetration; coagulation); argon, KTP, diode.
- **Classes 1–4** by hazard; surgical lasers are **class 4** (hazard to eyes, skin and fire risk).
- **Safety:** designated laser protection supervisor, warning signs, restricted access, **wavelength-specific eye protection** for staff and patient, covered windows, matt instruments, damp swabs.
- **Airway surgery:** use **laser-resistant tracheal tubes** (metal or foil-wrapped) with cuffs filled with **saline (± dye)**; keep FiO~2~ as low as possible (ideally ≤ 0.3) and avoid N~2~O; or use tubeless techniques (jet ventilation, apnoea). If an **airway fire** occurs: stop the laser, stop gas flow, remove the tube, pour saline, then ventilate with air, re-intubate and bronchoscopy.

## Fires and explosions

- **Fire triangle:** **fuel** (drapes, skin preparations with alcohol, tubes, hair), **oxidiser** (oxygen, **N~2~O**, which supports combustion), and **ignition source** (diathermy, lasers, fibreoptic light sources, sparks).
- Prevention: let **alcoholic skin preparations dry** fully, avoid oxygen pooling under drapes (particularly head and neck surgery with nasal oxygen), use the lowest FiO~2~ needed, and communicate with the surgical team.
- Flammable anaesthetic agents (ether, cyclopropane) are historical; the zone of risk was within 25 cm of the gas outlet.
