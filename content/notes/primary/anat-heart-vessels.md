## The heart

- **Chambers and valves:** tricuspid and pulmonary valves (right), mitral and aortic (left). The **right ventricle** is anterior (most of the anterior surface); the **left atrium** is posterior (next to the oesophagus: why TOE views it well).
- **Conducting system:** SA node (upper right atrium near the SVC) → AV node (interatrial septum, near the coronary sinus) → bundle of His → left and right bundle branches → Purkinje fibres.
- **Coronary arteries:** the **left main stem** divides into the **LAD** (anterior LV, anterior two-thirds of the septum) and **circumflex** (lateral LV); the **RCA** supplies the RV, inferior LV (in right-dominant hearts, about 70–85%), SA node (~60%) and AV node (~80–90%). ECG territories: anterior V1–V4 (LAD), lateral I, aVL, V5–6 (circumflex), inferior II, III, aVF (RCA usually).
- **Venous drainage:** coronary sinus to the right atrium; Thebesian veins directly into the chambers.
- **Pericardium:** fibrous and serous layers; tamponade physiology.
- **Innervation:** sympathetic (T1–T4 cardiac accelerator fibres) and vagus.

## Great vessels and neck veins

- The **aortic arch** gives off the brachiocephalic trunk, left common carotid and left subclavian arteries.
- **Internal jugular vein (IJV):** runs in the carotid sheath **lateral to the common carotid artery** (with the vagus posterior between them), deep to sternocleidomastoid, joining the subclavian to form the brachiocephalic vein behind the sternoclavicular joint. The right IJV gives the most direct route to the SVC. Ultrasound shows the IJV as compressible and variable in size, and the carotid as pulsatile and round.
- **Subclavian vein:** passes over the first rib anterior to the scalene anterior (the artery is behind it); lower infection risk but higher pneumothorax risk and non-compressible.
- **Femoral vein:** in the **femoral triangle** (inguinal ligament, sartorius, adductor longus), **medial to the artery** ("NAVY" from lateral: nerve, artery, vein, Y-fronts); below the inguinal ligament.

## Peripheral access and arteries

- **Antecubital fossa:** from lateral to medial — **biceps tendon, brachial artery, median nerve**; the median cubital vein lies superficial; the **bicipital aponeurosis** separates the vein from the artery. Beware accidental arterial cannulation.
- **Radial artery:** lateral to the flexor carpi radialis tendon at the wrist; collateral supply via the ulnar artery and palmar arches. Others: ulnar, brachial, femoral, dorsalis pedis.
- **Intraosseous access:** proximal tibia (1–2 cm below the tibial tuberosity, medially), proximal humerus (greater tubercle), distal femur in children. All drugs and fluids can be given; flush and use pressure for fluids.
