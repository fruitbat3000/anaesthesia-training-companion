## Monro–Kellie doctrine

The skull is a rigid box containing **brain (~80%), blood (~10%) and CSF (~10%)**. An increase in one component (tumour, haematoma, oedema) must be offset by a decrease in another, first by displacing CSF into the spinal subarachnoid space and venous blood out of the skull. Once compensation is exhausted, **ICP rises steeply** for small increases in volume (the exponential **ICP–volume (elastance) curve**).

- Normal ICP: **5–15 mmHg** (supine adult).
- **CPP = MAP − ICP** (or − CVP if higher). Adult targets after severe head injury are usually a CPP of about **60–70 mmHg** and ICP under about **20–22 mmHg**.

## Regulation of cerebral blood flow

- **Autoregulation** holds flow roughly constant over a range of MAP (classically 50–150 mmHg; wider variation between individuals than once thought), via myogenic and metabolic mechanisms.
- **PaCO~2~**: the most potent short-term controller (vasodilation with hypercapnia). Hyperventilation lowers ICP within minutes but risks ischaemia; it is reserved for short-term rescue of impending herniation.
- **PaO~2~**: flow rises sharply below about 6.7–8 kPa.
- **Metabolic coupling:** CMRO~2~ about 3.5 mL/100 g/min; reduced by propofol, barbiturates, hypothermia; increased by seizures and fever.
- **Volatile agents** are cerebral vasodilators (dose-dependent, increasing flow while reducing metabolism: "uncoupling"); propofol preserves coupling and reduces flow and ICP. Ketamine was traditionally avoided but has little effect on ICP when ventilation is controlled.

## CSF

- About **500 mL/day** is produced, mainly by the **choroid plexus** (active secretion of Na^+^, Cl^−^ and HCO~3~^−^, with water following); total volume about **150 mL** (roughly half in the spinal compartment).
- Circulation: lateral ventricles → foramina of Monro → third ventricle → cerebral aqueduct → fourth ventricle → foramina of Magendie and Luschka → subarachnoid space → reabsorbed through **arachnoid granulations** into the venous sinuses.
- Compared with plasma: similar Na^+^, **higher Cl^−^**, **lower glucose (~60% of plasma)**, lower K^+^, very little protein, slightly lower pH. CSF pH is the stimulus for central chemoreceptors.

## Signs of raised ICP and herniation

Headache, vomiting, papilloedema, falling GCS; **Cushing's response** (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular breathing) is a late sign of brainstem compression; a fixed dilated pupil suggests uncal herniation compressing the oculomotor nerve.
