## Nociceptors and afferents

- Free nerve endings responding to mechanical, thermal and chemical stimuli. **Aδ fibres** (thinly myelinated): fast, sharp, well-localised "first pain". **C fibres** (unmyelinated): slow, dull, burning, poorly localised "second pain".
- Inflammatory mediators (bradykinin, prostaglandins, H^+^, histamine, serotonin, nerve growth factor) sensitise nociceptors: **peripheral sensitisation** → primary hyperalgesia.

## The dorsal horn

- Afferents enter via the dorsal root, travel a segment or two in **Lissauer's tract** and synapse in the **dorsal horn laminae**: Aδ in laminae I and V; C fibres in laminae I and **II (substantia gelatinosa)**.
- Transmitters: **glutamate** (AMPA receptors for fast transmission; **NMDA** receptors recruited with sustained input), **substance P** and **CGRP**.
- **Gate control theory (Melzack and Wall):** activity in large Aβ touch fibres activates inhibitory interneurones in lamina II that reduce nociceptive transmission (the basis for rubbing an injury and TENS).

## Ascending pathways

- **Spinothalamic tract:** second-order neurones **cross within one or two segments** in the anterior white commissure and ascend in the anterolateral cord to the **thalamus** (lateral: discriminative; medial: affective), then to the somatosensory and cingulate cortex and insula.
- Spinoreticular and spinomesencephalic tracts contribute to arousal and emotional aspects.

## Descending modulation

From the **periaqueductal grey** and **rostroventral medulla** to the dorsal horn, using **endogenous opioids**, **noradrenaline** (α~2~) and **serotonin**. This is a target for opioids, and for tricyclics, SNRIs and α~2~-agonists.

## Central sensitisation and chronic pain

- Repeated C-fibre input → **wind-up** (NMDA-dependent increase in dorsal horn response), expanded receptive fields, **secondary hyperalgesia** and **allodynia** (pain from normally non-painful stimuli).
- **Neuropathic pain** follows damage to the nervous system: ectopic discharges, sodium channel changes, loss of inhibition. Treated with gabapentinoids, tricyclics and SNRIs rather than simple analgesics.
- **Visceral pain** is poorly localised and **referred** to the dermatomes that share spinal segments (e.g. the heart to T1–T4 and the left arm).
- **Labour pain:** first stage via visceral afferents to **T10–L1**; second stage adds somatic pain from the perineum via the **pudendal nerve (S2–4)**.
- Acute pain that is poorly controlled after surgery is a risk factor for **chronic post-surgical pain**.
