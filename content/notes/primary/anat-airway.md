## Nose and pharynx

- **Nasal cavity:** septum (Little's area anteriorly bleeds easily), three turbinates; the floor runs horizontally backwards — pass nasal tubes **along the floor**, not upwards.
- **Pharynx:** nasopharynx (adenoids), oropharynx, laryngopharynx (hypopharynx, including the piriform fossae), from the skull base to **C6** where it becomes the oesophagus at the cricopharyngeus.

## The larynx (C3–C6 in adults)

- **Cartilages:** unpaired **thyroid**, **cricoid** (the only complete ring; at **C6**), epiglottis; paired **arytenoids**, corniculate, cuneiform.
- **Membranes:** thyrohyoid membrane (pierced by the internal laryngeal nerve), **cricothyroid membrane** (between thyroid and cricoid; about 10 mm high in adults; the site for emergency front-of-neck access), cricotracheal ligament.
- **Vocal cords** attach from the arytenoids to the back of the thyroid cartilage. The glottis is the narrowest part of the adult airway.

## Muscles

| Muscle | Action | Nerve |
|---|---|---|
| **Posterior cricoarytenoid** | The **only abductor** (opens the cords) | Recurrent laryngeal |
| Lateral cricoarytenoid, transverse and oblique arytenoids | Adduction | Recurrent laryngeal |
| Thyroarytenoid (vocalis) | Relaxes/shortens cords | Recurrent laryngeal |
| **Cricothyroid** | **Tenses** the cords (pitch) | **External branch of the superior laryngeal nerve** |

## Innervation

- **Sensory:** above the cords — **internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve** (vagus). Below the cords — **recurrent laryngeal nerve**. Posterior tongue and oropharynx — **glossopharyngeal nerve**. Nose — trigeminal branches.
- **Motor:** all intrinsic muscles by the **recurrent laryngeal nerve** except cricothyroid.
- The **left recurrent laryngeal nerve** loops under the aortic arch (at risk in thoracic surgery and with mediastinal masses); the right loops under the subclavian artery. Both are at risk in thyroidectomy.
- **Nerve injuries:** unilateral RLN palsy → hoarse voice (cord in paramedian position); **bilateral RLN palsy → stridor and airway obstruction** (cords adducted); SLN injury → weak, low-pitched voice.
- **Laryngospasm:** reflex adduction mediated by the superior laryngeal nerve (sensory) and recurrent laryngeal nerve (motor).

## Landmarks

- **Cricoid pressure:** on the cricoid cartilage (C6), about 10 N when awake and 30 N after loss of consciousness, compressing the oesophagus against the vertebral body (evidence for its effectiveness is debated; release if it impedes laryngoscopy or ventilation).
- **Front-of-neck access:** identify the cricothyroid membrane by palpation (laryngeal handshake) or ultrasound **before** induction in predicted difficult airways. DAS recommends a **scalpel–bougie–tube** technique for emergency surgical cricothyroidotomy.
- **Airway blocks** for awake intubation: glossopharyngeal (posterior tongue), superior laryngeal (at the greater horn of the hyoid), and transtracheal injection through the cricothyroid membrane; or topical local anaesthesia (spray-as-you-go).
