## Principles (DAS)

The DAS guidelines (2025 update, superseding 2015) set out a structured, stepwise approach. The consistent principles are:

- **Oxygenation is the priority**, not intubation. Maintain oxygenation throughout (pre-oxygenation, apnoeic oxygenation).
- **Limit attempts** at each technique to avoid airway trauma and swelling; each attempt should be the best possible (optimise position, device, adjuncts such as a bougie, external laryngeal manipulation, video laryngoscopy, adequate neuromuscular block).
- **Declare the difficulty** early and **call for help**; use clear, shared language.
- After failed intubation, **rescue oxygenation with a second-generation supraglottic airway**; if that fails, attempt **facemask ventilation** (two-person technique, adjuncts).
- If oxygenation is possible, **stop and think**: wake the patient up (reverse, e.g. sugammadex after rocuronium), continue surgery via the SGA if necessary and safe, or intubate through the SGA with a flexible scope, or perform a tracheostomy/cricothyroidotomy.
- **Can't intubate, can't oxygenate (CICO):** **declare it** and proceed to **emergency front-of-neck access** without delay: ensure neuromuscular block, extend the neck, and perform a **scalpel–bougie–tube cricothyroidotomy** (a transverse stab incision through the cricothyroid membrane, rotate the blade, pass a bougie, railroad a small cuffed tracheal tube of the size set out in the DAS guidance). Use a vertical skin incision first if the membrane is impalpable.
- **After the event:** document, explain to the patient, complete an airway alert (letter to the patient and GP, entry in the notes), report as an incident, and ensure follow-up.

**Always read the current DAS guideline and algorithm** (BJA 2026 publication and DAS website): the details (number of attempts, specific steps) are set there, and local departments teach and rehearse them.

## Obstetric and critical care variations

- **Obstetrics (OAA/DAS):** a pre-planned decision about whether to **wake or proceed** with surgery via the SGA, depending on maternal and fetal factors; failed intubation drills are practised by the whole team.
- **Critically ill patients (DAS/ICS/FICM/RCoA 2018):** a checklist, pre-oxygenation with CPAP/NIV or HFNO, video laryngoscopy, and early front-of-neck access, recognising that physiological difficulty (hypoxaemia, hypotension) adds risk.

## NAP4 lessons

NAP4 (2011) found that airway complications were more likely in ICU and ED, with obesity, with failure to use capnography, and with poor planning. Waveform capnography is now mandatory wherever patients are intubated.
