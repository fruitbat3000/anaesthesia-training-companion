## Flow and volume measurement

| Device | Principle | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Wright respirometer** | A vane rotates as gas passes through tangential slots; gears count volume | Measures **volume**; over-reads at high flows and under-reads at low flows (inertia); unidirectional |
| **Pneumotachograph** (Fleisch) | Gas flows through a **laminar-flow resistor**; the **pressure drop is proportional to flow** (Hagen–Poiseuille); integration gives volume | Heated to prevent condensation; calibrated for gas viscosity |
| **Pitot tube** | Pressure difference between openings facing towards and away from flow | Used in some anaesthetic spirometry (e.g. D-lite) |
| **Hot-wire anemometer** | Gas flow cools a heated wire; the current needed to keep its temperature constant relates to flow | ICU ventilators |
| **Ultrasonic flowmeter** | Transit-time differences of ultrasound up- and downstream | |
| **Rotameter** | Variable-orifice flowmeter (see the anaesthetic machine) | |
| Peak flow meter | Variable orifice (Wright peak flow meter) | Peak expiratory flow (L/min) |

## Spirometry

- **Vitalograph** (bellows-type) or electronic spirometers record volume against time: **FEV~1~** (forced expiratory volume in 1 s), **FVC**, and **FEV~1~/FVC**.
- **Obstructive** (asthma, COPD): **FEV~1~/FVC < 0.7** (or below the lower limit of normal), FVC normal or reduced. **Restrictive** (fibrosis, chest wall disease, obesity): both reduced proportionally, ratio **normal or high**.
- **Flow–volume loops:** obstructive airways give a scooped expiratory limb. **Fixed upper airway obstruction** (e.g. tracheal stenosis) flattens **both** limbs; **variable extrathoracic** obstruction (e.g. vocal cord palsy) flattens the **inspiratory** limb; **variable intrathoracic** obstruction (e.g. tracheomalacia, a mid-tracheal tumour) flattens the **expiratory** limb.

## FRC and diffusion

- **FRC and RV:** helium dilution, nitrogen washout, or body plethysmography (see lung volumes).
- **Transfer factor (DLCO / TLCO):** single-breath carbon monoxide uptake (CO binds Hb avidly, so uptake is diffusion-limited). Reduced in emphysema, fibrosis, pulmonary vascular disease and anaemia; increased with polycythaemia and alveolar haemorrhage. KCO corrects for alveolar volume.
- **Perioperative relevance:** predicted postoperative FEV~1~ and DLCO guide risk in lung resection (Final syllabus).
