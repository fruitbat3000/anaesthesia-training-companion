## Phase I and phase II

- **Phase I (functionalisation):** oxidation (mainly **cytochrome P450**, in the smooth ER), reduction and hydrolysis. May produce **active metabolites** (e.g. morphine-6-glucuronide is formed in phase II; codeine → morphine by CYP2D6 in phase I; diazepam → desmethyldiazepam).
- **Phase II (conjugation):** glucuronidation, sulphation, acetylation, methylation, glutathione conjugation → water-soluble products excreted in urine or bile.
- **Pro-drugs** need metabolism to become active: codeine, tramadol (partly), clopidogrel, parecoxib (→ valdecoxib), enalapril.

## Cytochrome P450

The main enzymes: **CYP3A4** (about half of all drugs, including midazolam, fentanyl, alfentanil, lidocaine, ciclosporin), **CYP2D6** (codeine, tramadol, many antidepressants and β-blockers), CYP2C9 (warfarin, NSAIDs), CYP2E1 (volatile agents, ethanol, paracetamol toxicity), CYP1A2 (caffeine, theophylline).

| Inducers (↑ metabolism; onset over days) | Inhibitors (↓ metabolism; rapid onset) |
|---|---|
| Carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, rifampicin, St John's wort, chronic alcohol, smoking (CYP1A2) | Erythromycin and clarithromycin, azole antifungals, ritonavir, amiodarone, cimetidine, grapefruit juice, SSRIs (CYP2D6), acute alcohol |

Example: erythromycin inhibits CYP3A4 → prolonged midazolam and alfentanil.

## Elimination without liver enzymes

- **Hofmann degradation:** spontaneous, pH- and temperature-dependent breakdown in plasma (**atracurium**, **cisatracurium**). Slowed by hypothermia and acidosis. **Laudanosine** is a metabolite (CNS excitation at very high levels).
- **Ester hydrolysis:** **plasma cholinesterase** (suxamethonium, mivacurium, ester local anaesthetics); **non-specific tissue and red cell esterases** (**remifentanil**, esmolol, atracurium partly).
- **Renal excretion** of water-soluble drugs and metabolites (filtration, tubular secretion, pH-dependent reabsorption); **biliary** (vecuronium, rocuronium in part); **exhalation** (volatiles); breast milk and sweat (minor).

## Factors that alter elimination

Age (neonates and elderly), hepatic and renal disease, cardiac output (flow-limited drugs), hypothermia, pregnancy, obesity, interactions, genetics.

## Pharmacogenetics

| Variation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Plasma cholinesterase variants** (e.g. atypical, silent genes; dibucaine number) | Prolonged apnoea after suxamethonium or mivacurium |
| **CYP2D6 poor metabolisers** (~7–10% of white Europeans) | Little analgesia from codeine/tramadol |
| **CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolisers** (up to about 30% in parts of East Africa such as Ethiopia; also common in North African and Middle Eastern populations) | Morphine toxicity from codeine (deaths in children after tonsillectomy) |
| **Slow acetylators** (NAT2; about half of Europeans) | Toxicity with hydralazine, isoniazid, procainamide |
| **Malignant hyperthermia susceptibility** (RYR1, CACNA1S) | MH with volatiles or suxamethonium |
| **G6PD deficiency** | Haemolysis with oxidant drugs |
| **Acute porphyrias** | Attacks precipitated by enzyme inducers (e.g. barbiturates) |
