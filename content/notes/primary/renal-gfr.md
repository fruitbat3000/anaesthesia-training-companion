## Renal blood flow

- About **1.2 L/min (20–25% of cardiac output)**; plasma flow about 650 mL/min.
- **Autoregulation** keeps flow and GFR fairly constant between a MAP of about **80–180 mmHg** (quoted ranges vary), by:
  - the **myogenic response** of the afferent arteriole (stretch → constriction);
  - **tubuloglomerular feedback**: the **macula densa** senses NaCl delivery to the distal tubule; increased delivery → afferent vasoconstriction (adenosine) and less renin.
- Sympathetic stimulation, catecholamines and angiotensin II reduce flow. Angiotensin II constricts the **efferent** arteriole preferentially, supporting GFR when perfusion is low; this is why ACE inhibitors and ARBs can precipitate AKI in hypovolaemia or renal artery stenosis. NSAIDs remove prostaglandin-mediated afferent dilatation.
- The **outer cortex** is well perfused; the **medulla** receives about 10% of flow and runs a low PO~2~ (countercurrent exchange in the vasa recta), making it vulnerable to hypoxic injury.

## Glomerular filtration

- **GFR ≈ 125 mL/min (180 L/day)** in a young adult; **filtration fraction** (GFR / renal plasma flow) ≈ **20%**.
- Starling forces across the glomerular capillary: **GFR = Kf [(P~GC~ − P~BS~) − (π~GC~ − π~BS~)]**. Glomerular capillary pressure is high (about 45–60 mmHg; figures vary between sources) because the efferent arteriole provides downstream resistance.
- The filtration barrier (fenestrated endothelium, negatively charged basement membrane, podocyte slit diaphragms) excludes molecules above about 70 kDa; albumin (~69 kDa, negatively charged) is largely retained.

## Clearance

- **Clearance = (U × V) / P**: the volume of plasma completely cleared of a substance per minute.
- **Inulin** is freely filtered, neither reabsorbed nor secreted: its clearance = GFR (the gold standard).
- **Creatinine** clearance slightly **overestimates** GFR because a little creatinine is secreted.
- **Para-aminohippuric acid (PAH)** is filtered and almost completely secreted in one pass: its clearance ≈ effective renal plasma flow.
- **eGFR** (e.g. CKD-EPI) estimates GFR from plasma creatinine, age and sex; it is unreliable when creatinine is not at steady state (AKI) or muscle mass is abnormal. **Cockcroft–Gault** estimates creatinine clearance and is still used for some drug dosing.

## Urea and creatinine

- **Creatinine** comes from muscle creatine at a fairly constant rate; plasma level rises only after GFR has fallen substantially and depends on muscle mass.
- **Urea** is filtered and partly reabsorbed (more when flow is low, so a high urea:creatinine ratio suggests hypovolaemia); it also rises with protein load, GI bleeding, catabolism and steroids.

## Acute kidney injury

- Defined (KDIGO, as adopted by NICE) by a rise in creatinine of ≥ 26 µmol/L within 48 h or ≥ 1.5 × baseline within 7 days, or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for more than 6 hours.
- Perioperative risk factors: CKD, age, diabetes, heart failure, sepsis, hypovolaemia and hypotension, nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, contrast), major and emergency surgery.
- Prevention: maintain perfusion and volume, avoid nephrotoxins, review ACE inhibitors and diuretics, monitor urine output and creatinine.
