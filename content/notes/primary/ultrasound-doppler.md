## Ultrasound

- Sound above **20 kHz**; medical imaging uses about **2–15 MHz**. Generated and received by **piezoelectric crystals**, which vibrate when a voltage is applied and generate a voltage when struck by returning echoes.
- Speed in soft tissue ≈ **1540 m/s**; the machine calculates depth from echo return time.
- **Acoustic impedance** (density × speed) differences at tissue interfaces cause reflection. Very large differences (tissue–air, tissue–bone) reflect almost everything (hence gel, and why bone and air cast shadows).
- **Frequency trade-off:** **higher frequency → better resolution but less penetration** (more attenuation). Use high-frequency linear probes (e.g. 6–15 MHz) for superficial nerves and vessels; low-frequency curvilinear probes (2–5 MHz) for deep structures.
- **Echogenicity:** hyperechoic (bright: fascia, bone surface, needles), hypoechoic, anechoic (black: fluid, vessels).

## Artefacts

**Acoustic shadowing** (behind bone or calculi), **posterior acoustic enhancement** (behind fluid-filled structures, e.g. deep to arteries), **reverberation** (between parallel reflectors, e.g. a needle shaft: comet-tail), **mirror image**, **anisotropy** (a nerve or tendon changes brightness with probe angle), **bayonet artefact** (needle appears bent because of differing tissue speeds).

## Needle guidance

- **In-plane:** the whole needle shaft is visible; keep it aligned with the narrow beam. **Out-of-plane:** the needle appears as a dot; the tip can be missed.
- Steep angles reduce needle visibility; echogenic needles and a shallow approach help. Always see the tip before injecting; hydrodissect with small volumes.

## Doppler

- **Doppler shift:** the frequency of reflected sound changes when the reflector moves: **Δf = 2 f~0~ v cos θ / c**. Velocity is estimated from the shift; accuracy depends on the **angle of insonation** (cos θ; errors grow above about 60°, and at 90° there is no shift).
- **Colour Doppler** shows direction and mean velocity (conventionally red towards and blue away from the probe: "BART"); **pulsed-wave** measures velocity at a chosen depth (limited by aliasing); **continuous-wave** measures high velocities without depth information.
- Uses: vascular identification, echocardiography, **oesophageal Doppler cardiac output** (stroke distance × aortic cross-sectional area), transcranial Doppler, fetal monitoring.

## Optical fibres

Light travels along glass fibres by **total internal reflection** (a core with a higher refractive index than its cladding, with light striking the boundary beyond the critical angle). Used in fibreoptic bronchoscopes (coherent bundles transmit an image; incoherent bundles carry light) and some sensors.
