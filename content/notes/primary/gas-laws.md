## The laws

| Law | Statement | Clinical example |
|---|---|---|
| **Boyle** | At constant temperature, **PV = constant** | Estimating cylinder contents; plethysmography; gas expansion at altitude and in diving |
| **Charles** | At constant pressure, **V ∝ T** (kelvin) | |
| **Gay-Lussac (third law)** | At constant volume, **P ∝ T** | Cylinder pressure rises if the cylinder heats up |
| **Ideal gas equation** | **PV = nRT** | 1 mole of gas occupies **22.4 L at STP** (0°C, 101.3 kPa) |
| **Avogadro** | Equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules | Calculating vapour volumes from liquid anaesthetic |
| **Dalton** | Total pressure = sum of partial pressures | Alveolar gas equation; P~H2O~ at 37°C = 6.3 kPa |
| **Henry** | At a given temperature, dissolved gas ∝ its partial pressure | Dissolved O~2~ (0.023 mL/dL/kPa); decompression sickness |
| **Graham** | Rate of diffusion ∝ 1/√(molecular weight) | |

**Example:** how much vapour does 1 mL of liquid isoflurane (MW 184.5, density 1.5 g/mL) produce? 1.5 g / 184.5 = 0.0081 mol × 22.4 L = about 0.18 L at STP (about 195 mL at 20°C).

## Critical temperature and pressure

- **Critical temperature:** the temperature above which a gas **cannot be liquefied by pressure alone**. **Critical pressure:** the pressure needed to liquefy it at its critical temperature.
- A **gas** is above its critical temperature; a **vapour** is below it.
- **Oxygen** (critical temperature −119°C) is a gas at room temperature: cylinder pressure falls **in proportion to contents** (Boyle), so the gauge shows how full it is.
- **Nitrous oxide** (critical temperature **36.5°C**) is a **liquid in equilibrium with its vapour** in the cylinder at room temperature: the gauge reads the **saturated vapour pressure (~44 bar at 15°C, ~52 bar at 20°C)** until almost all the liquid has gone, so **contents must be judged by weight**. Cylinders are filled to a **filling ratio** (mass of N~2~O / mass of water the cylinder could hold) of **0.75** in temperate climates (0.67 in the tropics) to allow for thermal expansion.
- **Entonox** (50:50 O~2~/N~2~O) has a **pseudo-critical temperature of about −6°C**: below it, N~2~O liquefies and separates (the Poynting effect keeps it mixed above this).
- **Carbon dioxide** (critical temperature 31°C) is also stored as a liquid.
