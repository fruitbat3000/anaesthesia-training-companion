## Non-invasive (oscillometric) blood pressure

- A cuff inflates above systolic then deflates in steps; a transducer detects **oscillations** in cuff pressure caused by arterial pulsation.
- **Mean arterial pressure = the point of maximum oscillation** (the most accurately measured value). Systolic and diastolic are **calculated** by algorithms (proprietary).
- **Cuff size:** bladder width about **40% of the arm circumference** and length about 80%. **Too small a cuff overestimates** blood pressure; too large underestimates.
- **Errors:** arrhythmias (AF), shivering and movement, very low or high pressures, external compression. Tends to overestimate at low pressures and underestimate at high pressures. Complications: ulnar nerve injury, skin damage, compartment syndrome with frequent cycling.
- Manual methods: **Korotkoff sounds** (phase I systolic; phase V disappearance = diastolic); Riva-Rocci; von Recklinghausen oscillotonometer (historical).

## Invasive arterial pressure

- **Components:** arterial cannula → non-compliant, fluid-filled tubing → **transducer** (a diaphragm moving a **strain gauge** arranged in a **Wheatstone bridge**, which converts changes in resistance to a voltage) → amplifier and display. A pressurised flush (about 300 mmHg, ~3 mL/h) keeps the line patent.
- **Zeroing:** open the transducer to air to set atmospheric pressure as zero. **Levelling:** position the transducer at the **level of the heart** (the phlebostatic axis: 4th intercostal space, mid-axillary line) — or at the external auditory meatus for cerebral perfusion pressure in neurosurgery. A transducer **10 cm below** the heart reads **~7.5 mmHg too high**.
- **Waveform information:** systolic, diastolic and mean; **dicrotic notch** (aortic valve closure); slope of upstroke (contractility, roughly); area under the systolic curve (stroke volume, used by pulse contour devices); **pulse pressure and systolic pressure variation** with ventilation (fluid responsiveness); rhythm. The waveform gets **narrower and taller distally** (systolic amplification), while mean pressure falls slightly.
- **Indications:** beat-to-beat monitoring (major surgery, haemodynamic instability, vasoactive drugs), frequent blood sampling, and when NIBP is impossible.
- **Complications:** thrombosis and distal ischaemia (check collateral flow; Allen's test is unreliable), haemorrhage from disconnection, infection, **inadvertent intra-arterial drug injection** (label clearly), air embolism, false aneurysm, nerve damage.
