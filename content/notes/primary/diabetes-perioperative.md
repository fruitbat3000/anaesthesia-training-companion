## Before surgery

- Identify diabetes, its type, treatment, control (HbA1c), complications (cardiac, renal, autonomic neuropathy — gastroparesis and cardiovascular instability, "stiff joint syndrome" with difficult laryngoscopy) and hypoglycaemia awareness.
- Optimise control where time allows; plan for a **short fast** and a **first-on-the-list** slot; provide written instructions for medication; involve the diabetes team for complex regimens.

## On the day

- Check **capillary glucose** on arrival and at least hourly during surgery (more often if on insulin infusion). Aim for about **6–10 mmol/L** (up to 12 acceptable). Check ketones in type 1 diabetes and SGLT2 inhibitor users if unwell or glucose high.
- Medication is adjusted **drug by drug** following the current guideline (e.g. hold sulphonylureas while fasting; continue long-acting basal insulin at a reduced dose; SGLT2 inhibitors are usually stopped beforehand; consider GLP-1 agonist implications for gastric emptying).
- **Variable-rate intravenous insulin infusion (VRIII)** when more than one meal will be missed, control is poor, or the usual regimen cannot be used: soluble insulin with a substrate fluid containing glucose and potassium (e.g. glucose–saline with KCl). **Continue basal insulin** alongside a VRIII.
- Avoid excessive dexamethasone doses in poorly controlled patients (hyperglycaemia); watch for hypoglycaemia in recovery.
- Return to the usual regimen when eating and drinking; overlap the first subcutaneous insulin dose before stopping the VRIII.

## Emergencies

| | **DKA** | **HHS** | **Hypoglycaemia** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Features | Glucose > 11 (or known diabetes; may be normal with SGLT2i), **ketones ≥ 3 mmol/L**, pH < 7.3 or HCO~3~^−^ < 15 | Very high glucose (often > 30), hyperosmolality > 320 mOsm/kg, severe dehydration, little ketosis; older type 2 patients | Glucose < 4 mmol/L; under anaesthesia signs are masked (sweating, tachycardia) |
| Treatment | Fluids, **fixed-rate IV insulin** (0.1 unit/kg/h), potassium, glucose once < 14, treat the cause | Slow fluid replacement, cautious sodium correction, low-dose insulin later, VTE prophylaxis | IV glucose (e.g. 75–100 mL of 20% glucose) if fasting or unconscious; oral carbohydrate if able; review the cause |
