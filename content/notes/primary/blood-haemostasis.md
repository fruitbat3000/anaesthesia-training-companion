## Primary haemostasis

Vessel injury → vasoconstriction → **platelet adhesion** to exposed collagen via **von Willebrand factor** (binding platelet **GPIb**) → **activation** (shape change; release of ADP, thromboxane A~2~, serotonin) → **aggregation** via **GPIIb/IIIa** receptors cross-linked by fibrinogen. Normal platelet count 150–400 × 10^9^/L; lifespan 7–10 days.

## Coagulation: the cell-based model

1. **Initiation:** **tissue factor** (TF) on subendothelial cells binds **factor VIIa**, activating small amounts of IX and X → a little thrombin.
2. **Amplification:** thrombin activates platelets and factors **V, VIII and XI**.
3. **Propagation:** on the activated platelet surface, the **tenase (IXa–VIIIa)** and **prothrombinase (Xa–Va)** complexes generate a burst of thrombin, which converts **fibrinogen to fibrin**; **factor XIIIa** cross-links it.

- The classical **extrinsic** (TF, VII) and **intrinsic** (XII, XI, IX, VIII) pathways remain useful for interpreting laboratory tests.
- **Vitamin K-dependent factors:** II, VII, IX, X (and proteins C and S). Calcium is required for most steps (citrate anticoagulates stored blood).

## Natural anticoagulants and fibrinolysis

- **Antithrombin** inactivates thrombin and Xa (accelerated about 1000-fold by heparin). **Protein C** (activated by thrombin–thrombomodulin) with **protein S** inactivates Va and VIIIa. **Tissue factor pathway inhibitor** limits initiation.
- **Fibrinolysis:** **tPA** converts plasminogen to **plasmin**, which breaks down fibrin into fibrin degradation products (**D-dimers**). **Tranexamic acid** blocks lysine-binding sites on plasminogen.

## Tests

| Test | Measures | Prolonged by |
|---|---|---|
| **PT / INR** | Extrinsic and common (VII, X, V, II, fibrinogen) | Warfarin, liver disease, vitamin K deficiency, DIC |
| **APTT** | Intrinsic and common (XII, XI, IX, VIII, X, V, II) | Unfractionated heparin, haemophilia, lupus anticoagulant (in vitro) |
| **Fibrinogen (Clauss)** | Normal ~2–4 g/L; ≥ 2 g/L is a common target in obstetric haemorrhage | Dilution, consumption (DIC, obstetric haemorrhage) |
| Anti-Xa assay | Heparin effect (LMWH, UFH) and some DOACs | |
| **Viscoelastic tests (TEG, ROTEM)** | Whole-blood clot formation, strength and lysis | Guide targeted treatment in major haemorrhage |

- Viscoelastic tests show the **time to start clotting** (factor deficiency), **clot strength** (fibrinogen and platelets) and **lysis** (hyperfibrinolysis), within minutes. Their use in obstetric haemorrhage is supported by a national consensus.

## Common disorders

- **Haemophilia A (VIII) and B (IX):** X-linked; prolonged APTT; factor replacement before surgery with haematology input.
- **Von Willebrand disease:** the commonest inherited bleeding disorder; mucosal bleeding; **desmopressin** releases vWF in type 1.
- **DIC:** widespread activation with consumption of factors and platelets and secondary fibrinolysis (sepsis, obstetric emergencies, trauma).
- **Liver disease:** reduced synthesis of both pro- and anticoagulant factors ("rebalanced" haemostasis): INR overstates bleeding risk.
