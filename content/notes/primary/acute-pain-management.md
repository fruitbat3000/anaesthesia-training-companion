## Assessment

- **Numerical rating scale** (0–10), verbal rating scales; **functional assessment** (can the patient cough, breathe deeply and mobilise?) is often more useful than the score alone.
- **Special groups:** children (FLACC, faces scales), cognitive impairment and dementia (PAINAD, Abbey pain scale), communication difficulties and learning disability (involve carers), the unconscious (physiological and behavioural signs).
- The **biopsychosocial model**: anxiety, catastrophising, expectations, culture and previous experience affect pain.

## Multimodal analgesia

- Combine drugs and techniques with different mechanisms to improve analgesia and reduce opioid side effects: **paracetamol**, **NSAIDs** (if not contraindicated), **opioids** (oral where possible; PCA when needed), **local and regional anaesthesia** (infiltration, peripheral blocks, neuraxial), and adjuncts (dexamethasone, ketamine, α~2~-agonists, gabapentinoids in selected patients, IV lidocaine in some settings).
- **Rescue analgesia:** a protocol for severe pain (titrated IV opioid with monitoring, or a regional technique), after checking for new causes of pain (compartment syndrome, surgical complications).
- **Epidural and PCA management:** monitoring, side effects (sedation, respiratory depression, hypotension, pruritus, nausea, motor block), and when to call the anaesthetist.

## The acute pain service

A multidisciplinary team (nurses, anaesthetists, pharmacists) that reviews patients with complex analgesia, supports wards with protocols and education, audits outcomes, and plans transition to oral analgesia and discharge (including safe opioid prescribing and deprescribing).

## Chronic pain and opioid tolerance

- **Chronic pain** (pain for more than 3 months) is common. Many patients take long-term opioids, gabapentinoids or antidepressants.
- **Opioid-tolerant patients:** continue usual opioids (or equivalent) perioperatively to prevent withdrawal, anticipate higher requirements, maximise non-opioid and regional techniques, consider ketamine; **buprenorphine** and **methadone** need specific plans (usually continued); involve the pain team; plan discharge analgesia and follow-up.
- **Limitations of pain medicine:** opioids have poor long-term effectiveness for chronic non-cancer pain and carry risks of dependence; acute pain management aims to restore function, not to abolish all pain.
- Poorly controlled acute pain is a risk factor for **chronic post-surgical pain**.
