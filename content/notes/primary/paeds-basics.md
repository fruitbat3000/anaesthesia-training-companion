## Preparation

- **Assessment:** history from child and parent, previous anaesthetics, prematurity, syndromes, airway, **loose teeth**, recent infections, bleeding tendency, weight.
- **Psychological preparation:** play specialists, explanation in age-appropriate language, parental presence at induction where appropriate, distraction.
- **Fasting (UK consensus):** solids and formula 6 h, **breast milk 4 h**, **clear fluids until 1 h** before anaesthesia.
- **Premedication:** topical local anaesthetic cream (EMLA, tetracaine) for cannulation; oral midazolam or intranasal dexmedetomidine/clonidine for anxious children.
- **Consent:** from a person with parental responsibility; **Gillick competence** in under-16s who understand the treatment; a young person's refusal may be overridden in limited circumstances (legal advice).

## Upper respiratory tract infection (URTI)

Increases the risk of **perioperative respiratory adverse events** (laryngospasm, bronchospasm, desaturation), especially with airway surgery, intubation, young age, passive smoking and moist cough/fever. **Postpone elective surgery** if the child is systemically unwell, febrile, with a productive cough or chest signs; otherwise weigh the risks (airway hyper-reactivity lasts for weeks). Using an SGA rather than a tube, avoiding desflurane, and experienced anaesthetists reduce the risk.

## Conduct of anaesthesia (children over 5 in Stage 1)

- **Induction:** IV (with topical anaesthesia) or **inhalational with sevoflurane** (in oxygen ± N~2~O). Beware of laryngospasm in light planes.
- **Equipment:** appropriately sized masks, airways, SGAs (by weight), tracheal tubes (cuffed tubes now common; check leak or cuff pressure), T-piece (Mapleson F) for small children, warming.
- **Drugs by weight**, with double-checking: for example atropine 20 µg/kg, suxamethonium 1–2 mg/kg (higher in infants), paracetamol and NSAIDs by weight (check BNF for Children), fluids 10 mL/kg boluses.
- **Analgesia:** paracetamol, NSAIDs, opioids by weight, and regional techniques such as the **caudal block** (e.g. levobupivacaine 0.2%, 0.5–1 mL/kg depending on the height needed, keeping within the maximum dose — see the BNF for Children and local guidance), penile block, ilioinguinal block, and wound infiltration.
- **PONV:** common after age 3; ondansetron and dexamethasone.
- **Emergence:** sevoflurane agitation (exclude pain, hypoxia, full bladder); dexmedetomidine or clonidine can reduce it.

## The sick child

- **Recognition:** ABCDE with paediatric normal values; tachypnoea, recession, grunting, poor feeding, lethargy, prolonged capillary refill, tachycardia; bradycardia and hypotension are late signs.
- **Airway emergencies:** **croup** (viral; barking cough; nebulised adrenaline, dexamethasone), **epiglottitis** (toxic, drooling, sitting forward; do not upset the child; senior anaesthetist and ENT; gas induction in theatre), **inhaled foreign body** (rigid bronchoscopy; spontaneous ventilation strategy often preferred).
- **Paediatric life support (RCUK):** 5 rescue breaths, compressions at 15:2, adrenaline 10 µg/kg, defibrillation 4 J/kg; know the local escalation and transfer pathway (retrieval teams).
